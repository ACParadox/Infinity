# Infinity N5.3 — Overall Faction & Sectorial Rankings (1–45)

*Last reviewed: 13 November 2026. Includes all 45 Vanilla factions, sectorials and NA2 armies in the repository's N5.3 roster set.*

## What this ranking means

This is a **qualitative, editorial competitive-strength ranking**, **not** measured faction win rates, an official Corvus Belli tier list, or a statistical prediction of a particular matchup. It preserves the **user-provided tier boundaries and exact left-to-right order within each tier** as the complete #1–#45 ranking. The accompanying one-line explanations are qualitative context informed by the faction guides and external discussion, not independent justification for changing those positions. Adjacent positions, especially in the same tier, are close calls.

The primary contemporary discussion is Robert Shepherd with Jordan (*Loss of Lieutenant*) in their [2026 all-factions comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1dW3i9dpI34) (published **13 June 2026**, video title says “July 2026 Mazebreaker Update”). This is cross-checked against their [October 2025 N5 tier review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gHIXG1moDZw), *Loss of Lieutenant* [Episode 132 — N5 Faction Tierlist](https://lossoflieutenant.podbean.com/e/episode-132-n5-faction-tierlist/) (**24 December 2024**; older first impressions), and our N5.3 [sectorial capability matrix](sectorial-capability-access-matrix-n5-3.md), [individual listbuilding guides](factions/) and [battle-report mission analysis](battle-reports/cross-report-mission-performance-by-sectorial.md). The content creators do **not** necessarily endorse the exact positions below.

## Parent-faction rankings — common strengths across armies

This is a **separate #1–#11 ranking of the parent factions/army families**, judging what works **repeatedly across most of their constituent Vanilla and sectorial rosters**, rather than how strong their single best army is. It considers shared offensive and defensive tools, mission play, points/Order efficiency, breadth of reliable plans and the typical floor across the family. It is **not** a mean of the #1–#45 placements or a statistically measured tournament ranking. The ranks in parentheses are cross-checks against the unchanged detailed table below; **Next Wave belongs to Combined Army**, not O-12. Parent-family tiers are comparative within this table, not a recalculation of constituent army tiers.

| # | Tier | Parent faction | Constituent army ranks (from below) | Strengths common across its armies | Recurring limits / why not higher |
| ---: | :---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **S** | **ALEPH** | 2, 3, 16 | Elite, efficient individual pieces; sophisticated attack and positioning options; strong mission-capable specialists and high-quality defenses across all three rosters. | High points costs reduce redundancy; Steel Phalanx plays differently and has less of the OSS-style Repeater/Hacking network. |
| **2** | **S** | **PanOceania** | 1, 5, 8, 13, 18, 20, 40 | Excellent ranged gunfighting, visors/anti-marker shooting, tough premium threats and credible TAG/HI-supported play appear repeatedly; six of seven armies are B+ or better. | Almost no conventional Smoke and some expensive, more linear solutions; WinterFor is a significant low-end exception. ALEPH's three rosters are collectively more consistent. |
| **3** | **A** | **Nomads** | 11, 15, 24, 32 | Broad, repeatable hacking and Repeater access, Smoke/Eclipse options, flexible midfield control, efficient disruptive units and specialists in every roster. | Premium raw shooting and durability are less universal, and several lists depend on combining interlocking support packages. |
| **4** | **A** | **Haqqislam** | 4, 21, 23, 33 | Strong mission specialists, Smoke, asymmetric midfield threats, efficient disruption and viable hacking recur across Vanilla, Hassassins, Qapu and Ramah. | The standout Vanilla command/Impersonation engine is not common to all; some armies have thinner long-range dominance, resilience or spare Orders. |
| **5** | **A−** | **Combined Army** | 6, 7, 12, 35, 41 | Dangerous advanced weapons and hackers, credible TAGs, and unusual offensive options across all five rosters; several have exceptional ceilings. | The specific winning packages vary sharply between Morats, Next Wave, Shasvastii and Onyx; two C-range armies reduce family-wide reliability. |
| **6** | **A−** | **Yu Jing** | 9, 19, 27, 29 | Capable heavy infantry, strong direct guns, practical hacking, command/Order efficiencies and flexible supporting troops throughout the family. | Premium Hackable pieces can consume too much of a list; the top-end Camo, Smoke and independent-threat breadth of Vanilla is not shared equally. |
| **7** | **B+** | **Ariadna** | 10, 14, 22, 30, 37, 39 | Cheap Smoke and trading pieces, non-Hackable troops, mines/Camouflage or forward pressure and efficient attrition plans are repeatedly available. | Limited high-grade hacking, Repeaters, visors and advanced-tech counters create recurring matchup and mission constraints. |
| **8** | **B** | **JSA** | 25, 26, 31 | Aggressive close-range and melee threats, Smoke/marker play, mobile attackers and mission-capable ambushers define a coherent family. | Long-range answers, robust Hacking Area projection and all-purpose shooting are less consistently available; attack plans can be matchup-sensitive. |
| **9** | **B−** | **O-12** | 28, 34, 36 | Competent elite guns, quality specialists, hackers and nonlethal objective-control tools recur across all three rosters. | Expensive models and teams make Order redundancy, cheap screening and layered mission coverage consistently harder. **Next Wave is not an O-12 army.** |
| **10** | **C** | **Tohaa** | 38 | Distinctive Triads, Symbiont resilience and defensive/visibility tricks give a real, coherent identity. | Only **one** Tohaa roster is represented, so there is no cross-sectorial consistency test; its present ceiling and matchup breadth rank below most multi-army families. |
| **11** | **C−** | **Non-Aligned Armies (NA2)** | 17, 42, 43, 44, 45 | Mixed mercenary tools, unusual Fireteam compositions and selective efficient trades are recurring themes. | NA2 is an umbrella of heterogeneous companies, **not** a single shared roster identity: most struggle with dependable combined-arms depth. White Company is the clear high-performing exception. |

**Reading the order:** ALEPH beats PanO here on the *typical member*, despite Vanilla PanO being #1 individually. Nomads edge Haqqislam on consistency of shared networks/tools, not on Vanilla strength. Combined Army stays ahead of Yu Jing for its repeatable high-end technology and three very strong rosters, but loses ground for internal volatility. JSA is above O-12 because its constituent entries are more consistently competitive even though O-12 has several excellent individual units. Tohaa is a single-army case; NA2 is an especially loose collective, so ranks #10–11 are less directly comparable to the other parent factions.
