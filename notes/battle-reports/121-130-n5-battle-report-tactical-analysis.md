## #121 — White Banner vs Corregidor: Akial Interference

[Full Robert Shepherd report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQ58Ez86Vi8). **ITS17 Akial**, exact played N5 patch not confirmed. **DRAW**, after Corregidor made a last-turn successful antenna cancellation and failed a second attempt.

- **Observed:** White Banner intentionally ran **two Yin-She + Ye Mao Fireteam pairs** rather than its conventional configuration, especially **Ye Mao AP Spitfire with Yin-She Specialist Operative**. The duo used Super-Jump/vertical LoF to kill a Corregidor Jaguar, then advanced together to complete **HVT Follow-Up** and other classified interactions. **Liang Kai** and other high-threat bodies provided further pressure.
- **Observed:** Corregidor used **Mobile Brigada Fireteam, Tomcat Engineer, Moran/Repeater and Jaguar**. Despite first-time Akial play, its player completed important Classifieds, including **Vigilance and Suspected Infiltration**. A Tomcat dropped and **ran into CC with Liang Kai**, dying to numerous saves but scoring the required scenario task first — a clear **sacrificial specialist** use case.
- **Observed:** White Banner attempted to use the **central antenna to cancel Corregidor's matching symbol Classified**. In the last turn Corregidor activated that same antenna, **cancelled one White Banner completed Classified**, and survived reactive fire/Carbonite; its next WIP roll failed, preserving a draw. The author explicitly confirms tie.
- **Player opinion:** The author likes **Ye Mao AP Spitfire + Yin-She Specialist** as a promising alternative to repeatedly fielding Jin Ko, not as a fully proven must-have. He cautions Akial demands many different skill types; later-game specialists are essential.
- **Inference:** White Banner's **mobile gun + scoring specialist Duo** can exploit Corregidor's Jaguar/Brigada defenses in an Akial task race, but **Corregidor Tomcat/Mobile Brigada redundancy and late antenna cancellation** can overturn a seeming loss. No advantage proved.
- **Caveat:** Distinguish completed Classifieds from official OP. The two players made late WIP/Survival attempts and this was a newer Corregidor player's first Akial game.

**Mission lesson:** keep an **eligible central-antenna runner and overlapping classified symbols** for late cancellation, even after losing your primary attacker.

## #122 — Tunguska vs Neoterra: ITS17 Crossing Lines

[Full Robert Shepherd report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qfEt6kxda6E). Creator [dated Dec 20 2025](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/robert-shepherd-index-batreps.43336/page-2). **N5.2 / ITS17**. **Tunguska wins by one objective point**, full numeric tally not explicitly certified.

- **Observed:** The list was designed to test **two Tunguska Triggermen**, newly improved by **BS Attack (+1 Special Die)**. One was an independent reactive shooter; the other shared a **Securitate Repeater/Triggerman/Interventor Fireteam**. A **Kriza Borac, Zellenkrieger and Puppetbots/Oxots** provided additional tools.
- **Observed:** Instead, **three Interventors and the projected Repeater network** were the real winners. Guided missiles eliminated **over one third of Neoterra's army by the speaker's account**, causing Retreat early. The Triggermen accomplished little: an ostensibly favorable four-dice shot failed and one Triggerman even failed its Regen, dying outright.
- **Observed:** Neoterra used **Auxilia/Auxbots with heavy flamethrowers, Bolt/linked defenders and other elite firing positions**. Its early Auxilia trades cost Tunguska several Orders. Tunguska's **early Interventor button activation** was the single objective point that converted an otherwise likely Crossing Lines draw into a win.
- **Player opinion:** Triggermen remain potentially playable after the October patch, especially with extra Special Dice, but **this match does not demonstrate Triggerman efficiency**. Player would consider more Zellenkrieger Smoke and different Interventor Lieutenant decoy profiles.
- **Inference:** **Interventor x3 + Pitcher/Repeater + Guided** can be devastating into Neoterra's exposed support network. In **Crossing Lines**, a **central button specialist is necessary even when the enemy retreats** because opposing dead zones are difficult to invade.
- **Caveat:** Favorable Guided damage and terrible Triggerman rolls are high-variance outcomes. Do not mark Triggermen must-have or avoid from this test.

**Mission lesson:** **press at least one antenna in turn one**; do not assume dominating your nearest dead zone is sufficient.

## #123 — Morat Aggression Force vs Onyx Contact Force: CUSTOM Mothball

[Original Robert Shepherd report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qcvbP0Try84). **Unofficial Mothball Incident global-campaign mission** by A Tale of Miniatures and Dice, running Jul 18–Aug 17 (campaign year likely 2025). **Morats approximately 6–5 Onyx**, approximate closing score.

- **Observed — special mission:** Troops enter dangerous **MAZ zones** and roll WIP to scan them, risking damage 10. Scans may reveal **MAZ creatures** (worth points to kill) or the **Mothball object**. Once found, Mothball can be picked up and used in attacks for points but **cannot simply be extracted from the central exclusion area**. The scenario intentionally produces a central free-for-all.
- **Observed:** Morats used **Suryat/Sogarat-type Lieutenant, Bultrak TAG/HRMC and Oznat Smoke**, with **airborne attackers** to retrieve/activate the Mothball. **Bultrak** advanced into hidden **Onyx Noctifer missile** and **Nokias ARO**; a split-Burst response showed the tradeoff between the dangerous rocket and a critical Flash Pulse/defense.
- **Observed:** Onyx protected the object with **Noctifer Hidden Deployment, Nokias, Zeodron and Umbra Samaritan**, while Morat used **Oznat Smoke + another drop trooper** to reach a contested Mothball. The final swing involved the opposing **Zeodron holding the Mothball** and Morats trying to induce it to spend/drop it.
- **Player opinion:** The central-area tether is a good way to produce Biotechvore-like **close-range knife fights without forced general environmental damage**; author suggests small scoring changes if adapting for events.
- **Inference:** **Oznat Smoke + Combat Jump/Parachutist objective runners + Bultrak diversion** has value specifically in a **central constrained-object scenario**. Onyx's **Noctifer plus Nokias/Zeodron** is a credible counter to exposed TAG paths and ball carriers.
- **Caveat:** All MAZ scanning, Mothball attacks, creature scoring and exclusion mechanics are **CUSTOM**, not official ITS or regular N5 faction advantages.

**Mission lesson:** preposition a **second carrier** and control *who can force the Mothball to be spent and dropped*, not just who has the best HMG.

## #124 — Vanilla O-12 vs USAriadna Ranger Force: Hunting Party

[Original Fast Panda Gaming battle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZuGLkJ3aU6Q). **Old/event Hunting Party mission** used as practice for a Durham event; exact N5 revision unverified. **USARF 3–2 O-12** (host confirms own score as three).

- **Observed:** USARF used **two Blackjacks, a long-range sniper/Grunt network and a Warcor**, generating thick sector/ARO lines and destroying exposed O-12 specialists. **The Blackjack duo's resilience** mattered; both finished alive, one hurt.
- **Observed:** O-12 brought **Hippolyta, Cuervo Combat Jump, Cyberghost, Raveneye, Moonraker and other specialists**. **Cuervo failed the Combat Jump**, depriving O-12 of a planned way to threaten USAriadna rear positions. The O-12 team nonetheless achieved its Classified.
- **Observed:** The late USARF player tried several routes to remove a **Moonraker** and secure HVT; on the final Order a Grunt rifle at long range missed, leaving both sides unable to secure their HVT. Score: **both connected antenna, USAriadna killed more specialists, O-12 completed a Classified, no LT kills** = **3–2 USARF**.
- **Player opinion:** The participants called the Blackjacks effective in this game, while Cuervo's failed landing was a decisive issue; they also discussed **Deactivator** interactions after a later update.
- **Inference:** **Blackjack long-range guns + resilient Grunt reaction** help USARF in Hunting Party **when the opponent's airborne assassination fails**, but Cuervo/Hippolyta and O-12 Specialists were a meaningful counter threat.
- **Caveat:** Hunting Party is **older/event packet**, not necessarily a current ITS18 mission. Do not assert a formal N5.2.5 patch from the speaker's casual reference.

**Mission lesson:** cover all **specialist-kill scoring, antenna and Classified routes**, and plan a fallback if Combat Jump fails.

## #125 — Operation Mazebreaker starter demo: seven vs seven (not standard ITS)

[Original Fast Panda Gaming beginner-friendly demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ltvbms-cCYs). **N5.2 + May 2026 Maze­breaker Army era**, since Corvus Belli sent the new box models for a prerelease explanation. **DRAW** on the simplified server-objective contest, not an official ITS matched result.

- **Observed:** Players deliberately fielded **seven miniatures per side from the Mazebreaker starter** rather than normal 300-point roster construction, and explained AROs, movement, Combat Jump, marker states and face-to-face rolls to beginners.
- **Observed:** **OSS Asura, Naga and other ALEPH specialists** opposed **Corregidor Bearcat, Hacker/Repeater support and new box troops**. **Bearcat flank landing** threatened weak rear fighters; a **Naga Camo/Mine** protected the main center path. The **Asura's armor/MSV** could anchor a corridor yet was still vulnerable to a coordinated approach.
- **Observed:** The final objective was **to be in base contact with a server at the end of the third round**. The last attacker survived **a mine, Naga shotgun and Asura AP-Spitfire exchange**, ending with **equal points of bodies touching the server**; players called the game a draw.
- **Inference:** The starter demonstrates valuable basic tactics: **Bearcat arrival angle, Naga mine layer, repeater zone safety, and using prone movement/cover to reach an objective**. The two-faction demonstration does not show normal sectorial strength or ITS team legality.
- **Caveat:** The presenters explicitly note ordinary army-points tiebreakers could change the result, but **did not apply them**. Do not record an official OSS or Corregidor victory.

**Mission lesson:** a tough MSV Asura is an obstacle, not a guarantee; carefully time the last server contact rather than fighting the entire box.

## #126 — Corregidor vs Vanilla Haqqislam: ITS16 Decapitation

[Original Fast Panda Gaming report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lCCKIQ5LbtQ). **ITS16 Decapitation**, recorded while preparing for an ITC event in Germany, played N5 date/patch unknown inside ITS16. **Corregidor 5–4 Haqqislam**.

- **Observed:** Corregidor used **Intruder, Mobile Brigada, Evader and Vostok REM**, with **hacking/Repeaters/Koalas** to threaten the opposing Lieutenant. Haqqislam used a **heavily defended TAG, Janissary core, Asawira and Lasiq Viral Sniper** with own Hackers and close-attack.
- **Observed:** The high-value Haqq TAG went down **before generating its expected assault**, but Haqq still pressured the objective with durable Janissary/Asawira shooting. **Vostok was repaired/recovered** after being knocked down, which changed the surviving points tally and forced a desperate last attack.
- **Observed:** Haqq's last **Asawira/Al Hawwa assault** attempted to kill Corregidor's Lieutenant and swing Decapitation, but was killed in crossfire. Players explicitly counted **155 surviving army points for Corregidor versus 120 for Haqq**, producing a narrow **5–4 Corregidor**.
- **Inference:** **Vostok + Engineer support + Intruder + Repeater screen** can retain enough value to win Decapitation even against a hard Janissary/Asawira shell. Haqq must protect its expensive TAG from early hacking and preserve a backup LT-assassination approach.
- **Caveat:** Players changed/checked historical Decapitation scoring and Hacking live; **5–4** is their stated final.

**Mission lesson:** prevent easy Lieutenant exposure while maintaining **a recoverable high-points REM**, since army points remaining can decide a close Decapitation.

## #127 — OSS vs TAK: ITS16 Cutthroat (returning-player tutorial)

[Full Fast Panda Gaming report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xn34-0A2UJ0). **ITS16 Cutthroat**, recorded as a **returning N3 player's introductory N5 game** with coaching, not a tournament list test. **OSS 6–0 TAK**, separated by only **nine surviving army points** in the deciding measure.

- **Observed:** OSS brought **Asura Lieutenant with powerful Spitfire, Posthuman Proxies and other specialists**, while TAK brought **Voronin Lieutenant, Traktor Mul artillery, Scout/Tankhunter sniper/AP rifle, Strelok FO, Beast Hunter and Camo**.
- **Observed:** The veteran OSS player intentionally explained **rangebands, silhouette/cover, Irregular/Regular Orders and AROs**. Asura pressed into an exposed TAK position and remained hard to injure; **the last shooter exchanges swung on individual criticals**, so the apparently wide final OP result was actually very close in surviving army-point terms.
- **Player opinion:** The teacher notes TAK **deployed snipers too far forward to exploit their longer rangebands**. TAK's deployment had legal defensive options, but those weren't exercised by the inexperienced N5 player.
- **Inference:** **Asura/Proxy action economy** threatens a TAK opponent that exposes snipers at close Spitfire distance, but this does not demonstrate a competitive OSS faction advantage.
- **Caveat:** One side was actively coached and unfamiliar with N5. Do **not** count 6–0 toward matchup/tier ratings.

**Mission lesson:** in Cutthroat, **protect rangeband advantage and value of remaining troops**; a single 9-point survivor difference can separate scores dramatically.

## #128 — Vanilla O-12 vs Vanilla Nomads: ITS16 Biotechvore

[Original Fast Panda Gaming video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OiJXsvk5qx0). **ITS16 Biotechvore**, initial N5–N5.1.1 window, actual day unknown. **Vanilla O-12 5–2 Vanilla Nomads**.

- **Observed:** O-12 used **Varangian Smoke, Cyberghost, Raveneye, Betatrooper and Moonraker-style defense** to fight toward the safe center. Nomads mixed units from **multiple sectorials** (Mobile Brigada, Evader, Jazz, Intruder, Moderator, Morlock/Pupnik) and therefore should **not be labelled Corregidor**.
- **Observed:** Nomads' **Repeaters and Jazz network** significantly delayed O-12's central advance. In the midgame the **Biotechvore effects killed or incapacitated many Nomads** caught outside the safe area, even without O-12 directly killing them.
- **Observed:** Late O-12 units secured the opponent's HVT region while Nomads could not maintain many survivors; final **5–2 O-12**. Both speakers explicitly say the scenario rewards an early rush and punishes any player who tries to play cautiously like a normal midfield mission.
- **Inference:** **Varangian Smoke + tough O-12 center occupiers** is a useful way through Nomad Repeater zones in Biotechvore, while **Jazz hacking and deployables** could punish the rush if positioned to avoid environmental attrition.
- **Caveat:** Biotechvore is an unusual and historically controversial mission. The players hadn't played it for years. Its outcome does **not** indicate O-12 generally beats Vanilla Nomads.

**Mission lesson:** choose first turn when possible, pre-plan every Order needed to **leave the lethal deployment area** and don't trade the survival route for a fancy hacker fight.

## #129 — Dahshat Company vs Military Orders: Tic-Tac-Toe

[Fast Panda Gaming full report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9xP0OzDu9tc). **Old/local Tic-Tac-Toe nine-console mission**, exact N5 patch unknown. Auto-translated narration: **Military Orders 10–1 Dahshat**, score repeated in closing segment.

- **Observed:** Military Orders used **Knights with strong CC/short-range attack and a mobile button team including a Knight/Sergeant Hacker**; Dahshat fielded **McMurrough, Miranda Ashcroft, Zhencha Hacker, Bounty Hunters and ranged supporting mercenary pieces**. A **Zhencha** successfully activated the center console but could not recover enough objective-line scoring.
- **Observed:** Military Orders concentrated on **building connected Tic-Tac-Toe scoring lines** rather than just kills. Dahshat's late attack/console runs were repeatedly stopped by close reactive angles, and a too-aggressively positioned Lieutenant contributed to the deficit.
- **Player opinion:** The players agree the final 10–1 felt harsher than the quality of exchanges; the Dahshat player regretted **overextending an expensive LT** and failing to preserve enough Orders/scoring Specialists.
- **Inference:** **durable Knights plus fast console/room specialists** can excel in a line-completion Tic-Tac-Toe mission against mercenary lists whose power is concentrated in a few vulnerable pieces. **Dahshat McMurrough/Miranda/Zhencha** could challenge if a backup Specialist survives and a second line is planned.
- **Caveat:** Transcript underwent **severe automatic translation**, making several distinct knight/mercenary profile names uncertain. Do not infer exact legal Fireteam composition, unit-vs-unit kill or broader MO superiority.

**Mission lesson:** treat **connected lines of three** as the real victory condition; one isolated center console does not compensate for loss of two lines.

## #130 — Military Orders vs Shasvastii: ITS16 Supplies

[Original Fast Panda Gaming report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pttlHrEHmPA). **ITS16 Supplies**, played in N5 initial–N5.1.1 period; **Shasvastii 5–2 Military Orders**. Board used **locally modified low-vis/saturation zones**; distinguish from ordinary ITS terrain.

- **Observed:** Military Orders brought **Hospitaller Doctor, Knight with NCO/CC advance, Black Friar, other melee-heavy Knights and linked support**. Shasvastii used **Noctifer Hidden Missile Launcher, Cadmus Camouflage/Stealth, Victor Messer and other Shasvastii hidden/deceptive tools**.
- **Observed:** A Knight repeatedly **Dodged forward into close combat with a Noctifer**, eventually removing it despite the missile ARO, while the **Hospitaller Doctor revived a knocked-out ally**. The encounter was entertaining but Order-intensive and not always directly tied to Supplies scoring.
- **Observed:** In the final turn **Cadmus stayed prone, Cautious Moved and approached the final unattended Supply Box**, avoiding visible Knights, then lifted it. Victor Messer attempted an unrelated Classified/advance and fell short. Shasvastii finished with **one box and one Classified**, MO with **two Classifieds but zero boxes**, thus **5–2**.
- **Inference:** Shasvastii **Camo/Stealth Cadmus + Noctifer disruption** can outscore an MO assault that commits numerous Orders to hero CC instead of protecting the Supply Boxes. MO's Doctor and NCO remain valuable, but must escort a real carrier.
- **Caveat:** The crystal terrain affected firelanes, and the speakers debated some Dodge/ARO timing. Treat source as a tactical Supplies example, not a standard-table matchup theorem.

**Mission lesson:** protect the unattended box from **prone Stealth/Cautious Movement** and reserve a late carrier even after winning the central Noctifer fight.
