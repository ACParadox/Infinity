# Infinity N5 — What works, what fails, and when

**Prepared:** November 2, 2026. **Sources:** [master report index #001–#241](n5-battle-reports-and-tournament-reports.md), [unit effectiveness](unit-value-role-effectiveness-n5.md), [repeatable synergies](n5-repeatable-unit-synergies.md), and [victory mechanisms](n5-why-games-are-won.md). This document is an evidence-conditional cross-report guide, not a statistical ranking.

**Main conclusion:** Reliable performance comes from connecting **attack access + qualified mission scoring + Order economy + survivable/alternative routes**. A unit may be excellent at killing and still be a poor investment in the current mission.

## Evidence rules

- **R3:** independently repeated tactical mechanism in two or more distinct matches/events. **R2:** recurring but mixed or different exact pieces. **R1:** one credible case or one tournament. **R0:** proposed but not observed. These labels **do not** measure win rates.
- A successful combination requires an observed interaction, not just presence in a winning army. Separate directly described actions, player opinions, and our inference.
- The index's 241 entries include duplicates, interviews, multipart tournament descriptions, partial matches, narrative games, and rules variants. Do not treat them as 241 equal independent wins. #009/#028, #011/#030, #022/#026 and CanCon #161/#237 are known repeat references.
- Historical N5 loadouts and Fireteams must not be copied into N5.3 without checking current `sectorials/` references. Particular support units may be unlinked to a named Fireteam.

## 2. WHAT DOES NOT WORK, AND WHY?

| Recurrent failure | Cases | Mechanism of failure | Practical correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attacker eats scoring Orders** | #231 Sphinx ~9–10 Orders for ~4 kills, lost 0–10; #194 Nisse ~5–6 Orders on Cenobite, failed pickup timing; #005 Redeye spent most Orders versus Q-Drone and died. | Opening the lane is not scoring; the task chain still needs move + interact + retry + deliver. | Before attacking, reserve the minimum required task Orders. Bypass or delay nonessential defenders. #216 Shona is a counterexample: ~9 Orders **destroyed three scoring objectives**. |
| **Damage advantage mistaken for OP lead** | #199 NCA won **6–5** despite catastrophic losses; #146 OSS killed more but lost modified Neutralization **1–4**; #214 triple-TAG PanO lost to Hassassin Classifieds **2–5**. | Banked points, eligible task workers and objectives cannot be replaced by impressive casualties. | Track actual awarded OP and opponent's remaining legal route. #214 was a deliberately themed list. |
| **Hero exposed to intact cyber net** | #197 Achilles vs Tunguska, #102 Neoterra TAG vs Cyberghost, #213 extensive hacker standoff. | Immobilization, Isolation, Possession and required Resets burn Orders without needing wounds. | Clear/bypass Repeaters, preserve Engineer/Reset routes, use nonhackable alternatives when feasible. |
| **Too few independent legal scorers** | #103 TAK console workers eliminated by Liu Xing; #214 triple TAG skill scarcity; #004 experimental zero-specialist Torchlight list. | One loss, Classified type or required skill makes the list unable to complete scenario. | Two independent feasible task routes when budget allows; build for selected scenario's skill whitelist. |
| **Fixation on one ARO, marker or enemy elite model** | #189 Shas repeatedly fought TR HMG controlling both delivery zones; #196 Next Wave owned console but lost area presence; #239 Next Wave kills did not dislodge scoring occupants. | Wrong target; area geometry and final task are ignored. | Identify actual OP blocker, contest separate zones, use Sensor or bypass markers selectively. |
| **Guided engine without follow-through** | #005 critique of opponent additional Guided focus; #023 Guided required spare Orders; #118 Morats show effective opposite case. | Targeting and firing cost Orders even when a hit does not result in objective gain. | Only launch if target elimination can be converted into scoring/denial with adequate Orders remaining. |
| **Overkill triggers bad end condition** | #181 participant retrospective about an extra kill causing Retreat! consequences. | Earlier finish/changed turn sequence alters future mission scoring. | Confirm mission and patch-specific end/Retreat! timing before optional kill; R1 retrospective. |
| **Repair without residual purpose** | #003 player questions resurrecting Long Ya after its disposable ARO use. | Engineer/servant Orders exceed future useful contribution. | Recover model only if it can still shoot, contest or complete OP. #092/#115 demonstrate valuable repairs. |
| **Treat frontal link gun as invulnerable** | #003 Kestrel cleared Pitcher and flanked; #194 Cenobite stall; #216 Shona objective route. | Smoke, vision, off-angles, hacking and mission paths change what has to be fought. | Cover side/rear angles and actual objective corridor; preserve anti-flank/anti-hacking screen. |
| **Miss scenario version or setup rule** | #200 custom Infection starting state error; #233 radial Dig and projected score; #239 antenna reaction interactions. | Wrong skill, geometry or scoring assumptions drive apparently rational moves that do not score. | Verify original packet, exact patch and how mission awards OP, not the mission name alone. |

**Negative evidence is particularly important:** a package that wins one game and fails another is **conditional**, not a universal must-buy. #197 qualifies Next Wave hero plans; #213 qualifies Tunguska hacker/TAG plans; #230 qualifies Nomad hacking into Ariadna; #194 qualifies SWF premium active guns; #231 qualifies Sphinx alpha pushes.

## 3. WHEN DOES IT WORK?

### Mission

| Mission / task type | Prioritize | Avoid relying solely on | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hardlock, consoles, Unmasking** | Repeated WIP attempts, usable Orders, mobile legal interactors, actual HVT access | Premium gun killing irrelevant models | #093, #096, #102, #118 |
| **Supplies, Data Harvest, cargo** | End-to-end **pickup → transport → place** budget, spare carrier, protected approach | Removing first ARO without delivery Orders | #189, #194 |
| **Supremacy, Area of Interest, Battleground** | Distributed zone weight and timing, cheap/hidden bodies as permitted | Owning middle or killing one large attacker | #005, #092, #105, #115, #196, #231, #239 |
| **Outbreak** | Scenario-eligible medical/scanning/stabilization models and banked early OP | Generic Hacker/FO mistaken for correct infected-task actor | #116, #199 |
| **Mindwipe, Corporate Appropriation, Sabotage** | Anti-materiel/CC/D-Charge objective breakers and route delivery | Gunfight success not involving actual hardware | #216, #223, #232 |
| **Classified-heavy Countermeasures / Firefight** | Broad specialist skills and enough turns to do tasks | Themed triple TAGs with few Classified options | #212, #214 |
| **Kill/attrition-based categories** | Exact scoring kills and opponent command disruption | Claiming all kills equally valuable in other packets | #004, #137 |

### Matchup

- **Versus Hackable HI/TAGs:** defend or project Pitchers/Repeaters, hackers and cybermines (#102/#197); the attacked side should clear nodes first or use nonhackable/scoring alternatives. **Versus Ariadna:** expect fewer useful hacking targets; prioritize Sensor, flexible lanes, Camo counterplay and objective reach (#230/#237).
- **Versus defensive Fireteam or TAG gunline:** identify the **one line** that matters to mission delivery and remove/bypass it; do not try to win every frontal duel (#003/#194).
- **Versus Hidden/Camo area pressure:** multiple independent zone bodies and targeted Sensor/Discover help more than using an elite attacker to sweep irrelevant markers (#196/#239).
- **Versus rapid smoke/CC anti-materiel:** protect objective access, mines/templates and the task tool; do not assume distant firing lanes stop Shona/Wallace (#216/#223).
- **Versus airborne/DZ threats:** keep layered screening and at least one independent Order/scoring route; Liu Xing removed TAK's workers (#103) and Jamie reached the back scoring band (#231).

### Deployment and board geometry

- **Radial/quarter:** forward specialists, marker placement and opposing flank entry matter more. #233 Dig is **radial, projected ending**, and #239 Area of Interest had radial/diagonal scoring/antenna interactions.
- **Lateral/narrow DZ:** fewer escape lanes make attacker movement and counterattack angles decisive. #092 Varuna needed Engineer recovery and a final REM move.
- **Several dispersed scoring zones:** spreading Shrouded/Hidden or cheap scoring bodies pays (#196/#239), whereas a single protected gun cannot automatically contest all areas.
- **Constricted chokepoints:** one ARO can consume the whole turn (#194 Cenobite, #189 TR HMG). But #189's placement of scoring circles unusually close together was an atypical setup advantage.
- **Airborne-compatible rear access:** protect deployment-zone order pieces and task actors (#103); aggressive rear arrivals can win deep scoring bands (#231).

### First versus second player

- **Early score banking can survive late casualties:** #199 Outbreak Neoterra; #116 suggests that mission's first-mover snowball possibilities, not a demonstrated population-wide first-turn advantage.
- **Late scoring requires surviving area movers:** #092 one final REM movement changed the zone score; #115 late Interventor console; #239 end-of-round Hacker/zone control.
- **Attack must finish before scoring deadline:** #005, #189, #194, #231 show how losing the remaining Order budget undermines a seemingly powerful turn. #093 shows a winning last-turn first Order.
- **Retreat! or end-of-round timing must be checked:** #181 retrospective extra kill could worsen scoring; #239 played through a disputed end-state rule.

## 4. Practical checks before and during games

**Before building:** For each mission write down every OP source, the necessary skill, scoring timing, scenario-eligible attack tools, scoring-zone geometry and cargo chain. Include multiple qualified task actors, attack-route support (smoke/White Noise/Disco/CC/hacking as appropriate), a defensive delay layer, and spare Regular/usable Orders. Check N5.3 unit/profile and Fireteam legality in `sectorials/` for current play.

**Before starting a turn:** Calculate the **complete task budget**: movement to objective + required WIP attempts + likely retry reserve + pickup/delivery/place + final area position. The extra Orders are the **actual discretionary gunfight budget**. Identify the *specific* enemy ARO, hacker or counter-scorer blocking that route; stop attacking when marginal kills cannot help finish it.

**Before ending turn:** Verify objective **actually awards OP now**, whether a surviving model can contest/defend the scoring moment, and whether an optional attack can cause Retreat!/finish-state consequences. Save an independent scorer or recovery path for the next turn.

**After the match:** Record both positive and **failed** instances of each package, exact game ID, source, mission, opponent, deployment, initiative, scoring timing, N5/ITS version, who completed which tasks, Order totals when filmed, and whether final OP was counted or inferred. A 241-entry index without consistent per-match denominators cannot justify quantitative win-rate claims.

## 5. Main conclusions

1. **Reliable wins depend on scoring conversion**, not gun strength alone; #199 is a decisive counterexample to the equation “more kills = victory.”
2. **The strongest repeatable architecture is modular:** route-maker / threat remover + qualified specialist + positional denial + redundancy/Order reserve. Exact 3–5 model combinations are less repeatedly proven than their roles.
3. **The clearest repeated failure is misallocated Orders:** #005, #189, #194 and #231 show different forms. #216 provides the equally important exception: large Order expenditure succeeds when it directly scores.
4. **Opponent counters target a role, not merely a named unit:** clear repeaters before TAG advances, remove eligible mission workers, approach hidden-zone teams through several routes, and contest actual scoring geometry.
5. **Mission, matchup, map, initiative and version are not footnotes.** They determine whether the same unit combination is winning, mediocre or unusable.

**Future evidence standard:** classify distinct completed matches with denominator, score confidence, exact task timeline, winning *and losing* packages, and patch/mission stratification. Do not derive faction tier lists or win probabilities from this qualitative synthesis alone.
