## #071 — Corregidor Surface vs Morat Aggression Force: Ghost in the Feed

**Video:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness, Ground Corregidor vs Morats](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TPI9RSi4HmQ). **Mission:** CUSTOM *The Monday Signal / Monday Transmissions* **Ghost in the Feed**, written by Robert Shepherd, with **three consoles that reveal three opponent-side clues/boxes**. Only **Doctor, Paramedic, Chain of Command, Forward Observer, Hacker or Engineer** can activate them; generic Specialist Operative/“specialist” status is *not automatically enough*. **Reported result: Corregidor 6–0 Morats**.

- **[Observed]** **Ground/Surface Corregidor** used **Jazz Hacking, a Gator TAG, Wildcat/Mobile Brigada-style Fireteam, Paramedic/FO and cheap REMs** for mixed fighting and actual scenario access. **Jazz/Repeaters + CrazyKoalas** threatened Morat approaches while an independent specialist **flipped a central console** to reveal a clue. The final win came from **one retrieved clue, additional consoles, protected HVT and Classified/telemetry scoring**, not eliminating every Morat.
- **[Observed]** Morats spent approximately **ten Orders** attempting to disable/kill the **Gator** according to the player, without generating a comparable scoring advantage. Its **survivability and continued hacking vulnerability** tied up attacks that could have gone into console control.
- **[Observed]** The Morat army included several units that were **Specialist Troops in the broad sense** but *lacked the scenario's narrow qualifying skill list*. At the end the player explicitly said **he could barely play the mission**, having insufficient eligible console activators. Morat **Dartok/Hacker** could still attempt a late button, but was blocked by the Corregidor reaction network.
- **[Inference]** **Gator + protected Jazz + multiple true FO/Paramedic/Hacker bodies** is excellent **CUSTOM Ghost in the Feed tech**: the Gator distracts the rival while eligible cheap models collect the only scoring items. This does **not** mean Corregidor generally counters Morats.
- **[Player assessment]** The Morat player said his list would have suited a more conventional **direct fight or table-edge assault mission**, not this narrow **console-skill-gated** mission.
- **[Caution]** The presenters dispute Fireteam/hacker/peripheral declarations. Do not use their first interpretation of **Possessed**, Firewall or who qualifies for a *custom* console as current N5.3 law.

**Actionable:** read the entire **scenario's exact Specialist whitelist before list-building**, then carry **two or more eligible, independently positioned buttons** instead of betting on your TAG's survival.

## #072 — New OSS vs Hassassin Bahram: Raid the Kazureibu

**Video:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness, OSSS vs Hassassins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jPNaVibKDKU). **Mission:** CUSTOM Monday Transmissions **Raid the Kazureibu**, involving **five moving rave-party civilians/info targets**, **scan interactions**, control and evolving drift/Smoke zones. **Reported final: OSS 8–3 Hassassin Bahram**.

- **[Observed]** Revised **Operations Subsection** took **Maximus (new HMG/Max configuration), Asura, Sophotect, Rudra K1 Marksman and support/Baggage REMs**. The **Asura / Rudra** supplied high-end combat/control, **Sophotect** handled specialist tasks, and **Max** advanced to a central position and repeatedly used **Suppressive Fire** to anchor the relevant scoring/control zone.
- **[Observed]** Hassassin used **Asawira Spitfire, Asawira Doctor and another Asawira, Lasiq Viral/AP Marksman, Muyib HRL, Barid KHD, Fiday, Ghazi Muttawi'ah Jammers and EVO/cheap REM**. **Ghazi Jammer** successfully **Isolated Sophotect** and constrained OSS specialist sequencing, showing real counterplay even though Hassassins lost.
- **[Observed]** The OSS player explicitly praises **Max's ability to go into Suppressive Fire** as the game-changing newly available option. Max held a valuable central approach and forced the Hassassin player into **Hacking/Breaker/Isolation** alternatives that the chosen list struggled to deploy.
- **[Observed]** OSS continued collecting **moving target scans/control** while the rival invested attacks into Max/Asura. The game swung on **repeatable mission interactions and control of drifting targets**, not simply HMG survival.
- **[Inference]** **Max + Sophotect + mobile Rudra/Asura** can be outstanding in a *custom mission rewarding repeated control/scans*, especially if the opponent cannot threaten Max with **appropriate Hacking/Breaker/Isolation**. Hassassin **Ghazi Jammer, Fiday and Lasiq Viral** offer plausible independent answers, but weren't enough here.
- **[Caution]** **Do not classify as ITS Raid/Corporate, or translate CUSTOM civilian drift into ITS Evacuation rules.** Max's historical profile and Suppressive Fire eligibility should be checked for the specific Army patch.

**Actionable:** **two mobile scanners + central suppressive anchor**; when fighting Max, include a **nonstandard state/weapon counter**, not only normal rifles.

## #073 — ALEPH vs Ramah Taskforce: ITS17.2 Outbreak

**Video:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness, ALEPH vs Ramah — Outbreak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kpolDiKOlyg). **N5.2-era ITS17.2** explicitly described in the intro. **Outbreak:** five infected/neutral characters, with **CivVac toward treatment zones, cure/stabilization, data scans and special “alpha” infected scoring**; no regular HVT/Classified deck. **Reported final: ALEPH 8–5 Ramah**.

- **[Observed]** **ALEPH** used **Asura and Maximus-type elite units, a fast supporting specialist and Netrods**, while **Ramah** brought **Ghulam-linked shooters/HMG, specialist coverage and mobile treatment/scoring bodies**. The game opens with a **linked Ramah HMG** dropping a major ALEPH attacker and forcing the ALEPH player to reassess the combat-first plan.
- **[Observed]** ALEPH **Asura and remaining mobile troopers** spent several late Orders **scanning infected and CivVac-ing/stabilizing them**. The final score is calculated by **total scans, more stabilized infected and mission control**. One crucial **alpha infected** ended up in Ramah's possession, yet ALEPH still won via the **other scoring categories**.
- **[Observed]** **Smoke/visibility and defensive reactive fire** mattered for protecting CivVac routes; the presenters emphasize that both sides had useful scoring activities *every turn*, with no reason to spend all Orders eliminating the opponent.
- **[Inference]** **ALEPH fast elite Specialists + robust order base** can be well suited to Outbreak *if the Asura is used for treatment/scans rather than endless duels*. Ramah's **linked HMG and multiple specialist bodies** can reliably contest one zone even when outgunned overall.
- **[Caution]** Do not generalize “ALEPH > Ramah” from an **8–5 task win**; Outbreak's infected manipulation is much more specialized than Annihilation.

**Actionable:** assign **at least two independent infected carriers/scan performers** and a recovery path, then decide whether to fight based on **who controls the alpha and the number stabilized**.

## #074 — Operation Mazebreaker: OSS vs Corregidor, Crossing Lines

**Video:** [Official Corvus Belli × Vanguard Tactics Mazebreaker report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=awtMxsNJ5b4). **Opponents:** **ALEPH Operations Subsection vs Nomads Corregidor** (not unspecified armies). **Mission:** **Crossing Lines**, with **a second forward deployment zone on the opponent's half, two “dead zones” and two central antennas**. The video does not disclose a clear official final numeric winner; **do not invent one**.

- **[Observed]** OSS fielded **Asura Lieutenant/elite shooter, Dikpala Tacbot Fireteam, Naga Camo specialist, Shukra console scorer and airborne unit**. **Dikpala** advanced and *occupied one dead zone* while **Shukra achieved a WIP activation of an antenna**. An **Asura in Suppressive Fire** made counter-entry costly.
- **[Observed]** Corregidor's **Bearcat Combat Jump** failed, consuming a planned second-angle assault. A **Coyote** then fought the OSS Dikpala line and was eliminated. Remaining **Camo/marker and Shifter** elements attempted to contest zones, but the order budget was strained.
- **[Observed]** A **Naga Boarding Shotgun** threatened a forward Corregidor piece; in a late turn the OSS player **fired the Asura into an existing close combat**, failed badly and **killed his own Naga**. This is a concrete *friendly-fire/excess attack* example: once a dead zone is secured, another risky gunfight can be net negative.
- **[Inference]** **OSS Dikpala mobility + Shukra WIP scorer + Asura hard anchor** is well suited to **Crossing Lines**, because it can establish **forward zone presence and activate antennas** on separate Order paths. Corregidor **Bearcat/Coyote flank pressure** might counter a thin OSS backline, but not when Combat Jump fails and multiple pieces are pinned.
- **[Caution]** Operation Mazebreaker released in 2026; verify its exact **Crossing Lines variant** and historical Fireteam options, not current ITS18 text.

**Actionable:** deploy **a cheap scoring body directly into a dead zone, plus an independent antenna specialist**. Protect the flank from Bearcat rather than spending all Orders on Asura gunfights.

## #075 — Ikari Company vs Svalarheima Winter Force: Area of Interest

**Video:** [Champion of Terra — Ikari vs Svalarheima](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EcyodGckZUU). **Area of Interest** with two personal zones, **special turn-scoring Area of Interest** and central **communication antenna**, plus Classifieds. **N5 date/patch unverified. Reported Svalarheima 5–3 Ikari** from final narrated scoring.

- **[Observed]** Ikari used **Alpha Seed heavy launcher, Brawler MSV2 Sniper + Druze Hacker Duo, Wu Ming, Tanko, War Driver, Yojimbo, Holo/Decoy Lieutenant and Beast Hunter**. The combined package could attack from ranged and marker directions but did not maintain control of the special Area of Interest throughout the game.
- **[Observed]** Svalarheima relied on **Karhu Feuerbach/Paramedic, Nisse and other active guns, Liang Kai, Shona Carano and Beast Hunter**, providing both long-range defense and **fast close combat**. **Shona Super-Jump/CC** removed an important Ikari model and helped satisfy a **Predator Classified**; **Liang Kai** removed another vulnerable target.
- **[Observed]** The final **5–3** was driven by **special Area of Interest scoring over several turns and central-area control**, not by simply destroying Ikari's large guns. One surviving **cheap Lieutenant/unit** contested an area late and made the result closer.
- **[Inference]** Svalarheima's **Karhu specialist pair + Liang Kai/Shona** is flexible for **Area of Interest**: ranged weight, specialist activation and **two independent melee disruptors**. Ikari can challenge via **Brawler/Druze/Alpha Seed**, but must actually keep a scoring unit in the right area.
- **[Caution]** Dates, exact legal Fireteams and a few translation-corrupted unit names are unresolved; **do not rank Shona/Liang Kai on this one favorable CC exchange**.

**Actionable:** build for **repeatable round-end special-zone dominance plus one antenna specialist**, not only the end-of-game three zones.

## #076 — Haqqislam mirror: The Dig

**Video:** [Tabletop Throwdown — The Dig, Haqq vs Haqq](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HxT7vTO8yS0). **Mission:** **The Dig**, with **buttons revealing opponent-chosen buried artifacts/neutral markers** and Classified/consoles. It appears in ITS18; actual event date/version in recording **unverified**. The presenter starts **partway through the game** and identifies two **Vanilla Haqqislam** lists, not two distinct Haqq sectorials. **Fes defeated Grayscale 5–0**.

- **[Observed]** Both sides used **Saladin Strategos/Inspiring Leadership order economy, Zuleyka, Zhayedan, Hayyam and Ghazi Muttawi'ah**. The commentator calls **Hayyam + Zhayedan** a particularly useful gun fork: **Hayyam Mimetism/visor versus Zhayedan Albedo/BS attack mods** can punish opponents using the counter normally chosen for the other.
- **[Observed]** The Grayscale list also included **Daylami Camo, Barid with Trinity, a Hidden Tuareg Hacker**, plus **two airborne threats**. Fes instead fielded **more Ghazis, Fiday, two Mukhtar specialists, Farzan/Al-Hawwa and Daylami**, with distinct fast Classified/console routes.
- **[Observed]** The report repeatedly showed **a smoking Ghazi/Zuleyka attack not being itself sufficient for excavation**. The final scorer used **appropriate Specialists**, activated more **consoles**, completed **two Classifieds**, and neutralized an opponent's technology/artifact.
- **[Player assessment]** The presenter says **Saladin cheap-Order conversion** allows a strong Irregular warband shell, but if that shell only kills, its total utility in The Dig is low.
- **[Inference]** **Fiday disruption + Mukhtar/Farzan specialists + Saladin economy** can be more flexible than a **Hayyam/Zhayedan + double-airborne** shell in **The Dig**, when the latter doesn't keep enough uncommitted button-pressing Orders. Since both are Haqq lists, **no sectorial-vs-sectorial ranking** is appropriate.
- **[Caution]** The cast begins **mid-match**; exact starting deployment and all previous turns are not fully recorded. The players/host also debate Impetuous movement and scoring.

**Actionable:** **Saladin + diverse Camo/fast specialists**, with Hayyam/Zhayedan covering the route, instead of investing all remaining Orders into a kill spree.

## #077 — Morat Aggression Force vs Vanilla Haqqislam: Annihilation

**Video:** [Fast Panda Gaming — Morats vs Haqq](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBfTcv7wug8). **Casual Annihilation**, expressly with **non-optimized painted collections**, no play date/patch established. **Final score stated 8–3**; heavily auto-translated speaker attribution makes the **winner-to-faction mapping insufficiently certain to record a verified victory by faction**.

- **[Observed]** Morats fielded **Bultrak HRMC TAG, Kornak Gazarot, Kaitok Chain Rifle/Flammenspeer, Yaogat MULTI Sniper, Kurgat Portable Autocannon, Dartok Hacker and an Oznat+Gaki/Pretas Fireteam**. **Oznat Fireteam Master** converted cheap Gaki/Pretas into a Regular-Order package, after which Morats changed Fireteams using a Command Token to field **Yaogat sniper with supporting link bonuses**.
- **[Observed]** Vanilla Haqqislam presented **Scarface + Cordelia Engineer, Rāhanī Doctor, Lasiq Viral Sniper, Hunzakut Camo, Yuan Yuan and Bashi Bazouk**, plus mobile troops. **Cordelia repaired Scarface** repeatedly; this was a meaningful **TAG/Engineer attrition shell**.
- **[Observed]** Morat **Bultrak soaked many attacks**, while **Yuan Yuan and Bashi Bazouk flank pressure** eliminated or threatened **Kornak and the Bultrak**. This is a rare direct illustration of **cheap airborne mercenaries hitting premium Morat investments** in an otherwise gun-focused game.
- **[Player assessment]** A Morat participant praises the **Morat Vanguard HMG BS Attack (-3)** as a cost-effective attack piece; the players agree **Oznat+Gaki cheap Orders** were efficient and a relevant alternative to costly high-end units. Those are **player assessments**, not confirmed whole-game cost-efficiency rankings.
- **[Inference]** **Morat TAG+Yaogat shooting** can dominate an open board, while **Haqq Yuan Yuan/Bashi + Scarface/Cordelia** creates a more difficult two-angle endgame. In **Annihilation** the unit-kill/point differential dominates; this does not transfer directly to Provisioning/Evacuation.
- **[Caution]** Keep the **8–3 report** but **do not claim a proven Morat or Haqq winner** until the original, non-translated closing commentary/scorecard resolves who received eight.

**Actionable:** decide whether to spend on **TAG durability** or protect against **two separate airborne rear attacks**; engineer access must be safe if relying on repairs.

## #078 — Corregidor Surface vs Starmada: Evacuation

**Video:** [Tabletop Throwdown — Evacuation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U2A2Y5BxMVs). **N5.2 + May Army or N5.3 unresolved**; episode includes **Tactical Support cards** giving nonstandard Specialist status. **CivVac** civilians/HVTs to evacuation zones and two Classified objectives. **Final reported: Corregidor 7–5 Starmada.**

- **[Observed]** Corregidor Surface brought **Iguana TAG, two Moran Repeaters/Koalas, Jazz Hacker, Coyote KHD, Jaguars, Vertigo Guided REM and Baggage**, among other specialists. One **Tactical Support card made Iguana a Specialist**, which is **NOT baseline Iguana profile access**. The TAG could therefore contribute directly to the evac mission on this *specific game setup*.
- **[Observed]** Starmada fielded **Raveneyes FO/Minelayer, Kappa/PSI-Cop link, Cyberghost, Epsilon MSV2 HMG, Beta Trooper Paramedic, Roadbots and Crusher**, with an extra eligible specialist from its card. The varied **Roadbot/Paramedic/Crusher/Cyberghost** mix had multiple ways to reach civilians.
- **[Observed]** **Moran Koalas + Jazz Spotlight → Vertigo Guided Missile** attacked a Starmada link and created a severe Order tax. The counterargument was not another long gun, but **moving an eligible specialist through a narrow safe zone between Repeaters**.
- **[Observed]** Starmada obtained late **CivVac points via mobile specialists/Smoke/Disco**, and the narrator describes the **7–5** as closer than expected. **Beta/Roadbot durability** helped, but expending too many Orders attacking the Iguana rather than extracting had real opportunity cost.
- **[Player assessment]** The presenter questions **TR Thunderbolt REM** as a top-tier ARO investment now that many attackers have **BS Attack (-3), Mimetism and MSV**; this is a *situational live opinion*, not grounds for “never take.”
- **[Inference]** **Corregidor's Moran/Jazz/Vertigo network + durable Iguana** can be a serious Evacuation obstacle. **Starmada Roadbots, Paramedics, Crushers and Raveneyes** offer alternative CivVac lanes, but need protection from **Spotlight/Guided**.
- **[Caution]** Never promote the **card-augmented Iguana Specialist** as an ordinary legal Corregidor build, nor blindly copy live Trinity/Koala/Guided rulings.

**Actionable:** include **a CivVac specialist outside the primary Repeater corridor**, and reserve Orders for target-to-terminal delivery after disabling the main TAG.

## #080 — Deep Space Corregidor vs Vanilla Haqqislam: Neutralization

**Video:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness — Neutralization](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UIgdTtv2CVM). Narrator explicitly locates it **the week before Interplanetario 2026 and after Mazebreaker launched at Gen Con**, thus **N5.2 with May 2026 Army / ITS17**. **Neutralization** includes **central boxes to carry to antennas / scoring home-side neutralization areas**, plus a Classified. **Reported final: Vanilla Haqqislam 7–0 Deep Space Corregidor.**

- **[Observed]** **Deep Space Corregidor** roster had **Jazz Hacker, Gator TAG, Evader Engineer with AP Spitfire, Wildcat, Moran Koalas/Repeater, Vostok Spitfire, Alguacil Paramedic/FO and Transductor REMs**, giving good *potential* attack, recovery and network. **Gator** became the pivotal expensive piece and generated a lot of local fighting, but did not carry/neutralize the required box.
- **[Observed]** Vanilla Haqqislam brought **Saladin order economy, Khayyam, Leila Sharif, Ghazi Muttawi'ah Jammers/Chain Rifles, Shakush HMG TAG and an Engineer**, plus forward pressure. **Shakush supported by Ghazi/Smoke + Panzerfaust/E/M threats** repeatedly taxed Corregidor's Gator/leader defense.
- **[Observed]** One participant **failed to protect a valuable Lieutenant from an unexpected Panzerfaust angle**, and the player explicitly calls this a decisive deployment/movement error. A critical Gator/leader engagement consumed **many attacks and remaining Orders**, without the Nomads scoring a single box.
- **[Observed]** The final tally is explicitly **7–0 in favor of Vanilla Haqqislam**, despite players initially talking through a possible lower-point result. **Haqq retained the neutralization/antenna objectives** while Corregidor spent almost everything in gunfights.
- **[Inference]** **Saladin + cheap Ghazi/Smoke state threats + Shakush** is a credible **anti-Gator / route-control package** for Neutralization. **Jazz/Moran + Evader/Gator/Vostok** would need a separate protected **box runner and Lieutenant-safe position** to contest.
- **[Player assessment]** The Corregidor player says he focused on the **visible Shakush and shotgun threat** and forgot about the **Panzerfaust**, acknowledging that better deployment/protection could have changed the mission outcome.
- **[Caution]** This was **an Interplanetario mission preview**, not evidence of an actual tournament result or a global Haqq-over-Corregidor hierarchy.

**Actionable:** score **box pickup → return to antenna/home area → neutralize**, reserve the needed specialist/Orders, and do not spend all three turns trying to kill Shakush.
