## #141 — Onyx Contact Force vs Vanilla Haqqislam: Evacuation

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=coGTW8gvgP8)**. **Onyx 5–1 Haqq**, independently tallied at the end: Onyx two 2-OP Classifieds plus one evacuee; Haqq one evacuee.

- **Observed:** Onyx brought **Umbra Samaritan with Protheion/Super-Jump, two Umbra Legates (one hacker and Pitcher), Suryat MSV1 Heavy Rocket Launcher/TinBot, Ikadrons and small support**. It had both attacking power and specialist diversity. Haqq relied on **Odalisque Fireteam/CivVac escort and other Haqq specialists**, including a Farzan.
- **Observed:** Haqq **broke its Fireteam to perform CivVac** and required additional movement/Orders to escort the captive. Onyx kept independent Classified-capable operatives alive and scored them rather than trying to annihilate everyone. An Onyx Legate removed a late Haqq target and the Onyx player finished the second task.
- **Player opinion:** Because Classifieds were worth **two points each**, the commentator rechecked his initial description of the size of the Onyx victory. The unusual CivVac/Fireteam interface was a real tactical constraint.
- **Inference:** **Umbra Legate specialist + Samaritan aggressive screen** can be a useful Evacuation package against an Odalisque-oriented Haqq escort. Haqq needs *separate* Fireteam-independent CivVac operators and a path for a second escort, not simply more guns.
- **Caution:** Source calls the second army **Haqqislam**, not a verified named sectorial; preserve Vanilla Haqq identification provisionally. Historical Fireteam/CivVac rules were debated.

**Mission lesson:** Budget the Fireteam break, one extraction lane and **two independent two-point Classified specialists**.

## #142 — Next Wave vs Shindenbutai: one-TAG limited-insertion Superiority

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yf0vzdZ1Buo)**. **Next Wave 10–1 Shindenbutai**. Played under unusual **exactly one TAG and one combat group** requirements for a forthcoming narrative event, not a regular free-form list.

- **Observed:** Next Wave chose **Juggernaut TAG/Guard, Skyhound vehicle with mines, Pandora, Stinger MSV2, and two Blackhearts** with high-burst T2/Thunderbolt options. Shindenbutai deployed its **TAG and attached small bot, Tanuki HMG, Hidden Ninja Killer Hacker, Samurai and specialists**.
- **Observed:** One **Blackheart** killed or disabled **Tanuki HMG, an expensive Japanese close-assault piece and the TAG** in reactive combat; even though the opponent eliminated one Blackheart early, the second stayed decisive. Next Wave retained the quadrant weight for **Superiority** and pressed the remaining scoring consoles for 10 OP.
- **Player opinion:** Both players singled out **Blackheart's strong ARO/extra shot package**, rather than the Juggernaut merely overrunning the table. The Japanese TAG's loss is disproportionately harmful when the format forces one TAG and only one Order group.
- **Inference:** **Blackheart x2 + Pandora/Stinger plus Juggernaut/Skyhound** can punish a forced elite Shindenbutai TAG list on quadrant missions. Shindenbutai could use Hidden Ninja or other indirect attacks to remove Blackheart before offering an expensive TAG into its LoF.
- **Caution:** The tournament's **one-TAG-only format** and updated N5.2 Next Wave profiles rule out a faction-wide win-rate claim.

**Mission lesson:** Protect the sole TAG and scout Blackheart sightlines before committing it to score the third quadrant.

## #143 — Vanilla PanOceania vs Vanilla Haqqislam: Corporate Appropriation

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h13UW7iWz44)**. **PanO 5–4 Haqq**. Prototype destruction, Panoply and Classified points outweighed nominal TAG firefight performance.

- **Observed:** PanO brought **Cutter Hidden Deployment TAG and multiple cheap support/auxiliary and mission-capable bodies**. The **Cutter made very few effective kills** and repeatedly failed to displace a **Mukhtar MSV2 Red Fury**, yet PanO's smaller pieces still accomplished mission actions.
- **Observed:** Haqq used **Fiday Impersonator, Zuleyka fast Smoke biker, hidden Tuareg Killer Hacker/D-Charges, Mukhtar MSV2 and Daylami**. **Smoke + MSV2 Mukhtar** threatened PanO sightlines, while Fiday threatened the DZ before a **Biometric Visor/Impersonation interaction** interfered.
- **Observed:** Haqq destroyed its prototype and completed a Classified; PanO received sufficient **Classified, Panoply and uncaptured/prototype scenario points** for a **5–4** win. The presenter corrected some prototype-related arithmetic while scoring.
- **Inference:** **Cutter as threat plus small PanO specialists** can succeed into **Haqq Mukhtar/Fiday/Tuareg** in Corporate Appropriation without Cutter winning gun duels. The Haqq **Fiday/Smoke/MSV2** remains a credible counter-threat, not a guaranteed faction advantage.
- **Caution:** Exact historical Biometric Visor, prototype and Hacking interactions require the actual played ITS text.

**Mission lesson:** Take **two independent Classified/Panoply paths** and keep a second Anti-Materiel plan rather than assuming Cutter will table Haqq.

## #144 — OSS vs Corregidor: Data Harvest, Lobo League

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C1cBFmwn25s)**. **OSS 5–1 Corregidor** in a league's Data Harvest format. Both players remarked on the significance of **scoring from an extraction run on the final turn**.

- **Observed:** OSS used **Asura, Yadu, Posthuman specialist/proxy and Racerbot/Pilot X movement** for surviving mission access. Corregidor brought **three Evaders with Deactivators, Vortex Spec-ops and CrazyKoala, Jaguars, Alguacil Hacker, Lupe Balboa and Team-ops support**.
- **Observed:** The **Evaders' Deactivators did not replace the printed WIP roll on the harvest objective**. OSS survived late fights against Evader/cheap defensive pieces, picked up a data item and moved it to the enemy DZ while the Corregidor list was low on reaction and movement options.
- **Player opinion:** Corregidor's early combat was not poor, but the scoring was strongly decided by the last **data carrier's journey**. Both players discussed how this mission heavily rewards late access and does not reward killing the enemy by itself.
- **Inference:** OSS **Asura + fast independent data runner** can be especially useful against Corregidor **Evader/Koala control** if it retains a safe second-half turn route. Corregidor should defend the *exit corridor*, not only the button or the extraction object.
- **Caution:** Lobo League setting and on-camera mission-rule checks; this is not a generic ITS point forecast.

**Mission lesson:** Save at least **three Orders for pickup plus evacuation movement** after clearing the defender.

## #145 — Next Wave vs Operations Subsection: Hardlock

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dY6UGAVUTLk)**. **Next Wave 8–0 OSS**, but last-turn HVT/beacon points were **assigned by mutual projection**, not fully rolled/moved.

- **Observed:** Next Wave had **Achilles, Harbinger/Puzzler specialist and durable NanoScreen/Stealth attack and control**, while OSS fielded **MI Maximus Lieutenant, Claire Doctor, Pilot X/Optimates, Danavas Hacker, Andromeda and other specialist/gun pieces**.
- **Observed:** OSS **Repeater/Spotlight lines** constrained Achilles: the Next Wave player avoided a route that crossed Danavas and another Hacker's area. However, Next Wave killed/scored enough elsewhere that OSS missed the critical Hardlock round scoring windows.
- **Player opinion:** The host initially proposed a **Maximus + Claire + Pilot X Fireteam** and then re-read roster restrictions to identify the qualifying **Optimate/MaxOps** type. This is exactly the kind of source-era Fireteam interaction that must not be copied without the historical Army chart.
- **Observed — final:** Participants agreed Next Wave could **Secure HVT and touch its necessary beacon**; they **called the game at 8–0**, with a possible additional Classified movement left unplayed.
- **Inference:** **Achilles + protected Puzzler/Harbinger mission runners** can overwhelm an OSS roster that lacks remaining buttons, but **Danavas cyber area** is a meaningful counter if OSS preserves a second runner.
- **Caution:** **Agreed projected score** and N5.2-era profiles, not a strict fully executed standard tournament result.

**Mission lesson:** Keep a **repeater-safe button route** and do not sacrifice Danavas just to threaten Achilles when the actual Hardlock console remains uncontested.

## #146 — OSS vs Vanilla PanOceania: Neutralization, modified league score

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=35LN4SnE8xE)**. **Vanilla PanO 4–1 OSS**, retrieved via alternate short YouTube link. **Lobo League altered scoring**; players also agreed special table geometry and one Tactical Support effect.

- **Observed:** OSS deployed **Tarxia flyer, Shukra, Posthuman/Danavas group, Pilot X/Racerbot and Guided/Spotlight support**, removing far more PanO pieces than it lost. PanO had **Redeye, Croc Man and small resilient zone/antenna defenders**. A Croc Man had been the desired late objective carrier but was eliminated before it could complete that escape.
- **Observed:** The last OSS turn lacked enough Orders/positioning to **neutralize, pick up and carry a mission object**, even though OSS had superior gun attrition. PanO held enough antenna control. The final recount was **PanO three objective-control points plus one Classified**, **OSS one Classified**.
- **Player opinion:** Unusual **multi-level consoles and difficult under-building lines of sight** drove several choices; the map required player agreement before play.
- **Inference:** **Vanilla PanO late antenna holder/Redeye** can beat **OSS Tarxia/Guided gun superiority** in Neutralization because objective access, not casualties, determines success.
- **Caution:** Explicit **league-adjusted OP**, N5 patch and exact mission geometry unverified outside the video. No generic PanO > OSS claim.

**Mission lesson:** Do not burn late Orders guided-firing a model if you cannot **move an eligible courier to neutralize and extract the objective**.

## #147 — Vanilla Combined Army vs Vanilla Haqqislam: Cutthroat

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sKQ7Y-w6MfA)**. **Haqq 10–0 Combined**. Host discusses rapid attacks and Combined defensive placement errors.

- **Observed:** Haqq fielded a **fast, durable Haytham, Fiday impersonator, Zuleyka/biker Smoke and Daylami/Yuan Yuan-style nuisance support**. Combined used **Sheskiin, Anathematic hacker, Bit & Kiss/Pitcher, Daturazi, Libertos/mines and Ikadrons**.
- **Observed:** **Haytham/Fiday pressure** removed multiple important Combined models or prevented them from contributing. Combined's **Anathematic was far back and too slow** to recover center scoring, and the **Ikadron/Daturazi/minelayer defense** did not deny the actual attacker route.
- **Player opinion:** Combined owner would redeploy screening templates/mines to force the Haqq approach to stop and place the Anathematic centrally. The hosts particularly valued **Haytham's aggression relative to traditional Anathematic support**.
- **Inference:** Haqq's **Fiday + Haytham dual alpha** can counter a Combined high-value elite force whose cheap defense is incorrectly positioned. It is not proof Haqq universally wins Cutthroat against a properly screened Sheskiin/Anathematic list.
- **Caution:** Host spends time speculating about nonexistent new weapon rules; those discussions are **not N5 rule facts**. Exact played patch unknown.

**Mission lesson:** Protect the **Lieutenant/high-value EI backline** with credible Camo mines and cheap templates, not just a powerful hacker.

## #148 — JSA Oban vs Qapu Khalqi: Area of Interest

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mgw4YE95iN8)**. **Oban 8–2 Qapu Khalqi**, Lobo League; some transcript phrases are machine-translated.

- **Observed:** Oban used **Shikami fast vertical specialists, Shakush TAG/pilot and other Japanese HI** to take sector control and flip consoles. QK used **Scarface and support, Brawler/Kaplan-type pieces, powerful shooting and point weight**, but brought **no Hacker**.
- **Observed:** The host emphasized that **Area of Interest allows a defender's Hacker to oppose some button actions**; on top of that, both teams contained valuable Hackable models. Oban reached relevant buttons/areas while QK could not contest them electronically. Result **8–2 Oban**.
- **Player opinion:** **Zero Hackers** was a critical QK design weakness for this specific mission. The QK owner initially considered it unimportant but admitted that the opponent's eligible specialist coverage was better.
- **Inference:** **Shikami + Shakush objective access** is a good plan into a QK Scarface force without Hacker defense. **QK with a protected Hacker**, plus independent button operator, would present a different matchup.
- **Caution:** Treat translated model names and local league scoring as provisional, do not generalize that Oban naturally dominates QK.

**Mission lesson:** Bring a **Hacker for opposing WIP interactions and containing enemy TAG/HI**, even if the primary army plan is aggressive shooting.

## #149 — Shock Army of Acontecimento vs Tunguska: Last Launch

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JmU33JEMgyM)**. **Tunguska 6–1 Acontecimento**; probably Nov 2025 N5.2 based on US Thanksgiving comments and the current **Triggerman Regeneration** profile.

- **Observed — mission:** **Side console WIP grants launch ticket/ID, then reach the central launch console**; a model carrying a ticket cannot rely on normal Camo hiding. Other points reward **specialists, extraction and Classifieds**.
- **Observed:** Acontecimento had **Bagh-Mari shooter, Sila Killer Hacker/Pitcher, Peacemaker + Auxbot, Fugazi, Rao, Regular snipers and Machinist**. Tunguska used **Triggerman with Regeneration, Hollow Men, Raoul Spector, Heckler, Spectr and Hacking/support**.
- **Observed:** Acontecimento repeatedly downed **Triggerman**, who **regenerated/recovered three times**, exhausting their good Orders. **Hollow Men/Raoul** supplied more decisive extraction/scoring. A **hidden Spectr** was accidentally left unused, a player-acknowledged mistake.
- **Player opinion:** Acontecimento player realized **Triggerman counts as Hackable HI** only after many attacks and would have **used Sila Pitcher + Hacker to disable it early**. The model's jacket/visual appearance had misled him.
- **Inference:** **Triggerman regen + Hollow Men/Raoul** is strong Last Launch attrition/evacuation tech against Acontecimento, but **Sila repeaters plus protected Hacker** are a genuine counter missed on this table.
- **Caution:** Do not extrapolate repeated successful Regen rolls into expected results or use November 2025 as a certified filming date.

**Mission lesson:** Prioritize **ticket acquisition and a live specialist launch route**, rather than repeated shots at a recoverable Triggerman.

## #150 — Kosmoflot vs Imperial Service: Outbreak, new ITS17 mission

**[Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8yPTQ0jm3OY)**. **Kosmoflot 10–1 ISS**. Explicit first-turn advantage and full scorecard narrated at the end.

- **Observed:** Kosmoflot used **Bearpode/Polaris assault, Volkolak T2 shooting and several medical/eligible infected handlers**. ISS brought **Su-Jian, Pheasant Doctor, Celestial Guard + Kuang Shi, Parachutist Paramedic and E/M grenade/line support**.
- **Observed:** Kosmoflot **scanned 3 (3 OP), stabilized more and the alpha (3), CivVac'd more (2), secured at least one (1), secured alpha (1)** = 10. ISS **scanned one (1)** but failed to complete enough rescue interactions; Pheasant Doctor was out of reach / limited by remaining Command Tokens.
- **Player opinion:** This game supports the worry that **first-player alpha stabilization can snowball** in Outbreak, forcing the second player to score almost perfectly to recover. The hosts discussed **house spacing changes**, explicitly not official rules.
- **Inference:** **Bearpode/Volkolak cover for multiple qualified rescue specialists** can be especially effective against an ISS list where **Su-Jian is positioned poorly for alpha denial**. ISS needs to contest the alpha on turn one, preserve two eligible handlers and deny CivVac passage.
- **Caution:** A **new experimental mission** and poor ISS rolls/positioning; cannot conclude Kosmoflot generally stronger than ISS.

**Mission lesson:** For the second player, deploy to **contest the alpha before its first-turn stabilization**, rather than chase exposed combat targets.
