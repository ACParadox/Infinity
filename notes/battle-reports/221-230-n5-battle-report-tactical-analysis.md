# Infinity N5 — Battle-report tactical analysis #221–#230

**Research date:** 2026-10-30. **Index:** [master N5 battle-report index](n5-battle-reports-and-tournament-reports.md). **Previous batch:** [#211–#220](211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md). **Next unanalysed batch:** #231–#240.

Ten original, distinct YouTube **battle-report transcripts** were reviewed for numbered index entries **#221–#230**, including a retrieved multi-chunk transcript for #228. The goal is to identify **what units actually did**, which **combinations and counterplay** mattered, which **mission mechanics** affected results, and the historically plausible **N5 rules/Army revision**. This file is not a 2026 N5.3 unit-tier list, and these ten results cannot be converted into faction win rates without filtering concessions, mirrors, asymmetric/custom missions, dice outliers and narration-only outcomes.

**Evidence labels:** **Observed** = clearly stated list, game action or ending in the source; **player/postgame view** = the player's assessment or proposal, not proven by a played move; **inference** = cautious strategic implication. Mispronounced auto-transcript profiles and unofficial/custom scenario interactions are not silently normalized into current Army rules. **Projected** scores are *not completed results*.

### Version classification method

Historical dates, rules and current edition matter because they affect Fireteams, Lt/NCO/Tactical Awareness efficiency, weapon traits and Counterintelligence/hacking interactions. The repository's [master chronology](n5-battle-reports-and-tournament-reports.md) documents **initial N5 from Dec 16 2024**, **N5.1 from Apr 3 2025**, **N5.1.1 from Apr 24 2025**, **N5.2 from Oct 15 2025**, **May 27 2026 Army update**, and **N5.3 with ITS18 from Sep 1 2026**, with official citations:

- [April first rules revision](https://infinityuniverse.com/en/news/infinity-n5-rules-update), [April correction](https://infinityuniverse.com/en/news/infinity-n5-update-5-1-1), [October 2025 N5.2 Army update](https://infinityuniverse.com/en/news/army-update-october-2025-part2), [May 2026 Mazebreaker Army update](https://infinityuniverse.com/en/news/army-update-mazebreaker), [Sep 2026 N5.3](https://infinityuniverse.com/en/news/infinity-rules-update-5-3).
- #223's creator says **“welcome to 2026”** at the end of the recorded report, a valuable played-era clue but **not an absolute upload/play date**. #224 was filmed after CanCon 2026. #225 explicitly reviews newly upgraded Umbra/Combined profiles, anchoring a later-than-launch N5 environment; **do not claim N5.3 without a verified September-or-later played day**.
- #228 commentary mentions **ITS17 treasure** and upcoming Thanksgiving, but that does not establish the specific year of recording or prove which N5.2 Army patch was used. #221, #222, #226, #227, #229 and #230 cannot be assigned precise PDF revisions simply from their mission names. B-Pong, Crossing Lines and Uplink Center should be matched to the historical *packet actually used*, not silently converted to ITS18.
- In #226 **the single-group, exactly-one-TAG, TAG-Booty event format materially changes normal faction choices**; #222 is a custom Dire Foes-style mission with free named operatives. Neither represents an ordinary 300pt ITS competitive comparison.

## #221 — Military Orders vs Military Orders: same lists, radically different choices

[Original Fast Panda report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xM5WItbSMlw). **Superiority, 200pts.** **Second player wins 6–4.** The players intentionally brought **identical models/profiles**, but configured **different Fireteams, group roles, selected operative and attack/defense priorities**. This is a controlled comparison of *player decisions*, not a faction-matchup study.

- **Observed — shared tools:** **Knight of Justice Spitfire (Lt)**, **Knight Commander Chain of Command**, **Knight of Santiago Killer Hacker**, **two Teutonic Knights** (Specialists with SMG/shotgun and Panzerfaust), **Black Friar** (narration mistranslates as “Black Brother”), **Crosiers** and **Warcor**. Both have 10 models at 200pts and several 2-Wound or robust armor threats.
- **Observed — differing structure:** One side built a **five-member Knight of Justice/Crosier/Knight Commander Core** and a **two-Teuton + Black Friar Haris** to concentrate the Spitfire/Burst bonus and Panzerfaust coverage, with **Santiago** independent. The other spread the Teutons into aggressive roles and combined the Knight of Justice with Santiago, making different points available to occupy quadrants. The supposedly identical purchases did *not* lead to identical Order efficiency.
- **Observed — scoring:** The game moved from a contested mid-round position into **6–4** after the second-turn player manipulated **quadrant points and consoles**. The players explicitly discuss that if the surviving **Knight of Justice** died, the score could shift to **5–5**, so the leading player **stopped attacking** with spare Orders rather than risking a trophy gunfight.
- **Observed — Santiago's value:** A **Knight of Santiago KHD** repeatedly forced attention and threatened a late flank; the opponent had to commit limited Orders to deal with him. A **Black Friar Flash/stun tool** and cheap Warcor Flash Pulse disrupted risky approaches. **Religious/Warhorse and multi-Wound Knights** provided durability but did not guarantee console success.
- **Player view:** Going **second** proved helpful because the player could see the current quadrant totals before spending the last Orders. The chosen **Crosier + Spitfire** position was harder to remove than the first player anticipated. The players also explicitly discuss dice variance and the merits of moving rather than fighting.
- **Inference — unit combos:** **Knight of Justice + Crosier/Knight Commander link** is a stable mid-range gun/score platform; **Teutonic Knights + Black Friar** provide Panzerfaust and sensory coverage; **Santiago KHD** can be a useful off-angle counterattacker or console Specialist. Against another MO link, the best “counter” can simply be **preserving an Order and occupying a higher-value quadrant**, not racing to kill the enemy Knight.
- **Limit:** 200pt mirror; outcomes cannot establish a sectorial advantage, and this is not a proof that any one Fireteam configuration is generally optimal in 300pt ITS.

**Mission tactic:** In **Superiority**, calculate **unit-point totals in every scoring quadrant and the exact console states** before using the last Order; never expose the Lt once you already lead.

## #222 — Nomads vs O-12: Blocking Zone's moving roller-girl objective

[Original Fast Panda report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XsV1FCd3fnI). **O-12 4–2 Nomads** in a **Dire Foes/custom Blocking Zone**, not standard ITS. Players received **free Lupe Balboa and Sonia Lacroix**, infiltrators could start unusually far into an enemy zone, and the tracked target **relocated each turn**. The source says the older mission is unofficial/outside ITS.

- **Observed — Nomads:** **Mary Problems (KHD / HD)**, **Jazz and Billie repeater/Pitcher**, **Taskmaster Red Fury with CrazyKoalas**, **Moderator/paramedic and M-Spider**, **Morlocks**, **intruder/rounder-style ranged support** and **Lupe Balboa**. Layered specialist/hacking options helped when the target came into range, but the faction lost an early **Lieutenant**, sharply limiting response.
- **Observed — O-12:** **Gamma gunfighter**, **Gangbuster** reserve/infiltration, **Cyberghost Lt**, **Bluecoat**, **Monstrucker Engineer**, and **Varangian** supported **Sonia Lacroix**. Gamma's multi-shot rifle was decisive in **late specialist hunting**, bringing the relevant secondary category toward O-12. The O-12 commander knowingly planned to **go second** and survive the first turn before contesting the moving marker.
- **Observed — mobility and tempo:** One side scored the objective in **round one and three**; the other won the **middle round** and **specialist-kill bonus**, resulting **4–2 O-12**. The target literally moved between rounds: neither player could hold a single final-turn camping position and assume that would suffice.
- **Observed — tactical mistakes:** Nomads/players **ignored the real CrazyKoala threat** on a critical angle, costing an important model; both sides had difficulty coordinating infiltration/LoF and target interaction. The players openly discuss an **early Lieutenant kill**, several specialist attrition trades, and how few useful Orders remained at the end.
- **Player view:** The creators enjoyed the **changing scoring position** and free character experimentation. This is a design assessment, **not** proof the included Dire Foes characters are optimal buys for Nomads or O-12.
- **Inference — counters:** An **O-12 Gamma + Gangbuster/Monstrucker + Cyberghost** mix can pressure Nomad **Jazz/Mary/Taskmaster** through both direct combat and specialist attrition, while the **Varangian** contests late HVT/zone points. The stronger answer to **Nomad repeaters/Koalas** is **route discipline plus spare Specialists**, not assuming a Gamma can activate every terminal itself. Conversely, Nomads should not reveal every Hacker while its Lieutenant and opponent's Gamma remain active.
- **Limit:** Free assigned characters, custom deployment, scoring moves and an unusually narrow objective. It is **not** evidence that O-12 beats Nomads in ordinary ITS.

**Mission tactic:** For **moving objectives**, build a **per-turn extraction/contest plan**; preserving the *last Specialist* matters more than holding a dominant fire lane for three turns.

## #223 — Vanilla Ariadna vs USAriadna: Wallace as the Corporate Appropriation breaker

[Original Robert Shepherd report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQjNDT-k9zg). **Vanilla Ariadna decisive victory** in **Corporate Appropriation ITS17-era**, creator estimates **~10–1 or 10–2** rather than counting a confirmed score. The opponent at one point **offered to concede**, but they continued to play out useful scoring routes.

- **Observed — vanilla/Tartary-based package:** **Colonel Voronin Lt + two Strelok Forward Observers/Decoys**, **Scout Minelayer (E/M mines)**, **two Tankhunter Portable Autocannons**, **Dynamo Duo (AP Spitfire + Boarding Shotgun Paramedic / smoke + mine dispenser)**, **Traktor Mul** resupply, **Dozer/KVP**, **Kövlk**, and **William Wallace** with **Chain of Command**. The creator intentionally traded TAK **linked Veteran Kazaks and multiple Scout availability** for cross-Ariadna units. This is a **vanilla Ariadna** experiment, not an actual Tartary Army Corps army list.
- **Observed — USAriadna defense/counterattack:** **Unknown Ranger**, **Foxtrot FOs**, **Maverick/Dynamo-type smoke**, **Minutemen**, **Beast Hunter**, **Sasha** and **Van Zant** were used to defend the enemy corporate objective and attempt a last-turn backfield strike. The scenario also had **panoplies and Classifieds** that could swing otherwise close totals.
- **Deciding sequence — Wallace:** **Wallace** moved on a flank, dealt with mines and **two Foxtrots**, then **destroyed the enemy objective in one close-combat engagement**, and continued to reveal and kill a **Foxtrot Lieutenant**. This caused **Loss of Lieutenant** at the key moment. Some Foxtrot **Flash Pulse** attempts narrowly failed; Wallace did not simply ignore their defensive tools.
- **Observed — Tankhunter counter:** An **Autocannon Tankhunter** took advantage of **Unknown Ranger splitting Burst**, won the face-to-face and removed a key attacker. In Ariadna's final turn another Tankhunter attacked and killed **Minutemen/Beast Hunter** using very dangerous explosive AP saves, while Wallace finished **Predator** by removing unconscious targets. **Dynamo Paramedic/FO** helped with side tasks.
- **Observed — USARF's last chance:** **Sasha** prepared smoke for **Van Zant**, who arrived near Voronin/the objective, killed Voronin, and **Berserked into the mission target**, but crossed too many mines/defensive lines. The attempt did **two wounds** to the object; the source says **three were required**, so it failed. Ariadna's victory remained substantial, not simply an opponent being tabled.
- **Player view — buy/list implications:** **One Autocannon Tankhunter** is high variance and two can be more consistent, but the creator warns that *two aren't strictly stronger than one for their price* (single-Wound ~36pt risk). He calls **Dynamo AP Spitfire + Paramedic** a high-utility 8–4 smoke/shooting pairing but concedes the AP Spitfire **didn't itself do much in this particular match**. He proposes alternate 49–60pt “flex” packages after the game; these are not observed results.
- **Inference — useful matchups:** **Wallace + Chain of Command + nearby smoke + anti-material melee** is a serious **Corporate Appropriation objective-killer** for **vanilla Ariadna** against **USARF Foxtrot screening**. **Double Tankhunter Autocannon** gives credible anti-heavy-gun answers to **Unknown Ranger/Minutemen** if positioned to force a favorable single shot. USAriadna should **protect its Lieutenant from close-range discover/kills, stack mine/smoke lanes, and stage Van Zant earlier** if the final corporate object needs multiple Damage rolls.
- **Limit:** Wallace got through Flash Pulse checks on favorable dice, USARF voluntarily played after a possible concession, and creator's score is **only approximate**. Do not automatically extend vanilla Ariadna results to **TAK** due to meaningful Fireteam/availability differences.

**Mission tactic:** In **Corporate Appropriation**, pre-plan a route with **anti-material target damage** plus **late Lieutenant-screen clearing**. A powerful attacker such as Wallace is more useful when **smoke comes from the other group**; the creator identifies that as a future improvement, **not played this time**.

## #224 — Shindenbutai vs Tohaa: Critical Intervention defender against the fortress

[Original Robert Shepherd report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vPztpiEWMy8). **Shindenbutai wins convincingly** as **Critical Intervention defender**; Tohaa attacked. No unambiguous final numeric OP is used here because the narration says **near-clean sweep** rather than presenting a verified signed score.

- **Observed — Shinden construction:** **Two Hatamoto Spitfires**, one **Lieutenant** with extra Lt Orders and one **NCO**, creating two strong Lt-Order consumers; **Tanuki Hacker** alongside **Shizoku Strategic Deployment rocket launcher** and **Yamabushi FTO/Paramedic** link (including an extra special shooting die), **two Keisotsu/Kyorjin-style Killers**, **Flash/Baggage REM**, **guided missile REM**, **Monstrucker**, and **foot Yojimbo with CrazyKoalas**. Source calls the **4 irregular troopers a real weakness**, and says after the game he'd trim them.
- **Observed — Tohaa strategy:** Tohaa attacker **Ed** used defensive **Kriegel Agent pheroware/E/M area**, **Sukeul**, **Makaul Eclipse**, **Libertos** and camouflaged/specialist tools to reach the **objective room**, open the data console, and escort a **data-pack carrier** away. One attempted opening on turn two failed repeatedly, and the attacker made a sound decision to pull back rather than leave the carrier exposed.
- **Deciding sequence:** Tohaa had severe first-turn failures, leaving insufficient tempo to build its fortress. The **Hatamoto** repeatedly used **Spitfires and powerful melee** to threaten room defenders and ultimately **killed the final Tohaa Paramedic holding the data pack**. Shinden had **central-room domination** and a further **Vigilance Classified** action, so the attacker scored its console opening but could not convert it into the intended protected extraction.
- **Observed — tactical counters:** **Guided missiles + quality Pitchers** pressure a massed Tohaa defense without accepting every **Kriegel Agent E/M** zone-of-control trigger. Hatamoto **Mimetism/Nanoscreen** can strafe and close into melee against **Makauls**, but the defender specifically warns **not** to casually enter a Kriegel Agent's E/M area: ignoring Stealth there can stall even a strong HI attack. **Foot Yojimbo** was deliberately used as a more deployable/prone **Koala support** body than the faster bike.
- **Player views:** The Shinden player favors a future **MSV2 Tanuki gunner / Rui Shi** addition and **two Yamabushi Paramedics**, while cutting the **Shizoku/fireteam cost or extra irregular troopers**. These are *ideas*; the current played list used the Shizoku, not that revised roster. For Tohaa, he suggests **Combat Group 2 smoke, Samsa or Gao-Tarsos** as first-turn ways to attack rather than only fortifying; these were **not observed counterplays** in this game.
- **Inference — counterplay:** **Hatamoto NCO/Lt duo + guided missile + Pitcher/Tanuki Hacker**, supported by expendable **Koalas**, gives **Shindenbutai good tools for attacking into a Tohaa defensive room** in this **specific asymmetrical mission**. Tohaa can still leverage **Kriegel E/M, Eclipse and deep symbiote resilience**; its list must **plan for going first/attacking** and not rely exclusively on being a second-turn fortress.
- **Limit:** Opponent's spectacularly poor early dice, asymmetry, and no verified exact OP prevent any defensible claim that “Shindenbutai counters Tohaa” universally. Historical **Strategic Deployment** nuance: the player explicitly rejects stacking native Yamabushi Forward Deployment with scenario-only forward-deployment enhancement; check the N5 version before copying any deployment claim.

**Mission tactic:** **Critical Intervention isn't symmetrical**: the attacker must budget **open console + carry data pack + escape + protect carrier**, while the defender's goal is to **kill the carrier and dominate the room**. The defender can survive until late and then reverse the entire attacking turn.

## #225 — Vanilla Combined Army vs Steel Phalanx: multispecies challenge list and Sepsitor snowball

[Original Robert Shepherd report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGFPRacaq1g). **Combined Army wins decisively** in **Area of Interest**, with **Steel Phalanx 2 OP** as recounted and **CA's exact total unverified**. The vanilla CA composition was a supporter-requested **“one of every CA species” challenge**, not a regular optimally filtered list.

- **Observed — CA package:** **Charontid Lieutenant Hacker / Sepsitor Plus** with substantial Orders, **Umbra Legate** with **Forward Deployment + Tactical Awareness and Vulcan Shotgun**, **Daturazi smoke**, **Vector Plasma Sniper** fireteam with **Unidron FO + K1 combi**, an **underused hidden Puzzler** with **E/M deployment**, **Libertos**, and other race-representative species. The presenter particularly praises **Vector Mimetism-3/MSV2 + burst bonus + plasma sniper in a smoke-capable list**; he states he'd under-rated it previously.
- **Observed — Steel Phalanx:** **Ajax**, **Phoenix**, **Eudoros**, **Nesaie Alke**, **Myrmidon Officer**, and a support screen attempted midfield area and console scoring. Losing **Ajax to the Vector plasma sniper** early was a catastrophic reduction of close-combat/board-control strength.
- **Deciding sequence — hidden E/M:** An unrevealed **Puzzler** intercepted **Eudoros**, **Isolated/Immobilized** him (the narration says “E/MaRATed”), forcing him to choose between spending Orders to kill a **Libertos** or clearing the threat and reaching the scoring area. He killed Libertos but could not recover Eudoros before later CA Orders **Umbra-shotgun** him down.
- **Deciding sequence — Sepsitor:** **Charontid** moved up under **Cybermask/Submask** play, attacked scattered **Phoenix/Myrmidon/Thorakites** positions and **Sepsitored two Steel models**. Those models immediately became **reactive template threats against their former comrades**, and **Phoenix died to the converted troops' AROs** on the next Steel activation. **Sepsitor is not merely a kill**: it turns enemy order pieces into additional screening bodies and reactive threats.
- **Observed — finishing score:** CA retained the antenna/Area of Interest passive scoring while Steel's only **two OP** came from a late **Myrmidon Officer** movement/classified effort. The CA player emphasizes that the game swung on **unfortunate early dice**, especially Ajax and Eudoros, alongside strong Puzzler timing.
- **Player opinion:** CA would normally bring *more* Daturazi/Krakots than this challenge permitted; he liked the **Umbra's Tactical Awareness and Vulcan burst** (but took an optimistic risk and suffered an Unconscious wound in one exchange). Against Steel, he recommends **locating a Baggage REM for redeployable Para/EM mines**; Steel's player hadn't created this mine defense early. These are roster advice and after-action hypotheses, not fully tested counterplay.
- **Inference — specific matchup:** **Smoke + Vector plasma/MSV2**, a hidden **Puzzler E/M trap**, and **Charontid Sepsitor** provide vanilla CA several orthogonal threats to **Steel Phalanx elite fireteams**, whose high-value melee figures can be stalled by E/M and punished by plasma. Conversely, Steel should **screen Ajax's approach against unexpected plasma angles, deny Charontid penetration with mines/templates, and force the Puzzler to reveal before its elite core advances**.
- **Limit:** A deliberately constrained all-species list, extreme dice timing, converted-trooper snowball and N5 patch ambiguity; **not a general Combined-vs-Steel tier verdict**. Recheck current Army Sepsitor/Cybermask legal interactions before exporting tactics to N5.3.

**Mission tactic:** In **Area of Interest**, the ability to **hold antenna scoring and a special-area point while the attacking opponent must spend Orders clearing an E/M trap** can decide the game even before tabling is considered.

## #226 — Druze vs Varuna: Crossing Lines with one TAG and one combat group (PROJECTED 8–0)

[Original Tabletop Throwdown stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DI0qkopD9u8). **Druze declared winner after early stop**, host **projects 8–0**, not a completed final OP. **Format constraint:** *one combat group*, **exactly one TAG in each list**, special **Booty on TAG**, with **unusual 75-point forward deployment** and two exclusion/dead zones. This is **not ordinary ITS** despite using a Crossing Lines-like objective.

- **Observed, Druze:** **Scarface + Cordelia**, **Fiddler**, **Kunai Solutions sniper**, **Brawler shock/MSV/ranged fire support**, **Killer Hacker/Pitcher** and specialists formed the only legal compact group. Scarface was able to exert very long-range threat (including **Panzerfaust reaction fire**) while **Kunai/Brawler** covered unexpected low-visibility flanks. Fiddler supplied speed/engineering options and late console access if the game had continued.
- **Observed, Varuna:** **Cutter TAG NCO** began as a hidden **Mimetism-6** centerpiece; **Patsy Garnett with Helot FTO/Neurokinetics/Sensor**, **ORC + Kamau Haris**, **Bipandra**, **Uma**, **Techbee** and **specialists** provided quality BS and some medical/technical ability, but *no Hacker* as commentators noticed. The Cutter had no easy external recovery plan after entering a dense multi-gun web.
- **Deciding sequence:** Varuna advanced the **Cutter** very aggressively and it was caught by overlapping AROs, including a **Druze hacker/Pitcher lane, Scarface and long-range sniper/Brawler**. The Cutter failed to break free and was ultimately destroyed by close combat after failing reactive defense. The **ORC/Kamau** survivors then tried to retreat/recover but were picked apart; **Scarface Panzerfaust** took the ORC down. The players chose to stop rather than spend a full final mission round.
- **Explicit rules dispute:** Varuna player and opponent argued about **which Hacker could see the Cutter over the roof and therefore where the Pitcher/ARO came from**. Some attacks had been declared based on different Line-of-Fire understandings, and the players acknowledged communication confusion afterward. Do **not** treat that sequence as a pristine proof of TAG countertech or legal final OP.
- **Player opinion:** The observers propose **fighting the regular Hacker with Cutter earlier** or suppressing an opening fire lane rather than chasing the Brawler. The Varuna player describes **TAG tunnel vision** and unfamiliarity with Cutter/hidden-TAG play; do not infer that Cutter is inherently weak to Druze.
- **Inference — situational counter:** **Druze Scarface/Cordelia + Kunai + Brawler + Pitcher/KHD**, in a **single-group/one-TAG event format**, can **saturate the Cutter's exit lanes** and force expensive repairs, especially if the Cutter reveals at the wrong angle. Against this screen, Varuna should **probe repeaters/ARO ownership first** and use **Patsy/Helot sensor/long-lane support** rather than exposing the TAG to multiple threats simultaneously.
- **Limit:** **Projected 8–0**, model familiarity/LoF dispute, custom roster rules and an *uncompleted* game. **Do not count 8–0 as a played victory margin or change must-have/buy lists from this single match.**

**Mission tactic:** Crossing Lines' forward-zone/dead-zone geometry changes every movement. **Deploying 75 points near the enemy** can be an alpha threat, but a layered ARO trap can punish a single expensive Cutter trying to control both areas.

## #227 — Neoterran Capitaline Army vs Ramah: Evacuation (NCA 7–1)

[Original Tabletop Throwdown game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NVN3qXXhsN4). **NCA 7–1 Ramah**; stream first casually says **3–1** until both Classifieds and multiple evacuated civilians are correctly counted. Played in an **East Coast online tournament** context, with participant results included in event score tally.

- **Observed, NCA:** **Uhlan TAG** (camo / Feuerbach), **Aquila Guard FTO MSV3**, **Shona Carano**, **Richard Quinn Chain of Command**, **Bolt sniper**, **Hexa KHD** and **Blocker Hacker**, **CSU**, **Techbee**, and support REMs/flash bot. Hidden/interlocking BS threats protected evacuation approaches while **Shona + CSU/Blocker** handled **CivEvac** collection and delivery.
- **Observed, Ramah:** Highly compressed **8-model** list with **Tarik Mansuri Lieutenant**, **Khawarij Core/links (Doctor + NCO MSV2 Mark12 + Chain of Command/Drop Bears + HRL)**, **two Namurr (Tactical Awareness specialist)**, and **Nahab** (infiltration). Multiple **Tactical Awareness** plus **Lt/NCO** gave nominally many Orders, but the limited **distinct bodies** made every casualty disproportionately costly.
- **Deciding sequence:** Ramah attempted an aggressive forward opening, but valuable troops were removed quickly and **Tarik** could no longer lead a recovery. With sparse orders and **Retreat!**, the Ramah side could not collect enough civilians in time. **Shona** physically moved and evacuated **multiple HVTs**, and NCA scored two **Classifieds**, bringing the correct result to **7–1**. NCA lost relatively little army value (the players discuss how a few remaining units could still defend lanes).
- **Observed / critique:** The opponents explicitly corrected their **CivEvac rule interpretation** around carrying **two civilians** and the first-turn extraction prohibition. Those mechanics are central: a proposed early rush to grab-and-immediately-extract civilians was impossible under the mission packet.
- **Player view:** The Ramah commander says the list was **not a strong NCA matchup**, and with a short starting roster should possibly have **deployed defensively** on turn one. ### General tactical rules from these ten games

1. **First-vs-second-turn is mission dependent, not faction dependent:** the **MO mirror** #221 explicitly favors second-turn scoring in that one game, #224 has an **asymmetric attacker/defender room**, #229 scores **every round** so neglecting early zones is unrecoverable, while #230's last-turn central touch can **deny** a three-point objective. Use the **actual mission pack** before selecting Initiative.
2. **High-point pieces must convert their extra Orders into objectives:** **Hatamoto NCO/Lt** (#224), **Kornak** (#228/#229), **Aquila/Uhlan vs Shona specialists** (#227), and **Wallace** (#223) are not interchangeable. Lt/Tactical Awareness on a gun is useful only when the list also has specialists that can spend Orders on real scoring.
3. **Anti-hacking and anti-elite counterplay are not identical:** **Kriegel E/M zones** delay Hatamoto (#224), **Puzzler E/M and Charontid Sepsitor** dismantle Steel (#225), but **Kosmoflot's non-Hackable gun/smoke network** forces Nomads to use other tools (#230). A matched list must consider each enemy's vulnerability rather than including one generic Hacker answer.
4. **Deployment threatens the opponent before the game starts:** 75pt staggered forward pockets change Crossing Lines for **Druze/Varuna** (#226) and **Morats/Tunguska** (#229); **Moran/Koala vs Gangbuster** (#222) becomes pivotal under custom opposite-DZ deployment. Avoid porting these deployment assumptions into regular ITS.
5. **Reactive geometry, mines and cheap bodies buy Orders:** a **Koala**, **SAS mine**, **Puzzler**, **Kriegel Agent** or **Heckler** can save its army simply by forcing the opponent to **discover, dodge, clear, detour, or spend more Orders**. Don't count a unit as “failed” just because it didn't kill.
6. **Rule mistakes can completely change the apparent result:** #226 involved an **ARO Line-of-Sight disagreement** and concession; #228 had missed **Reset** declarations and mission repositioning confusion; #230 ended on a highly precise **touching/LoF/Peripheral movement** interaction. Document the dispute rather than laundering the margin into matchup strength.
7. **Avoid buying/proxying based on one episode:** #221 a **200pt mirror**, #222 **Dire Foes custom**, #223 a **vanilla Ariadna Wallace special list**, #225 an **all-species vanity challenge**, #226 an **exactly one TAG** event, and #224 a poor-dice asymmetric defense. Only recurring role evidence across current N5.3 list contexts should affect faction “must-have / go-to / avoid” files.
