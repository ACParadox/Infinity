# Four- and five-model Fireteams versus three-member Cores — N5 battle-report extraction

## Scope and confidence

This synthesis reviews the numbered tactical dossiers **#001–#241** in this directory, including opponents and different submitted lists where named. It studies **physical models linked**, not the word Core in isolation. A Core can have only three physical members. The numbered catalog contains duplicate videos (for example #010/#033, #011/#030, #009/#028, #006/#031) and repeated tournament interviews (#175/#176); **rows are evidence records, not independent games or independent wins**.

- **E — explicit:** count and member roles actually named in the dossier or deployment; still historical, not a certified N5.3 Army export.
- **C — qualified:** count identified or supported but one or more names, formation types or specific rounds uncertain. Never fill missing slots from Army legality alone.
- **Excluded:** discussed/unplayed links, peripherals, Decoys/Holoechoes, grouped combat-group troops and mere Fireteam Level references. Examples: #014's Level 5 Alguacils is not necessarily five physical models; #116's discussed five-Montesa mixture was not proved played; #162's five Harbinger/Blackheart list was submitted but unplayed; #178's four Karhu are roster bodies, not a proven link.

The **current** N5.3 [Fireteam Bonuses](../../rules/6_Command%20and%20Fireteams/Fireteam_Bonuses.md) specify Level 1 linked activation, Level 2 +1 **Special Die** on BS Attacks, Level 3 +3 Discover/+1 Dodge, Level 4 +1 BS, and Level 5 Sixth Sense. These levels depend on matching-unit/counts-as **qualifying** members; they do not follow physical size alone. Historical formation permission/profile costs must be checked against the relevant battle-era chart and a current N5.3 sectorial source before using a formation today.

## What larger Fireteams are actually buying

### A. Defensive platform + otherwise useful order bodies — most reliable large-Core rationale

The clearest architectural examples are:

- **Kestrel Griffin/Fennec/Bipandra five** (#175, and #002's four-body version): long ARO plus repair/healing and cheap Fennec specialists; the winning CanCon list also had **Hetkari + Firefly Duo**, Banshee, Scarecrow and warbands for independent mission routes. The 5-model Core is the *anchor*, not the entire attacking army.
- **Varuna Fusilier/Quinn five** (#203): cheaply protected Hacker/Paramedic/Quinn beside **two independent TAGs**. Those TAGs, not the Fusilier formation, provide much of the mobile hitting power. This is a case where adding cheap regular troopers has comparatively low opportunity cost.
- **TAK Line Kazak/Frontovik five** (#087) and **Oban Keisotsu/Kempeitai five** (#069): one or two valuable guns/command specialists ride with ordinary order contributors. The Core can protect one lane while other models do the mission.
- **Military Orders five Crosiers/Knight Commander** (#017): cheap-enough reactive line with defensive weapons; active Knights can be deployed elsewhere.
- **Bakunin four-member Reverend groups** (#054/#059/#060/#095): a four-person shell combines hacker, healer, and credible ranged gun, useful if several roles were needed anyway.
- **Onyx Unidron/Nexus four** (#176): backfield gun/Firewall/Lt architecture rather than a mobile assault brick.

**Prefer four/five over three** when the additional members would be purchased for Regular Orders, relevant ranged AROs, medical/engineering access or mission security even *without* the Fireteam. Do not spend high premiums just to reach a nominal Level 4/5: check whether all members qualify.

### B. Mobile mixed-arms attack and specialist delivery — mission/route dependent

- **Torchlight four** (#231): Hellblazer Flammenspeer, Wave Rider Hacker, Jackboot HMG NCO and Kludger Engineer, **plus** independent Moonrakers and Jamie Arantes. In Battleground the latter flankers and deep-band occupation produced the decisive swing; the four-person team supported the plan but did not win alone. Reported **10–0** was one lopsided match.
- **Varuna four** (#019): Kamau gun + two Montesas + Pathfinder, in two distinct submitted configurations. Provides fast attack and specialists on a common route; not necessarily efficient if the Pathfinder must separate to score.
- **Hassassin four** (#088): Asawira guns + Ghulam NCO/Smoke + Barid KHD/Pitcher. Large enough to bring attack, vision and hacking protection on one approach, but the army still needs Fiday/Ghazi/shrouded threats elsewhere.
- **Tunguska four Hollow Men/Perseus** (#108): ML ARO, Hacker, Pitcher and durable combatant can advance in stages and be supported by an **independent Clockmaker**. Do not assume an attached Engineer unless actually listed.
- **Morat five** (#198): Suryat/Kaitoks/Vanguard/Dartok Core plus separate Harises. Against Military Orders' compact linked specialists it lost **2–6**, illustrating that five durable bodies do not ensure the last scoring interaction.
- **Druze four + other small teams** (#202): four-body gun/hacker/paramedic Core, Brawler Haris of three and Scarface/Cordelia Duo. Valuable because jobs are *distributed* across teams, rather than building only one huge attack group.
- **Shinden four** (#233): Yamabushi/linked launcher/Hatamoto/G-Samurai plus independent Yojimbo, Tanuki and objective tools. The source stops at a **projected 9–1**; its linked shooting impact is observed, not a full final result.

**Prefer four over three** when the fourth model actually adds a complementary role that the same attack route needs, or a qualifying fourth raises the decisive gun from Level 3 to 4. Prefer **three** when a fast specialist must peel away, when one member slows the other two, or when central mines, repeater arcs and templates make movement of four bases expensive.

### C. Overloaded elite Core — the most important failure mode

- **Ramah Tarik + four Khawarij** (#227): a probable five-body Core within an **eight-model list**, two Namurr and a Nahab. Tactical Awareness and Lieutenant/NCO options partly offset the small pool but cannot replace independent civilian scorers. **NCA 7–1 Ramah**: the smaller NCA attack team and free-operating mission workers did the evacuating. This is strong *opportunity-cost evidence*, not proof Tarik/Khawarij is intrinsically bad.
- **White Company five Hawkwood/Emily/Fusiliers** (#112): an enemy hacker attack killed a Fusilier Hacker; shrinking from five to four removed Sixth Sense and enabled Charontid to eliminate Hawkwood. This shows the defensive upgrade is **threshold-fragile**.
- **Tunguska four Securitate/Interventor/Grenzer** (#137) and **Neoterra four ORC/Blocker/Fusilier/Pathfinder** (#122): both cases explicitly note **Level 2** bonus despite four physical bodies. Added models can be useful for skills, but are not automatically delivering +1 BS.
- **Torchlight five Silverstar/Hellblazer/Wave Rider** (#053): five physical bodies, but the army lost Frontline **4–7** to a different Nomads scoring approach; do not attribute result solely to team size.
- **Torchlight four Hacker-heavy Core** (#199) versus **NCA three-person Bolt/Hannibal/Shona** plus Maximus/Pathfinder Duo: NCA won **6–5 Outbreak** despite losing most models, because it banked scan/stabilization points. This is an especially good argument for *independent task runners* rather than maximal linked concentration.

**Default to three** if the fourth or fifth is a premium combat model that duplicates the same gun/mission role, reduces independent scoring routes, or leaves a large part of the army in a fragile single coherency bubble.

## Three versus four versus five: an actionable list-building test

| Question | Three-member Core favored | Four-member Core favored | Five-member Core favored |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Extra model's standalone role** | Little role beyond possible bonus | Important Doctor/Hacker/Engineer/ARO/specialist | Cheap and useful order carrier **or** very important fifth utility |
| **Qualifying same-unit/counts-as models** | Two or three already do the job | Actual four qualifying models achieve Level 4 | Actual five qualifying models achieve Level 5 Sixth Sense |
| **Primary use** | Fast gun + protection + objective route | Gun + two support roles + redundancy | Backfield defense, resilient scoring fortress or specific synergistic assault |
| **Terrain / threats** | Narrow passages, many templates, repeaters, multiple tasks | Moderate lanes and shared objective route | Relatively static site or well-screened coordinated route |
| **Other army assets** | Several independent scorers and flankers | At least one independent mission/assault alternative | Still have separate high-quality scorer, angle-breaker and Orders |
| **Casualty threshold** | Lose bonus as three becomes two | Losing one drops Level 4 -> 3 if all qualified | Losing one drops **Sixth Sense** immediately even if five were qualified |

**Practical default:** Start with the **best legal three-member Core** for the mission; justify the fourth and fifth **one at a time**. For each marginal model, record its points/SWC, whether it increases the actual Fireteam **level**, which mission action/independent attack it displaces, and whether you would pay for it unlinked. The fourth usually has a clearer case than the fifth unless Level 5 Sixth Sense protects a vital ARO/defensive point.

For a **defensive budget Core**, be more permissive: a cheap fourth/fifth troop supplies a Regular Order regardless, so adding it to the formation may incur little *marginal list cost*. For a **mobile elite Core**, be stricter: another large base usually brings formation/coherency risks and often duplicates abilities a free actor could deliver better.

## Follow-up evidence to collect from future reports

For any specific comparison, capture: source/game and rules patch; mission and opponent; **physical Core size**; **qualifying same-unit/counts-as count**; exact composition and points/SWC; other simultaneous links; independent specialists; combat-group Order totals; initial versus re-formed team; actual mission actions contributed; whether the fourth/fifth **survived and activated**; and whether the losing side failed because of **link mechanics** or a separately identifiable scoring error.

Do **not** compute four/five-Core win rates from these dossiers: there is no consistent denominator, replays are non-independent, and games span tutorials, custom scenarios, concessions, different Army patches and full tournaments.
