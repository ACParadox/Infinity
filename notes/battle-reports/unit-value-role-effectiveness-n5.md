# What actually makes an Infinity N5 unit valuable? — battle-report evidence and role rankings

**Prepared:** 2026-11-01  
**Evidence baseline:** [Master N5 battle-report index, entries #001–#241](n5-battle-reports-and-tournament-reports.md) and its [#001–#010](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) through [#231–#240](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) analysis batches; [#241](241-241-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) is partial.  
**Purpose:** Evaluate **what units accomplish in the mission**, not how impressive their kill counts, datasheets, or final army survival look. This is a **cross-report qualitative synthesis**, not a statistically estimated faction win-rate or an N5.3 points-efficiency database.

> **Core finding:** A unit is valuable when it changes the *feasible scoring sequence*: creates a route, preserves a scorer, spends a small number of friendly Orders to gain mission points, forces the opponent to spend more Orders than they can afford, or denies a counterattack at the scoring moment. Damage is a means to those ends, not a substitute for them.

## 1. Dataset, scope and interpretation limits

The master index contains **241 entries**, *not 241 independent, equally documented competitive games*. It includes full filmed battles, podcast winner interviews, tournament recaps spanning several rounds, historical forum indexes, re-listed/duplicate sources, teaching games, 200/250-point games, custom narrative missions, remembered/conceded results, and occasional entries without a usable original transcript. Examples: #030 reuses #011; #082 is metadata-only; #120 includes a mutually agreed rewind; #146 uses modified league scoring; #194 is 200 points; #240 is a custom Megabeast game; #241 explicitly lacks primary gameplay evidence.

Interpretation rules:

1. **Observed** = original footage/transcript or a firsthand report explicitly describes the action. **Retrospective** = a participant recounts a tournament game; often useful but less precise on Order counts. **Player opinion** = a player's preference or attribution. **Inference** = our tactical interpretation; **not** another observed result. **Unverified** = insufficient evidence; never convert it to a zero.
2. Separate **units**, **profiles**, and **packages**. A Shrouded with Dazer is not interchangeable with every Shrouded; a Squalos + Pathfinder + Machinist result does not measure Squalos alone. Check the relevant historical Army revision before copying a loadout.
3. **Recorded results are conditional** on mission pack, terrain, initiative, Orders, deployment, opponent strategy, dice, and rules revision. The corpus spans initial N5, N5.1/1.1, N5.2, and N5.3/ITS18. An old interaction is not automatically legal or equally priced today.
4. **Do not treat inclusion in a winning list as causal contribution.** Record what the unit actually did. Likewise a losing list can contain an extremely efficient delaying unit.
5. Do not double-count the same tournament recounted in several episodes; for example, treat the CanCon Vanilla Ariadna discussion in #161 and #237 as **overlapping event evidence**, not two independent tournament wins.
6. **No general kill/order/point estimates are defensible for most units** from these written reports: full per-activation Order logs and complete army-point casualty ledgers are uncommon. Use exact numbers only when the source expressly states them.

### What is and is not ranked here

- **Ranked:** **role-performance patterns**, with named **illustrative units/packages**, evidence IDs and likely counterplay.
- **Not ranked:** a single faction-agnostic tier list of every N5 unit, universal points-per-Order, projected win probability, or every current profile/SWC choice.
- **Evidence levels:** **Repeated** = broadly recurring behavior across more than one distinct report/setting; **Case** = one concrete played example, possibly supported by adjacent similar cases; **Tentative** = player opinion, special format, unclear identity, or low-quality observation. These are *confidence descriptions*, not performance scores.
- **Ranking convention:** within each role, **1** means the strongest *currently demonstrated way to convert that role into mission advantage*, **2** a competitive alternative, and **3** a useful but more conditional or counterable expression. The order is an **analytical priority for list-building**, *not* an empirical claim that one named profile is mathematically stronger than another.

## 2. Six dimensions of unit value

| Dimension | What to measure | Strong performance | Failure mode / misleading proxy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Attack efficiency & own Orders** | Friendly Orders spent to make a required threat cease blocking scoring; relevant enemy Order engines/specialists removed; damage/mission access per Order; failed attempts and follow-up movement | A small attack opens a courier route, kills a Lieutenant/key specialist, or prevents several enemy activations *and still leaves Orders to score* | A TAG wipes four models over nearly a turn, or an HMG repeatedly grinds a noncritical ARO; enemy points destroyed alone exaggerates payoff |
| **B. Mission scoring** | Actual OP enabled/secured, objectives touched and held at the correct time, qualified WIP interactions, courier movement/return, Classified breadth | An otherwise unremarkable Specialist scores a button, retrieves and delivers a box, or occupies a key zone on the final Order | “Specialist” lacks the specific mission skill, or an impressive shooter never makes the delivery |
| **C. Opponent Order tax / denial** | Explicit enemy Orders spent Discovering, Dodging, Resetting, killing, repairing, circling, or clearing mines/repeaters; opponent Orders functionally lost by Isolation/LoL | A cheap mine/Flash/Camo/ARO piece delays the opponent until they cannot complete the mission | Defense gets bypassed with smoke, a safer route, or a single favorable shot; an unactivated defensive piece that nobody needed to engage earns no hypothetical credit |
| **D. Survivability & endgame utility** | Surviving mission-capable bodies, retained Regular Orders, ability to recover states/wounds, later-round zone weight, preserved specialists | A durable attacker can retreat and still support scoring, or a cheap second-line courier survives to secure final OP | High surviving army-point total that cannot reach, legally interact with, or hold the required objective |
| **E. Flexibility & counterplay** | Alternative attack paths, smoke/White Noise, hacking/anti-hacking, Sensor, mines, aerial/vertical arrival, different qualified specialist skills; resilience if primary plan fails | One list can win without fighting a Cutter, can solve a hidden marker or a TAG, and can switch between shooting and scoring | All capability is concentrated in one Hackable attacker, one Hacker, one qualified button-pusher or an easily bypassed sniper |
| **F. Order generation and support leverage** | Useful LT/NCO/Tactical Awareness/Strategos orders actually converted; coordinated/command-token group transfers; Engineer/Doctor/Servant repair; price of supporting the main piece | Extra action economy reaches the *correct* WIP test or restores an attacker *before* it matters | More nominal Orders but wrong combat group, no eligible specialist, inaccessible repair target or no spare movement Orders |

**Record six separate dimension ratings**, never add them blindly. An Order forced from the enemy is situationally more or less valuable depending on whether it prevents a winning action; mission OP in one scenario may be unavailable in another.

### A concrete annotation rule

For each use of a unit, write **Observed outcome → mechanism → counterplay**. Example: “Cenobite HRL remained a blocking threat; enemy Nisse spent ~5–6 Orders removing it; Machinist subsequently lacked the needed pickup tempo; alternative is bypassing it or removing it with fewer Orders” (#194). Avoid “Cenobite = six Orders of value in every game.”

Recommended dimension rubric for **one specific game**, with **N/O** always available:

- **0 = negative realized contribution:** its deployment/activation demonstrably consumed scarce Orders or exposed the scoring plan, without the intended gain. Do **not** mark 0 because a model died or did not get a kill.
- **1 = minor:** provided presence, a constrained option or modest pressure; no documented decisive scoring/tempo change.
- **2 = material:** opened a needed route, preserved scoring, removed a relevant threat, forced significant resources or carried out a valuable task.
- **3 = pivotal:** its documented action/survival/denial plausibly decided an OP category or blocked a mission-winning response; distinguish “decisive as narrated” from a proven counterfactual.
- **N/O = not observed / not attributable**, including missing activation evidence, passive deterrence without a specific opponent reaction, and profiles not reliably identified.

For **A** and **C** record actual Orders alongside this rubric *when narrated*; otherwise use qualitative tags such as “multiple,” “one key attempt,” or “not tracked,” never manufactured estimates. For **B**, distinguish **directly scored** from **enabled another unit to score**. For **F**, do not credit the same extra Order once to its Lieutenant and again to the scorer in a summed total.

## 3. Provisional role-specific rankings

These are **role-architecture rankings** derived from the reviewed examples, not fixed top-unit purchases. A named unit can rank highly in one role and be weak in another.

### 3.1 Mission acquisition, buttons and cargo delivery

| Role rank | Pattern and examples | Why it works | Evidence / caveat |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Protected, independent forward Specialist + delivery route** — Nomad **Zero** with Lunokhod/REM support; Ariadna **Strelok FO / Scout** with independent Dynamo/Kövlk; Shasvastii **Shrouded** as zone-preserving midfield body | Starts near the task, avoids unnecessary fights, survives after the primary gun goes down, turns hidden position and movement Orders into real OP | **Repeated motif**: #034 Zero beacon recovery/delivery; #161/#237 Ariadna mission-first; #196 Shrouded area occupation. Profiles/missions differ, so do not pool as a numerical unit leaderboard |
| **2** | **Durable or reusable Specialist network** — OSS **Posthuman Mk2/Mk5** with Garuda/Shukra; Tohaa **Sakiel/Rasail/Kaeltar** courier shell; ISS **Koga Ninja / Deva / Pheasant / Zhanying** redundancy | Retains several independent qualified scorers after an alpha, and can recover a lost plan | #114 Posthuman final console (opponent built for another mission); #189 Tohaa Data Harvest (circle placement abnormal); #193 ISS Unmasking (custom radial deployment) |
| **3** | **Fast secondary REM/flyer/vehicle scorer** — **Pathfinder**, **Bulleteer**, **Akal**, **Garuda**, **Jamie Arantes** when mission-compatible | A relatively few-Order flank/last-turn run turns prior attack pressure into a score, or reaches a zone the main gun cannot | #019 Squalos/Pathfinder package; #092 decisive final REM zone move; #116 Akal alpha extraction; #114 Garuda geometry; #231 Jamie flanking. **Do not assume every one of these loadouts is an eligible Specialist in every mission** |

**Opposing pattern:** The three-TAG Vanilla PanO army had frightening attacks but minimal Classified coverage and lost **5–2** to Hassassin in Firefight, where Hassassin completed the necessary Classified tasks (#214). This criticizes **role coverage in that list**, not Cutter/Tikbalang/Squalos universally.

### 3.2 Order taxation, deterrence and board control

| Role rank | Pattern and examples | What it buys | Evidence / counterplay |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Distributed Camo + mine/Dazer/Decoy footprint** — **Shrouded** Minelayer/Dazer, **Strelok Decoy** around Tankhunter, **SAS** mines, **Moran** CrazyKoala/Repeater | Multiple uncertain attack lanes and continued zone presence; forces decisions before the opposing Specialist can act | #196 Shrouded zone win and Dazer delay; #161 Strelok/Tankhunter deception in champion recap; #230 SAS central denial; #014/#034 Moran control. **Counter:** Sensor, safe templates, alternate routes, correctly timed Discover/Reveal |
| **2** | **Cheap reactive speed bumps** — **Fugazi Flash Pulse**, **Cenobite HRL**, **Gaki** threat, **Puzzler** adhesive/E/M trap | Absorbs opponent's best Orders, often without achieving a kill itself | #116 Fugazi repeatedly stopped scans; #194 Cenobite cost Nisse ~5–6 Orders; #215 Gaki psychological trade/space threat (*deterrence mostly player assessment*); #001 Puzzler stopped Su-Jian with adhesive control. **Counter:** route around, sacrificial clearance, smoke, efficient long-range removal |
| **3** | **Single heavy anchor / protected long-lane gun** — **Gator**, TR HMG REM, **Hawkwood**, **Blackheart** | May impose a severe resource tax where its lane physically covers the objective | #071 Morats spent ~10 Orders attacking Gator (**custom** scenario); #189 TR REM dominated unusually close delivery circles; #120 Hawkwood forced TAGs into poor exchanges but sequence was **rewound**; #162 Blackheart remarks are retrospective. **Counter:** avoid line, alternate objective, hacking, White Noise/Disco/smoke, flank |

**Important distinction:** the opponent must *actually spend or forgo* valuable Orders. A marker that stays hidden with no demonstrated effect is **unobserved deterrence**, not a confirmed “four Orders wasted.”

### 3.3 Active attack and mission-breaking

| Role rank | Pattern and examples | Benefit and limit | Evidence |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Attack that directly completes the scored task** — **Wallace** anti-materiel run for Corporate Appropriation; **Seraph** fast objective destruction with Defersen/Knight support; McMurrough + **Muyib/Yara/Leila** D-Charge/smoke route in Double Bind | Turns force into explicit objective points, not just casualties. The anti-materiel weapon/skill must match the mission | #223 Wallace destroyed target, then eliminated LT; #181 champion double-Seraph list on four rounds (retrospective; he switched lists versus Morats); #232 observed Hassassin Sabotage path with no reliable final numeric winner |
| **2** | **Gunfighter + another unit that scores** — **Squalos + Pathfinder + Machinist**; **Xeodron Duo + Kurnau/EVO**; **Cutter + Bulleteer + Engineer** | Main gun removes an actual obstacle or absorbs pressure; a different model finishes the objective | #019 3W–2L Varuna tournament; #212 Onyx scored four Countermeasures Classifieds with specialist support; #092 Varuna last-turn REM scored zone while Cutter survived/was repaired |
| **3** | **Solo high-damage alpha without objective reserve** — **Sphinx** deep attack, isolated premium TAG or all-in flyer package | Can become powerful when enemy Order/specialist structure collapses; otherwise very Order hungry and answerable by alternate scoring | #231 Sphinx ~4 kills for 9–10 Orders, then lost 10–0 on Battleground; #214 three-TAG shooting lacked Classifieds; #193 Haqq double-flyer alpha removed pieces but ISS retained enough objective Specialists. **Not a negative rating of the profiles independent of those plans** |

**Decision:** Before a premium attacker is activated, ask “What **specific scoring obstacle** must it remove, how many Orders can it spend, and what remaining body gets the points?” If there is no answer, its expected *mission* return is uncertain even if its combat return is high.

### 3.4 Hacking, status control and electronic denial

| Role rank | Pattern and examples | Evidence and counterplay |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | **Repeater/hacker coverage directly blocking the scoring route** — Tunguska **Interventor + Heckler/Hollow Man** network, Nomad **Jazz/Moran**, Bakunin **Zoe + Initiate Repeaters** | #151: Steel Phalanx spent Orders Resetting Isolation/IMM while Tunguska scored Hardlock 5–0 (**unusually favorable hacking rolls**). #034: Jazz Pitcher network consumed recovery Orders in B-Pong. #194: Zoe Carbonite stopped a threatening REM so Bakunin collected Supplies |
| **2** | **Attack plus cyber-specific insurance** — **Defersen + Seraph**, **Zulu-Cobra KHD/Pitcher + Fusilier Hacker**, **Dartok Pitcher + guided support** | #181: double-Seraph winner explicitly paired TAGs with anti-hacking; #019: Zulu-Cobra hacking isolated a Zeta; #118: Dartok Repeaters enabled guided attacks on protected Svalarheima troops. Judge whether the cyber sequence uses fewer Orders than shooting/going around |
| **3** | **Network without suitable targets, protected nodes, or mission agency** | #230: Nomad Jazz/Interventor hacking mattered less against largely non-Hackable Kosmoflot; mines, Puppets and movement decided central contest. #001: Next Wave narrator identified only having Pandora as a failure-sensitive source of hacking/Spotlight. Build a non-hacking scoring path |

**Counters:** non-Hackable route, distance from repeater net, Engineer/Reset and sufficient Order pool, killing/isolating the hacker, safe approach under visibility control. **Status only matters if it stays relevant long enough to change a mission action.**

### 3.5 Order engines, repair and sacrifice

| Role rank | Pattern and examples | Documented value | Limitation |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Additional useful Orders delivered to the right mission Specialist** — **Saladin**, plus an **Odalisque** button runner; **Voronin/cheap Ariadna support + Dynamo** | #096 Saladin transferred the needed group Orders, giving 3–4 possible WIP attempts; Odalisque succeeded on attempt two and was called game-winning. #164 Saladin + Mukhtar/Ragik mission breadth in tournament win; #161/#237 Ariadna preserved specialist economy in five-round first-place recap | Need actual available targets and the correct combat group; #096 is **one game**, not a measured Saladin-wide conversion rate |
| **2** | **Accessible Engineer/Doctor/Servant restoring relevant capability** — **Machinist**, **Hospitaller Doctor**, **ISS Engineer/Servant** | #019 Machinist restored disabled Squalos; #092 restored Cutter Structure before late zone scoring; #193 ISS repaired/returned multiple pieces after flyer alpha; #130 Hospitaller revived a Knight | Healing an irrelevant attacker can consume the same Orders needed for a box/terminal; #014 attempted SilverStar recovery failed and worsened Torchlight tempo |
| **3** | **Deliberate low-cost sacrifice to protect the actual scorer** — **Onyx E-Drone EVO**, expendable **Daylami**, Gaki/Flash-pulse screens | #212 E-Drone deliberately triggered a mine so Kurnau could reach Countermeasures task; #232 Daylami cleared mines ahead of D-Charge carriers; #116 Fugazi kept an enemy busy | Loss is a *cost*, not automatically good; award value only when an evidenced scoring route opens or a better piece survives |

**Order engines are multipliers:** they do not automatically rank above a Specialist. Evaluate the *engine + recipient + mission* together.

### 3.6 Last-turn objective control and insurance

| Role rank | Pattern | Illustrative evidence |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | **Independent cheap/fast scorer held in reserve** | #092 Varuna REM crossing into zone changed the score to 6–3; #096 Odalisque button plus retained attempts; #114 Posthuman final console; #034 Zero beacon delivery |
| **2** | **Several retained bodies / hidden zone occupants** | #196 Shrouded markers occupied zones even while Next Wave held a console; #239 O-12 Camo/forward screens + Cyberghost kept Area of Interest 4–2 against a more aggressive Next Wave damage plan; #230 Nomads Puppets/Billie and Kosmoflot SAS/Volkolak both affected late Uplink Center control, producing 3–3 |
| **3** | **Heavy survivor alone, after support/couriers have died** | #214 triple PanO TAGs retained combat threat but lost Classified race; #231 Sphinx damage did not preserve winning Battleground zone agency; #146 OSS attrition superiority lost to PanO's antenna score under **modified league scoring** |

**Timing matters:** an early zone hold may be irrelevant if the packet rewards the last round; conversely an early round-scoring mission rewards keeping multiple occupants throughout. Use the **played mission's** scoring schedule.

### High-damage *counterexamples*: do not over-generalize

- **Sphinx** in #231 is evidence of an **inefficient attack sequence on a specific Battleground table**, not a global “avoid Sphinx” finding.
- **Nisse HMG** in #194 spent too long on a required or perceived defensive target; the underlying failure includes **route choice, dice, and subsequent pickup timing**. Not proof a Cenobite always beats a Nisse.
- **Cutter** was **bypassed** by the CanCon winner's Corporate Appropriation route (#161/#237), while Varuna's **Cutter + repair + cheap scoring REM** functioned on Area of Interest (#092). **Its value is highly scenario-dependent.**
- **Dynamo AP Spitfire** is praised as a useful **smoke/firepower + Paramedic** duo architecture in #223, yet the player says the AP Spitfire itself did little in *that* battle. **List selection value ≠ realized activation value**.
- **Noctifer, Swiss Guard, Blackheart, Hawkwood** can offer severe hidden/long-lane threats, but do not assign them universal order taxes absent logged opponent reactions. #170's Swiss Guard praise is a **participant's retrospective** rather than an isolated measured efficiency statistic.
- Winning games with **poor roll luck** (#233 projected outcome, #151 exceptional hacking) and losses due to **wrong mission preparation** (#114 opponent, #071 missing qualified skills) should not become permanent faction/unit rankings.

## 5. Choosing a unit: role-value decision matrix

| If the mission rewards… | Prioritize | Avoid paying extra for… | Check counterplay |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consoles / Classified variety** | Several *actually eligible* independent Specialists, extra WIP attempts, safe Repeater paths; e.g. Posthumans, Koga/Deva, Kurnau, Odalisque | One additional premium assault gun while only one qualified scorer remains | Loss of Lieutenant, cyber Isolation, narrow whitelist, relevant Fireteam/profile eligibility (#071, #096, #212) |
| **Boxes / extraction / Data Harvest** | Fast courier, payload handoff, surviving last-round mover, ARO clearance, **full travel budget** | Removing a defender whose lane is irrelevant to the final delivery route | Mines, hidden midfield, delivery-circle LoF, safe return path (#019, #034, #189, #194) |
| **Areas / zone weight / room control** | Bodies that persist until the specified scoring moment, cheap REM runners, durable close survivors and distributed Camo | All-in attack that leaves no bodies in the correct bands | Geometry, Smoke, Camo reveal timing, own Retreat!, enemy last Order (#092, #196, #239) |
| **Anti-materiel objective destruction** | D-Charges/CC anti-materiel delivery + smoke/screen + multiple attack routes | Ordinary gun kills while objective remains intact | ARO/mines, exact anti-materiel requirements and Order reserve (#181, #223, #232) |
| **Opponent has expensive Hackable pieces** | Pitcher/repeater/hacker + a *distinct* physical scoring actor | Long guided/Spotlight sequence if simple bypass is faster | Firewall, Engineers, non-Hackable alternatives, surviving target relevance (#019, #118, #151) |
| **Opponent has dense Camo/deployables** | Sensor/Discover access, expendable clearance, lateral/flyer access, small repeatable specialist tasks | Spending premium active-turn Orders trying to kill every marker | Hidden ARO, mines, Decoys, value of leaving an irrelevant marker untouched (#116, #161, #196, #230) |

## 6. How to capture true unit efficiency in future reports

The current prose enables useful **qualitative** rankings, but not a fair 241-row numerical league table. A future structured extraction should use **one record per unit per distinct game**, retaining a source provenance key and not counting the same game twice. Suggested fields:

| Field | Example / definition |
|---|---|
| **Source identity** | Report #, original video URL, match/event ID, played date, format, mission, historical N5/ITS revision, whether linked recount overlaps |
| **Unit identity** | Faction, sectorial, exact unit + profile/loadout if securely known, price/SWC **at played revision**, combat group, intended role |
| **Activation / attack** | Active Orders spent, failed orders, opposing points **actually** disabled/killed, whether a key LT/specialist/ARO was affected, route opened |
| **Mission conversion** | Direct OP and objective categories, movement/task Orders, who physically scored, which enablers protected that action |
| **Opponent cost** | Explicit enemy Orders spent clearing it, resetting, reviving, discovering, bypassing; relevant future Orders denied; unknown where unlogged |
| **Survival** | Alive/unconscious/removed at end of rounds, repairs, retained Orders, actual ability to score, late zone/room weight |
| **Flexibility** | Alternate arrival, Smoke/Disco/White Noise, Sensor, mines, hacking, mission skills, rescue tools used versus merely available |
| **Attribution** | Observed / retrospective / opinion / inference; confidence; exception flags for custom/200pt/tutorial/rewind/misplay/projected score |
| **Opponent response** | What the opponent actually chose; feasible alternatives *clearly labelled counterfactual*, not assumed as proven |
| **Outcome** | Player OP, opponent OP, concessions/estimated/projected flag; **do not derive per-unit credit solely from winner** |

### Suggested quantitative indicators **only when the inputs exist**

- **Own Order cost per relevant objective:** task-and-travel Orders divided by completed scoring actions; track WIP retries and failed approaches.
- **Threat-clearance efficiency:** relevant defender neutralized or scoring route opened per *actual* active Orders, with separate note of enemy point cost. A zero-kill smoke bypass can be best.
- **Defensive Order tax:** *observed* opposing Orders spent clearing/Resetting/circling around the piece, reported as a range if narrator approximates. **Never substitute a guessed number** for an ambiguous transcript.
- **Scoring agency retained:** number of end-of-turn/round **eligible and reachable** Specialists/couriers, not just models remaining; can be reported as 0, 1, 2+ when lists and board position are known.
- **Opponent action denial:** confirmed Loss of Lieutenant, Isolated state, immobilized carrier, destroyed Order source, blocked task, or failed scoring window, with *causal evidence*. Do not naively add this to Orders spent.
- **Deployment robustness:** whether the list has independent A/B plans versus Hackable threats, Camo/Hidden defense and distant scoring areas; this can only be fairly assessed when both army codes/scenario are available.

**Do not compute** “(enemy points killed + OP × arbitrary points + enemy Orders × arbitrary points) / unit points.” That embeds unvalidated weights, double-counts enablers, and mistakes mission packet OP scales for universal unit quality. A future multi-dimensional scorecard may plot each unit in its **own role** with coverage/confidence and scenario filters, rather than ranking a TAG against a Warcor.

### Example of two honest records

**#194, Cenobite HRL (N5.1.1, 200pt Supplies):** Attack = unmeasured; mission directly scored = N/O; defensive tax = approximately **5–6 enemy Nisse Orders** narrated; endgame survival = not used as evidence; indirect impact = materially reduced SWF pickup tempo; counter = route/attack choice and WIP pickup efficiency; confidence = **Case, with format caveat**.

**#231, Sphinx (300pt Battleground):** Attack = about **four kills** for **nine or ten Orders**, per players; direct mission OP = no documented positive score from that push; denial generated = unmeasured; opponent's surviving counterattack reached far band; confidence = **Case**. **Do not translate four kills into assumed points destroyed.**

## 7. Working conclusions for list-building

1. **Spend points on a scoring route, not on abstract damage.** Pair every expensive active attacker with a protected, eligible finisher and enough movement/WIP retries. The clearest recurring failures are **damage without task completion** (#214, #231, #146, #194).
2. **Low-cost denial often has the strongest marginal mission impact.** One mine, marker, Flash Pulse or awkward ARO can cost an opponent an entire needed scoring window (#116, #194, #196), but it must be positioned on a *real* route.
3. **Mission specificity is a major part of a unit's value.** A Cutter can be bypassed in Corporate Appropriation (#161), while Cutter with repair plus REM scoring performs in Area of Interest (#092). The same unit deserves different role ratings by packet.
4. **Order support is useful only when usable.** Saladin-to-Odalisque (#096) and cheap clearance protecting Kurnau (#212) show how Order/resources translate into task success. Failing repair/attack sequences (#014) show the opposite.
5. **Late-turn survival means capability, not just remaining wounds.** Keep an independent scorer able to move and interact, not only a premium gun able to survive (#092, #114, #239).
6. **Resilience includes avoiding fights.** The CanCon Ariadna plan scored around a Cutter (#161/#237); the strongest anti-TAG plan sometimes is to **win elsewhere**, not prove one model outshoots it.
7. **Use qualitative, role-separated rankings until action-level data exists.** The sample supports strong tactical claims and concrete anecdotes, **not population-wide numerical estimates** of unit win contribution.
