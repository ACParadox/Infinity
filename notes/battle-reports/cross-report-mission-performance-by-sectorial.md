# N5 battle-report mission performance by sectorial and army composition

**Qualitative review:** 2026-11-01. **Source set:** [numbered N5 battle-report dossiers](./) #001–#241 and [master report index](n5-battle-reports-and-tournament-reports.md). **Companion:** [cross-report unit effectiveness and combinations](cross-report-unit-effectiveness-and-combinations.md). This is an *evidence map for list selection*, not official faction win percentages or rules authority.

## Method and quality gates

- **Observed (O):** match play/score or specific mission interaction documented in a dossier; **tournament recollection (R):** a first-hand multi-round recap whose reported list and mission can be trusted qualitatively but are not each independent videos; **tentative (T):** strong tactical fit with one report or a scenario-specific inference, insufficient for a comparative sectorial ranking; **counterexample (X):** a list failed because of missing mission tools or timing.
- Do **not** count 241 index lines as independent wins. Known repeated content includes #009/#028, #011/#030, #006/#031, #010/#033, #022/#026; #161 and #237 cover the **same 2026 CanCon Vanilla Ariadna winner**, and #169/#170 the **same 2025 Borderbash PanO event**. Retrospectives with five rounds are **one event**, not five independent indexed units.
- **Special exclusions:** #241 lacks a verified play-by-play; #208 has **disputed Order-count integrity** (interpret late-console mechanism, not clean win rate); #233 and #226 report **projected/early-stop** rather than final OP; #217 is **provisional**; #235 uses modified Team Ops; #240 custom Megabeast; #104 metadata only; several tutorials/low-point demos are lower-confidence; #237 round 5 includes an acknowledged consequential rules mistake. #006 reported a Capture & Protect HVT mistake. Do not count these as clean outcome evidence.
- The dossiers span different **N5 releases and ITS season mission packets**. A scenario name does not guarantee identical rules across periods. **Current N5.3 availability, specialists, costs and legal Fireteams** remain controlled by `sectorials/` and `rules/`, not by the historical played roster.
- Mission suitability is **composition-dependent**: *route + scoring specialist + support + Orders + scoring timing*. A 10–0 single match is evidence that the listed plan can work on that table, not that its sectorial beats that opponent or mission on average.

## Application

1. For each mission, fill in **what scores, at what time, and by which eligible skill**, then pick a *specific played-style list* with a path to each needed action.
2. Ask whether the list has **at least two independent scoring routes**, a **delivery/enabling mechanism** (vision, hacking, mines, AD or movement), a **resilient surviving scorer** for end-round/end-game objectives, and **Orders left after combat**.
3. For **zone families**, record bodies/army-point weight *in the scored region*; for **Classifieds**, track specialist **types**; for **sabotage**, the actual legal anti-materiel action; for **cargo**, total route Orders and extraction timing.
4. Before copying any historical Fireteam or unit composition, verify current N5.3 profiles and legality from each sectorial's canonical `## Fireteams`. Do not elevate a qualitative observation into a future unit tier, guaranteed matchup, or faction win percentage.
