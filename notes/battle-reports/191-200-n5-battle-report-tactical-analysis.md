## #191 — Vanilla ALEPH vs Shasvastii: ITS18 Neutralization

[Original GMG / Tactical Awareness full video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aPJvOGd6EeE). **ALEPH 5–3 Shasvastii.** Both armies deliberately practised an ITS18 **Burlington Brawl** scenario involving retrieving items and **neutralizing them at antennas**, with late extra points for **antenna control** and Classifieds.

- **Observed — ALEPH:** **Maximus with heavy gun/Suppressive Fire**, **Asura**, Warcor/Probot, flash/missile REMs and supporting Netrods were the core. **Asura went down early**, but the **Maximus** gun and a swarm of small **difficult-to-isolate six-point models** made a durable late control screen, pushing Shasvastii to kill cheap bodies to change scoring.
- **Observed — Shasvastii:** **Sphinx, Sheskiin, two Calibans, Malignos Hacker, Mentor, Nox Paramedic, M-Drone and Ikadron/Syndron** split between offense and pushing/stealing the antenna state. A **Caliban's late move** successfully wrested **control of more antennas**, denying ALEPH the **three-point bonus** and turning a probable **8–0 swing into a 5–3**.
- **Observed — scoring:** ALEPH earned **two Classified/other final points, two neutralizations and one “more” point = 5** in the postgame recount. Shasvastii obtained **three from more antennas**, rather than the match being decided solely by who killed Asura or Sphinx.
- **Player assessment:** **Maximus BP-HMG/Suppression** was very hard to dislodge; the speaker also noted a cheap clustered **veteran, non-Isolatable** order network denies conventional hacking as a way to clear the scoring zone.
- **Inference:** **Maximus plus small ALEPH supporting REMs/Netrods** can win Neutralization after losing an elite Asura, *if specialists have already moved the objects*. **Shasvastii Caliban/Sphinx** can still reverse the antenna swing late. Do not treat 5–3 as ALEPH generally outmatching Shasvastii.
- **Caution:** Exact point allocation is interpreted from heavily translated postgame narration; verify ITS18 condition texts before exporting list-tech verbatim.

**Mission takeaway:** Neutralize items early, then **budget enough remaining Orders to touch/contest antennas**; losing a key attacker is recoverable, losing the late antenna swing is not.


## #192 — Onyx Contact Force vs PanOceania: ITS18 The Dig

[Original full GMG report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LzbqKD-CNac). **3–3 draw**, final score explicitly counted. Both tested new ITS18 **The Dig** with six technology pieces and consoles that change which piece is eligible.

- **Observed — Onyx:** **Kornak, TinBot Unidrons, M-Drone, two Xeodron TAGs (K1/other weapon), Umbra Samaritan, Med-Tech Obsidon/Doctor Worm + helper, Q-Drone HMG and Ikadron/Imetron order support** created several strong gun and tech-recovery routes.
- **Observed — PanO:** **Liang Kai and Shona Carano**, Fusiliers, **Locust Killer Hacker in reserve/hidden position**, Auxilia/flash REM, **Pathfinder Tactical Awareness**, assorted Specialists and a linked **PanO fire-support element** supplied independent terminal pushes. A key late **Pathfinder/REM console switch** mattered more than how many Onyx units had been eliminated.
- **Observed — narrative:** Both sides had opportunities to **destroy a key Lieutenant or engineer**, but moving the **data console at the end** changed the otherwise **4–2** outcome into a **3–3 draw**. One **Locust** was held as a reserve threat that failed to contribute to the chosen objective route.
- **Player opinion:** The players found **the opponent's choice of technology target after a console interaction** and required decontamination actions surprisingly Order-intensive; efficient mobility, not sheer killing, determines whether the console points flip late.
- **Inference:** **Onyx Xeodron/Kornak/Med-Tech** can defend tech pieces and attack a central passage; **PanO Pathfinder/Fusilier/Locust utility** can contest the same mission without beating every Xeodron in combat.
- **Caution:** The players re-read several eligibility/console interactions on camera, and automatic transcription garbles Xeodron and unit nicknames. No general Onyx-versus-PanO supremacy inference.

**Mission takeaway:** Keep **one fast specialist able to flip the final console**, and do not assume the enemy's own decontamination route cannot be interrupted on turn three.


## #193 — Imperial Service vs Saladin Haqqislam: radial Unmasking, double flyers

[Original Robert Shepherd full video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c1G0J1A8__k). **Imperial Service 10–0 Haqqislam**. A deliberate viewer-requested test of **ISS going second** into **Saladin + two powerful Haqq flyers** on a **radial variant of Unmasking**, not an ordinary randomized competitive pairing.

- **Observed — ISS defensive technology:** A **15-Regular-Order force** spread **Koga Ninja specialists, Zhanying/Engineer with E/Mitter and Triangulated Fire, Pheasant/Zhanying specialist Duo, Deva and Hackers/Repeaters** around the DZ. **Cheap screens and multiple Engineers/Paramedics** let the list absorb the opening enemy flyer/impersonator attack while preserving enough Orders to counterattack.
- **Observed — Haqq:** **Saladin extra Order/initiative package, Haytham (heavy shotgun), Fiday, Rafiq, Djanbazan Panzerfaust and both Haqq offensive flyers** were designed to kill and pin ISS before it could push Unmasking. One Fiday threat was **positionally contained** so its active choices became less efficient.
- **Observed — first two turns:** ISS lost **three pieces** to the violent turn-one offense, then **reconstituted two and removed four dangerous attackers**, nearly a perfect defensive recovery. **Zhanying/Engineer E/M** bricked a visible offensive piece without first killing it; **Djanbazan's Panzerfaust crit knocked out an ISS heavy**, which was later recovered by the Engineer/Servant network.
- **Observed — mission:** ISS retained a broad mix of **Koga Ninja, Deva, Pheasant, Zhanying and Hacker** Specialists, enabling a **10–0 Unmasking mission score** after Haqq's opening attack had left its objective coverage too thin.
- **Player opinion:** Flyers may deserve *small* profile adjustments, but this is evidence of **surviving them with layered defense**, not proof they are harmless. Both lists were chosen for a specific user-requested matchup; the Haqq list was acknowledged as not fully optimized.
- **Inference:** **ISS Koga Ninja/Engineer/E-Mitter/Pheasant layered defense** can defeat a **Saladin Fiday + double-flyer turn-one package** by **surrendering only expendable models and recovering the rest**, then reaching Radial Unmasking targets.
- **Caution:** **Radial Unmasking is a custom deployment variant** and the played date/N5.2–5.3 patch cannot be certified. Do not extrapolate 10–0 as a normal ISS-versus-Haqq faction score.

**Mission takeaway:** Against flyers when going second, **deploy in layers with expendable screens, a recoverable second line and multiple real Unmasking Specialists**.


## #194 — Bakunin vs Svalarheima Winter Force: Supplies at 200 points

[Original Champion of Terra video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VmYyESZx9WU) · [creator's dated historical forum entry](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/cot-battle-reports.43148/). **Bakunin won on Supplies**, final narrative shows **two boxes against one and a Classified**, but no unambiguous fully counted total was spoken. **July 28, 2025; N5.1.1**, only **200 points**, and a short narrative scenario.

- **Observed — Bakunin:** **Riot Grrl defensive gun, Custodier Hacker, Zoe, Cenobites (Heavy Rocket Launcher/Flash Pulse), two midfield Initiates with repeaters, moderators and Fiddler/Pi-Well support** supplied mission-specialist and electronic density.
- **Observed — SWF:** **Nisse HMG + WinterFor Machinist Duo, Karhu Paramedic/Fireball Duo, Sierra TR HMG, Fugazi, Liang Kai and Beast Hunter double Heavy Flamethrower** allowed both early attack and hidden approach to Supplies consoles.
- **Observed — important trade:** SWF's **Nisse HMG** consumed roughly **five to six Orders neutralizing a Cenobite Heavy Rocket**, leaving too little time to complete an initially failed **Machinist WIP Supplies pickup**. In response, Bakunin **Initiate Repeater → Zoe Carbonite** Immobilized a threatening PanO REM and let **Custodier/linked teammates** get to a later supply box.
- **Observed:** Bakunin got the **two-box majority** with its late specialist movement and succeeded in a **Vigilance Classified action**. SWF's early pressure yielded a box but lacked both mission delivery and enough Order-efficient support.
- **Inference:** **Bakunin Custodier + Zoe/Initiate Repeater and Cenobite reactive delay** is an effective small-format Supplies list into **Nisse/REM-heavy SWF**. **SWF Nisse + Machinist Duo** is potent if it clears only the *necessary* ARO and spends Orders on the pickup rather than grinding.
- **Caution:** **200pt mission and July 2025 profiles** cannot be treated as present-day 300pt N5.3 balance. The full OP was not spoken clearly, so avoid inventing 6–2/6–3.

**Mission takeaway:** Count the **Orders remaining after removing a Cenobite ARO**, and position a secondary Specialist before starting a long gunfight.


## #195 — Road to Infinity #9: Yu Jing vs Operations Subsection

[Original German DICED full video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YxxfctavXtc). **Stefan's Yu Jing beat OSS by a narrow margin**, with the speaker emphasizing **one decisive end-of-game console**. **Early N5 learning/tutorial game**, precise playing date and whether initial N5 vs N5.1 is uncertain; **not automatically ITS17**.

- **Observed — OSS:** **Asura high-cost Lieutenant with extra Lieutenant Orders, Dakinis, Sofotect/Engineer support, an elite sniper and reactive pieces** contested **quadrants and four hackable terminals**. The OSS player exposed **Asura** late on a rooftop behind weak cover while not taking a nearby terminal.
- **Observed — Yu Jing:** **Tiger Soldier, Shaolin, Kuang Shi/cheap units, Su-Jian-like mobile REM/gun and durable ARO rocket launcher** limited OSS movement. **Shaolin Chain Rifle/CC** ultimately brought down **Asura** after she had overextended; the Yu Jing player also **re-hacked an opponent-controlled terminal**.
- **Observed — scoring misunderstanding:** OSS player had believed consoles granted points **for each successful use**, and was surprised that **control belonged to the last player to hack the console at game's end**. The final spoken tally is distorted by translation, but the narrative clearly identifies **Stefan/Yu Jing as the winner by one deciding console**. Do not manufacture exact total OP.
- **Player opinion:** OSS player regretted **not attacking the Tiger Soldier covering the terminal and not moving Dakinis to secure the proper scoring zones**. Asura's raw stats could not rescue a failure to understand terminal-control rules.
- **Inference:** **Cheap Shaolin + Tiger Soldier/rocket ARO and last-turn terminal Hacker** can beat an OSS **Asura/Dakini** list in a terminal-control scenario; OSS needs a **protected Specialist and a late-console order reserve**, not solely an Asura advance.
- **Caution:** The recorded player's accusation of being “cheated” is humorous frustration from a **rules misunderstanding**, not evidence of malicious or illegal play. This is not a competitive roster-ranking sample.

**Mission takeaway:** Before turn one, confirm whether **terminals flip ownership or score repeatedly**, and save the last turn's active Hacker for the decisive flip.


## #196 — LIT episode 1: Shasvastii vs Next Wave, ITS17 Area of Interest

[Original London Infinity Tabletop full game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WJH5JPA4rro). **Shasvastii 6–5 Next Wave**, final total confirmed. **N5.2 or later**, Next Wave existed and the title specifies ITS17.

- **Observed — Shasvastii:** A **quad-Shrouded + double Malignos/marker-saturation** list, **Speculo Killer**, and **Shrouded Minelayer with Dazers** placed subtle speed bumps near key areas. **Shrouded Multi Sniper** actively removed attackers while Hidden/Camo tokens kept zones contested without offering a normal firefight.
- **Observed — Next Wave:** **Patroclus/Pandora floating Fireteam, Harbinger, Puzzler Hacking/Repeaters, Tech Dragon with mine dispensers and other midfield attackers** pressed the center. It **owned the console** for much of the match, but lost **zone scoring** when Shasvastii kept sufficient marker bodies hidden in all three scoring areas.
- **Observed:** **Speculo killed Teucer** in an early attack. The **Dazer** delayed a crucial Next Wave movement and a concealed **Shrouded re-camo + Minelayer** created awkward decisions for Pandora's repeater coverage. The Next Wave player explicitly lacked sufficient remaining Orders to reclaim enough zones, though holding the button kept it close.
- **Player opinion:** Players highlighted the **one-point Dazer upgrade on Shrouded Boarding Shotgun** as an excellent **zone/tempo denial**, and argued **quad-Shrouded** may be stronger than investing in another expensive gun. The Next Wave player liked Puzzlers/Tech Dragon but admitted some Fireteam/weapon misses were unusually bad.
- **Inference:** **Shasvastii Shrouded sniper + Dazer/marker saturation + Speculo** can counter an elite **Next Wave Pandora/Patroclus/Puzzler** list in **Area of Interest** by controlling zone footprints without wasting Orders on killing the hero link.
- **Caution:** Several **Cliff-Jumper movement and tactical rules were explicitly misplayed** on camera. Use current Army/n5 rules to validate Dazer, Fireteam and Specialist interactions.

**Mission takeaway:** **One active button does not win Area of Interest** if the opponent can maintain hidden **multiple scoring-zone occupiers**.


## #197 — LIT episode 3: Tunguska vs Next Wave, ITS17 Akial Interference

[Original London Infinity Tabletop full game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SxRlMtkTRtM). **Tunguska 7–3 Next Wave**, expressly recounted. **N5.2+ / ITS17**, exact patch unknown.

- **Observed — Tunguska:** **Perseus + Hollow Man Hacker/TinBot Haris, Salamandra/Gecko-class ranged presence, Kriza Borac gun, Kulak Killer Hacker with Disco Balls/Cybermines and Fiddler/cheap Specialists** supported **Classified diversity and Cybersecurity**. **Kulak** held a short-range interception lane with a KHD/Oblivion threat.
- **Observed — Next Wave:** **Achilles main assault and Lieutenant group, Pandora/NCO/Harbinger support, Blackheart and Puzzler tech** killed several Tunguska troops and completed early Classifieds but **overextended Achilles** into range of an **Interventor/Cybermine/Kulak** network.
- **Observed:** Tunguska **bricked Achilles with Hacking**, drained Orders that Next Wave needed for Akial tasks, and **Kulak even landed a melee wound while Achilles was forced to Reset**. Tunguska then completed **two Classifieds/Telemetry and personal tasks**, while Next Wave lacked the midfield Specialist breadth to redraw/cycle unfavorable cards.
- **Observed — score:** **7–3 Tunguska**. Postgame participants explicitly identify **first-turn failed Blackheart shooting and the accidental forward position of Achilles** as swing factors; Tunguska's important counter was the **Hacker/Cybermine net**, not the main gun necessarily outshooting Blackheart.
- **Inference:** **Tunguska Perseus/Hollow Man + Kulak Cybermine/Hacker support** can contain **Next Wave Achilles/Pandora** on Classified missions, especially when the next cards demand Specialist roles Achilles cannot cover.
- **Caution:** The first-turn dice favored Tunguska after a missed Next Wave opening. Do not call Kulak a guaranteed Achilles kill; the win required Hacking position and mission timing.

**Mission takeaway:** For Akial, **reserve a durable Hacker and broad Classified-skill coverage**, rather than letting Achilles consume the only Orders that can score.


## #198 — LIT episode 5: Military Orders vs Morat Aggression Force, ITS17 Superiority

[Original full London Infinity Tabletop game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AbNk32eGkVI). **Military Orders 6–2 Morats**, final count confirmed. ITS17; exact N5.1.1–5.2 version not independently verified.

- **Observed — Military Orders:** **Black Friar/MSV ranged tool, Knight of Justice elite gun/mission actor, Trinitarian specialist/mine layer, Order Sergeants, a Haris and a linked long-range shooter** combined good ARO coverage with **more surviving quadrant points**. The speaker specifically called **Black Friar the match MVP** for disrupting multiple Morat guns and forward attackers.
- **Observed — Morats:** **Kaitok x2, Suryat reactive gun, Oznat with Preta, Rindak, Daturazi, and a linked powerful ranged shooter** presented a formidable attrition front but had to enter expensive Black Friar/MSV sightlines to contest the zone buttons.
- **Observed — important actions:** Black Friar inflicted wounds on **Kaitok and Suryat**, and later removed a **Preta/Oznat**, reducing Morat's capacity to rush into the Knights. A **Rindak** eventually killed or threatened an exposed piece but got **Flash Pulsed/Stunned while trying to take the last scoring button**.
- **Observed:** MO finished with **three scoring zones and defensive Key Ops**, while Morats preserved fewer able-bodied units to change the last turn. Final **6–2** discussed; a player admitted overextending a killer on turn two rather than securing the objective earlier.
- **Player opinion:** Morat MSV/long-range MSR can still be excellent into Knights if used in the right range and smoke geometry; **Black Friar's versatility and multiple range bands** is not proof Morats generally lack anti-MO tools.
- **Inference:** **MO Black Friar + Trinitarian/Haris zone workers** can be good against **Morat Suryat/Kaitok/Preta** in **Superiority**, but success depends on correct active sightlines and preserving bodies for end-of-turn quadrant math.

**Mission takeaway:** Do not sacrifice a **Rindak specialist on a late Flash Pulse** if a safer button path keeps the fourth quadrant contested.


## #199 — Neoterran Capitaline Army vs Torchlight Brigade: ITS17 Outbreak

[Original Infinity the Dante + Loss of Lieutenant full game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i9CNGTPN9G4). **NCA 6–5 Torchlight Brigade**, explicitly tallied. N5.2/ITS17 midseason Outbreak probable, precise date not independently verified.

- **Observed — NCA:** **Hannibal/Shona/Bolt Haris, two Auxilia, Tech-Bee, Pathfinder TAC Aware, Trauma-Doc/Servant, Maximus, forward-deployed Sāchā** and inexpensive scanner/stabilization specialists. The force suffered devastating casualties yet was still **mission-scoring with a forward Sāchā**.
- **Observed — Torchlight:** **Stormbot with gun/area control, Vidoc, SilverStar, Moonrakers, Warcor/Engineer and Yellowjacket jump/airborne threat** removed most of NCA's models. The list tried to **prevent late scanning and stabilize infected for itself**, but repeatedly failed critical scan rolls on its second half of the game.
- **Observed — points:** NCA **scanned three (3), stabilized more (2), secured at least one infected (1) = 6**. Torchlight **scanned two, stabilized the alpha and secured an infected / alpha-related objectives = 5**. Both players debated which mission actions were worthwhile, then agreed on **6–5** despite NCA being almost tabled; remaining army points were irrelevant to most OP.
- **Player opinion:** Mission's extra **Doctor/Paramedic + CivVac advantages** bias lists toward those capabilities. **TAG peripherals/ancillaries** with different action skill sets were discussed as potential special-purpose infected runners; they corrected a mistaken assumption that Peripheral: Ancillary has the same zone-limit as other Peripheral rules. The latter was **list-planning discussion**, not a played TAG Ancillary rescue success.
- **Inference:** **NCA cheap Auxilia/Sāchā/Pathfinder/Hannibal specialists** can win Outbreak against **Torchlight Stormbot/SliverStar/Yellowjacket**, even when losing most of the army. Torchlight should **interrupt the first scan/stabilize pipeline**, not only table the enemy.
- **Caution:** Players acknowledge bad/erratic scan rolls, different placement could alter outcome, and mission might favor the first mover. Don't infer NCA > Torchlight in normal gunfights.

**Mission takeaway:** Protect **early scan and stabilize access, especially the alpha**, and understand that eliminating 90% of the opponent's army may **not recover already scored OP**.


## #200 — Tohaa vs Shasvastii: custom German-championship Infection

[Original burlesford slideshow/tournament game report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tsVc8Xe6nSg). **Tohaa 4–1 Shasvastii**. This is **not ITS17 Outbreak or ITS18 infection**: it is **a custom “Infection” mission written by a Munich local for the German championship**. Report uses N5 but exact played day/patch unknown.

- **Observed — Tohaa:** **Sukeul sniper/HMG, Kaauri or Kotail/“Cortile” self-cleaning utility, Gao-Rael, a mobile potential Bounty Hunter, Libertos and defensive Chaksa flame templates** were able to **neutralize infected attackers without accidentally increasing Tohaa's own count**.
- **Observed — Shasvastii:** **Mentor Lieutenant with Decoy/Counterintelligence, Taighas, Gwailo/Guilo reserve and agent Dukash deployment attack** tried to use **Impulsive infected Taigha movement plus death-infection spread**. Shas player **misread that both players began with equal infected models**, but actually started in a **0–2 infected-unit deficit**.
- **Observed — key rule trick:** Tohaa inferred that the opponent had **Counterintelligence** when only one Order could be stripped. The opposing Lieutenant's **WIP14** plus **Mentor's Decoy** revealed that a supposed extra Camo marker was actually a decoy, leaving possible Hidden/Reserve threats. Tohaa **deployed safely by accident**, reducing the impact of **Dukash**.
- **Observed:** **Sukeul sniper stopped infected Taighas before they could reach base contact**. Tohaa's own infected forward piece used the mission's **purge/decontaminate interaction** repeatedly to reduce its infected count. Shas's attempt to get its Taighas *killed near enemy models* was unreliable because they sometimes **survived the ARO** and therefore failed to spread infection. Final **4–1 Tohaa**.
- **Player opinion:** For an aggressive infection plan, use a **fast cheap infected troop (possibly motorized Bounty Hunter) and a Greif-Op impersonator/forward receiver** so that infection begins close to the enemy DZ whether the runner survives or dies. **This was proposed after the match**, not successfully played.
- **Inference:** **Tohaa Sukeul sniper + sturdy Chaksa/Tohaa screen + repeatable purge** counters **Shasvastii infected Taigha rush** in this particular custom scenario; its scoring has no bearing on official Outbreak.
- **Caution:** The report acknowledges missing pictures and some uncertainty over when specific Taighas died. **Don't call the German custom mission a standard ITS test or backdate ITS17 revisions from its name.**

**Mission takeaway:** Under this custom Infection rule, **knowing initial infected counts and the exact on-death transmission condition** matters far more than usual troop-killing tempo.

## Consolidated lessons across ten sources

| Theme | Reports | Generalizable question for list design |
|---|---|---|
| **Objective delivery and last-turn interaction often outweigh killed models** | 191–192, 194–195, 199 | Can each list safely **move a specialist / flip the terminal / carry a box** before the final active Orders disappear? |
| **Zone and hidden-marker control punish a single powerful attacker** | 191, 196, 198 | Can you contest all zones if an Asura, Achilles or Black Friar wins the main shooting duel but the opponent hides Camo on scoring zones? |
| **Multi-layer anti-Hackable defenses can work** | 193, 197 | Can **ISS recovery and E/M** or **Tunguska Kulak/Cybermines** absorb an elite/flyer alpha and still score? |
| **Don't overfit a mission counter** | 194, 196–197, 200 | SWF spent too many Orders on a Cenobite, Next Wave overcommitted Achilles, and Shas had a custom Infection plan based on incorrect setup assumptions |
| **Unit buying/sectorial tier evidence is bounded** | 193–195, 199–200 | Custom radial, 200pt narrative, tutorial, infection trial, bad dice and list-tailored drills are not competitive balance samples |
