Ten **previously unindexed, distinct videos** were found and their original YouTube transcripts reviewed; they have now been added to the master list. These are ten *games*, not ten independent proofs of faction strength. The analysis separates **Observed** (played/listed/recounted in the report), **Player opinion** (postgame assertion or proposed alternative), and **Inference** (limited interpretation). Scores from spoken postgame recounts are marked appropriately; a concession, teaching game, video commentary, translated transcript, and disputed rules play are not tournament win-rate data. Names that a transcript renders unreliably are qualified rather than silently “corrected.”

## #201 — Vanilla Nomads vs vanilla Yu Jing: Last Launch, flyer showcase

[Full Fast Panda Gaming report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tve5DXbtdUs). **Nomads 6–4 Yu Jing**, confirmed after the last active Orders. Both players deliberately brought their newly painted flyers; the flyers were an experiment, not evidence that vehicles are the strongest Last Launch archetype.

- **Observed — Nomads package:** A **Moran with CrazyKoala**, **Chimera/Pupnik**, **Wolfgang Amadeus Wolff**, **Morlock smoke/E/M**, **Interventor** and support Hackers, **Gecko Mark 12**, **GoPod/GoBot**, **Clockmaker**, and cheap **flash-pulse/Thunderbolt AROs** created a layered midfield and objective-room defense. The **Moran successfully extracted through the mission mechanism on turn two**, generating enough early score to force Yu Jing to chase the clock.
- **Observed — Yu Jing package:** **Zúyǒng Invincible Haris** (HMG, Paramedic, close-range shotgun/E-M option), **Rúì Shī Spitfire/MSV**, **Shaolin smoke**, **Lù Duān decoy/E-M**, and Yu Jing vehicle/support tried to clear the central room and escort a final extraction. The Zúyǒng moved decisively in the last turn, killing or incapacitating multiple Nomad blocking pieces.
- **Observed — deciding turn:** Despite successful close-range exchanges, the Yu Jing carrier needed **approximately half an Order more** to complete the required final extraction. A **Wolfgang/Chimera/Pupnik/Koala-style body and template screen** made every crossing expensive. The Nomad player counted **6–4**, including its mission/secondary scoring.
- **Player opinion:** Both emphasized that **Moran extraction in turn two changed the rest of the game**; the Yu Jing player identified the conversion/availability of one extra Order as a realistic counterfactual.
- **Inference:** Against **Zúyǒng HMG/shotgun Haris** mission runners, **Nomad Moran/Koala plus durable short-range bodies and hacking/flash screens** can win *Last Launch* without winning the late gunfight. Conversely, Yu Jing has the tools to open the door, but should **budget the pickup, door crossing and final teleport action before committing Orders to kills**.
- **Limits:** Two vanilla forces, bespoke flyer choices and near-miss timing; no claim that Nomads in general counter Yu Jing or that the flyer profiles are cost-effective.

**Mission takeaway:** Score an early specialist extraction, then **count the exact move–pickup–exit sequence**; denying just one short action can protect a 6–4 lead.

## #202 — Druze Bayram Security vs Shasvastii: ITS17 Evacuation

[Full Fast Panda Gaming report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cHi-W_7TeYI). **Druze 6–1 Shasvastii** by the closing discussion; Shasvastii's third turn was curtailed because its remaining four Orders could not meaningfully reverse the game.

- **Observed — Druze:** **Scarface + Cordelia**, **Valerya Gromoz Hacker** (name distorted as “Valyria”), **Monstrucker Engineer**, **Bashi Bazouk**, cheap AROs and a **Chain of Command / Counterintelligence backup** supported **evacuating civilians/HVTs while completing Classifieds**. Valerya completed **repeated Spotlight rolls for a Classified** late; Cordelia's mobility/specialist access enabled a civilian delivery.
- **Observed — Shasvastii:** A **Gwailo heavy gun**, Camo/hidden threats, and other fragile attack pieces failed to stop the evacuation. The opponent had to consider a possible **Noctifer missile-launcher ARO**, which constrained movement even without the feared missile being revealed at that spot; this is a **threat-of-presence** effect, not a verified Noctifer kill.
- **Observed — causation:** The Shas player lost attack pieces and Order economy in repeated adverse firefights. Druze did not merely table an opponent: the winning margin also came from **civilian delivery and late Classified actions**, which still needed a functioning specialist network.
- **Player opinion:** The Shasvastii player was unhappy with **Gwailo value relative to Sheskiin** and said Sheskiin *felt* like a near-auto-pick. This is a **postgame preference**, not comparative tested efficiency: the report does not directly test both profiles under equal conditions. The Druze player preferred a different long-range loadout in hindsight.
- **Inference:** **Druze Scarface/Cordelia + Valerya/Monstrucker + Bashi** offers distinct routes to **Evacuation** points: durability/repair, specialist hacking, and off-angle mobility. **Shasvastii marker deception** can slow a runner, but a failing frontline leaves the adversary enough Orders to complete multiple tasks.
- **Limits:** Early concession, severe dice swings and limited Shas roster experience; do **not** use a single 6–1 as Druze > Shasvastii competitive evidence.

**Mission takeaway:** Reserve **one uncommitted civilian courier and two viable Classified skill sets** after the first attack wave; mission actions are the score, not an afterthought.

## #203 — Varuna vs USARF: ITS17 Area of Interest

[Full Fast Panda Gaming report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqkn0M7mrEU). **Varuna 6–4 USARF** after a **spoken score correction**: the players initially said 5–4, then recounted Varuna's special-area scoring across earlier rounds.

- **Observed — Varuna:** A **Cutter camouflaged TAG** sat on the *special Area of Interest* to generate zone points without offering normal shooting targets, while a **Squalo Mk II**, **Fusilier Core**, **Machinist/Palbot**, and **Zulu-Cobra Camo** covered other lanes/areas. The **Cutter's extreme gun and point weight** made ordinary USARF assault attempts expensive; a **Zulu-Cobra's 23-point footprint** narrowly outvalued a last-turn **USARF 18-point marker** in a separate circle.
- **Observed — USARF:** **Unknown Ranger, Minutemen, Grunts, Maverick bikes/smoke, Fox Rangers and Hardcase** contested midfield and tried to score outside the Cutter's dominant lane. Mavericks attempted smoke/dodge routes to reverse a contested zone, but were removed by the TAG/support ARO. Other USARF bodies still preserved enough zone/Classified score to keep it close.
- **Observed — mission swing:** Varuna's **Cutter-as-hidden-zone-weight** scored over several turns. The final **Zulu-Cobra vs low-cost USARF marker** point comparison determined another Area, despite neither force needing to exterminate the other. The players explicitly corrected the final result to **6–4**.
- **Player opinion:** Varuna player built the list around the fact that **camouflage can secure areas**, intentionally bringing **two TAGs** as a test. USARF player praised **Unknown Ranger and Minuteman extra-die shooting**, which did not overcome the specific deployment geometry.
- **Inference:** **Varuna Cutter + cheap Camo zone holder + Fusilier/Machinist support** is a credible **Area of Interest** scoring architecture against USARF's **Maverick/Unknown Ranger/Fox Ranger** spread. A USARF counterplan needs **anti-TAG AP lanes, smoke that does not expose bikes to unopposed shots, and the exact troop-point total in each zone**.
- **Limits:** One unusual double-TAG, terrain-dependent game. Two TAGs are not automatically efficient in ordinary Varuna missions.

**Mission takeaway:** In Area of Interest, **marker state, scoring-zone placement, point weight and end-of-round scoring** matter at least as much as attrition.

## #204 — Next Wave vs Steel Phalanx: ITS17 Critical Intervention

[Full LIT episode 6](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aXkM06jx6sg). **Next Wave wins overwhelmingly**; the players loosely estimate **“10–0 or something around there”** at the end, so **do not store 10–0 as an independently verified official result**.

- **Observed — Next Wave defensive web:** **Patroclus**, **Pandora**, **Harbingers including Paramedic**, **Wraith FTO**, **Blackheart**, **Puzzler**, **Techjack** and supporting REMs produced an aggressive first-turn exchange and then a **mined/templated room perimeter**. The **Techjack's deployable-mine role and baggage-supported reloading** sustained a defense where each later Steel Phalanx approach risked templates and isolation.
- **Observed — Steel Phalanx:** A high-value **Achilles/Myrmidon-style attack force** tried to force the **Critical Intervention objective room** after trades had thinned its Order pools. One late surviving attacker reached the edge of a feasible path but was hit by **Isolated**; its remaining available Orders became unusable for the required objective-room run.
- **Observed — objective outcome:** Next Wave held the key room/objective condition and enough kill/denial conditions to make the attacker's final push irrelevant. A small number of defensive pieces repeatedly forced attackers into bad approach options instead of open normal rolls.
- **Player opinion:** Players called the **~25-point Techjack** an especially strong area-denial value because its **multiple mine outputs and reload logistics** compound over turns. They praised flexible Haris organization for a mission where the player may be assigned **attacker or defender**.
- **Inference:** **Next Wave Techjack + Harbinger/Patroclus/Pandora short-range network** is particularly useful when **defending Critical Intervention** against a **Steel Phalanx Achilles-led room assault**. For the attacker, **kill or bypass the isolation/template sources first**, and keep an independent room Specialist outside the Achilles assault order plan.
- **Limits:** Position-asymmetric objective, few late Steel Orders, and imprecise OP; not a blanket Next Wave vs Steel Phalanx power ranking.

**Mission takeaway:** Build **both an attack-route plan and a defended-room plan** before knowing who goes first; a last-turn Isolated elite cannot substitute for an independent objective specialist.

## #205 — Hassassin Bahram vs vanilla ALEPH: ITS17 Provisioning

[Full LIT episode 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aEIhT0MyfJ4). **Hassassin Bahram 7–1 vanilla ALEPH**, directly recounted.

- **Observed — Hassassin:** **Asawira AP Spitfire with Muyib Doctor and Govad Killer Hacker**, secondary **Ghulam/Yara Haddad/Bahram hacker** team, **Daylami Camo ambushes**, specialists, and Hacking/ARO support advanced through the three tech-coffin pickup routes. **A concealed Daylami** compelled ALEPH's heavy attacker to spend additional Orders evaluating, discovering or bypassing the marker rather than proceeding freely to the cargo/safe area.
- **Observed — ALEPH:** **Maximus AP HMG**, **Penny**, **Posthuman Mk1 Engineer/Mk5**, **Shukra Chain of Command**, **Sàchà**, **Probot/Netrods**, **Pilot X/Racerbots** and **HMG TR REM** offered powerful tools but uneven Order allocation. **Penny failed important smoke attempts**, Maximus was committed too far forward, and several late specialist routes collapsed.
- **Observed — defining defense:** The **Muyib** survived multiple confrontations and denied further pickup/safe-area movement. HB kept enough cargo under control to claim Provisioning's **large “more supplies in safe area” swing**. ALEPH conceded after losing accessible specialists.
- **Player opinion:** The ALEPH player thought **his second combat group was poorly built for meaningful Orders**; splitting Posthumans or Maximus differently might preserve an active mission route. HB player highlighted **high-WIP specialists and disciplined delayed Camo reveals**, noting that even if discovered, a cheap Daylami could have consumed more than its points in Orders.
- **Inference:** **Hassassin Asawira/Muyib/Govad + Daylami ambush screen** is a compelling **Provisioning** package into an ALEPH **Maximus/Penny elite-gun approach**. ALEPH's engineer/network does not help if **every scoring specialist is committed to the same blocked corridor**.
- **Limits:** Several unlucky smoke/defense rolls and a relatively new ALEPH list. “7–1” is one practiced HB lineup beating this specific ALEPH plan, not evidence that HB invariably beats ALEPH.

**Mission takeaway:** Provisioning needs **two viable pickup couriers, a safe-area route and Orders left for the big safe-area majority swing**; a camouflaged cheap defender can win by consuming time.

## #206 — Kestrel Colonial Force vs Corregidor: attrition and deployment traps

[Full DefectiveDice episode 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hJdOJpSoZlc). **Kestrel wins by concession**; the transcript does **not** establish a trustworthy official mission title or numeric OP.

- **Observed — Kestrel:** **Fennec Fusiliers with Thunderbolts and Forward Observer/Sensor**, a **Black A.I.R. Neurokinetics sniper**, **Redeye**, **Sforza viral weapon/Albedo**, a **Hecate-class heavy and supporting turret/Engineer**, **Jackals** and other specialists. Kestrel had an unusually generous **Order/Tactical Awareness** architecture, and its long-range overwatch forced exposed Corregidor models to make unfavorable approach choices.
- **Observed — Corregidor:** **McMurrough**, **Jaguars**, **two Morans**, **Jazz/Alguacil Hacking support**, **Coyote**, and a few fresh Corregidor pieces were fielded from **what the player had painted**, not a fully free-choice competitive roster. A bad early **McMurrough ARO trade** removed a central assault mechanism before he could convert smoke/CC into a breach.
- **Observed — actual interactions:** The **Black A.I.R. sniper** dominated an over-open firing lane. **Sforza's Albedo/viral threat** was unexpectedly annoying for the Corregidor side; the players said this was a favorable point-cost trade, but did not compare it under controlled conditions to another full Kestrel option. Kestrel's linked **specialist movement/coordinated Orders** multiplied midboard reach.
- **Player opinion/rules warning:** The Corregidor player said he **may have wrongly assumed airborne deployment could never enter a central exclusion zone**. They checked an AI-search answer during the game and openly admitted it might be incorrect. **Do not treat the claimed revised deployment ruling as verified official N5** without the mission packet/wiki.
- **Inference:** **Kestrel Black A.I.R./Fennec Thunderbolt/Sforza ranged ARO layering** can punish an early **Corregidor McMurrough/Moran/Jaguar** move when the table has exposed corridors. The sensible counter is **safer smoke and staging angles, a legitimate flank/AD deployment check, and preserved hacking/Repeater routes**, not assuming McMurrough must duel a Neurokinetics sniper.
- **Limits:** The Kestrel player noted **lucky high-value reactive shots**; the other list was paint-limited, and terrain/rules errors were discussed. This is **not** a clean Kestrel > Corregidor matchup signal.

**Mission takeaway:** The **deployment map and real LOS lanes** are tactical resources. Validate exclusion-zone wording from official rules and keep at least one alternate midboard approach.

## #207 — Shindenbutai vs Imperial Service: Corporate Appropriation

[Full Infinity Gamer video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TLcZtMyuhcQ). Mission mechanics in the narration include **capturing enemy prototypes into the DZ, panoply acquisitions, Classifieds and HVT tasks**, matching **ITS17 Corporate Appropriation**. The closing audio says **6–3**, but speaker and translated text do not reliably associate the winning figure with a faction. **Winner deliberately left unverified**.

- **Observed — Imperial Service mobility:** **Sù-Jiàn** (including transformation into its climbing-capable form), **Motorized Bounty Hunter**, **Kanren decoys**, reactive guns and specialist helpers presented a **two-carrier fork**: if the defender eliminated the bounty hunter, **Sù-Jiàn could take over the extracted prototype**; if Sù-Jiàn drew fire, the cheap fast model remained a courier. The player credited an **Irregular-to-Regular Command Token conversion** with providing enough final extraction distance.
- **Observed — Shindenbutai denial:** **Hatamoto**, **Tanuki fireteam/HMG or Hacker**, **Teishin-type Hacker**, and specialist shooters used **Panzerfaust/Thunderbolt and point-blank AROs** to contest the carrier and then tackle the **panoply/Classified** swing. Several expensive shots failed to kill a primary courier in the desired sequence; later Shindenbutai attempted to equalize through special actions.
- **Observed — logistics:** One player secured the mission's key prototype early; both later traded late **panoply rolls, HVT approaches and support actions**. The last turn included repeated failed WIP rolls for a Classified before one succeeded; simply reaching an objective did not guarantee conversion.
- **Player opinion:** The Sù-Jiàn player called the **Bounty Hunter/Sù-Jiàn fork two bad options for the defender** and emphasized that the attempt spent real Orders setting up the run. One participant criticized a **Kanren** choice and said the list felt Order-light; these remain personal evaluations.
- **Inference:** **ISS Sù-Jiàn + cheap fast courier + optional extra Regular Order** is an attractive **Corporate Appropriation prototype delivery** architecture, **even against Shindenbutai Hatamoto/Tanuki high-burst ARO**. Shindenbutai should **attack the handoff route and its support Orders**, not focus only on the most expensive runner. The result is **not** evidence of either sectorial winning the head-to-head until score ownership is checked.
- **Limits:** Some spoken unit names and closing score syntax are unreliable. Match is useful for **courier branching and order budgeting**, not quantified faction performance.

**Mission takeaway:** Prepare **an A and B prototype carrier and a credible last-order HVT/Classified option**. A potentially recoverable courier changes the defender's target priority.

## #208 — IX Interplanetario final: Bakunin vs OSS, Hardlock

[Original grand-final footage with Robert Shepherd commentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j_kbocTPdJ0). **Axel (Operations Subsection) defeats Cobraprime (Bakunin)** in the **August 14–16, 2026** Interplanetario final. **N5.2 + May Army, ITS17**: this is *not* N5.3 despite its September 2026 video release.

- **Observed — Bakunin:** **Cenobite Heavy Rocket Launcher + Penitent Red Fury + Orphan Engineer Haris**, **two Riot Grrls with Uhahu Hacker**, **Rounder Hacker with Trinity**, **Initiate Minelayer/Repeater, Zero Forward Observer, Sin-Eater HMG, Chimera/Morlocks and a Moderator** comprised two forward-capable fireteams and a strong reactive **hacking/mine/visibility control** network. Cobraprime chose **first turn** after winning the initiative and achieved early midfield pressure.
- **Observed — OSS:** **Asura Lieutenant Hacker** with formidable **WIP/BTS, Trinity and MSV3**, **Posthuman Proxies**, **Kyra/Kira Sharma** (spelling varies in transcript) and a preserved order/specialist network endured the initial assault. **Asura used MSV3 to engage a camouflaged mine directly** while protected/low-exposure Proxies pushed consoles.
- **Observed — late scoreboard reversal:** Bakunin established AROs, multiple mines and secured some objective/control footholds, but **OSS going second flipped contested consoles on the last active turn**. A **Posthuman** took a heavily defended console and **Kyra Sharma** completed a remaining console, leaving **all three in OSS control** and deciding the reported winner.
- **Player/commentator analysis:** The commentator recommends treating **Hardlock going second** as an opportunity to **survive and preserve enough mission Orders for the final console sweep**. Bakunin's mine/Repeater network is strong but did not prevent the final wave. OSS Asura + Proxies + Kyra combine an elite Hacker's threat with **independent last-turn buttons**.
- **Severe integrity caveat:** The **commentary itself notes potential Order-count issues**; [contemporary viewer discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/InfinityTheGame/comments/1wjodi1/grand_finals_ix_interplanetario_cobraprime/) specifically alleges **five or six excess Orders**. Those claims are not independently adjudicated here. Preserve **“winner as presented in recording”**, **do not invent final OP**, and **do not use the match as legally clean competitive evidence or replicate the Order sequence uncritically**.
- **Inference:** Within the *presented* sequence, **OSS Asura/Proxy/Kyra** is an excellent example of **last-turn multi-console access in Hardlock** against **Bakunin Cenobite/Riot Grrl/Morlock screens**. The lesson about avoiding early specialist/Order exhaustion is robust even if particular plays were illegal.

**Mission takeaway:** **Hardlock endgame first:** choose deployment, defense and Order reservation around the **last two console flips**, not the turn-one score alone.

## #209 — JSA vs PanO/Kestrel: Operation Sandtrap starter game at 150 points

[Full MiniWarGaming episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJlEzxrxPuQ), published [2024-12-24](https://www.miniwargaming.com/show/infinity-battle-reports). **JSA takes control and PanO fast-forwards/concedes the cleanup** after two rounds. **Initial N5 v1.0**, **150-point starter/learning format**, *not* a 300-point ITS test.

- **Observed — JSA:** **Hatamoto Lieutenant with two Lieutenant Orders**, a **Nokizaru Forward Observer / agile ninja**, **Jizamurai**, a **Raiden Heavy Rocket + mine/Hidden Deployment option**, and other close-range JSA assets. The ninja **used Climbing Plus, a Dodge bonus and vertical/short-distance approach angles** to reach PanO targets despite multiple ARO chances.
- **Observed — PanO:** **Griffin HMG Lieutenant, three Fennec Fusiliers (including Paramedics), Banshee Shotgun, Black A.I.R. Neurokinetics and Scarecrow Minelayer** represented only starter-bundle-level force breadth. Their shooting looked threatening, but one **Hatamoto close-combat exchange and ninja climb** collapsed a small model/order base.
- **Observed — teaching outcome:** Players explicitly said they were **learning ranges, ARO dice and skills**, reviewed a few mistakes and skipped a full third-round scoring exercise after remaining PanO resistance became negligible. **There is no verified numerical OP.**
- **Player opinion:** The JSA player liked the **ninja's Dodge movement** and **Hatamoto's self-sufficient attack**, and struggled to find a good use for the Raiden's **forward mine/hidden combination** on this small table.
- **Inference:** **Hatamoto + vertical Dodge/Climbing ninja** is a legitimate **small-table close-assault pairing** against **Fennec/Banshee/Black A.I.R.** if shooting corridors can be crossed. PanO should **layer templates/mines around climb exits and protect the HMG Lieutenant**, but **do not transfer this result to tournament-scale balance**.
- **Limits:** The source calls out beginner errors; units, points, missions and body count are unlike a full game.

**Mission takeaway:** In training scenarios, practice **ARO geometry against Climbing Plus and late Dodge movement** rather than measuring which starter sectorial is “better.”

## #210 — Vanilla Ariadna vs Torchlight Brigade: ITS17 Corporate Appropriation

[Full LIT episode 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KOSG_d-r1ow). **Torchlight Brigade 5–4 vanilla Ariadna**, final points explicitly reconstructed by players. Each side destroyed the opposing prototype, so the margin came from **mission side objectives and an HVT/Classified**, not army points.

- **Observed — Ariadna:** **Voronin Lieutenant**, **Tankhunter Autocannon with extra attack die**, **backup Tankhunter with Chain of Command/Minelayer/Panzerfaust**, **Kazak missile, Antipode pack, SAS Minelayer, Dog-Warrior, Bearpode/controller, Loup-Garou/Briscard Haris and a Para-Commando** delivered lethal wide-flank threats. The **Tankhunter Autocannon** punched far above expectations in early fire; the **CoC Tankhunter** provided insurance for the visibly obvious Voronin.
- **Observed — Torchlight:** **Hippolyta**, **Kludger Engineer + Nimrod MSV2 AP gun Haris**, **Jack HMG/NCO + Wave Rider Hacker + Hellblazer Flammenspeer Haris**, **Vidoc Doctor**, **Raveneye/Mint infiltrators** and **Sàchà** supplied **D-Charges, varied specialists and independent endgame button runners**. Torchlight absorbed Ariadna's early pressure but still had the necessary last-turn reach.
- **Observed — high-risk trades:** Ariadna's **Bearpode** consumed many Orders and mostly traded for an inexpensive REM after counter-fire/positioning; the Antipodes and other flank threats advanced farther but not efficiently enough to finish the mission. **Both prototypes were destroyed very late** (one on the last Order), leaving a **4–4 provisional tie** before Torchlight completed a final HVT/security scoring condition for **5–4**.
- **Player opinion:** Both regarded the **extra-die Tankhunter Autocannon** and **cheap Chain-of-Command Tankhunter** as attractive Ariadna tools; the bearer said he could instead have **speculatively thrown a smoke/grenade route for the Bearpode**, explicitly an *unplayed* alternative. They cautioned that **spending five Orders on a one-point panoply** is often the wrong exchange.
- **Inference:** **Ariadna Tankhunter + CoC + Antipode/Bearpode diversified assault** can threaten **Torchlight Nimrod/Haris/Doctor** plans, but **TLB Hippolyta/Vidoc/Sàchà D-Charge and HVT access** can still outscore the offense in Corporate Appropriation. The match does **not** prove Torchlight is stronger in a stand-up fight.
- **Limits:** Extremely narrow score, multiple improbable dice, a nonoptimized Ariadna flanking attack and historical ITS17 profiles.

**Mission takeaway:** **Destroying both prototypes can still leave a tie**; keep Orders for a **secondary HVT/Classified** and don't spend a whole combat pool on a low-value panoply.

### Shared tactical lessons

1. **Mission actions are full Order sequences:** Last Launch evacuation (#201), Evacuation civilian delivery (#202), Provisioning safe-area delivery (#205) and Corporate Appropriation prototype demolition (#210) all failed or succeeded on **one or two remaining Orders**.
2. **Midfield defense can be cheaper than the attack it stops:** Moran/Koala, Daylami, Techjack mines, and even an unrevealed possible Noctifer made high-point attacks inefficient (#201, #202, #204, #205).
3. **Zone scoring should be calculated, not guessed:** The double-TAG Cutter/Camo game was **rescored**, and the precise **Zulu-Cobra vs Fox Ranger** value mattered (#203). Do not claim a unit “dominates” without checking objective-zone rules.
4. **Independent scoring actors beat a single hero's damage output:** Nomads forced a Yu Jing carrier delay (#201), Hassassin preserved safe-area specialists while ALEPH committed Maximus (#205), and OSS's Posthuman/Kyra routes bypassed the nearly complete Bakunin screen (#208; rules caveat).
5. **Matchup claims are bounded:** A novice Sandtrap game, paint-limited Corregidor, an asymmetric Critical Intervention match, and a grand final with questionable Order accounting are useful **technique examples**, not ranked faction-versus-faction evidence.
