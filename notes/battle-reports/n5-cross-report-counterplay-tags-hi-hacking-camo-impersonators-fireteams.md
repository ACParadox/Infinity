## Evidence, reproducibility and scope

- **O = directly observed** action described in one battle report; **R = tournament retrospective** containing first-hand reported actions; **I = inference** supported by actions but **not** an independently confirmed tactic. A second episode with similar tools strengthens a *mechanism*, not proof of matchup dominance.
- **Independent repetitions matter:** [#161](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#237](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) review the *same* CanCon champion; [#169](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#170](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) cover *one* Borderbash event; [#006](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#031](031-040-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#009](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#028](021-030-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#010](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#033](031-040-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#011](011-020-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#030](021-030-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) and [#022](021-030-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#026](021-030-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) are duplicates or re-uploads. Do not count as separate confirmations. [#241](241-241-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) lacks verified action. Tourney recaps may cover five rounds without five independent indexed games.
- **Not clean normal-ITS evidence:** [#208](201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) has serious disputed Order counts; [#226](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) is a one-TAG/single-group special format with a projected score and disputed LoF; [#233](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) is projected, not completed; [#235](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) uses Team Ops alterations; [#237](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) includes a consequential rules error in a late round; [#006](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) includes a Capture & Protect HVT error; [#187](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) involved a critical E/M hit, not average reliability. A single lucky critical does not establish a reliable counter.
- **Rules baseline:** multiple N5 revisions, tournament packets and historical Army builds. Verify N5.3 profile, hacking programs, Firewall, equipment, status effects and legal Fireteams in this repository's current `sectorials/` and `rules/`. **Countered/disabled**, **removed**, **forced to Reset**, **delayed** and **outscored** are *different outcomes*.
- **Judge success by** (a) enemy Orders spent, (b) important enemy actions/specialists disabled, (c) own Orders cost, (d) mission access gained, (e) ability to repeat after counterplay or repair. Most counters fail when they **don't leave an independent scoring body**.

## 1 — TAGs and other premium targets

### A. Disable or divert first; kill only if necessary

**Observed:**
- **Scout E/M mine → Marut Lieutenant disabled**, opening vanilla Ariadna's forward attack before OSS conceded in CanCon Crossing Lines ([#237](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R). This is an **E/M trap + mission entry**, not a clean completed five-turn TAG-kill result; its late event rules issue also limits outcome use.
- **Sāchā E/M grenade → Skyhound disabled** ([#187](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). The Shasvastii counter succeeded on a reported *critical* hit but the expensive flyer list still scored Provisioning; compare the **Order cost** of stopping a flyer with preserving normal scorers.
- **Interventor/Cybermine/Kulak web → Achilles stuck Resetting/disabled**, Tunguska then scored Akial Classifieds, 7–3 ([#197](191-200-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). Similar layered Spotlight/Isolation into Shindenbutai's Hatamoto/HI occurred at [#056](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O; target is elite HI rather than a TAG.
- **Starmada Cyberghost repeatedly Possessed an NCA TAG** while a Roadbot-style unit flipped Hardlock buttons, 7–1 ([#102](101-110-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). This directly demonstrates why control effects plus an **independent objective runner** are stronger than merely attempting TAG damage.
- **Kestrel Banshee hacker forcing Achilles to drop Suppression → Redeye firing lane** ([#158](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R); the named gunfighter was finished *after* cyber positional pressure, not by a stand-alone Redeye alpha ([#005](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O counterexample).
- **Bakunin Koala wounded Shang Ji** after failed Dodge ([#060](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O); cheap deployables can tax expensive HI in ways analogous to TAG protection. **Kriigel pheroware/E/M** made even Hatamoto wary of entering a Tohaa objective room ([#224](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).

**Non-hacking solutions:** a **Tankhunter Autocannon** punished an exposed split-Burst Unknown Ranger ([#223](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O), **Black A.I.R. K1** threatened a Cameronian ([#158](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R), **Karakuri/Tanko Monofilament CC** punished an expensive threat after delivery in [#099](091-100-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O, and a **Kaeltar/Chaksa/Rasail direct-template corridor** delayed Shasvastii attacks ([#189](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). Exact E/M, AP, K1, Monofilament and anti-materiel loadouts are profile-specific; do not assume one is universally best at every range/armor.

### B. Winning around the TAG can be the stronger counter

- **Vanilla Ariadna bypassed Varuna Cutter**, took Corporate Appropriation prototype using **Dynamo Smoke + Sasha** with minimal shooting, while the Cutter spent Orders on kills rather than getting a Specialist to the displaced objective ([#237](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R; consistent with [#161](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), same event).
- A **hidden Cutter held Area of Interest zone weight** against USARF ([#203](201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O), so *avoiding* it is not automatically correct when the TAG itself scores passively. Defeat the **zone/weight calculation** using another region or eligible contesting points, or actually remove/expel it.
- **Hassassin Bahram versus triple PanO TAGs**: Daylami/Panzerfaust and Muyib slowed the big guns, but the *win condition* was **three Classifieds for 5 OP** against PanO's 2 OP from kills ([#214](211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). This is **mission denial**, not a claim the Fidays killed all three TAGs.
- **Shasvastii Sphinx** killed ~four defenders but spent ~9–10 Orders while Torchlight **Jamie Parachutist + Moonraker** dismantled exposed Med-Tech/EVO/order support and took Battleground bands, 10–0 ([#231](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). Deny the TAG's **support and score**, not necessarily its armor.
- **Critical counter-counterexample:** Double **Seraph + Defersen/repair** successfully attacked Tunguska TAG-hacking networks in Corporate/Hardlock ([#181](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R). A hacker grid without direct protection, own KHD/Firewall checks or safe objective access is not a guaranteed anti-TAG win.

**Practical sequence:** locate Repeater/Hacker and mine zones; choose **E/M/control, K1/AP/CC, flank backline, or bypass-to-score**; protect the enabler and reserve mission Orders; account for Engineer/Reset/repair; do not automatically chase the TAG to destruction.

## 2 — Heavy Infantry and defensive HI Fireteams

### A. Separate link bonuses, range and network access

- **Tunguska Pitcher → Spotlight → Guided → Oblivion** made **Shindenbutai Hatamoto-linked HI** spend Orders on Reset/Isolation rather than Battleground strips ([#056](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
- **Morat double Dartok linked Pitchers + Guided** removed/disabled Svalarheima protected guns while **Rodoks** pressed Unmasking; the Suryat surviving a reactive roll was lucky, not the reason the tactic works in general ([#118](111-120-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
- **Tunguska Interventor/Mary Problems → Guided** broke **TAK Veteran Kazak/Frontovik Suppression** ([#166](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R). **Exposed premium defenders are not automatically safe just because they win open fire lanes**.
- **Tunguska Interventor/Heckler/Hollow Man hacking** repeatedly Isolated/Targeted/IMM'd Steel Phalanx and prevented timely Hardlock consoles (5–0, unusually good rolls; [#151](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
- **Combined Pitcher → Bit & Kiss killed WhiteCo's Fusilier Hacker → a Fireteam lost described Sixth Sense protection → Charontid killed Hawkwood** ([#112](111-120-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). The key here is *removing the defensive team member and changing the subsequent gun odds*, not directly shooting the highest ARM first.
- **Bakunin Repeater/Koala** wounded **Shang Ji** while pressing Superiority ([#060](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). **Caledonian Wulvers/Mormaer and warbands** blocked White Banner heavy infantry from a Critical Intervention room by close templates and short-range CC rather than premium AP gun duels ([#086](081-090-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
- **Tunguska Grenzer/reactive gun + cheap Bounty Hunter** beat a Military Orders multiple-elite-Fireteam approach in Firefight 7–0; two Classifieds/Panoply mattered as much as the gunfight ([#137](131-140-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).

### B. What fails

- Trying to trade one expensive HI in a perfect lane **without seeing the template/Koala or projected Pitcher** ([#060](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#056](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)).
- Investing all Orders in **Guided** after the board has opened while forgetting the actual scoring pieces ([#005](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O, [#229](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
- Assuming links are weak simply because they are hackable: **double Seraph/Defersen** and **TinBot/Engineer** support can punish a poorly protected hacking network ([#181](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R); **Hassassin Asawira/Muyib/Govad** kept supplies against ALEPH despite enemy elite attackers ([#205](201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).

**Checklist:** Is the *member carrying TinBot/Engineer/Hacker/Second Number* exposed? Does destroying one Repeater change the Order path? Can one **template/Disco/Pitcher** reach multiple models without illegal assumptions about Coherency/line of fire? Can a non-hackable Specialist bypass the defended quadrant?

## 3 — Hacking networks, Pitchers and Guided

### A. Attack infrastructure rather than pushing hackable units through it

1. **Shoot or safely clear the forward Repeater/Pitcher** while keeping important HI/TAG outside the projected area. Kestrel **Firefly/Griffin removed White Banner's Pitcher** to let Hetkari work ([#003](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O); Kestrel likewise cleared Combined Repeaters and then took Supremacy consoles/zones ([#005](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
2. **Project your own Repeater into the vulnerable Hacker, then use a legal KHD/program.** **ISS Zhanying/Wu Ming Pitcher → Deva KHD Trinity killed Jazz** in Corregidor's Moran/Jazz network ([#238](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O), freeing Adil's lane, though Adil was subsequently punished for staying exposed. **Combined Bit & Kiss** similarly killed the exposed **Fusilier Hacker** protecting a WhiteCo team ([#112](111-120-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
3. **Use non-hackable pressure, vision screens or other routes** when the attacker's own HI/TAG would trigger several enemy AROs. **TAK Camo/Vystrel** caused trouble for double Combined Aspects, while Combined's Repeaters were much less effective against much of nonhackable TAK ([#064](061-070-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). **O-12 Varangian Smoke plus durable advance** continued through a Nomad Jazzy network in Biotechvore and won 5–2, albeit with environmental time pressure on both sides ([#128](121-130-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
4. **Attack the backfield hacker, repair/order base or deployment edge**: Torchlight Jamie + Moonraker reached Shasvastii **Med-Tech/EVO** ([#231](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O); the general lesson is offensive access behind the front, not automatic immunity to a hacking net.
5. **Keep the mission Specialist alive after the net changes:** [#078](071-080-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O Starmada needed a narrow route between **Moran Repeaters** for CivVac even while Corregidor Spotlight+Vertigo punished links. [#238](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O **Taowu backup** scored after Adil's exposed team was lost.

### B. Counter-network failure examples

- **Banshee KHD vs Interventor KHD**: Banshee won rolls but **Firewall/BTS prevented a meaningful wound and Banshee was lost** ([#115](111-120-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). “Bring any KHD” is not a reliable answer.
- **Tunguska network suppressed Achilles** ([#197](191-200-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O), whereas **MO double Seraph/Defersen** reached and attacked Tunguska hacking support ([#181](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R). Network *delivery* and coverage matter as much as cyber statline.
- **Tunguska three Interventors + Guided** killed large portions of NCA ([#122](121-130-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O), but **the early console action** still determined the final objective difference; do not equate damage with scoring.
- **Kestrel [#005](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)** showed another Guided volley may be inferior to securing a quadrant; count all Pitcher, program, missile, clearance and mission Orders.

**Audit:** Identify *every* Repeater path, who can hack through it, Firewall and BTS, attacker's vulnerability to enemy KHDs, **which specific Repeaters can be destroyed**, whether safe bypass exists, and the remaining scoring route. Consult [hacking and network projection](../list-building/hacking-and-network-projection.md) for N5.3 mechanics.

For the specific **Spotlight → Guided** breakpoints, contested Reset/Dodge and multiple Hacker AROs, use [Playbook F](#playbook-f--countering-guided-missile-strategies-spotlight--targeted--guided) below.

## 4 — Camo, Hidden Deployment and Decoys

### A. Reveal selectively and otherwise deny the marker's mission

- **Redeye Sensor → reveal Camo; Black A.I.R. K1 pressure** against Caledonian threats ([#158](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R). **Shasvastii M-Drone Sensor** revealed a hidden Anathematic in a Combined tournament game ([#155](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R); separate Sensor success from the later game winner.
- **Asura MSV3** shot a camouflaged mine during the Hardlock final ([#208](201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O, **score/Order-count disputed**). The reusable idea is **right visibility tool versus the right marker**, not treating a contested match as clean competitive data.
- **WhiteCo/linked MSV vs Smoke** and **Yojimbo/Eclipse/Disco** illustrate *specific* visor and line-of-fire interactions ([#010](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R, [#041](041-050-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O, [#233](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) early stop). **MSV is not automatically Sensor**. Use exact N5.3 ARO/Discover and visibility rules.
- **Cheap model or expendable REM into a mine** can save the actual button operator: in Onyx [#212](211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O, an **E-Drone deliberately absorbed a mine so Kurnau could reach the Classified** (6–0 Countermeasures). In [#232](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O, **Daylami** absorbed/cleared a Shasvastii minefield on the actual Sabotage route.
- **Attack the mission while Camo is elsewhere:** [#196](191-200-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O **quad-Shrouded/double Malignos** won zone occupancy from Next Wave through hidden markers and Dazer delay, but Next Wave still held a console for part of the game. Camo is strongest when it *scores or prevents scoring*; forcing every marker to reveal can be the wrong objective.
- **Minelayer/Decoy defenses tax both sides:** [#067](061-070-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O TAK's ~13 apparent markers slowed Ikari; [#161](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)/[#237](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R Ariadna's Strelok Decoys screened Tankhunters; [#159](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R Shasvastii marker saturation diverted Achilles/Pandora from zones. These illustrate **why selective Sensor/Discover, route choice and marker triage** matter.

### B. Detection can be less valuable than preserving Orders

Choose the marker to handle by **what it can score or block now**. A Camo shell without an actual valid specialist path can be outscored. Avoid indiscriminate Discover loops that give up last-turn consoles ([#189](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) and [#208](201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) mission lessons). **Do not invent abilities** such as a particular visor automatically ignoring Impersonation or Holoecho.

## 5 — Impersonators, Fidays, Speculos and DZ alpha attackers

### A. The reliable principle is *deployment geometry* rather than a single counter-model

- **Do not expose Lieutenant + sole Hacker + Engineer/CoC in one accessible attack pocket.** Hassassin **Fiday** threatened Shindenbutai's **Tanuki Chain of Command/Hacker and Panguling** before Frontline scoring ([#088](081-090-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O). **Fiday + Haytham** repeatedly pressured high-value Combined army actors when the cheap **Ikadron/Dāturazi/minelayer screen was mispositioned** ([#147](141-150-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O).
- **Keep cheap templates, mines, Koalas, warbands and a reserve shooter** in positions forcing an Impersonator to cross **two separate threats**, not merely one impressive sniper. JSA **Hidden Oniwaban + Minelayer (2)** punished an airborne **Liu Xing** arrival ([#051](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O): **it is an anti-drop example, not proof the Oniwaban counters Impersonation**. In [#223](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O, mines/guards stopped Van Zant from *finishing* the prototype after he killed Voronin.
- **Protect the actual mission object/carrier:** Shasvastii **Sheskiin/Speculo** penetrated WinterFor shooting in Critical Intervention ([#182](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O), but WinterFor **Peacemaker mine, deployable turrets and last-turn REM/room worker** won **9–5**. Defending/going second gave this build time to punish the mandatory retrieval path.
- **Cheap anti-assassin deterrent:** [#215](211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) O players praised cheap **Gakis** for making Fiday/McMurrough-like approaches costly even when they did not directly kill those pieces. This is **deterrence**, not documented successful Fiday destruction. **Shujae Hidden E/M Minelayers + Ghazi Jammers** helped Hassassin survive alpha attacks in a winner retrospective ([#173](171-180-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R).
- **Counterexample:** Shasvastii **double Speculo/triple Caliban** exploited insufficiently protected hero lists and marker confusion in a tournament recap ([#159](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) R). A hypothetical one-line mine screen is not guaranteed to prevent a coordinated infiltration.

**Use current rules to distinguish** Impersonation versus Camouflage, normal Discover, Biometric Visor, Sensor, enemy deployment restrictions and what triggers a valid ARO. **[#143](141-150-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md)** includes a real Fiday versus Biometric Visor/Impersonation interaction, but the report does not support a universal “one visor removes all impersonators” rule.

## Practical counterplay playbooks — decision guides from battle reports (2026-11-02)

These are **specific questions and action sequences**, not faction-versus-faction rankings. “Reliable” means the *decision mechanism* has multiple supporting observations or is robust to common counter-counterplay; it never guarantees a die result. Each example is from the **played historical roster**. Verify current N5.3 equipment, hacker programs, immunities, Fireteams and the actual ITS packet before adopting it.

### Playbook A — How do PanO lists overcome dense hacking networks?

**Problem:** An expensive TAG/HI moves into multiple enemy Repeater areas, then spends its active pool Resetting, accepting Oblivion/Spotlight, or being hit by Guided. The wrong solution is to drive its best gun straight through and expect BTS to solve the route.

**Decision order:**
1. **Map the projected network before activating the premium attacker.** Locate the *physical* Pitchers, FastPandas, repeaters, Hackers and missile REM, and note whether a nearby Mine/Koala makes shooting the Repeater unsafe. Ask which ordinary and Killer Hacker attacks are actually legal against each troop. If one Repeater carries the whole fork, **remove it first**.
2. **Use a nonhackable enabler or independent ranged piece to clear the node** before advancing the TAG/HI. A PanO gunfighter may clear visible Pitchers through an alternate angle while an Engineer waits behind. Ensure the gun's chosen line is not covered by a second Pitcher.
3. **Bring counterhack and recovery, not just raw BTS.** A protected KHD/HD, Firewall or TinBot where valid, and Machinist/Palbot or appropriate Engineer creates a way to survive the first attack and still use the expensive piece. Preserve a second, preferably nonhackable, Specialist path to the actual objective.
4. **Use a distinct approach channel:** smoke/Disco or geometry can break the opponent's shooting support, while a forward/deep-deployment Specialist scores around the network. **Do not claim Smoke blocks hacking by itself**; the actual Hacking Area, equipment and program matter.
5. **If the network is too costly to destroy, outscore it** by moving a nonhackable/less exposed scorer on the other flank. Count the Orders for each path before committing to the Repeater trap.

**Observed sequences and what they teach:**
- **PanO-affiliated Kestrel versus White Banner:** Firefly/Griffin removed a projected hostile Pitcher, **then Hetkari** attacked. The opposite order would have exposed the expensive assault first. **Kestrel versus Combined** likewise cleared Repeaters later and recovered Supremacy scoring, but **Redeye first-turn direct alpha versus Q-Drone** had already wasted many Orders. [#003](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#005](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) — *direct observations*.
- **Varuna versus O-12:** Zulu-Cobra KHD/Pitcher and cheap Fusilier Hacker provided a thin cyber screen; **Machinist recovered Squalos** after enemy disable, and **Pathfinder** remained the delivery/scoring actor. This is the useful **attacker / counterhacker / Engineer / scorer** separation. [#019](011-020-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) — *tournament recollection*.
- **Military Orders versus Tunguska:** Double Seraph + Defersen and Engineer/tech support **reached vulnerable hacking support** in a 10–0 Corporate round and worked in Hardlock. Same player **swapped away from double Seraph** against Morat Dartok/Yaogat anti-TAG tools. Cyber superiority alone is not a guaranteed TAG counter. [#181](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) — *tournament recollection; historical Fireteam*.
- **Neoterra counterexamples:** Tunguska's three Interventors/Repeaters/Guided removed over a third of NCA's force and caused Retreat in Crossing Lines; Starmada **Cyberghost Possessed an NCA TAG** while Roadbot won Hardlock buttons 7–1. Static Bolt/TAG quality did not defend the cyber or objective routes. [#122](121-130-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#102](101-110-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) — *single games*.
- **PanO triple TAG counterexample:** Cutter/Squalo/Tikbalang could shoot but lost Firefight **5–2** because Hassassin accomplished Classifieds. Even if no hacker hurts your TAGs, zero specialist variety can still lose the actual mission. [#214](211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).

**Plan-selection rule:** For a **few exposed Pitchers**, clear them and assault. For a **redundant network**, threaten its Hacker with KHD/independent attacker **and** score the weak flank. Against **KHD/TinBot support**, compare actual Firewall/BTS and attack angle; never assert “any PanO KHD counters Interventor.” [#115](111-120-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) documents a Banshee KHD that failed to damage Interventor despite good rolls.

### Playbook B — How does Ariadna handle heavy TAG lists without conventional hacking?

**Problem:** PanO/OSS/Combined TAGs may outshoot regular Ariadna guns; much of Ariadna cannot threaten them with hacker programs. The goal is **disable, force bad angles, trade efficiently or score elsewhere**, not obligatorily destroy the armor.

**Decision order:**
1. **Screen the approach with hidden anti-heavy threats:** Scout **E/M Minelayer** (if on the played/current profile), Camo/Decoy Streloks, hidden Tankhunter Autocannon or other actual AP/EXP tools. Use their uncertainty to force Discover, Dodge and inefficient move paths; do not expose the Tankhunter on a low-value target.
2. **Separate anti-TAG jobs:** the **E/M piece disables or delays**, Tankhunter/Vystrel/anti-materiel weapon punishes exposure, **Dog-Warrior/Galwegian/Cameronian/Wallace** can close a route at short range, while Dynamo/Sasha/Kövlk or other eligible unit completes the mission. The expensive shooter and mission runner need not be the same model.
3. **Control vision and entry** with Dynamo/Maverick smoke and expendable cheap screens. A smoke-delivered melee attempt is contingent on actual access, enemy visors/templates and reactive mine positions—not automatic protection from a TAG.
4. **Before entering the gun's optimal range, ask if it can be bypassed.** In prototype/cargo scenarios, take the alternate flank and finish; in repeated zones where **a Cutter itself supplies scoring weight**, plan to **displace or outweigh** that model rather than simply ignore it.
5. **Protect remaining scoring Orders and the Lieutenant/CoC.** One successful anti-armor duel can still lose if it consumes the entire active turn.

**Observed outcomes:**
- **Marut disabled:** In the CanCon **vanilla Ariadna** Crossing Lines round, Scout E/M mine Isolated/Immobilized OSS **Marut Lieutenant**, then mobile Dog-Warrior/Galwegian and Dynamo threats exploited a poorly defended deployment. **OSS conceded**; this is a demonstrated disable + structural advantage, **not an independently verified full-game TAG kill**. The same tournament had a material rules issue in a different round. [#237](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).
- **Cutter bypassed:** Against Varuna **Cutter** in Corporate Appropriation, **Dynamo smoke + Sasha extracted a prototype** while Cutter took inefficient combat Orders. Wallace offered a separate CC/anti-materiel destroy option. [#161](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#237](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) — **one event, not two confirmations**. Earlier standalone Wallace/Tankhunter vs USARF prototype attack is a distinct relevant anti-materiel example. [#223](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).
- **Non-hacking pressure can have real risk:** Tankhunter Autocannon punished Unknown Ranger's split-Burst action (#223), but the champion said Tankhunters were only “mostly okay” across the tournament; avoid converting 1–2 explosive exchanges into guaranteed anti-TAG performance. [#223](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#237](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).
- **TAG that scores itself breaks the bypass approach:** A hidden **Varuna Cutter** accrued Area of Interest zone weight against USAriadna despite Maverick smoke and Unknown Ranger/Minuteman attacks; Varuna won **6–4**. [#203](201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md). In this packet you need *territorial points*, not just a safe cargo path.
- **Expensive melee is not always efficient:** Ariadna Bearpode consumed many Orders for a cheap REM trade versus Torchlight in Corporate, while more mundane Tankhunter and specialist actions mattered. [#210](201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).

**List test:** Include one meaningful **E/M or AP/EXP threat**, one **smoke/marker** approach, a **short-range trade** if legal, and **two independent scoring options**. Do not assume each named model exists in TAK/USARF/Caledonia/Kosmo because it appeared in **vanilla Ariadna**.

**For physical walls, Smoke/MSV, Eclipse/Disco, White Noise, templates and Sensor**, see [Playbook G](#playbook-g--fighting-when-normal-line-of-fire-is-blocked) below; a vision-control answer must not be mistaken for an all-purpose anti-Fireteam answer.

**Re-evaluate after first exchange.** If a lone reactive model costs more than **two to three extra Orders without opening the objective route**, stop the duel and recalculate. This is a practical decision trigger, **not** a measured universal optimal threshold. When a linked gun's defense is still protected by another piece, take that piece's ARO into account. The identity of the **key Fireteam member** and its legal bonuses must be verified from the current canonical Fireteam chart.

### Playbook D — When should you attack a Camo marker versus bypass it?

**Ask what this exact marker can do to your present scoring path.** Camo/Decoy's chief value often is *forcing a low-information Order*, not killing troops.

| Situation | Default action | Reason / observed cases |
|---|---|---|
| Marker directly guards a **mandatory button, carrier route or last scoring zone** | **Test/clear it deliberately**, often with a cheap model or Sensor if legal; then move the Specialist | Onyx **E-Drone walked into a mine** to protect Kurnau's Classified route [#212](211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md); Hassassin Daylami absorbed mines before Double Bind D-Charges [#232](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) |
| Marker may be Decoy or expendable and has **no immediate scoring power** | **Bypass; do not repeatedly Discover** just for a kill | Ariadna Strelok Decoys disguised Tankhunters and Shasvastii Camo density diverted opponent Orders [#067](061-070-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#159](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#161](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) |
| Unrevealed marker **counts in an end-of-round zone** | You must **contest weight/occupancy, reveal/remove, or control another region**; mere avoidance may lose | Shasvastii quad-Shrouded/double Malignos held Area of Interest bands despite Next Wave having the console [#196](191-200-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) |
| You own **Sensor** or appropriate **MSV** and can exploit the reveal immediately | Test from protected position if payoff exceeds the reveal/approach cost | Redeye Sensor identified Caledonian marker while Black A.I.R. K1 threatened the exposed problem [#158](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md); M-Drone Sensor exposed hidden Anathematic [#155](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) |
| ARO shot is covered by **mines, template and second hidden model** | **Force reveal with cheap expendable actor** or find a non-ARO corridor; avoid feeding main gun | Hidden **Oniwaban/Minelayer** caught arriving Liu Xing [#051](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md); TAK Vystrel/Strelok layers taxed opposing top-tier shooters [#064](061-070-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#067](061-070-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) |
| Targeting Camo gives no objective benefit this turn | Usually **score elsewhere**, preserving Orders for button/cargo/end turn | Next Wave attacked Shas marker shell but lost area occupancy despite fighting; actual objective needed zone bodies [#196](191-200-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) |

**Decision rule:** Prioritize **(1) mandatory scoring blocker**, **(2) a marker able to reveal into a lethal ARO at the imminent route**, **(3) a marker actually counting toward scoring**. A distant unidentified marker with no relevant lane often ranks below an open objective. **MSV is not inherently Sensor**; “can see”, “may Discover”, “can target a Marker”, “reveals hidden state” and “ignores Mimetism” are different printed rule interactions. An Impersonator has its **own** rules.

**Counterexample:** Shasvastii deliberately held a **Hidden Malignos** for an anti-flyer opportunity so long that it had too few Orders left to help its own Provisioning plan; merely keeping an unseen model is not valuable if it cannot act when needed. [#187](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).

### Playbook E — How do you protect against first-turn deployment-zone attacks?

This is principally a **deployment, reserve and activation-access problem**. The attacker can be an Impersonator (Fiday/Speculo), Parachutist (Banshee, Jamie, Van Zant), Combat Jump unit (Bearcat/Liu Xing) or very fast melee/vehicle assault. **They do not share one arrival/Discover/ARO rule.**

**Deployment sequence:**
1. **Identify what ends the game if hit:** obvious Lieutenant, sole Engineer, single Hacker controlling the network, only medic/eligible Specialist, Order-generator cluster, and the *mission object*. Put mission-critical pieces behind cover and avoid lining two or three support targets inside one template or easy move-shoot angle.
2. **Create two-layer rear protection**: cheap template/mine/Koala/Flash pieces near likely arrival corridors, plus a **second independent responder** watching the route after the first guard is traded. Watch *lateral board edges, roof/vertical angles and blind arcs*, not just the DZ's front line.
3. **Preserve reserve information**: hidden/safe deployment, actual Sensor/Discover or anti-impersonation specialist if present, and backup LT/CoC can reduce the impact of one successful assassination; a Reserve model or a decoy does not automatically block Combat Jump.
4. **Don't present one irresistible first-turn alpha target.** Deny multi-kill angles for airborne shotgun/plasma/large template, and force the enemy to spend Orders moving from safe entry to the valuable support.
5. **After the first attack, prioritize re-forming the scoring floor:** rescue or replace vital support, restore a Hacker/Engineer if possible, reposition vulnerable backs and protect last-turn objectives. A successful DZ defense must preserve **game continuation**, not necessarily kill the assassin immediately.

**Observed successes and failures:**
- **Hidden interception worked:** JSA **Oniwaban Hidden/Minelayer (2)** killed **Liu Xing** arriving by Combat Jump near its defender; that specific deployment punished a particular landing, not every possible airborne lane. [#051](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) — *direct action*.
- **Screens stopped mission destruction despite Lt death:** **Van Zant killed Voronin** after arriving against vanilla Ariadna, but mines/defensive positions prevented doing the last wound to the Corporate objective. Protecting **the mission target** can be enough even if the Lt falls. [#223](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).
- **Multi-layer room defense survived early infiltration:** Shas **Speculo/Sheskiin** killed WinterFor defenders first turn, yet **Peacemaker mine, deployable turrets and last-turn room actor** prevented data-package conversion; WinterFor won **9–5**. [#182](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).
- **Poor rear protection lost to AD and flank:** Torchlight **Jamie Parachutist + Moonraker** reached Sphinx army's **Med-Tech/EVO** backend, causing command/Order collapse and far-band scoring, **10–0**. [#231](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) — *direct action*. Kestrel **Banshee** similarly punished an exposed back angle after initial Hetkari pressure [#003](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).
- **Impersonator punished critical support:** **Fiday** reached Shindenbutai **Tanuki CoC/Hacker/Panguling** in Frontline; **Fiday + Haytham** disrupted Combined **Sheskiin/Anathematic** support when cheap minelayer/templates did not actually guard the approach. [#088](081-090-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [#147](141-150-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).
- **Even elite long-gun defense can be bypassed:** MO's double Seraph rushed Tunguska hacker support despite front Repeaters [#181](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md). A strong TR gun by itself will not secure every blind back edge.
- **Countermeasure has opportunity cost:** Shujae E/M Minelayers plus Ghazi Jammers helped a Hassassin player survive five consecutive second turns [#173](171-180-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), but a first-turn screen-heavy list may sacrifice a scoring unit. Evaluate **which attack vector is realistic on this mission/table**.

**Mission-sensitive exceptions:** In **Crossing Lines** the extra forward blocks and dead-zone geometry radically alter threat directions [#229](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md); in **Evacuation** entry/exit and CivVac rules alter the value of early attack [#227](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md); for **Critical Intervention** the defender can lose models then still protect an untransferable carrier/room [#182](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md). Do not copy one defensive DZ template between packets without changing deployment.

### Playbook F — Countering Guided Missile strategies: Spotlight → Targeted → Guided

**Question:** Do you kill the Repeater, eliminate the Hacker, Reset, or destroy the missile REM? **Answer:** choose the cheapest action that removes the **current** bottleneck. A Repeater/Hacker enables a *new* Targeted State; Reset/Engineer clears an *existing* one; killing the **Guided launcher** removes that payload. These are distinct effects. Measure success in Orders and objective access, not just enemy casualties.

#### The three-part kill chain and where to break it

1. **Coverage:** a live Hacker has a legal Hacking Area via its own zone and/or allied Repeater/Pitcher/FastPanda/native REM. Each Repeater extends *every* friendly Hacker's area; it is **not** paired to one Hacker. A Repeater that is destroyed ceases to provide *that* projection, but other nodes may overlap. A safe hidden Hacker still requires a live projection route to attack a distant target.
2. **Target acquisition:** **Spotlight in N5.3 is not automatic Targeted**. The active Hacker rolls up to B2 (subject to actual skill/profile); successful program rolls force **AP, PS5 Saving Rolls against halved BTS**, and Targeted applies only on a failed save. Reactive Hackers can Spotlight in ARO when eligible. **Non-Hackable is not immune to Spotlight.** Firewall modifies the Comms attacker's WIP and grants the defender **+3 to Comms Saving Rolls**; distinguish the program roll, save, and subsequent missile.
3. **Payload:** once the victim is **Targeted**, a Guided-capable launcher can attack without LoF *even if the original Hacker or Repeater is now dead*. The missile must still use the legal weapon mode/template, maximum range, Range MODs and attack restrictions. It is normally B1. The target may **Dodge** (typically −3 if it cannot see the template attacker) or **Reset** (−3 WIP from Targeted) in a Face-to-Face Roll. A successful Reset also removes Targeted. Destroying the missile REM stops *that* Guided launcher, **not** the Targeted State or any other source of attacks benefiting from Targeted (+3 BS/Comms/Discover). Standard missile Guided is a **Blast/template** attack, not an assumed single-target AP+EXP mode. See [Spotlight](../../rules/3_Combat/Spotlight.md), [Targeted](../../rules/8_Game%20States%20and%20Glossary/Targeted_State.md), [Reset](../../rules/5a_Common%20Skills/Reset.md), [BS Attack (Guided)](../../rules/5a_Common%20Skills/BS_Attack.md) and the [current indirect-fire profile guide](../list-building/indirect-fire-speculative-guided-apex-value.md).

| Position right now | First option to evaluate | What it actually solves | Why it can fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Not yet Targeted; one exposed Pitcher/node projects into intended attacker** | Shoot/remove that **Repeater** with a cheap, suitable gun or other legal attacker **before** activating HI/TAG | Breaks a projection path and may deny all backline Hackers access to that position | A second node, Hacker's native area or newly fired Pitcher recreates coverage; the node may be protected by mines/ARO |
| **Not yet Targeted; accessible enemy Hacker behind projection** | Project own Repeater and **Trinity-capable** KHD/hybrid attack; or physically flank/kill Hacker | Removes a **particular** source of future Spotlights and other programs | Firewall/BTS, another Hacker, a second KHD, or spending more Orders than the mission permits |
| **Already Targeted; can activate during own turn** | **Reset at WIP −3**, or nearby **Engineer in Silhouette contact** if practical | Cancels current Targeted on success; avoids firing at the opponent's whole network | Reset may take several Orders; enemy may retain Hacking Area and Spotlight you again; Engineer needs access and WIP roll |
| **Already Targeted; opponent declares Guided** | Choose **Reset** or **Dodge** after comparing WIP/PH, MODs, template collateral and whether removal of Targeted matters | Either can contest that attack; successful Reset also clears Targeted | Both are Face-to-Face and can fail; Reset carries Targeted's −3, Dodge may carry no-LoF −3; other troopers under template also need legal protection |
| **Launcher is genuinely reachable cheaply** | Attack **missile REM**, optionally through flank/AD/long gun | Prevents this platform launching more Guided missiles | Launcher often hides deep; another launcher or different indirect attack may exist; **Targeted stays** |
| **Dense/redundant network, poor dismantling odds, objective accessible elsewhere** | Spread units, **avoid/bypass coverage where possible**, move an independent scorer | Denies high-value missile templates and preserves mission Orders | Avoiding hacking *does not inherently stop* Guided against an already-Targeted model; non-Hackable troops still risk Spotlight |

**Recommended Order sequence:** (1) identify which enemy models can be Spotlighted on your intended route and count **every live Repeater covering them**; (2) check if a low-cost node kill makes the route safe; (3) if not, check whether a protected counter-Hacker or fast attacker can eliminate the **relevant** Hacker or missile REM; (4) if already Targeted, evaluate **Reset/Engineer before** committing further high-value movement, or contest the incoming Guided legally; (5) only spend Orders on this fight if it **opens a scoring action now**. An opponent may have a Guided-capable **Katyusha/Haetae** or FO-based Targeted source rather than the classic Spotlight + 16pt missile REM, so verify the **actual chassis and targeter**.

#### Multiple Hackers covering one target: the priority changes

- **One common Repeater, several remote Hackers:** shooting the **shared forward Repeater** can be better than killing one Interventor/Jazz/Dartok. Removing **one Hacker does not eliminate the others' Hacking Area**. Re-measure the other Repeaters and the Hackers' own zones before moving in.
- **Several overlapping Repeaters and Hackers:** one node kill is rarely a complete answer. Identify the **smallest set of nodes** that exposes a useful corridor, or use a distinct non-repeater approach. Force the enemy to spend Orders replacing destroyed Pitchers/Repeaters instead of spending your own turn Resetting every model.
- **Several eligible Hackers declaring Spotlight AROs:** each **eligible Hacker makes its own ARO** (normally B1 in ARO); do **not** combine them into one Hacker with increased Burst. An active target's **one Reset Roll contests all relevant Comms Attacks in that Order**, but it must beat the opposing rolls where required; each uncancelled Spotlight success still leads to its separate AP/PS5 save. Even a good Reset can lose to one of multiple Hackers. Check ARO eligibility and who has LoF/area rather than assuming every Hacker anywhere can react.
- **Your counter-Hacker enters the enemy network:** attacking a Hacker **through the opponent's Repeater** applies the hostile-Repeater Firewall restrictions; high BTS or TinBot/Fairy Dust may further protect that Hacker (only **one** applicable Firewall). A KHD is not a universally winning exchange. Use your own projected route where possible and do not expose the sole scoring Hacker.
- **Stealth is conditional, not invisibility:** an untargeted trooper using permitted Stealth movement can deny many hacking-area AROs **if no LoF or overriding Sixth Sense/Combat Instinct interaction**; using non-movement skills restores AROs. **Targeted disables Stealth**, and Smoke by itself does **not** shut down a Repeater. A non-Hackable warband avoids many anti-HI programs but **still can be Spotlighted**.

#### Deployment and list-building checks

- **Before deployment:** locate accessible positions for **one expendable Repeater-clearing gun**, a **protected anti-Hacker program** if available, and **two mission-ready scorers**. Keep important 1W Specialists, Lieutenant and support units **out of a single Blast template**; separate Fireteam spacing where legal. Watch for **Turn-0** Moran/native Repeaters and projected **Pitchers/FastPandas** in the first enemy Order.
- **Going second:** a reactive threat may start with an enemy Repeater already covering your valuable pieces. Plan the **first active Order** around removing that node or escaping the projected area, instead of activating a TAG and accepting the whole cyber fork. If already Targeted before your turn, consider the Order cost and likelihood of Reset before moving.
- **Going first:** do not assume the enemy needs to lay a Pitcher to begin; forward/native Repeaters can already cover the central route. **Stealth/hidden approach and cheap node cleanup** are most valuable *before* the important trooper enters the network; once Targeted, hiding behind a wall does not erase the condition.
- **During enemy active turn:** if a missile is declared at your Targeted unit, choose **Reset versus Dodge deliberately**; there is no free Reactive-Turn Reset merely because the opponent threatens to attack someday. Protect **other models within the circular template**, not just the primary target. If an Engineer is nearby, it can clear Targeted **on your later active turn** with its specified WIP action; it does not replace the target's reactive defense against a missile.
- **Stop condition:** once the necessary lane or scoring opportunity is open, finish the button/cargo/zone before searching for more Hacker kills. The mirror risk is the attacker burning too many Orders on Spotlight + missiles and losing on objectives, as in #005's narrated Combined choice and #122's one-point button margin.

**Practical decision rule:** **one projected node** → clear node; **one accessible Hacker and redundant nodes** → kill Hacker if safe; **multiple Hackers sharing one node** → clear node first; **already Targeted** → Reset/Engineer or defend against the actual Guided attack; **exposed single Guided REM** → kill REM if cheaper than prolonged Reset; **dense protected network** → use a protected counter-hacker/physical flank **or** score away from it. Recheck remaining Hacking Areas after every kill. None of those actions alone removes all components of the chain.

### Playbook G — Fighting when normal Line of Fire is blocked

**Question:** Normal shooting is obstructed or the opponent operates behind **Smoke + MSV**. When should you use another MSV, **Eclipse/Disco**, **White Noise**, **templates/mines**, **Sensor**, indirect attacks or a different approach? **First identify *why* LoF is unavailable**. A concrete wall, Zero Visibility, White Noise, an unrevealed Camo Marker and a strong Mimetism/Partial Cover stack are **five different problems**. An expensive MSV gun is not a universal answer.

#### Fast decision matrix: distinguish the obstruction

| What stops your attack? | Highest-value first test | What **does not** solve it / key exception |
|---|---|---|
| **Actual scenery/Total Cover, blind corner, wrong facing, elevated or back angle** | **Reposition** (slice the angle, climb/jump/flank/Parachutist) or attack via a *legal* no-LoF option (Speculative, Guided *if Targeted*, applicable hacking); otherwise take another objective route | **MSV and Sensor cannot see or shoot through solid walls.** Direct templates do **not** wrap around Total Cover. A grenade still needs a legal trajectory and range |
| **Ordinary Smoke / Zero Visibility Zone** | **MSV2/3** gun if it has a safe real firing angle; **MSV1** may draw LoF but suffers **−6** through Zero Visibility; alternatively use Eclipse/Disco for crossing, appropriate non-LoF attacks or flank outside the template | A non-MSV long gun cannot simply declare ordinary proactive sight through Smoke. Smoke does not block hacking that otherwise has a legal Hacking Area; enemy **MSV** can shoot through normal Smoke |
| **Eclipse / Disco Baller's Eclipse Zone** | **Go around, wait for expiration, remove the source where possible, pressure exits with legal mines/templates**, or employ an attack **not requiring LoF** when its requirements are satisfied | **No level of MSV sees through Eclipse.** Sixth Sense is a defensive response rule, not general permission to fire proactively through Eclipse. Disco activates after its placement Order, not retroactively |
| **White Noise Zone** | Attack with a gun **without MSV or Marksmanship** from an eligible line, go around the White Noise, or attack its **Hacker/network enabler**; use a legal non-LoF attack where appropriate | White Noise selectively interferes with **MSV/Marksmanship**, not every gun. **Do not** commit your own MSV specialist into your own White Noise and assume it retains an ordinary clear view |
| **Camo/Hidden Deployment or suspected mine behind a corner** | **Sensor WIP+6 within ZoC** (no LoF needed), or **Discover** with a legal LoF; use a cheap probe and preserve the scorer; MSV3 has special active-turn anti-Camo rules | **MSV1/2 does not automatically Discover Camo or detect Hidden Deployment.** Sensor reveals threats; it does **not** let a gun shoot through the wall afterward. A Camo Marker is not automatically a valid main target for a normal BS Attack |
| **Visible Mimetism/Partial Cover/poor gun odds**, not absent LoF | Appropriate **MSV level**, advantageous range/angle, higher-Burst direct gun, or **legal** direct/impact-template mode | MSV reduces **Mimetism/Visibility** modifiers as printed, not all range/cover/fireteam penalties. A template is not guaranteed to hit around scenery, nor is the relevant weapon necessarily Intuitive/Speculative |
| **Smoke + enemy MSV shooter** | **Eclipse/Disco first**, **White Noise + a non-MSV gun**, counter-MSV from a viable angle, or **flank/attack the smoke provider and score elsewhere** | Throwing **more ordinary Smoke** into a visor's line is **not** a counter; your non-MSV gun may be worse off while the enemy MSV2/3 retains clear sight through Smoke |

**Rules detail that changes the gunfight:** Under local [Visibility Conditions](../../rules/7_Terrain%2C%20Scenery%2C%20Triumph%2C%20Table%2C%20Scenarios%2C%20ITS/Visibility_Conditions.md), a trooper targeted by a BS Attack **through Zero Visibility** can draw reactive LoF to its attacker by treating the Zone as **Poor Visibility**, normally incurring **−6**. This is *not* an unconditional “the non-MSV defender can never return fire.” The actual ARO/LoF conditions still matter. **Sixth Sense** can remove that visibility penalty *when its user is the target of a BS Attack*, but does not give an ordinary proactive Smoke-through sightline. By contrast, MSV1 shoots through Smoke at **−6** and MSV2/3 ignore the Smoke visibility MOD, but ordinary **Eclipse affects every MSV level**. Do not borrow the N4 / early-N5 visor interaction from a video without checking current rules.

#### 1. Countering an opponent's Smoke + MSV gun

**Typical threat:** A **Varangian + Epsilon MSV2**, **Jaguar/Morlock + Intruder**, **Shaolin/Yojimbo + Rui Shi**, **Dāturazi + Yaogat**, or comparable pairing uses an inexpensive smoke source to change the sightline so its visor shoots at far better odds than a normal defender. The MSV shooter and the objective runner are sometimes **different models**.

1. **Verify the firing geometry.** Is the LoF passing *through ordinary Smoke*, **not** Eclipse/Disco, White Noise or actual scenery? What is the enemy's *actual* MSV level, printed weapon range, Mimetism, link bonus and its active Burst? The Smoke source may need to spend a preliminary Order to place the template; it can fail or be countered on placement.
2. **If the same lane must be held, use an anti-MSV screen.** **Eclipse or a successfully placed Disco Ball** blocks the enemy visor; place the screen so both the attacker and scoring route are protected. A Disco effect begins on **Conclusion after its successful placement**, so do not treat it as protection against ARO shots generated in the Order that places it. **White Noise** can defeat the visor's proactive viewing through that zone, but your follow-up **non-MSV / non-Marksmanship attacker** must still have a legal view and range.
3. **If you can contest it directly, use the right countergun.** Your own **MSV2/3** (or suitable MSV1 at acceptable −6 conditions) can work, but once *both* sides see through Smoke there is **no automatic one-way advantage**. Change the weapon's range, Burst and angle; avoid feeding a non-MSV ARO a −6 reciprocal shot when a different defender can force a good Face-to-Face.
4. **Attack the package's geometry and enabling pieces.** A forward gun/airborne flank that sees the smoke thrower or visor **outside** the cloud can remove a cheap enabler or make its next active turn dangerous. Safe pieces can threaten the *next* Smoke placement area with mines/templates **where legal**, or cover the **exit** from the zone. Do not assume an ordinary mine triggers through Smoke/Total Cover automatically: measure its actual Trigger Area and scenery/visibility restrictions.
5. **Ignore the visor lane when the mission allows it.** Use **Eclipse, flank, hidden deployment or an independent Specialist** to reach the button, carrier or scoring zone. The objective is to make the strong firing lane irrelevant, not necessarily to kill the visor.

**Illustrated outcomes:** [#041](041-050-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) **Varangian Smoke → Epsilon MSV2** attacked PanO Tikbalang (observed), but PanO **Trinitarian/Bulleteer movement** still threatened the actual room; [#117](111-120-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) **Yojimbo Smoke → Rui Shi MSV2** bypassed an ordinary Total-Reaction gun, but Dahshat's broader turret/mine shell still won **6–0** (initial N5); [#021](021-030-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) explicitly recommends **Jaguar Smoke + Intruder** or **Jazz White Noise + non-visor gun** in N5.3, but that recommendation is **player assessment**, not a verified repeated successful shot sequence. [#050](041-050-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) demonstrates **McMurrough/Jaguar Smoke + Wildcat MSV1/Intruder** forcing a route choice; Corregidor–WinterFor ended **2–2**, not a clean victory for either vision system.

#### 2. Templates, grenades and minefields when nobody can take a normal shot

- **Direct templates:** chain rifles, shotguns' appropriate modes, flamethrowers, pulzars and comparable weapons can punish an **exposed model or clustered fireteam without needing a BS hit roll**. But [Direct Template](../../rules/3_Combat/Direct_Template_Weapons.md) still requires **valid template placement and Area of Effect**; scenery/Total Cover block effects, and the template may not curl around a solid corner. Their **Normal-roll trade** can be excellent when a cheap warband sacrifices itself for two expensive troopers. They do **not** automatically strike a protected Camo Marker.
- **Intuitive Attack:** for a **weapon with the actual Intuitive Attack Trait**, this is a **Long Skill, B1, unmodified WIP** alternative against eligible targets not visible because of a Zero Visibility Zone, or a Marker State that ordinarily blocks BS attacks. A failed WIP roll restricts repeating against that same target until the next Active Turn. This is **not** a blanket property of all templates. See [Intuitive Attack](../../rules/3_Combat/Intuitive_Attack.md).
- **Speculative Attack:** grenades or a weapon *with the printed trait* can threaten unseen models from a **legal trajectory**, usually **−6 plus Range MOD**, B1, ignoring visibility and ordinary negative Mimetism/cover modifiers. **Total Cover from a ceiling/solid enclosure can still make trajectory invalid**. Compare this with moving around one corner; several weak speculative attempts can consume the whole objective budget. [Speculative Attack](../../rules/3_Combat/Speculative_Attack.md).
- **Mines:** best as **pre-positioned denial of the approach or exit**, especially against an enemy who must leave Smoke/Eclipse to score. Mine Trigger Areas exclude positions in **Total Cover** from the template's Blast Focus, and they are **deployable template weapons with −3 Dodge**. A cheap sacrificial REM/warband or legal Deactivator/discovery may be more efficient than risking the carrier. A Mine is not a magic omnidirectional attack through intervening walls. [Mines](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/Mines.md).
- **Blast spacing:** spread models, scoring supports and Fireteam members so one missile/HRL template cannot catch a vulnerable second body. Re-evaluate **scenery, blast focus and live AROs**; even an Unconscious model can be a consequential template casualty.

**Observed direct counterplay:** [#059](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) USAriadna linked Missile Launcher/templates punished exposed **Bakunin Riot Grrl clustering**; Bakunin **conceded** rather than play a completed three-turn result. [#212](211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) Onyx deliberately **sacrificed a cheap E-Drone to trigger an enemy mine** so Kurnau could perform Countermeasures tasks (**6–0**); the sacrificial clearance, not “strong MSV,” created access. [#163](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) Bakunin surrounded a **Provisioning** box with **mines, Suppression and CC interceptors** after extracting the first with **Chimera Eclipse**; killing other Bakunin models still did not win the boxes. [#232](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) **N5.3** Hassassin used expendable **Daylami** to clear Shasvastii mines, with **McMurrough Smoke + D-Charge Haris** completing Double Bind sabotage; **no formal OP winner was verified**. Do not treat one exceptional template hit or mine-clearing trade as universal reliability.

#### 3. Sensor and MSV: information versus actual shooting

- **Sensor** declares a **WIP+6 Normal Roll** to simultaneously Discover eligible **Camouflaged or Hidden Deployment** threats inside the user's **Zone of Control**, **without LoF**; it also grants **+6 Discover WIP against Camo Markers** when ordinary Discover is legal. It is excellent **around a blocked corner/room or dense midfield** when exposing the marker lets the next model enter or score. It does **not** reveal every marker type, guarantee discovery, negate Smoke for a later BS Attack or bypass physical Cover.
- **MSV1** reduces Mimetism −3 / Low Visibility to 0, Mimetism −6 / Poor Visibility to −3, and can shoot through normal Smoke with **−6**. **MSV2** ignores Mimetism and the usual visibility MOD through normal Smoke; **MSV3** also has **automatic-success Discover vs Camo** and a specific **active-turn BS Attack on Camo Markers in LoF**. No MSV passes through solid scenery or Eclipse. Use [MSV](../../rules/5c_Equipment/Multispectral_Visor.md) / [Sensor](../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Sensor.md), not the historical spoken “Sensor/visor” shorthand.
- **Sensor and gun should be separate when efficient.** A sensor REM can discover a Hidden infiltrator while a different gun or mission worker capitalizes. Against an unrevealed marker irrelevant to this turn's objective, often **bypass instead of paying the scan plus attack Orders**.

**Observed:** In [#155](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), a **Starmada M-Drone Sensor detected a Hidden Anathematic** in Hardlock (tournament retrospective), directly challenging an expensive hidden plan. [#158](151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) describes **Kestrel Redeye Sensor uncovering Camo**, with **Black A.I.R. K1** available for subsequent Caledonian threat pressure (winner retrospective, not an exact casualty ledger). [#230](221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) shows **Kosmoflot's MSV1 Kosmosoldat** was an underappreciated counter to **Nomad Mimetism**; the Uplink Center match nevertheless finished **3–3**. [#208](201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) describes **Asura MSV3 attacking a Camo mine directly**, but that final contains a **serious unresolved Order-count controversy**: cite only the observed capability and verify its N5.2 versus N5.3 rules, not the match as clean proof.

#### 4. When crossing is more valuable than shooting

1. **Stage the screen before the carrier moves**; choose **ordinary Smoke against non-visors**, **Eclipse/Disco against visors**, and **White Noise against MSV/Marksmanship** only if the planned actor/attack can exploit it. Smoke is not protection against ordinary **hacking** from a legal Hacking Area.
2. **Expose one ARO at a time** via a new angle/elevation/Parachutist approach instead of walking the valuable Specialist into several crossed firelanes. Sometimes a **fast scoring body** costs fewer Orders than eliminating an MSV sniper.
3. **Clear only the necessary mines/Camo** with Sensor, Discover, expendable trigger or legal Intuitive Attack; do **not** automatically clear every marker.
4. **Score immediately and retreat if the mission demands extraction.** The fog may expire; the enemy can bring a new visor/angle next turn. Count **smoke placement + approach + objective action + extraction** together, not just the first shooting Order.
5. **Leave a second body that can actually score.** A superb Smoke/MSV kill sequence is a failure if the last Order cannot move the box, flip the button or hold the required zone.

**Observed positive and negative comparisons:** [#105](101-110-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) **Tankō Disco Baller protected Musashi's TAG-killing CC approach**, contributing to an **Ōban 7–2 Supremacy** win (early N5 / ITS16.5 historical rules, not N5.3 profile proof). [#163](161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) **Chimera/Pupnik Eclipse** carried and extracted a Provisioning box in a tournament winner's account; [#236](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) **Tankō Disco/Smoke + Shikami** enabled an Ōban beacon approach in B-Pong, but the same Ōban player later **lost 8–0 in Cutthroat to a Zeta + MSV2 sniper on an open table**. [#022](021-030-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) is the **failed sequence**: Military Orders spent Orders fighting instead of **placing Konstantinos' available Disco protection first**, leaving the Corporate Appropriation prototype vulnerable; the player lost **0–7**. [#120](111-120-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) Bakunin learned to prioritize **Smoke + box evacuation over repeated attacks into Hawkwood**, but a **voluntarily agreed replay** makes the final Supplies victory non-clean tournament evidence. [#053](051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) Nomads **Jazz White Noise/hacking** altered the Torchlight HI/visor engagements and won Frontline **7–4**, but this is a combined infowar/vision/scoring mechanism, not White Noise alone winning.

#### Fails, counter-counterplay and confidence

| Tempting shortcut | Why it fails | Observation / status |
|---|---|---|
| **“MSV sees through anything.”** | Doesn't beat **walls, Eclipse, White Noise**, and MSV1 remains −6 into regular Smoke; MSV2/3 have specific rather than universal rights | **Rules**, plus #236 open-table MSV2 denial and #105 Disco delivery |
| **“Smoke always makes my carrier safe.”** | Enemy **MSV** may see through it; hacking does not normally require LoF; smoke may fail to deploy or fade before exit | **Rules**; #041 offensive Smoke/MSV, #022 missed protection |
| **“Smoke + MSV guarantees a free kill.”** | Defender may get legal reciprocal fire through Zero Visibility at the **Poor Visibility penalty**, and **Sixth Sense** may protect its defensive roll; gun range, Cover and missions still matter | **Rules**; #117 bypassed TR but **lost** the wider game; #050 **tied** |
| **“My flame/template reaches around this wall.”** | Template Area of Effect is stopped by scenery; target/placement and any Intuitive trait requirements still apply | **Rules**; #059 is exposed-cluster punishment, not wall penetration |
| **“Sensor is a free kill or a giant-table scanner.”** | Zone of Control, a **WIP roll**, target-State restrictions and follow-up Orders apply; it discovers but does not shoot | **Rules**; #155 / #158 show reveal as a *component* |

They do **not** provide a controlled sample quantifying which counter produces the highest win rate. #041's full roster/score attribution, #105's historical loadouts, #208's Order dispute, #120's replay, #022's poor-audio retrospective and #236's approximate individual event scores must remain qualified. **Do not double-count** the identical #022/#026 or #011/#030 recordings.

### Playbook H — Countering fast melee attackers: Achilles, McMurrough, Cameronians, Shona, Liang Kai and Dog-Warriors

**Question:** How do you keep a fast, durable close-combat threat from reaching your Lieutenant, Hacker, Engineer, premium gun or mission object? **Answer:** defend the **route and timing**, not merely the last model. The strongest cross-report pattern is **several *different* defensive decisions in sequence**: an expendable first contact, a legal mine/template or counter-hacking fork, a covered counterattack, and a separate mission-preserving unit. A single sniper, or one tightly packed mine cluster, is often a solvable problem.

**Do not classify all six units as one profile.** Achilles in the played Next Wave/Combined lists can be pressured through a legal **hacking network**, while a **Dog-Warrior/Cameronian/McMurrough** approach often must be solved with **physical range/positioning, templates, mines, and actual weapon effects** rather than assuming a Hackable target. **Shona and Liang Kai** bring compact, high-quality melee with mobile delivery; **Super-Jump** or Smoke support can invalidate a screen that guards only a ground-level corridor. Verify the **current N5.3 profile, states, actual Smoke/Stealth/Impetuous/Berserk and available weapons** separately: historical appearances and sectorial eligibility are not interchangeable.

#### Threat-specific counter selection

| Enemy rush | First defensive test | Secondary defense if it breaches | Specific failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Achilles** (often with Pandora/Patroclus or Smoke) | Can a **live Hacker/Repeater/Cybermine** legally threaten this played profile on its *chosen route*, or can an angled gun force it off Suppression? | Covered independent gun, attack that changes state, protected Specialist and another objective path | Letting it kill the first guard **and** the only Hacker/terminal worker in the same sweep. **#197**'s Interventor/Cybermine/Kulak web bricked Achilles and bought a **7–3 Tunguska** win; **#158**'s Banshee Hacker forced the suppression decision before Redeye firing. Both are *specific* network geometries |
| **McMurrough** (Smoke, fast CC and close templates in played lists) | A **safe long-angle ARO before Smoke**, **MSV against ordinary Smoke**, and **spaced** cheap guards whose deaths do not expose the next guard | Separate **mine/perimeter or direct-template** threats near *different* exits; a surviving countergun or sacrificial interceptor | Clustered supports: **#091** McMurrough's double Chain Rifles punished **StarCo grouped around a room**, even though he was eventually removed; **#206** a badly chosen early McMurrough ARO lost the assault piece to a sniper |
| **Cameronians / Dog-Warriors** | Can they cross in **one unbroken movement/smoke line**, and where will a large silhouette be exposed to a real gun when Smoke ends? | Stagger **guns, mines and cheap bodies** at physically separate gates; cover the room from another angle; preserve expensive targets beyond the second gate | Trying to fight the whole rush at **chain-rifle/template distance** with several Order generators together. **#237** Dog-Warriors removed Knights/Trinitarians and advanced Provisioning boxes, yet a **Seraph counterattack** punished a thin post-assault screen; the event's Hardlock round has a separate acknowledged rules error |
| **Shona Carano** (often supported by Smoke and a gun team) | Are **server/mission objects** guarded by actual legal **physical interception routes**, rather than only a far-off HMG or Hacker? | Space vulnerable specialists, make Smoke delivery costly, screen **vertical approaches** and hold a second counterattack against the scoring route | Letting the delivery attacker touch *all* mission objects: **#216** Starmada Shona crossed and destroyed **three Mindwipe servers** (~nine Orders) for **10–1**; Ramah's Tarik was stopped **in CC by her Varangian partner**, not by an abstract guaranteed anti-Shona melee rule |
| **Liang Kai** (fast Super-Jump / close assassination) | Do roof, balcony, HVT and back edges have actual LoF/mine coverage **from the direction he can land**, not only the front door? | Make a jump end where a second gun/template/guard can retaliate; use a protected Specialist or HVT placement beyond the accessible jump | Assuming a **wall** or elevated target is safe: **#069** Liang Kai's Super-Jump → CC reached an HVT for a **custom** assassination score; it is evidence of an access vector, not standard ITS scoring |
| **Other fast warbands / Berserk jumpers / CC escorts** | Keep your valuable objective **behind an expendable first target and a second line**, not merely within a single 8-inch bubble | **Hidden/Minelayer, disposable short-range ARO, Suppressive Fire only if line survives**, or a counter-CC model with real legal contact | A disposable threat can still force an unequal exchange: **#173** a Hassassin winner **sacrificed an Asawira** to stop a cheap Beast Hunter. Your defense should not require a 40+ point piece to trade for every rushing warband |

**Classification notes:** McMurrough and Cameronians share *strategic* roles but are not a single universally interchangeable rules profile. A Dog-Warrior that changes form/silhouette, a large-base model climbing, a Stealth trooper and a Berserk attacker demand **different exact ARO/geometry checks**. Do not import a range, a height or a particular Smoke weapon from the name alone.

#### The four defensive belts that repeatedly matter

1. **Outside gate: choose a first warning / tax, not the most valuable unit.** Place **Camo/Hidden, a cheap flash/short-range ARO, a visible inexpensive defender or a real long-range weapon** where the enemy must either reveal a direction, spend Smoke, choose a slower route or attack. Cover *its next stopping point*, not only its starting point. **#011** Varuna Helot/Knights/visor layers forced **Steel Phalanx Myrmidon/Hippolyta** pressure to use careful approaches; **#164** Haqq **Mutt/Jammer + Daylami** screens weathered a Combined **Achilles + Anathematic** opening in a tournament winner's account. Neither proves an automatic melee kill.
2. **Midboard gate: mix **different answers**, not five identical AROs.** A **mine that checks a doorway**, a **gun that watches the space beyond that mine** and a **second legal short-range response** impose competing decisions. **#173**'s **Shujae E/M Hidden Minelayer + Ghazi Jammer** strategy survived five games going second (retrospective); **#204**'s **Techjack mines + reloading + isolation/template room network** stopped the late Achilles/Myrmidon room run. Unlike conventional shotguns, a **mine can trigger even against some Stealth movements**, subject to *actual trigger geometry and scenery*. Do **not** assume its template crosses Total Cover.
3. **Inner gate: protect the *valuable model*, not an attractive cluster of guards.** Keep the **Lieutenant/only Hacker/Engineer/Doctor, mission carrier and Order generators** out of a single **Chain Rifle/Flamethrower/blast** footprint and outside an immediately accessible follow-up attack angle. Set cheap screen positions so killing one **doesn't** reveal a second premium target or allow a **two-for-one template**. **#091** is the directly observed failure: McMurrough's template attack punished a grouped StarCo defense near the Corporate objective. **#189** is a distinct N5.3 example of **Rasail/Chaksa/mines/direct templates** closing an objective corridor against Shasvastii, 4–1; its opponent also wasted Orders trying to solve a TR gun.
4. **Second-turn gate: preserve a counterpunch and *someone who can score*.** The rushing piece may kill two guards and still fail strategically if a separate **engineer/specialist, secondary gun, scoring REM or hidden counterattacker** controls the needed object. **#092** Varuna absorbed Steel Phalanx Myrmidon pressure and **won 6–3** with Cutter/recovery plus a final REM zone move. **#159** Shasvastii took other Superiority zones while **Achilles/Pandora/Patroclus** spent Orders on a Decoy. **#164** defended against an elite CA push without needing to eliminate every attacker.

**Placement rule of thumb (not a universal distance):** Place **first mine/guard, second template/gun and vital support at *different* route stages** so one Dodge, one expendable mine-sweeper, one Smoke template or one Direct Template cannot neutralize all three. **Measure the actual Small Teardrop/Circular Blast footprint**, legal mine Trigger Area, Silhouette, elevation, firing arcs, 8-inch ZoC and Move+Berserk reach on your table. A fixed “space everyone 4 inches apart” formula is not supported by these reports and can be defeated by a different attacker/template or by terrain.

#### Choose the correct ARO when the attacker enters

| Attacker's decision | Response to consider | Risk / exact rule check |
|---|---|---|
| **Crossing open ground before Smoke is up** | **One credible BS/Flash firelane** plus a **second responder** covering the next position | Strong active Burst and Smoke setup can beat the first ARO; MSV counters **ordinary Smoke** only according to level, not Eclipse/Disco |
| **Moving through smoke / behind corner** | Mine/deployable on a **legal route**, **Intuitive Attack** only with a valid printed trait, a covered exit, or a **legal** no-LoF response | Smoke may stop ordinary proactive LoF but **does not make mines or templates magically pass walls**. Re-read [Playbook G](#playbook-g--fighting-when-normal-line-of-fire-is-blocked) |
| **Taking the first cheap body into CC** | Accept the cheap trade if it forces a **new Order and exposed final position**; use legal **Dodge away or into contact** when the defender's PH and geometry justify it | **Dodge** normally moves **up to 2 inches after a successful roll** and does not trigger deployables; it can also **enter Engaged** if legal. Do not assume Dodge always beats a high-CC attacker or creates a free ARO from another model |
| **Berserk into a valuable target** | Place a **cheap bait/model** outside the inner perimeter, deny the necessary **LoF + reachable Silhouette contact**, or arrange a **template/side gun** that can trade or retaliate | Under [Berserk](../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Berserk.md), the attacker declares **its entire movement path and target before AROs**; if contact is unreachable it **Idles**. The attack is **not** an unlimited teleport, and the *exact* opposing ARO/Face-to-Face depends on legal skills |
| **Using Stealth to reach a rear arc** | Require the path to cross actual **LoF**, force a **non-Stealth skill**, use **Sixth Sense/Combat Instinct** where present, or deploy a legal mine | [Stealth](../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Stealth.md) suppresses many **ZoC-only movement AROs**, but **not** deployables, or users of **Sixth Sense/Combat Instinct**. A guard placed behind a wall that has *no LoF* may fail to react until contact |
| **Elite or very durable attacker accepts a template hit** | Combine **the template with a different gate**: another ranged hit, a status effect where legally applicable, a later mine, or a reserve counterattack | **Direct templates do not roll to hit** but an attacker may choose to trade damage and keep going; **Dogged/No Wound Incapacitation/multiple wounds** can preserve a threat after one successful hit. Check profile/state before expecting an instant stop |
| **Hacker enters attack range against Achilles** | Maintain legal Hacker/Repeater/Cybermine coverage with an actual relevant program and a **different finishing gun** | A **KHD alone** may not possess the program you need; Repeater/Hacking Area, opponent BTS/Firewall and status/Reset rules determine success; anti-CC **Hacking** is profile-dependent, not usable against every wolf or Martial Artist |

**Rules that alter the decision:** [Mines](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/Mines.md) trigger only with valid **Trigger Area/Blast Focus**, never through Total Cover, and mine Dodge suffers **−3**; an overlapping single doorway can be cleared by one sacrificial model, so serial, nonidentical layers often produce more delay. [Place Deployable](../../rules/5a_Common%20Skills/Place_Deployable.md) resolves placement at **Conclusion**, so a mine placed as an ARO **does not trigger in that same Order**. [Direct Template Weapons](../../rules/3_Combat/Direct_Template_Weapons.md) don't require a BS hit roll, but need proper template placement and don't wrap scenery; if the target shoots/melees back into a direct template, consider the **Normal-roll trade**. [Dodge](../../rules/5a_Common%20Skills/Dodge.md) usually lets a defender roll against attacks and choose up to 2-inch movement only on a successful resolution; multiple direct templates/mine effects complicate this. Check **Fireteam ARO legality, actual models and current packet**, not unofficial battle-era table interpretations.

| **#164 / #159 — Haqq / Shas tournament recaps** | Daylami/Jammer screens **weathered Achilles + Anathematic**; a separate **Shas Decoy diverted Achilles group** while the player scored elsewhere | **Reported survival and bypass**, not precise direct melee kills |

**What *doesn't* work consistently:** (1) a single sniper as all your defense when **Smoke/Disco/vertical mobility** can bypass it; (2) packing **multiple supports** behind one screen when **McMurrough/chain templates** punish the whole cluster; (3) all the mines at the same entrance with no other ARO—**one cheap mine-sweeper** can remove them; (4) expecting a **legal hacker** to automatically stop a non-Hackable wolf; (5) assigning your **only mission Specialist** to be the mandatory first CC trade; (6) spending every active Order chasing one premium melee model when its teammate is scoring a distant zone.

**Order and mission test:** Trace the attacker's **start → Smoke/Disco setup → first contact → mine/template → CC/Berserk distance → valuable target → final scoring action**. If the attacker can clear all gates in a few Orders **while your remaining scoring piece becomes exposed**, the deployment is unsafe. If each gate imposes a different response and your second Specialist still scores, your counter is useful **even when the melee attacker survives**.

**Confidence:** Direct observations support **hacking/status against Achilles** (#197), **missed/objective-door defenses** (#216), **mine/template room protection** (#204), **packing failure against McMurrough** (#091), **and last-chance mission-object protection** (#223). Claims of exact mine-kill probabilities, a “minimum inches” screen against every Berserk movement, and assured Shona/Liang Kai counters **are not supported** by these reports. Distinct tournament recaps and historical patches are noted rather than pooled into a synthetic win rate. ### Recommended counterplay evaluation worksheet

For each opponent list or played game, record the following *separately* so a striking kill does not inflate a supposed counter matchup:

| Field | Example of a useful entry |
|---|---|
| Enemy threat and supporting tools | Cutter + Helots + Pathfinder, or Hollow Men + Heckler + Interventor |
| First vulnerable link | Exposed Pitcher, lone Hacker, cargo courier, mine on mandatory console path |
| Initial counter plan / backup | Clear Pitcher then fire Hetkari / use protected Fennec or alternate flank |
| Actual observed action | Disabled with E/M, forced Reset, killed backline, forced Discover, safely bypassed |
| Counter-counterplay / failure | Firewall/BTS stopped KHD; Engineer repaired TAG; Hidden marker still scored zone |
| Orders spent and mission conversion | Approximate only when report states it; did Specialist press button, carry box or hold zone? |
| Evidence and caveats | Numbered dossier, O/R/I, version, modified mission, duplicate tournament, disputed rules |
| Forward applicability | Which **current legal profile and Fireteam** actually replicates the job? |

**Practical conclusion:** Prefer **two independent ways of finishing the mission** and choose the *lowest-risk counter that opens those ways*. “Countered the TAG” means little if the target survives to score the zone; “ignored the sniper” is excellent if a Specialist completes the action. Each playbook is a conditional decision guide, not a ranking of PanO versus Nomads or Ariadna versus Combined.

## List-building and turn-by-turn counter checklist

1. **Identify** the *real obstacle*: armor and wounds, hackability/state, visor/line of fire, hidden marker, impersonator path, or opponent's **mission action**. A “TAG problem” often is a **scoring-geometry problem**.
2. **Pick one safe primary and one fallback mechanism**: e.g., E/M + K1/anti-materiel versus TAG; Pitcher + nonhackable flanker versus HI; clear Repeater + protected KHD against network; Sensor + sacrificial mine-clearing versus Camo; mines + CoC/spacing versus impersonator; Disco/Smoke + lateral scorer versus ARO Fireteam.
3. **Protect infrastructure**: own Hacker/Engineer, TinBot carrier, LT/CoC, and any delivery Specialist. Expect the opponent to hunt exactly those components.
4. **Count the complete Order line**: deploy/clear smoke or Pitcher, target/reset/hit, eliminate/displace obstacle, move specialist, roll WIP/plant D-Charge, move to safe scoring zone and survive. Avoid solving an obstacle with more Orders than the objective is worth.
5. **Check mission and legality**: an anti-TAG tool is not automatically an objective action; a Fireteam composition recorded in N5.1 may not exist in N5.3; an impersonator is not ordinary Camo; a Compulsory/Team Ops rule may change relevance.
6. **Record outcome honestly**: **removed**, **immobilized**, **isolated**, **Forced Reset**, **Order tax**, **outscored** or **failed**. Never classify “never activated but forced caution” as zero impact or classify “killed many, lost mission” as strategic success.
