## #231 — Shasvastii vs Torchlight Brigade, ITS17 Battleground (Torchlight 10–0)

[Primary London Infinity Tabletop video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=80Cxu4nfTz8) · 300pts · *Battleground*, three bands worth **1 / 3 / 4** by distance into enemy territory and additional **Kios** target interaction.

- **Observed Shasvastii:** **Sphinx**, **Caliban Vulcan Shotgun**, **Nox Hacker**, **Taigha**, **Seed Soldier** treasure hunter, **Ikadrons**, **Flash Pulse REMs**, **E-Drone EVO**, **Total Reaction HMG and plasma REMs**, **Med-Tech Obsidon / Doctor Worm and helper**. A sizeable investment in deployable/reactive REM fire and expensive stealth offense; Shasvastii's army struggled to get appropriate damage from its first-turn Sphinx push. The players say the Sphinx's assault **killed ~four things but cost nine or ten Orders**, rather than producing a decisive Order economy swing.
- **Observed Torchlight:** **Jackboot HMG NCO** in an **Hellblazer (Flammenspeer) + Wave Rider Hacker (Oblivion + extra Burst) + Cludger Engineer/Panzerfaust/Disco** SilverStar-style fireteam; **Vidoc MSV1 Sniper**, **two Moonrakers**, **Raveneye Lieutenant with two Lt Orders**, **TR HMG REM, EVO Fuzzbot, Kitta Flash REM, Beast Hunter, Varangian/Warhorse support**, plus **Jamie Arantes Parachutist with Plasma** and a **Ment agent** screen.
- **Deciding sequence:** Shasvastii's early assault inflicted casualties but failed to remove the critical **Torchlight Order and surviving long-lane structure**. On Torchlight's turn, **Moonraker** flanked a reactive defender; **Jamie Arantes parachuted into Shasvastii's rear**, threw down her **armed turret**, and took plasma/suppressive shots into **Med-Tech/EVO**. Removing supporting REMs and controlling the **farthest Battleground band** caused Shasvastii **Loss of Lieutenant and Retreat!**, ending the game; the players directly announce **Torchlight 10–0**.
- **Player assessment:** Shasvastii player considers **Sphinx** useful but complains that old Shasvastii units feel flimsy compared with more modern high-Burst/multi-Wound attackers. A separate suggested idea about granting Regeneration is a **house-balance wish**, not an existing rule; never treat it as legal N5.
- **Inference — matchup and mission:** **Torchlight Jamie Arantes Parachutist + Moonraker flanking + NCO/fireteam long guns** attack exactly the Shasvastii vulnerability here: a **fragile backline supporting a costly Sphinx**. **Shasvastii** needs its **Nox/Doctor Worm/EVO/REM backfield protected from edge-arrival plasma**, and should budget retreat-safe Orders for scoring the **far bands** even if it fails to cripple Torchlight. A TAG's initial four kills do not automatically beat **1/3/4 board-distance scoring**.
- **Limit:** This is a lopsided single game with extreme dice and a truncated end, not evidence that Torchlight wins every Shasvastii matchup.

**Mission tactic:** For Battleground, **score and physically occupy the farthest zone** with surviving models after removing the enemy's last useful Order pool. Parachutists landing behind reactive REMs can outperform a frontal TAG exchange.

## #232 — Hassassin Bahram vs Shasvastii, N5.3 ITS18 Double Bind (asymmetric paths)

[Primary London Infinity Tabletop video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umIWd67hh1k) · 300pts · **Double Bind**. The players explicitly say **ITS18 had just launched**. **Outcome:** Hassassin executed its destruction plan successfully while Shasvastii achieved part of its encryption plan. **Do not assign a formal numeric winner/OP** from this transcript: the postgame discussion compares difficulty rather than announcing a ledger.

- **Critical mission mechanic:** Player keeping **Deployment** chose **Sabotage** (destroy up to one antenna each turn using **D-Charges / anti-materiel attacks**, worth 2 per destroy); player keeping **Initiative** chose **Encryption** (hacker/specialist antenna interactions scored per round). The third available route was **Secure Vector**, controlling circles around antennas. Each chosen objective path may be scored independently: don't compare just who holds one central button or assume both play the same condition.
- **Observed Hassassin package:** **Farzan specialist**, **McMurrough** (fast smoke and anti-materiel close assault), **Muyib Spitfire** with **Yara Haddad + Leila Sharif Haris**, **Nazarine Engineer + Nasmat**, **Shakush pilot/Pandora proxy**, **Ghulam/Daylami (Panzerfaust/minelayers)**, **Lasiq viral rifle**, **EVO and guided missile REM**, **FO REM**. This supports a **smoke-delivered D-Charge attacker** backed by consistent midfield specialists and cheap disposable ARO denial.
- **Observed Shasvastii package:** **Malignos Hacker and BSG/Pulsar with viral mines**, **Shrouded Killer Hacker and minelayer**, **Caliban FO Vulcan Shotgun, Caliban MSV1 Spitfire**, **Mentor Vulcan**, **Seed Soldier Paramedic**, **Speculo Killer**, **Taigha**, **HMG TR REM**, **Flash REMs** and **Guilang/Noctifer-type hidden/Camo threats** (audio often misnames them). Shas set **large clusters of camo markers and mines** around all three consoles and used **repeated hacking and surprise AROs** to impose a high discover/clear cost.
- **Observed combat and objective conversion:** Hassassin used **McMurrough smoke and Muyib/Yara/Leila** gunfights to reach successive antennas; a **Haris unit repeatedly placed D-Charges and destroyed targets**, including an awkward final approach through **Malignos/Shrouded minefield**. Shasvastii's **Malignos/Caliban** late activations could flip a console but were short on Orders, and the players noted that the **Sabotage path felt less order-intensive** in this specific table/matchup. Hassassin used expendable **Daylami** to soak/clear mines so the expensive objective carriers survived.
- **Player assessment:** Both favor exploring **Sabotage** further and note **Secure Vector** may have been comparably workable, but **neither tested Secure Vector in this match**. They specifically argue that **smoke + anti-materiel D-Charges** is hard for Shasvastii to interrupt when its reactive ARO relies on an HMG REM without an MSV2 screen.
- **Inference — counters:** **Hassassin McMurrough + Muyib/Yara/Leila + D-Charge specialists** is promising **Double Bind Sabotage tech** against Shasvastii **Camo/Malignos/Shrouded**, *if* vision control is preserved. **Shasvastii should delay/reveal camo in positions that force a direct ARO on the bomber**; if a cheap Daylami can clear the last mine and the objective is unguarded, a relatively small force finishes a 2-OP destruction action.
- **Reliability:** N5.3 packet confirmed; precise round-by-round OP count not announced. The narrator casually says **Chain of Command** information is private; verify historical official rules rather than deriving universal rules from banter.

**Mission tactic:** Build a roster around the **chosen Double Bind scoring option**, including actual **D-Charges and smoke** for Sabotage or **multiple independent hackers** for Encryption. Winning gunfights is secondary when each antenna you destroy awards fixed OP.

## #233 — Shindenbutai vs Torchlight Brigade, N5.3 ITS18 The Dig (projected Shinden 9–1)

[Primary London Infinity Tabletop video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FvBYsI8A0jY) · 300pt · ITS18 **The Dig**, **radial/quarter deployment**. Players stopped ahead of the final turn and explicitly **talked out an expected Shindenbutai 9–1**; do not log as a fully played 9–1 or a clean opponent table wipe.

- **Mission mechanic:** Three central consoles activate or analyze **hyperthermal markers** placed before deployment by both players. On console interaction the opponent **selects which hidden/placed marker must be reached and neutralized**. The players initially misstated one OP category then corrected it: **most consoles activated**, not most neutralizations in that particular bonus. Two Classifieds and an end-of-round neutralization target increase the value of **widely spread Specialists**.
- **Observed Shinden roster:** **Haramaki Spitfire Lieutenant**, **Shizoku (+1 Burst Heavy Rocket Launcher) + Yamabushi Paramedic + Samurai** assault fireteam, **Yojimbo smoke/bike**, **Tanuki MSV2 Sniper + Hacker + Chain of Command**, **Raiden HRL**, **Keisotsu/Kyorjin Killer with Flammenspeer**, **Saito/Sasha proxy**, and **FO/sensor/flash and guided missile REMs**. Creator and guest call the **Shizoku-linked launcher** the standout gunnery unit, with **Yamabushi Paramedic** present to recover it if wounded.
- **Observed Torchlight roster:** **Raveneye Lt**, **Vidoc Sniper**, **Jackboot HMG NCO**, **Cludger engineer + Wave Rider Hacker (Oblivion)**, **Yellowjacket Hacker**, **Hellblazer**, **EVO Fuzzbot**, **Moonraker Camo Minelayers**, **Ment Agent**, **Beast Hunter**, and a **TR HMG REM**. Torchlight had considerable reactive options but revealed a Moonraker at a poor moment, losing a delaying opportunity.
- **Deciding sequence:** Shinden gained the advantage on the **first activation**, destroying or suppressing **Vidoc's sniper and exposed Torchlight pieces** with **Shizoku fireteam pressure** and **Yojimbo follow-up**. It activated/neutralized multiple Dig targets while Torchlight's attempts to clear Shinden screens suffered very bad dice. A late **Yellowjacket Hacker** advanced for a Classified/marker and was stopped by **hidden reactive pieces/Sasha E/M**, leaving Torchlight with limited scoring routes. Both players agreed the remaining cards and marker access strongly favored Shinden, estimating **9–1**.
- **Player opinion:** Players say **going first** may be unusually strong in The Dig because you can **touch the consoles/force marker selection before the opponent is ready**, although the opponent also has the choice of target markers. The Torchlight player says revealing some **Moonrakers too early** gave Shinden better attack opportunities.
- **Inference — matchup:** **Shinden Shizoku launcher + Yojimbo + Tanuki sniper and mobile Specialists** is very good at *this* radial mission when Torchlight Vidoc/Moonraker threats fall early. **Torchlight must protect an independent mobile hacker/engineer**, preserve hidden minelayers to force Shinden extra Orders, and challenge the **Shizoku assault team's long lane** on safe rangebands. Counterfactual **Yamabushi healing** was *available* but not needed for the success reported.
- **Limit:** Anticipated end, dice, single radial table and one Shizoku gun; not a general verdict that Shinden counters Torchlight across ITS18.

**Mission tactic:** In **The Dig**, a list must contain **several fast independent units** capable of reaching *whichever marker your opponent picks*. Precompute routes from each console to all legal hyperthermal positions.

## #234 — White Banner vs vanilla O-12: early-N5 Uplink Center tutorial (White Banner 7–3)

[Primary DefectiveDice report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SFQ6fnU55YU) · 300pt · simplified/direct-action **Uplink Center**, no Classified deck. Long teaching discussion of N4→N5 and timing. **Final explicitly counted White Banner 7–3 O-12.**

- **Observed White Banner:** **Blue Wolf TAG**, **Shang Ji**, **Octavia with rocket/sniper proxy** in **Zhanshi Hacker/Combi fireteam**, **Long Ya Minelayer/Flammenspeer**, **Mech-Engineer**, **Lun Kai + Shaolin Monks**, **Pangguling baggage** and **Warcor** with other camouflage pieces. **Blue Wolf's resilience** and multiple high-BS shooting angles stood out: the beginner felt unable to reliably finish it through normal gun attrition.
- **Observed O-12:** **Gamma**, **Kytta/Flash bots**, **Varangians**, **Cyberghost HD+**, **Ment Chain of Command**, **Raptor Hacker**, **Sirius Officer with Peripheral/Reapeater**, **Alpha Lt/Strategos**, **Lawkeeper Red Fury**, and **Raveneye**. **O-12 Sirius + Cyberghost hacking** successfully **Total Controlled/Possessed the Blue Wolf** at one point, showing a real anti-TAG line instead of requiring direct HMG exchange.
- **Deciding sequence:** The Blue Wolf player **spent a Command Token to clear Possessed at the start of the following turn**, then used its mobility/armor and supporting melee attackers to **outscore O-12 on the late objectives**. A late **Lun Kai/Monk melee run** violently removed a defender, but White Banner was already ahead on **console activation / control / central Tech-Coffin**. Players recalculated the end result from a provisional 6–4/5–3 into **7–3 White Banner**.
- **Observed anti-TAG lesson:** A **Pitcher/Repeater** can reach a TAG regardless of its armoring. O-12 **Raptor/Cyberghost** hackers imposed real risk; the White Banner TAG suffered action inefficiency to remove the state and keep scoring. Its supporting **monks and Link** still provided additional paths after that, so the hack did not dictate the final game.
- **Player opinion:** The White Banner novice learned to **identify Pitchers and keep the expensive TAG out of hacking arcs**, and the O-12 teacher emphasized that **going second** can be strong for controlling final objectives. Both mention several rule interactions that they were still learning; do not treat their spoken rule explanations as immutable FAQ authority.
- **Inference — matchups:** **O-12 Cyberghost/Sirius/Raptor hacking + Gamma** can contest a **White Banner Blue Wolf/Shang Ji** heavy setup, but the TAG is hard to finish once it receives even one recovered activation. **White Banner Blue Wolf + Zhanshi/Octavia link + Lun Kai/Shaolin smoke** can hold scoring positions while O-12's higher-quality midfield pieces are diverted to fighting the TAG. This is a teaching game, not an O-12-vs-White-Banner balance sample.

**Mission tactic:** **Uplink Center rewards end-of-game antenna/Tech-Coffin control**. Plan a protected late Blue Wolf (or Gamma) move only after checking control/possession vulnerability and *how* the target Specialist can finish the button press.
