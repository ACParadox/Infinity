- **Observed — winning MO list:** Owen reports using a **double Seraph TAG** list **four of five games**, with **Knight of Justice Hacker, Defersen, engineer/Tech-Bee and cheap order/repair support**. Seraphs' **fast movement/Super-Jump, aggressive close combat and ancillary models** meant an opponent could not simply park one defensive gun and expect to deny all paths.
- **Observed — matchups:** **Round 1 Corporate Appropriation vs Tunguska: MO 10–0**, despite **Interventors, Hecklers/Repeaters and Raoul Spector/Perseus** threatening Hackable TAGs. Seraphs destroyed the objective and aggressively reached Tunguska hackers; **Defersen** made hacking a much less safe reactive answer. Another **Hardlock vs Tunguska ended MO 8–0**; the opponent was nearly tabled/entered Loss of Lieutenant after two Seraphs killed many models, but the participants acknowledge significant dice variance.
- **Observed — adaptation:** Against **Morat Aggression Force** with **Dartok + Yaogat MSV/smoke** expected to punish TAGs, Owen **did NOT use double Seraph**. He switched to **Defersen / Knight of Justice missile / Hospitaller / Konstantinos / Krux Knight** elite shooting and Disco Ball. **Krux Knight was burned down by a critical reactive rocket**, after which **Knight of Justice missile and Disco cover** opened a lane into the midboard. This supports **list pairing versus a known threat**, not an automatic win.
- **Observed — other faction evidence:** Dan used **Imperial Service with Sun Tze and up to three Su-Jian**, combining **Sù-Jiàn attack flanks, Kuang Shi order economy, Koga Ninja E/M Minelayer and Sāchā grenades**. One game swung from likely Imperial Service success to an opponent's last-turn zone win after Dan attacked one extra model and accidentally triggered **Retreat**, demonstrating that **more kills can lose a points mission**. Organizer's recap lists **Varuna Squalo Mk-II + ORC Core + Knight of Montesa/Helot** as a defensive heavy-infantry architecture, not another source-confirmed champion.
- **Player opinion:** Owen calls **two Seraphs plus Defersen** a strong answer to gun/repeater-heavy opponents, but highlights severe exposure if enemies possess good anti-TAG hacking/MSV. Dan recommends a **simple, well-practiced 10-model list** rather than a brain-intensive new combination over five rounds. The live forum recap says **Critical Intervention favors defender-side objective control**.
- **Inference:** **Military Orders double Seraph/Defersen** has direct positive examples **versus Tunguska** on **Corporate Appropriation/Hardlock**; **Defersen/Knight of Justice missile and Disco** is an alternative **versus Morat Dartok/Yaogat**. **ISS Su-Jian/Koga/Sun Tze** is useful for mobile multi-zone scoring, but must check if triggering Retreat would change mission points.
- **Caution:** #181 is a multi-game recap, not one head-to-head. The winner is **Owen on Military Orders**; Dan's Imperial Service, a Varuna sample and host's games must not be attributed to Owen's champion list. Hardlock 8–0 and Corporate Appropriation 10–0 reflect particular opponents and volatile dice.

**Mission lesson:** Use **a TAG plan and an anti-MSV/hacking non-TAG backup list**, and count **Retreat thresholds before killing a final opponent**.

- **Observed — Shasvastii:** **Sheskiin with Nox Hacker and Tensho Disco Ball in a three-body Haris, plus a separate Speculo Impersonator** aggressively killed several **PanO TR REMs / Karhu** in the first turn. The attacker deliberately aimed to **reduce defenses for two turns and take the data packet only on turn three**, leaving fewer chances to hunt its carrier. **Plasma REM** and a different infiltration angle put pressure on the room.
- **Observed — Svalarheima:** Defender fielded **Knight of Justice Hacker, Peacemaker Minelayer, Bulleteer/REM recovery, Karhu specialists and Deployable Turrets**. After losing some fragile shooters, the defender kept **small mines and turret lines around the central room** rather than advancing into Shasvastii's position. A **Peacemaker mine** forced an infiltrator into an inefficient sacrificial move.
- **Observed:** Spending a **Command Token to activate turrets** inhibited a late **Shasvastii Ada/Speculo passage**, although the minelayers could not simply be planted inside the room. Final **room domination swing** flipped the scoring, resulting in **9–5 SWF**; one turn-three PanO REM/specialist reached the required contested central objective.
- **Player opinion:** **Defending/going second felt favorable** because the attacker has an affirmative series of actions and must preserve its unique packet carrier. PanO consumed **three of four Command Tokens before first turn** on Speedball, stripping Orders and enabling turrets; the presenter recommends considering **+1 Command Token Lt** in this scenario.
- **Inference:** **SWF Knight of Justice/Peacemaker + Turrets + Karhu repair redundancy** can hold **Critical Intervention** against **Sheskiin/Speculo's alpha** if the defender prevents a late packet run. Shasvastii's **delay-then-extract** strategy is viable until the attacker leaves the defender enough Orders to retake the room.
- **Caution:** Attacker/defender rules must be confirmed against ITS17; this is *one asymmetric game*, not evidence that SWF universally outperforms Shasvastii.

**Mission lesson:** **Protect room domination over kill count**, preserve the unique data carrier, and plan the **pre-turn Command Token budget** carefully.
