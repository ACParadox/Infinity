## #181 — Autumn Assault 2025: Military Orders champion and tournament meta

**Primary sources:** [Original Corvus Belli forum index](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-autumn-assault-2025.43704/) · [full Tactical Awareness event recap video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q8MTottG5MU) · [live mid-event discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T056GKohM8Y). The forum opened **October 11**, discussing an event **six weeks away**. The actual weekend was **November 22–23, 2025**, and used **new ITS17 missions under N5.2**. **Owen won the five-round Satellite with Military Orders**.

- **Observed — winning MO list:** Owen reports using a **double Seraph TAG** list **four of five games**, with **Knight of Justice Hacker, Defersen, engineer/Tech-Bee and cheap order/repair support**. Seraphs' **fast movement/Super-Jump, aggressive close combat and ancillary models** meant an opponent could not simply park one defensive gun and expect to deny all paths.
- **Observed — matchups:** **Round 1 Corporate Appropriation vs Tunguska: MO 10–0**, despite **Interventors, Hecklers/Repeaters and Raoul Spector/Perseus** threatening Hackable TAGs. Seraphs destroyed the objective and aggressively reached Tunguska hackers; **Defersen** made hacking a much less safe reactive answer. Another **Hardlock vs Tunguska ended MO 8–0**; the opponent was nearly tabled/entered Loss of Lieutenant after two Seraphs killed many models, but the participants acknowledge significant dice variance.
- **Observed — adaptation:** Against **Morat Aggression Force** with **Dartok + Yaogat MSV/smoke** expected to punish TAGs, Owen **did NOT use double Seraph**. He switched to **Defersen / Knight of Justice missile / Hospitaller / Konstantinos / Krux Knight** elite shooting and Disco Ball. **Krux Knight was burned down by a critical reactive rocket**, after which **Knight of Justice missile and Disco cover** opened a lane into the midboard. This supports **list pairing versus a known threat**, not an automatic win.
- **Observed — other faction evidence:** Dan used **Imperial Service with Sun Tze and up to three Su-Jian**, combining **Sù-Jiàn attack flanks, Kuang Shi order economy, Koga Ninja E/M Minelayer and Sāchā grenades**. One game swung from likely Imperial Service success to an opponent's last-turn zone win after Dan attacked one extra model and accidentally triggered **Retreat**, demonstrating that **more kills can lose a points mission**. Organizer's recap lists **Varuna Squalo Mk-II + ORC Core + Knight of Montesa/Helot** as a defensive heavy-infantry architecture, not another source-confirmed champion.
- **Player opinion:** Owen calls **two Seraphs plus Defersen** a strong answer to gun/repeater-heavy opponents, but highlights severe exposure if enemies possess good anti-TAG hacking/MSV. Dan recommends a **simple, well-practiced 10-model list** rather than a brain-intensive new combination over five rounds. The live forum recap says **Critical Intervention favors defender-side objective control**.
- **Inference:** **Military Orders double Seraph/Defersen** has direct positive examples **versus Tunguska** on **Corporate Appropriation/Hardlock**; **Defersen/Knight of Justice missile and Disco** is an alternative **versus Morat Dartok/Yaogat**. **ISS Su-Jian/Koga/Sun Tze** is useful for mobile multi-zone scoring, but must check if triggering Retreat would change mission points.
- **Caution:** #181 is a multi-game recap, not one head-to-head. The winner is **Owen on Military Orders**; Dan's Imperial Service, a Varuna sample and host's games must not be attributed to Owen's champion list. Hardlock 8–0 and Corporate Appropriation 10–0 reflect particular opponents and volatile dice.

**Mission lesson:** Use **a TAG plan and an anti-MSV/hacking non-TAG backup list**, and count **Retreat thresholds before killing a final opponent**.

## #182 — Svalarheima Winter Force vs Shasvastii: ITS17 Critical Intervention

**[Original detailed Infinity the Dante report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iYkViwfYJz8)**. **Svalarheima 9–5 Shasvastii**. **Svalarheima defended/played second** in this asymmetric scenario: unlock or stop the data console, carry or deny the *untransferable* packet, dominate the central room, secure kills/Classified.

- **Observed — Shasvastii:** **Sheskiin in a forward Fireteam with a Disco launcher and Speculo Impersonator** aggressively killed several **PanO TR REMs / Karhu** in the first turn. The attacker deliberately aimed to **reduce defenses for two turns and take the data packet only on turn three**, leaving fewer chances to hunt its carrier. **Plasma REM** and a different infiltration angle put pressure on the room.
- **Observed — Svalarheima:** Defender fielded **Knight of Justice Hacker, Peacemaker Minelayer, Bulleteer/REM recovery, Karhu specialists and Deployable Turrets**. After losing some fragile shooters, the defender kept **small mines and turret lines around the central room** rather than advancing into Shasvastii's position. A **Peacemaker mine** forced an infiltrator into an inefficient sacrificial move.
- **Observed:** Spending a **Command Token to activate turrets** inhibited a late **Shasvastii Ada/Speculo passage**, although the minelayers could not simply be planted inside the room. Final **room domination swing** flipped the scoring, resulting in **9–5 SWF**; one turn-three PanO REM/specialist reached the required contested central objective.
- **Player opinion:** **Defending/going second felt favorable** because the attacker has an affirmative series of actions and must preserve its unique packet carrier. PanO consumed **three of four Command Tokens before first turn** on Speedball, stripping Orders and enabling turrets; the presenter recommends considering **+1 Command Token Lt** in this scenario.
- **Inference:** **SWF Knight of Justice/Peacemaker + Turrets + Karhu repair redundancy** can hold **Critical Intervention** against **Sheskiin/Speculo's alpha** if the defender prevents a late packet run. Shasvastii's **delay-then-extract** strategy is viable until the attacker leaves the defender enough Orders to retake the room.
- **Caution:** Attacker/defender rules must be confirmed against ITS17; this is *one asymmetric game*, not evidence that SWF universally outperforms Shasvastii.

**Mission lesson:** **Protect room domination over kill count**, preserve the unique data carrier, and plan the **pre-turn Command Token budget** carefully.

## #183 — USARF vs Dahshat Company: Dire Foes 9 Nocturne

**[Original full Fast Panda report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IGPl-JrIKp4)**. **USARF 6–5 Dahshat Company**, with the final player explicitly tallying **NGO/civilian capture worth three OP**, deciding the one-point difference. **Custom/narrative Dire Foes 9 “Nocturne”**, not ITS; the players say they **didn't follow mandatory campaign factions**, only the mission rules. Exact N5 patch/day unknown.

- **Observed — Dahshat:** **McMurrough Smoke/Super-Jump** used a dangerous **multiple-order approach against the USARF Maverick**, while **Maghariba Guard, Scarface/Cordelia-type Engineer/recovery, Bashi Bazouk Holomask and specialist gun support** pressured the central zone. **Maghariba was repaired or stood back up**, forcing American guns to spend another full attack series.
- **Observed — USARF:** **Van Zant airborne flank, Blackjacks and Maverick fast screening, Minuteman/Grunt-type ARO and cheap mission worker** spread threat directions. At the finish, USARF had **much less surviving army** but had **successfully captured/extracted the NGO civilian** and kept an eligible HVT/console situation.
- **Observed — score:** USARF captured the **three-point civilian**, and Dahshat killed more; **6–5 USARF** after tally. A **Blackjack defensive gun** remained valuable even though heavy Dahshat shooting nearly cleared the Americans.
- **Inference:** **USARF Van Zant + Maverick/Blackjack + fast mission courier** can beat **McMurrough/Maghariba** in Nocturne by capturing the civilian rather than seeking an impossible full kill. Dahshat **Bashi and McMurrough smoke** can exploit USARF low-visibility blind spots.
- **Caution:** This is a **narrative mission, nonstandard faction pairing** with heavily auto-translated unit names. **No normal ITS mission or faction preference follows from a 6–5 Dire Foes result.**

**Mission lesson:** In Nocturne, do not sacrifice the **high-value capture** in exchange for more surviving army points.

## #184 — Military Orders vs Next Wave: Hardlock, constrained Next Wave starter force

**[Original full Fast Panda report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPDb9wX1DhI)**. **Military Orders 7–1 Next Wave**, final tally. **N5.2 or later** since Next Wave is introduced; players explicitly waited for a **recent points update**.

- **Observed — confounded list:** Next Wave player used **every model from a newly supplied promotional Next Wave bundle, stretched to 300 points**, creating **only about eleven models, split awkwardly into Order groups**. The player explicitly says this is **not a competent tuned tournament list**. The **Juggernaut TAG was Lieutenant** mainly because of bundle limitations, with too few cheap orders and no full Achilles/Pandora bench.
- **Observed — MO:** A conventional **Military Orders Knights/linked guns and Specialists** controlled the **three middle consoles and two beacons**. The Next Wave Juggernaut was damaged or pinned while the small pool couldn't simultaneously remove defenders, press consoles and meet the Classified requirement.
- **Observed:** The Next Wave player conceded when remaining Orders could **not reach a console or Classified**, and both counted **7–1 for MO**. This was not a normal free-choice Next Wave build.
- **Player opinion:** Next Wave owner would add **cheap R-Drones, Bit & Kiss, more order sources**, and **remove Juggernaut from Lieutenant duty** if competitive; **Steeljaw/tech dinosaur** bodies provide excellent cheap two-wound Sensor and template pressure for their cost when Orders exist.
- **Inference:** MO can punish a **Next Wave Juggernaut/Lt with too few scoring bodies** in Hardlock; but that is a **list-building deficiency**, not evidence that Military Orders generally counters tournament-strength Next Wave.
- **Caution:** Do not add the 7–1 as competitive faction win-rate evidence, given manufacturer-supplied display-box roster.

**Mission lesson:** In Hardlock, **three consoles need cheap independent Specialist/Order economy**, not a mandatory oversized TAG/Lt alone.

## #185 — Ikari Company vs Invincible Army: Akial Interference

**[Original Fast Panda full report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9yQXdKDQ5U)**. **7–4** in the closing discussion. **Winner attribution unverified:** machine-translated dialogue renders the named winner as “Copper,” without a dependable mapping to Ikari or IA; source result is **not reported as a certain faction win**. ITS17; exact N5 revision unknown.

- **Observed — Ikari:** **Armand Le Muet sniper, Yuan Yuan/Desperado biker, Tanko, Brawler Hacker, Wu Ming with TinBot, warband and a TAG/Disco delivery model** built several early aggression lanes; **Armand's Sniper** controlled a long diagonal while the cheap biker was exposed to reaction fire.
- **Observed — Invincible Army:** **Zhanshi Paramedic Duo, Zuyong Fireteam plus Bixie, named Engineer/Servant, Thunderbolt TR bot, Specialist hackers and Camo deployment** provided broad **Classified task types** and a durable shooting core.
- **Observed:** A **single long-range ARO/flash bot and reactive turret** repeatedly spoiled an attacker, while **Armand/other Camo gun** kept pressure on the enemy's specialists. **A late Classified/HVT score was revoked or debated** because the mission **rotates Common Classifieds and can cancel an enemy's private score**.
- **Observed — score caveat:** Players considered **5–4 or 7–4**, then settled **7–4** by their interpretation of whether the central antenna removes a private two-point Classified. They explicitly raised concerns about the mission's **negative scoring / point cancellation**, not a clean stable OP.
- **Inference:** **Ikari Armand + Tanko/Wu Ming gun/CC and Brawler hacker** can pressure **Invincible Army Zuyong/Bixie/Engineer specialists**, but **IA's cheap Paramedic/Engineer network** can match Akial's varied Classifieds. The important counter is **Classified breadth and protecting the scoring model**, not faction-wide shooting superiority.
- **Caution:** Exact **winner label and 7–4 scoring legality require a native-language result / official ITS17 packet** before entering the matchup as a known winning faction. Some machine-transcribed units are uncertain.

**Mission lesson:** In Akial, prepare **two or more independent Classified skill sets and a way to contest late central antenna cancellation**.

## #186 — Druze Bayram Security vs Corregidor: ITS17 Cutthroat

**[Original full Fast Panda video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ypVn75PRiRs)**. **Corregidor 6–3 Druze**, final explicit 6/3 tally; heavy-armor-themed game with **four TAGs present** (including **Corregidor Geckos x2** and **Druze Anaconda / Scarface**).

- **Observed — Corregidor:** **Two Geckos with Tactical Awareness Fireteam coordination, Intruder sniper/Mimetism, Jazz hacking, Jaguars/Flash Pulse and other cheap defensive pieces** used different angles. **Gecko AP Spitfire** repeatedly pressured a Druze TAG and kept surviving army points high.
- **Observed — Druze:** **Anaconda, Scarface + Cordelia, Druze link and Hacker, Yuan Yuan, Warcor/Flash REM and E/M threat** used elite shooting but suffered repeated failed recovery/trades. **Scarface remained threatening**, so the Corregidor player sometimes prioritized its hacker or screening piece rather than trying to destroy the whole TAG.
- **Observed:** An attacker exposed an **Anaconda to Flash Pulse**, losing an Order to Stunned and slowing its attack; other dice were unusually favorable for an **Intruder** and otherwise unlucky for TAG shots. Druze had opportunities to remove additional Specialists for **Cutthroat points** but didn't finish them.
- **Observed — score:** Corregidor won **6–3**, including **surviving Lieutenant/army and kills** as discussed; postgame Druze player said **Anaconda was surprisingly durable** and several failed TAG gun exchanges were poor probabilities, not merely weak equipment.
- **Inference:** **Corregidor double Gecko + Jazz hacking + Intruder lane coverage** can contain a **Druze Scarface/Anaconda double-heavy list** in Cutthroat, but cheap **Flash Pulse** and order denial mattered more than raw TAG stats.
- **Caution:** Four TAGs are a deliberately unusual matchup; double-Gecko legality and profile are historic. One game's lopsided rolls are not a generic Corregidor > Druze claim.

**Mission lesson:** In Cutthroat, prioritize **Specialist/Lieutenant-kill scoring and cheap Flash Pulse/Repeater speed bumps**, rather than spending all Orders on an armor duel.

## #187 — Shasvastii vs Next Wave: ITS17 Provisioning

**[Original QuickFinity postgame slideshow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4oGcVLjfG7M)**. **Next Wave wins**, but **no final numerical score** is stated in the recording. **N5.2 or later** because Next Wave exists; precise patch/date unverified.

- **Observed — Shasvastii anti-flyer tech:** **Shrouded Hacker and Hidden Malignos Hacker**, **two R-Drones, Q-Drone TR HMG, M-Drone Sensor/Tactical Awareness, Sāchā E/M Grenades, Mentor Decoy, Gwailo gun** created a seemingly credible **Skyhound/Next Wave Hacker and Sensor screen**.
- **Observed — Next Wave:** **Skyhound flyer, Stinger in a strong central Fireteam, Puzzler/Steeljaw or small dino with Panzerfaust and mine dispenser**, plus long-range covering model. It preserved enough **midboard Provisioning objectives** while the Shasvastii army lost its Order base.
- **Observed — turning point:** Shasvastii **left Malignos Hidden too long**, waiting for the flyer to enter its chosen zone. When a shooter was finally needed, there were not enough Orders to reveal and use it. The **Gwailo** exchanged ineffectual fire with Stinger and was subsequently killed by a small dinosaur's **Panzerfaust**; **Skyhound** crossed the board and cleared more troops. **Sāchā critically hit Skyhound with E/M Grenade and disabled it**, a genuine anti-flyer moment that came too late to reverse the match.
- **Player opinion:** Over-specializing the roster for flyers made it weaker when Next Wave instead **controlled the objective with ground units**. Creator suggests **Victor Messer plus more ordinary specialists** may be more useful than two expensive reserve Hackers in this matchup.
- **Inference:** **Shasvastii Shrouded/Malignos + Sāchā E/M** are legitimate **anti-Skyhound** tools; however, **Next Wave Stinger/Puzzler/cheap dino** can win Provisioning if the enemy wastes turns waiting for flyers instead of moving the cargo.
- **Caution:** Slideshow narrative is one player's retrospective, with **no reliable final OP** or full Army list verification.

**Mission lesson:** Do not **keep Hidden Hackers inactive for two turns** when winning requires **visible box runners now**.

## #188 — Imperial Service vs Next Wave: Battleground

**[Original Spanish-language Gravitypool Orbital Operations report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4PvEz1VFHEs)**. **Next Wave 8–0 Imperial Service**, explicitly counted; tournament round one at Quecolandia Miniatures. N5.2+; played date and exact patch uncertain.

- **Observed — ISS:** **Two Sù-Jiàn (one Spitfire, one close shotgun), Sun Tze/cheap order system, Zhanying, Son-Bae missile REM, Engineer/Servant and deployable E/M turrets** had a workable **Sensor/Repeater and Guided plan**. **Sù-Jiàn** could wound or threaten a heavy gun and **missile/Spotlight** removed a protruding Repeater/Puzzler early.
- **Observed — Next Wave:** **Ironside heavy gun, a durable Ranter/Puzzler advance and mission-capable midfield pieces** struck the same ISS flank repeatedly. The **Ironside** picked off ISS Bao, Zhanying and Son-Bae, collapsing the link bonus and leaving the two **Sù-Jiàn isolated as the last zone weight**.
- **Observed — luck:** ISS player says the match was **balanced until a Sù-Jiàn failed five consecutive ARM saves**, causing an abrupt 100-point swing and removing his flank's defenses. A well-placed **E/M turret** consumed a Next Wave Order, but couldn't make up for losing both strong attack bodies.
- **Observed — score:** **Next Wave 8–0 ISS**, earning every relevant Battleground OP except the opponent's Key Ops-related condition. The Spanish recording uses “Campo de Batalla” for **Battleground**, a Frontline-like quadrant/weight scenario.
- **Inference:** **Next Wave Ironside + durable Puzzler/Ranter pressure** can beat an **ISS double-Sù-Jiàn** zone plan on open firing lanes; ISS **Son-Bae/Repeater + Koga Ninja/E/M defense** can contest this when not destroyed early. Do not generalize from five sequential failed saves.
- **Caution:** Source is a slideshow in Spanish with auto-translated wording; exact Fireteam membership and the damage model are historical.

**Mission lesson:** **Spread the Sù-Jiàn or preserve a third zone-contesting Specialist** so one failed flank doesn't lose Battleground by itself.

## #189 — Tohaa vs Shasvastii: ITS18 Data Harvest

**[Original full GMG Infinity x Tactical Awareness game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h-JAbq545zk)**. **Tohaa 4–1 Shasvastii**, computed from narrated final tally; **N5.3 / ITS18** (Burlington Brawl prep, autumn 2026).

- **Observed — Tohaa:** **Neema Saatar multiwound Core, two Sakiel Boarding Shotgun/Paramedics with Flamethrower Chaksa companions, Sukeul HMG, Rasail Boarding Shotguns with Chaksa flamethrowers, Greif/“Gale” Spitfire/MSV, two Kaeltars SymbioMates/SymbioBombs**, and many wounds/order sources. Opposing Shasvastii lost ability to attack freely when a **Rasail + mine + direct-template web** covered the path.
- **Observed — Shasvastii:** **Sphinx TAG, Sheskiin, Caliban Engineer/FO, Malignos Hacker, Mentor, Nox Paramedic and Q-Drone TR HMG** had strong individual pieces but too few independent paths once **Q-Drone** created a very strong central lane. A Shas player tried repeatedly to eliminate a **TR HMG blocking both scoring areas**, losing the active turn's required Order budget.
- **Observed — mission geometry:** **Three consoles each dispense two data collectors**, which must be **carried to separately designated scoring circles and deployed with an additional action**. The players placed circles very close to one another, accidentally allowing **one TR HMG to watch both**. The player who tried to brute-force the ARO could not complete the pickup+delivery loop.
- **Observed — score:** Tohaa got **one per round when an active collector was present (two played scoring rounds), plus two for keeping opposing collectors inactive = 4**. Shasvastii's consolation **one Classified = 1**; game ended after turn-three scoring was processed despite truncating remaining actions.
- **Player opinion:** The winner and opponent identify **circle placement**, **lethal coverage of delivery corridor** and **too many Orders spent shooting rather than collecting** as the actual reasons, not Tohaa's general fighting superiority.
- **Inference:** **Tohaa multiwound Sakiel/Rasail/Kaeltar courier network** is attractive in Data Harvest, while **Shasvastii Sphinx/Sheskiin/Caliban** needs **early TR removal and one committed cargo route** rather than a prolonged gunfight.
- **Caution:** Final score is reconstructed from direct point narration, not a separately published scorecard. Some Tohaa names are corrupted in automatic translation; verify with Army.

**Mission lesson:** **Never place both delivery circles under one ARO**; expect **multiple Orders for pickup, transport and the final placement Skill**.

## #190 — TAK vs Steel Phalanx: ITS18 Double Bind

**[Original full GMG Infinity x Tactical Awareness report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ybPhzz14HbU)**. **Steel Phalanx 8–6 TAK**, directly tallied. **N5.3 / ITS18**, explicitly the new Double Bind practice mission recorded about one month before the November 2026 Burlington Brawl (the match itself was NOT part of the tournament packet).

- **Observed — TAK:** **Veteran Kazaks, Spetsnaz, Tankhunter Hidden Camo/Autocannon, Strelok/Shotgun midfield defenses, Line Kazak and mobile Specialists** retained plenty of army points late even when several troops were gone. One hidden **Tankhunter never fired its autocannon** because the opponent offered no good high-value target.
- **Observed — Steel Phalanx:** **Andromeda strong forward attack, Myrmidon Duo, Hoplite linked assault, Phoenix and TR defense**, with multiple **D-Charges/Anti-Materiel attackers**. The winner **placed or hit two target objectives with D-Charges efficiently**, took territory, then secured an HVT with a surviving active actor to push the score to eight.
- **Observed — pivotal exchange:** Steel Phalanx **Hoplite survived long enough to remain a mission threat** after a poor TAK attempt to eliminate it; **Andromeda** cleared important flank pieces, while **TR REM double crit** illustrates extraordinary luck that shouldn't be tier evidence.
- **Observed — score:** TAK recovered late **secured HVT, controlled zone and surviving army-point bonuses** to finish **6**, but **Steel Phalanx held 8** with the more efficient Double Bind target destruction.
- **Player opinion:** TAK owner was surprised by **the great quantity of quality 20–30-point units** that survived and scored army points despite casualties. He regretted not finishing the **Hoplite** while it was exposed.
- **Inference:** **Steel Phalanx Andromeda + Hoplite + D-Charges and Smoke** is good **Double Bind objective destruction** into **TAK Camo/Shotgun/Tankhunter**. **TAK Veteran Kazak/Spetsnaz + hidden gun** can recover enough late points to narrow the gap, but must protect Anti-Materiel and HVT plans before overcommitting to a shot.
- **Caution:** Some scenario-text and unit labels in transcript are mistranslated; score and broad roles are confirmed, but not every Fireteam/weapon interaction should be copied into modern N5 lists without original footage.

**Mission lesson:** **Do Anti-Materiel objective actions early** and leave mobile units to contest late HVT/army-point rewards; keeping an unused hidden Tankhunter is not automatically a successful defense.
