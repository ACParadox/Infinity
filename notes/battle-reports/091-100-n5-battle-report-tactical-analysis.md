## #091 — StarCo vs Hassassin Bahram: Corporate Appropriation

**Primary:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness filmed report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5Za_AmQX7M). **ITS17/N5.2**, Autumn Assault Nov 2025 preparation. **Final: Hassassin Bahram 4–2 StarCo**, explicitly counted.

- **Observed:** Corporate Appropriation rewards **destroying one's own prototype with Anti-Materiel attacks/D-Charges**, or **capturing the enemy prototype** and extracting it, plus Panoply/Classifieds. Here both prototypes endured far more attacks than players expected. **StarCo Hellcat D-Charges** repeatedly failed to destroy the target; players explicitly note **reloading with Speedball or bringing additional D-Charges** could have changed the tie.
- **Observed:** StarCo used **Raoul Spector airborne entry, Hellcat, Emily, Digger, Hardcase, Riot Grrl Missile Launcher and supporting mercenaries** to attack both room and target routes. **Raoul** pressured an Asawira defense but could not cleanly remove all room defenders.
- **Observed:** Hassassin's **McMurrough double Chain Rifles and CC, Asawira Fireteam and Doctor, Barid, Fiday/impersonator and other linked support** punished a StarCo group that clustered around an objective room. **McMurrough caused disproportionate multi-model damage**, even though he later went down.
- **Observed:** A Hassassin **Asawira/Doctor** repeatedly resisted attacks; the players discuss how **Frenzy and lost cover** changed the later gunfight. The retained high-value Haqq prototype/mission state secured the **4–2** despite both players' frustrating attacks.
- **Inference:** **McMurrough + Asawira resilient close defense** can stop StarCo's **Hellcat/Raoul dual-angle prototype rush**, but the decisive factor was **failed anti-objective damage/reloading**, not universal faction balance. StarCo should bring a *second* reachable D-Charge/Anti-Materiel plan.
- **Caution:** Exact 2025 Frenzy, D-Charge/ADS and Doctor rules were debated live; prefer the original ITS17 wording.

**List-building takeaway:** **Hellcat or Raoul + rearmable D-Charges + independent Panoply/Carrier** instead of depending on one attack roll to destroy the prototype.

## #092 — Steel Phalanx vs Varuna: Area of Interest

**Primary:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM4_o2fs9mw). **N5.2/ITS17**, Autumn Assault testing. **Final Varuna 6–3 Steel Phalanx**.

- **Observed:** **Lateral/corner deployment** compressed Varuna's starting space; Steel Phalanx's **Myrmidon Smoke and Mimetism, hard close-combat heroes and forward pressure** could deny Varuna escape routes. The **Special Area of Interest** scored repeatedly, making presence over the turns as important as kills.
- **Observed:** Varuna used **Cutter Hidden Deployment TAG, two Bulleteers (Spitfire/Shotgun), Zulu Cobras, Fugazi support, ORC Fireteam and Engineer/servant**. The **Cutter** threatened Steel Phalanx elite bodies and drew attacks; an **Engineer restored Cutter Structure** after it was wounded, preserving endgame zone weight.
- **Observed:** Steel Phalanx **Myrmidon** survived multiple unusually poor odds ranged attacks, forcing Varuna to invest several additional Orders to access a useful route. The player discovered **Varuna ORCs lack the easy Dodge movement** he had expected for this geometry.
- **Observed:** On the final turn a **Varuna REM** needed to re-enter one zone; the successful movement/counterattack changed the reported score from a possible **4–3** to **6–3**. The decisive action was **putting the surviving scoring body inside the area**, not killing every remaining hero.
- **Inference:** **Cutter + Bulleteer/cheap REM + Engineer** gives Varuna an effective *mission-specific* answer to a Steel Phalanx assault, while Steel's **Myrmidon/CC threat** remains dangerous on narrow DZ tables. Do not claim Varuna is generally favored.
- **Caution:** Verify mission-area scoring and historical ORC/Cutter repair interactions in the played ITS17 pack.

**List-building takeaway:** protect a **last-turn 1–2 Order REM zone runner** separate from the Cutter; Steel Phalanx must remove **scoring REMs**, not just the biggest TAG.

## #093 — Next Wave vs Tohaa: Hardlock, redundant dinosaur strategy

**Primary:** [Robert Shepherd's narrated full report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGiGPG3pNh4). **N5.2 or later**. **Next Wave wins** on the first Order of its final turn, when a needed Hardlock button succeeds; full official OP unknown.

- **Observed:** The player **deliberately declined Achilles + Skyhound all-in** because losing one expensive attacker can make an entire list unable to score. He instead built roughly **six Silhouette 5 dinosaur bodies** with **Patroclus + Steeljaw Duo, Pandora Fireteam with Blackheart/Stinger and Medikit support, Puzzlers, other specialists and NCO/Tactical Awareness**.
- **Observed:** **Patroclus** supplied the list's **regular Smoke**, personal Specialist task access, a viable gun and Fireteam Master Order economy; **Steeljaw is non-Hackable, hard to remove and provides a meaningful close-assault special-die package**. **Pandora/Blackheart Medikit (+1B)** provided a second source of recovery and hacking/attack, making the army less dependent on one premium trooper.
- **Observed:** Tohaa **Sukeul K1 Sniper, Triad/protected support and late beacon/console models** forced multiple difficult engagements. A **Next Wave Puzzler Dodged into CC with two Tohaa pieces**, imposing a tactical bottleneck and making their preferred scoring route harder.
- **Observed:** The author held sufficient Orders to **activate the critical Hardlock console immediately on the final turn**, making remaining combat/showboating mostly irrelevant to the match result. Tohaa still got a late **Sukeul into beacon contact**, but Next Wave retained the necessary button advantage.
- **Player assessment:** **Six-dinosaur redundancy** is a deliberate playstyle that can survive losing two pieces; the author explicitly acknowledges **Achilles + Skyhound may have higher raw offensive ceiling**. Neither approach is a universal must-have.
- **Inference:** A **Patroclus/Steeljaw + Pandora/Blackheart + Puzzler** multi-threat package can withstand Tohaa's K1/Triad attacks on **Hardlock** because it retains *another* mission specialist after attrition.
- **Caution:** Historical **Fireteam Master, Medikit, Smoke and Puzzler PARA CCW** legality should be rechecked before adopting a current N5.3 list.

**List-building takeaway:** prioritize **a second Specialist/Smoke/repair path** after the leading dinosaur dies; reserve Orders for last-turn Hardlock WIP.

## #094 — Shindenbutai vs Next Wave: Annihilation introductory report

**Primary:** [Corvus Belli × Vanguard Tactics video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zeUX9z3Lcpc). **N5.2 or later, reduced-points learn-to-play**. **Shindenbutai claims the decisive victory after a closing kill**, official OP total unverified.

- **Observed:** Shindenbutai brought **Hatamoto, Senku, Hidden Kurayami Ninja, Tai-shin airborne, Mechazoid Sakurenti TAG, Shizoku and support**; Next Wave used **Juggernaut, Steeljaw, Harbingers, Ironside, Rain Dancer, Contrabandero and Cliff-Jumper**. Mission directly rewarded **destroyed army/Lieutenant and survival**, unlike button missions.
- **Observed:** **Steeljaw** initially defeated/resisted an engaged Japanese fighter, then **Hatamoto in CC** removed it. Next Wave **Ironside destroyed Sakurenti TAG**, proving the Japanese heavy gun was not immune to a high-quality shooter.
- **Observed:** A **Hidden Ninja deliberately stayed concealed in a building** until **Juggernaut passed nearby**, then sprang an excellent **surprise CC exchange** that killed the high-Structure Juggernaut. This is a concrete **anti-elite hidden building defense**, not simply a lucky generic “Ninja good” claim.
- **Observed:** With few surviving models, **Hatamoto** took multiple Orders to attack the last Next Wave threat through repeated Dodges, eventually killed it and **ended the game**. Both players identify the Ninja assassination as an enormous swing.
- **Inference:** **Shindenbutai Hidden Ninja + Hatamoto CC cover** can punish **Next Wave Juggernaut/Steeljaw assaults** when the board has concealed approach buildings. Next Wave needs **Discover/marker screening and long-range denial** before advancing the highest-cost melee piece.
- **Caution:** A filmed learning/demo match is not evidence of Shindenbutai's competitive Annihilation win rate or a guaranteed Ninja vs Juggernaut outcome.

**List-building takeaway:** keep **a Hidden anti-charge Ninja** near the most tempting entrance; use Hatamoto only after an enemy attacker overcommits.

## #095 — Bakunin vs Neoterran Capitaline Army: Akial Interference

**Primary:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wZFt-dcPzoA). **Official ITS17 AKIAL INTERFERENCE**, filmed around **late Oct 2025**, close to Nov 1 publication; **N5.2**. **Bakunin narrow win via late Secure HVT**, final OP count not completely explicit.

- **Observed:** NCA used **Squalos MkII TAG, Swiss Guard, Engineer/TecBot, Hacker/FO/CSU and Flash support**. Bakunin had **Chimera + Pupniks, Morlocks, Reverend/Custodier hackers, Zero Specialist, Riot Grrl and a support/Speedball package**.
- **Observed:** **Akial Interference** cycles **Common Classifieds**, with antenna interaction capable of **cancelling an opponent's achieved Common Objective with the correct symbol**. Players valued **skill variety and completing a Classified every round**, not only reaching the center.
- **Observed:** Bakunin obtained **multiple Common-Classified completions after an advantageous Speedball/turn sequence**, recovered a Morlock, and then **Secure HVT tipped the game** after the major fighting was effectively tied.
- **Observed:** The NCA player spent several Orders trying a **GizmoKit interaction on the expensive TAG** as a way to complete a recovery-related task; the rolls went badly, providing neither the intended point nor meaningful board advantage. A strong **Squalos/Swiss Guard** line did not compensate for loss of actual HVT reach.
- **Inference:** **Bakunin Chimera/Pupnik/Zero + versatile Hacking/Doctor/Engineer** has strong *Akial mission coverage* against an NCA list where high-priced TAG/Swiss shooters absorb Orders. NCA should retain **two different Classified-action skills plus a living HVT-reaching specialist**.
- **Caution:** The unusual **self-injury and GizmoKit repair** idea is an in-game gamble, not a recommended automatic legal strategy. No verified exact 5–4/6–4 tally.

**List-building takeaway:** bring **broad Classified coverage, a Speedball-compatible backup, and one last-turn HVT runner** rather than maximize Squalos attacks.

## #096 — Vanilla Haqqislam vs Vanilla JSA: Hardlock

**Primary:** [Robert Shepherd's narrated report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d7ZnknGCnks). **Post-Oct 2025 N5.2+**, exact played day unverified. **Haqq wins a major Hardlock victory**, numeric OP not given.

- **Observed:** Haqq used **Saladin Strategos 2** to distribute regular Orders between combat groups; a **Janissary + Odalisque Fireteam** could remain static while a mission runner temporarily joined its group and used its otherwise idle pool. Other units included **Leila Sharif Hacker, Hunzakut FO Repeater, Fiday, two Ghazi Muttawi'ah (Jammer/E/M), Mukhtar, Zuleyka/Zamira and Zbeck vehicle**.
- **Observed:** Vanilla JSA used **Hatamoto, Ryuken-9, Hidden Ninjas/Oniwaban, Tanuki, Kempe and Shobu**. A **Ghazi with E/M/boarding shotgun fork** pressured Hatamoto; **Mukhtar burst fire** removed a revealed Ninja. Fiday and the vehicle could threaten the Japanese backline at distinct angles.
- **Observed — decisive order economy:** Saladin moved into **Combat Group 2 to ensure 3–4 WIP button attempts** for an **Odalisque**. The button succeeded on the second try and the author expressly identifies it as **game-winning**, not the number of Japanese killed.
- **Observed:** A final **Coordinated Order** involving **Fiday, Ghazi, vehicle/Zamira** cleared supporting AROs, helped **Secure HVT** and moved surviving bodies to Hardlock's final scoring positions.
- **Player assessment:** the author would replace **Shobu with Shikami** in this particular Vanilla JSA list because **Shikami mobility, resilience and specialist access** offer better mission utility. This is a **single player's list-specific opinion**, not an established “never buy Shobu.”
- **Inference:** **Saladin group-order transfer + Odalisque button + Ghazi/Fiday disruption** can be a particularly effective Hardlock counter-plan versus a **Hatamoto/Hiding-Ninja heavy Vanilla JSA build**. JSA's best answer is a separate, protected WIP runner and a retained Chain of Command/ARO.

**List-building takeaway:** **reserve multiple attempts at the decisive Hardlock console** rather than spending every Order shooting the Hatamoto.

## #097 — Tunguska vs Svalarheima: Akial Interference

**Primary:** [Champion of Terra full video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uv0ARwxYK8o). **ITS17 Akial Interference**, exact N5 patch and played date unknown. **Tunguska wins a late Common-Classified exchange**; the final full OP score remains unclear in translated dialog.

- **Observed:** Tunguska deployed **Grenzer Sniper/Securitate Fireteam, Interventor Hacker, Clockmaker + Zondbot, Vertigo Guided REM, Puppet Master with Forward Observer Puppets, Cheerkiller, Hollow Men**. WinterFor used **Karhu K1 Marksman/Paramedic, Nisse, Shona Carano, Machinist, REMs and linked gunfighters**.
- **Observed:** **Shona Super-Jump into CC** removed/disrupted valuable Tunguska specialists and puppet coverage. Tunguska's **Interventor Cybermask and Puppet FO** retained ways to move toward multiple HVT-related Classifieds without winning a straight Feuerbach/Nisse duel.
- **Observed:** In the last round a **Tunguska specialist/Puppet** reached the correct position, completed **Suspected Infiltration** and then **Vigilance**, with the commentators counting **two late Classifieds plus the more-achieved bonus**, while the opponent had only one of the compared tasks.
- **Inference:** **Interventor + Puppet FO + Clockmaker** provide broader repeating task coverage than a narrow gun-heavy roster, especially in **Akial Interference's rotating Common-Classified system**. WinterFor's Karhu/Paramedic/Machinist needs a *last-turn task route* protected from Cybermask and short-range movement.
- **Caution:** “Three versus one” is **the last-scoring Classifed comparison**, not necessarily a certified final OP tally. Exact speaker/score attribution should be rechecked in the original audio.

**List-building takeaway:** retain **FO Puppetbot + a couple of final Orders to reach the enemy HVT zone**, not only Interventor killing capability.

## #098 — Svalarheima vs Bakunin: Frostbite

**Primary:** [Champion of Terra full video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bm4hEFOTy_0). **Frostbite** mission, version/date unverified; **not a core ITS17 mission**, so do not misclassify as regular ITS17. **A one-point WinterFor win**, reconstructed **about 5–4**, needs scorecard confirmation.

- **Observed:** Frostbite includes **Specialist consoles activating heaters**, a cold-zone effect killing exposed non-exempt bodies, **exclusion-zone dominance**, casualties and Classifieds. WinterFor had **Uma Sorensen HMG + Nisse HMG, Nisse Sniper + Machinist, Shona, Karhu Paramedic, Liang Kai, Beast Hunter, Kunai Ninja, Fugazi and TR Thunderbolt REM**.
- **Observed:** Bakunin brought **Zoe/Pi-Well, Reverend Custodier Hacker, Riot Grrl, Bran do Castro, Orphan, Moderators/Initiates, Sin-Eater ARO, Vertigo Guided REM**. It used **White Noise and repeaters → Spotlight → Guided** to threaten a valuable PanO shooter even when a direct ARO trade was poor.
- **Observed:** The final count pitted **WinterFor exclusion-zone weight (Uma surviving for three points)** against **Bakunin's greater number of active heating units and a Classified**. The winner was determined by **army points killed** after the main 3–4 point categories.
- **Inference:** **Nisse/Uma plus Machinist and protected Karhu** is a useful cold/exclusion-zone package, while **Bakunin Custodier/Zoe/Vertigo and heater specialists** can deny the HMG force enough action economy to score a larger victory. The one-point swing prevents a confident general faction ranking.
- **Caution:** Transliteration of the final point assignment is imperfect. **One-point WinterFor edge is supported; exact 5–4 provisional**.

**List-building takeaway:** calculate **which bodies survive after heater effects** and protect an Engineer/console runner; mere killing is not enough.

## #099 — JSA Oban vs OSS: Corporate Appropriation, concession

**Primary:** [Tabletop Throwdown casual game](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ubSCli9i884). Played in **late-2025 holiday context**, N5.2/ITS17 plausible. **Oban wins by OSS concession**, no normal 3-turn final score.

- **Observed:** OSS included **Asura Lieutenant, Shukra Chain of Command, Danavas Hacker, Garuda Combat Jump, Probot Baggage, and a major Hawkwood gunfighter/team**, spending heavily on expensive focal pieces. Oban had **Tanko Mono CC, Shikami, Daiyokai, Keisotsu HRL, motorized Bounty Hunter and Eclipse/Smoke**.
- **Observed:** **Keisotsu Heavy Rocket Launcher** eliminated **Hawkwood** early in a poor-odds engagement for OSS. **Garuda's Combat Jump failed**, eliminating the planned flanking rescue attack. Asura did advance, but **Tanko Monofilament CC** defeated the remaining relevant contesting model.
- **Observed:** Oban **retrieved the enemy prototype** and was positioned to carry it safely into its DZ, while OSS had no reachable troop/Order route to intercept. The OSS player said they **could not win** and conceded.
- **Player assessment:** the host recommends considering **EVO support for Garuda landing and other Hacker synergy**; the OSS player also considers earlier **Asura flank movement** instead of overcommitting to the same firing lane. Those are *unplayed alternatives*, not tested wins.
- **Inference:** **Tanko/Shikami/Eclipse plus Keisotsu HRL** can protect a prototype carrier against an OSS force that depends on **one expensive primary attacker and a risky airborne backup**. Do not turn an OSS concession into a numeric faction result.

**List-building takeaway:** use **separate prototype courier and Anti-Materiel/D-Charge attacker**, with a ground-based backup if Garuda fails to land.

## #100 — Invincible Army vs Vanilla Yu Jing: Area of Interest (tutoring game)

**Primary:** [Tabletop Throwdown IA vs Vanilla Yu Jing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=33WisGOjvd0). An explicit **teacher-versus-newer-player** TTS match; **Area of Interest**, patch/date unknown. **Final: Invincible Army 9–1 Vanilla Yu Jing**.

- **Observed:** Experienced Invincible Army player chose **first turn** in a mission allowing early **Special Area of Interest placement** and generated a large lead through **HMG/sniper/Haidao lane support, TR/Flash REMs and protected zone scorers**.
- **Observed:** Beginner Vanilla Yu Jing had **Long Ya Camo/Minelayer, Bixie, a large HI/vehicle attack package, Beast Hunter and Hacker**. **Prematurely revealing a Camo piece** in ARO, parking a large attacker/pilot where buildings constrained it, and an awkward **Beast Hunter deployment far from the mission center** made the attack inefficient.
- **Observed:** IA retained first-turn area scoring and protected its own pieces while the newer player spent Orders unsuccessfully trying to open long shooting lanes. The final tally was **IA 9–1**; teacher and student discuss **Camo marker timing and mission deployment** in the postgame.
- **Inference:** Strong **IA gun coverage + advantageous Area placement** punishes a player with limited experience in radial/lateral mission geometry. **This is not proof IA is stronger than Vanilla Yu Jing or that Bixie/Long Ya are bad choices**.
- **Caution:** **Do not use this 9–1 tutoring result in sectorial win-rate or must-have/avoid rankings**. It is a deployment and objective-order lesson.

**List-building takeaway:** choose special-zone locations with the **actual scoring units and safe approach orders** in mind; retain **Long Ya/Camo threat** until revealing actually helps win the mission.
