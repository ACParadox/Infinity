**Research date:** 2026-10-29. **Linked catalog:** [N5 battle-report index](n5-battle-reports-and-tournament-reports.md), entries **1–10**, in the **same numbering and order**. These are *ten catalog entries*, several of which recap multiple separate matches. The source evidence is YouTube video transcripts accessed via the connected YouTube transcript reader plus the referenced Corvus Belli forum descriptions. **Do not turn these observations into statistical win rates**.

## How to read these notes

- **[Observed]** = the presenter/player or livestream identifies a specific event, play or outcome. Narrated reports may reconstruct an opponent list imperfectly; direct match footage carries greater weight.
- **[Player assessment]** = a first-hand player's opinion about utility, choices or what should have happened. It is not automatically a generally true matchup advantage.
- **[Inference]** = a tactical implication drawn from an observed example, worth testing elsewhere; never a universal sectorial-versus-sectorial ranking.
- **[Caution]** = a rules/mission or evidence limitation, including mistakes acknowledged by the players or commentary. Opponent lists, scores and successes not actually recoverable from a source are **not invented**.
- Exact points, SWC, Fireteam compositions and legality **must be checked against the game-date Army build**. Current N5.3 rules/rosters are useful for *present-day adaptations*, not proof that a 2025 report used those profiles. Refer to [Kestrel](../../sectorials/panoceania/kestrel-colonial-force.md), [Qapu Khalqi](../../sectorials/haqqislam/qapu-khalqi.md), [Military Orders](../../sectorials/panoceania/military-orders.md) and [White Company](../../sectorials/na2/white-company.md) for current legal options.

## #001 — Winning Borderbash 2026 — Next Wave multi-game tournament victory

**Primary:** [Infinity the Dante, full 2026 Borderbash report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ux3_bYsFUQ4) (approximately 101 minutes). **Corroboration:** [Corvus Belli event retrospective](https://infinityuniverse.com/en/news/event-recap-borderbash). **Rules:** N5.2 + May 2026 Army; ITS17. **Opponents discussed:** including a Next Wave/Achilles mirror, updated Operations Subsection, and Corregidor Deepspace; report also describes a Su-Jian-centered opponent. **Packet:** Superiority, B-Pong, Akial Interference, Cutthroat and Radial Unmasking. **Result:** presenter won the event.

### Units and combinations

- **[Player assessment] Achilles as Key Ops / center of the aggressive Next Wave list.** High personal impact, survivability and close combat make the expensive centerpiece attractive on Superiority and Cutthroat. However, fitting Achilles into Next Wave takes so many points that the rest of the list has **less redundancy** than the player would like. Multiple **Harbinger Forward Observers** supply cheap(ish) Regular Orders and real specialist bodies to restore some coverage; they are not merely cheerleaders. See video **0–13 min**.
- **[Observed] Harbingers + defensive Puzzlers with E/M/adhesive-type denial.** The Puzzler exposed itself at a critical corner and repeatedly frustrated the Su-Jian; eventually the Su-Jian was stopped by adhesive control rather than an expensive direct gunfight. Another Puzzler deployment was intended to fork Achilles between a projectile, a hacking exposure and an ARO at a choke point. **Application:** cover an elite attack route with more than one response type. See roughly **26–40 min**.
- **[Observed] Defensive deployment of Ikadrons/repeaters, Glyph Warcor, Shrouder/Shooter, and specialist Harbingers.** An opponent must pay Orders to penetrate the corridors and find the truly valuable units. The Akial Interference list used **Pandora** as its only ordinary hacker/Spotlight source; the narrator specifically calls her a **single point of failure** alongside a small number of Classified-capable pieces. Pairing a premium assault package with thin Classified coverage is risky. See roughly **12–25 min**.
- **[Observed] OSS counter-matchup:** Asura hacking plus a **Dikpala** shooting duo, Artalis Engineer, and later an OSS package with **Tarkshya / Racerbots / Post-Human support** illustrates a very different way to handle a costly Achilles army: hacking, repeatable durable shooting, and recovery. The Cutthroat round hinged on whether Achilles could cross an exposed firelane to reach the Asura. It did, surviving an enemy critical hit and removing the Asura, but later succumbed to a recovered attack piece. See roughly **54–94 min**.
- **[Player assessment] Key Ops and reserve construction are mission-specific.** The player made one highly aggressive list for the Key Ops-heavy missions and a different list for Akial Interference/Classified work, rather than pretending one Achilles configuration solves all scoring formats.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Observed] Next Wave vs updated OSS is a real contest of expensive individual capability vs layered hacking/recovery**, not evidence of a generic faction advantage. Losing an Asura or Pandora can abruptly change the threat network; losing a high-impact attacking piece can collapse Next Wave's mission tempo.
- **[Observed] The presenter deliberately changed the final against Corregidor from a cautious mission-centered plan to an Achilles-forward aggression plan** after seeing the matchup. Corvus Belli's recap confirms this change and the final-round win.
- **[Inference]** In order-hungry objective scenarios, count how many Harbingers and genuinely competent specialists remain *after* the centerpieces fight. Avoid spending early turns trying to win every gunfight if the protected scoring floor disappears.
- **[Caution]** Several outcomes depended on failed button rolls, extraordinary critical hits and saves. Do not label Achilles an automatic win against Asura/OSS or Corregidor based on the final placement.

**Reusable lesson:** A premium attack piece should be surrounded by *diverse scoring bodies, network defense and denial*, not merely more high-cost guns.

## #002 — ITC 2025 Round 1, Unmasking — Kestrel vs White Banner

**Primary:** [Maslow R1 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEHha2wc81Y); [tournament thread](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-itc-2025.43503/). **Played:** Apr 12–13, 2025. **N5.1 / ITS16.** **Result:** Kestrel win, per narrator.

### Units and combinations

- **[Player assessment] Black A.I.R. MULTI Sniper in a Fennec team** was the principal long-range, hard-to-ignore ARO; **Fennec FO** gives the same architecture mission-scoring capability. The player stresses that **Albedo or White Noise** are actual gear checks for the MSV-dependent sniper. It is not literally unbeatable.
- **[Player assessment] Griffin Feuerbach + Firefly Engineer/Deactivator** supplies anti-armor/anti-TAG reach, the ability to clear enemy Pitchers and a backup gun. **Blink X-Visor HRL** gives a *different* lane/response when the premium visor ARO is suppressed or denied vision. This is a layered ARO plan, not three identical guns.
- **[Player assessment] Hetkari as the midfield assault/fight-winning piece, Banshee as a flank/rear follow-up**, and a **Trauma-Doc** as recovery insurance. The list intentionally distributes threats so the opponent cannot defeat it by neutralizing the Black A.I.R. alone.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Observed] White Banner brought Camo/infiltration pieces and mobile specialist attackers**, and the narrator explains that Unmasking let the opponent attack HVTs **without challenging the premium ARO network head-on**. The two players largely raced HVTs/objectives instead of direct FtF engagements.
- **[Observed] This is not proof Kestrel beats White Banner in a firefight:** the player explicitly reports almost no meaningful opposed shooting between their models. The direct lesson is that an excellent gunline may fail to protect HVTs positioned in an accessible part of the board.
- **[Inference]** For Unmasking, deploy mines, close-range templates, repeater/marker detection and physically obstructed HVT approaches; do not value a beautiful Black A.I.R. sniper lane more than the HVT access route. White Banner's Camo trades can be *mission-efficient* even when they die.
- **[Caution]** The presenter reconstructs the **opponent's exact list from memory**. Treat opponent profile details as probable, not an independently verified Army import.

**Reusable lesson:** Excellent shooting can be strategically irrelevant if the opponent can score without exposing a valuable trooper.

## #003 — ITC 2025 Round 2, Frostbyte — Kestrel vs White Banner

**Primary:** [Maslow R2 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TvQU61YsPWw); [ITC thread](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-itc-2025.43503/). **Played:** Apr 12–13, 2025. **N5.1 / ITS16.** **Result:** Kestrel win, per narrator.

### Units and combinations

- **[Observed] White Banner Long Ya Camo AROs + Hundun sniper** act as an early defensive layer; **Haetae/Ye Mao/Jujak/Huáng fireteam** combined offensive weapons, TinBot, Disco Baller/vision control, a linkable Pitcher/hacker package and anti-armor utility. **Jing Qo + Shaolin Monks** supplied a dangerous low-cost movement/CC attack package.
- **[Player assessment] Long Ya is often valuable for its unrevealed state and first-use weaponry**, rather than because it can be repaired indefinitely. The narrator questions paying an Engineer/Servant to resurrect a Long Ya after it has already expended its disposable ARO weapons and lost its Camo uncertainty.
- **[Observed] Kestrel used Firefly/Griffin to clear a hostile Pitcher/repeater and important opposing team components**, opening a route for **Hetkari's direct attack**. Once Hetkari forced a defender's facing/dodge and threatened the middle, a **Banshee Parachutist** punished the exposed back angle.
- **[Observed] Jing Qo + Shaolin advanced through a vulnerable corner and killed several Kestrel support/order pieces**, threatening to reverse the game despite Kestrel's initial board advantage. The Griffin stopped Jing Qo after a lucky critical outcome; coverage gaps mattered more than the absolute quality of Black A.I.R.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Observed] Frostbyte requires correct heater/zone planning, not just tabling the opponent.** Kestrel used **repair/recovery → Sensor REM → console activation** to power a heater, while the White Banner threat of durable/key heavy troops in the central area remained relevant.
- **[Inference]** A premium Kestrel attack package can defeat White Banner's linked Hacking/Pitcher shell *if* the repeater is eliminated first. That is a **sequence-dependent** advantage; charging Hetkari into live TinBot/network coverage is a different matchup.
- **[Inference]** Check side lanes for Jing Qo/Shaolin and other fast close assault pieces even if the main sightline is guarded by the sniper. A single surprise flank may eliminate repair/support and force a change of plan.
- **[Caution]** Individual fireteam names and gear are reconstructed/narrated in imperfect speech-to-text; source yourself against the 2025 video, not N5.3 costs.

**Reusable lesson:** Clear enabling networks first; then commit expensive pieces; keep a cheap recovery route and scoring REM.

## #004 — ITC 2025 Round 3, Firefight — Kestrel vs Torchlight Brigade

**Primary:** [Maslow R3 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=py5_R6TU-hw); [ITC thread](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-itc-2025.43503/). **Played:** Apr 12–13, 2025. **N5.1 / ITS16.** **Result:** Kestrel win, per narrator.

### Units and combinations

- **[Observed] Torchlight tested an extreme zero-specialist list**: **Jamie + Hellblazers** aggressive link, **Wreckers** as durable heavy attackers, **Nightshade/Moonrakers** for approach/midfield pressure. It is presented by both players as a deliberate stress test for the **Firefight** mission, not a representative all-comers Torchlight list.
- **[Observed] Jamie found an unexpected line through the ARO network**, eliminated/disabled Kestrel pieces and threatened the scoring margin. The Kestrel player initially could not take ordinary fair engagements with the Wreckers efficiently.
- **[Observed] Fennec Disco Baller blocked the Wreckers' key line**, allowing **Hetkari** to reach Jamie. Jamie's adhesive/launcher interaction briefly stopped Hetkari. A **Speedball intervention and Sensor REM recovery** restored momentum; Hetkari subsequently killed Jamie. **Loss of Lieutenant** then reduced Torchlight's remaining action economy.
- **[Observed] Trauma-Doc recovered Black A.I.R.; Griffin/Firefly and Hetkari finished the remaining Wrecker pressure.** The player attributes the victory principally to **Griffin + Black A.I.R. + Hetkari**, with the recovery/vision-control pieces enabling them.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Player assessment] Zero-specialist Firefight can deny an opponent some specialist-kill scoring**, but becomes brittle if the attack centerpiece dies, and sacrifices flexibility/mission utility. The narrator thinks removing *all* specialists is too extreme even for this mission.
- **[Inference]** In Firefight, a specialized kill-focused squad may make sense; still retain enough Classified/opportunity coverage and independent attackers. Against a Jamie-led alpha team, hold **overlapping side/back angles**, not merely a straight sniper lane.
- **[Caution]** A lucky decisive FtF roll and failed/failed-recovered state checks affected the outcome; don't infer Kestrel inherently counters Torchlight or that the zero-specialist concept has been competitively validated.

**Reusable lesson:** Vision-blocking and repair can transform a trapped expensive gunfighter into a successful counterattack; losing the opposing LT can be more valuable than chasing every Wrecker.

## #005 — ITC 2025 Round 4, Supremacy — Kestrel vs Vanilla Combined Army

**Primary:** [Maslow R4 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8aj3Ueyvp3s); [ITC thread](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-itc-2025.43503/). **Played:** Apr 12–13, 2025. **N5.1 / ITS16.** **Result:** Kestrel comeback win, per narrator.

### Units and combinations

- **[Player assessment] Second Kestrel list was built for Supremacy/Supplies:** **Hidden Deployment Scarecrow** to start near a scoring zone/objective, **Yamabushi** as an independently deployed CC threat, **Crux FTO + Fennec + Bipandra Haris** for reliable mission access and high-value shooting, and **Redeye** as a fast reserve attacker/late-zone controller. Black A.I.R. + Blink still supplied defensive pressure.
- **[Observed] Combined Army used a classic Bit & Kiss / Anathematics-or-Charontid-level hacking shell, Q-Drone, Pitchers, remote repeaters and powerful active shooting**. The first-turn **Redeye** alpha looked promising on paper but burned most Orders and died trying to defeat the Q-Drone, leaving Combined's order pool mostly intact.
- **[Observed] Kestrel recovered by clearing active repeater/pitcher coverage**, using a **Disco Baller** to bypass a dangerous sightline, pushing **Crux** into exposed supports and **Bipandra/Crux/Fennec** into contesting positions. By the late game, the surviving Kestrel AROs and button control could restrict Combined's final scoring routes.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Observed] Supremacy rewards *quadrant weight, console access and timing*.** A fast expensive Redeye can contribute scoring points quickly, but an unsuccessful alpha strike is terrible when it leaves too few Orders for actual mission actions.
- **[Player assessment] The narrator believes Combined could have consolidated its advantage earlier by advancing a strong hacker/attacker more decisively**, rather than being distracted by additional Guided attacks. This is an opponent-decision critique, *not* an intrinsic Kestrel superiority.
- **[Important source-quality warning] The narrator suspects the Combined player mistakenly deployed an additional R-Drone that did not belong in the list**, and states its repeater coverage materially affected the Redeye's route. This is an allegation/after-the-fact observation, **not** an independently established rules infraction; do not use the round as clean statistical balancing evidence.
- **[Inference]** Build **independent scoring redundancy**: a failed Redeye attack need not end the game if Hidden Deployment specialists, durable Haris members and small consoles/zone controllers remain.

**Reusable lesson:** Never consume the scoring floor in a high-variance alpha attempt. Repeater destruction can be worth more than another low-percentage Guided attack.

## #006 — Burn City Brawl 2025: Qapu Khalqi five-round report

**Primary:** [Infinity the Dante Qapu Khalqi tournament recap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9BA7lqd1PMA). **Forum timestamps and opponents:** [Burn City Brawl post](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-burn-city-brawl-2025.43585/) — [list explanation 02:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9BA7lqd1PMA&t=175s), [R1 Torchlight Brigade 19:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9BA7lqd1PMA&t=1179s), [R2 Vanilla Combined 36:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9BA7lqd1PMA&t=2210s), [R3 Vanilla Ariadna 49:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9BA7lqd1PMA&t=2994s), [R4 White Banner 1:02:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9BA7lqd1PMA&t=3744s), [R5 Shindenbutai 1:17:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9BA7lqd1PMA&t=4667s). **Played Sep 13–14, 2025.** **N5.1.1 / ITS16.5 (explicit in source)**. Custom Hammer of Dawn was played alongside B-Pong, Evacuation, Firefight and Capture & Protect.

### Units and combinations

- **[Player assessment] Qapu Khalqi has strong *vision control*, but poor natural mobility compared with the chosen missions.** Pair **Yuan Yuan smoke** with **Djanbazan MSV2 AP Sniper/HMG** for variable-range smoke shooting and suppression; a Djanbazan Duo is a relatively compact buy-in. The narrator emphasizes Regeneration as a useful but probabilistic way to keep these guns relevant.
- **[Observed] Linked/integrated **Laxmee** hacking + a **TinBot-protected heavy body** made Pitcher assassination against her unattractive, while **Leila Sharif's Combat Instinct KHD/Carbonite toolkit** gave the list a different answer to aggressive hackers. This is *hacker protection plus threat projection*, not just a big gun and a healer.
- **[Player assessment]** **Bashi Bazouk** with Holoprojector and T2 Boarding Shotgun/extra AP templates is unusually good at finding safe rear/flank attacks and clearing expendable traps. A **Yuan Yuan** remains a cheap way to threaten bad angles and place Smoke; it is not interchangeable with a durable late-game objective carrier.
- **[Observed] Round 1 vs Torchlight:** a **Wave Rider Pitcher → Spotlight/Guided** sequence removed **Fiddler**, a mission-critical piece, early. This is excellent *tool targeting*: kill the enemy's actual scorer/delivery piece before their gunfighter. The presenter points to a misdeployed Thunderbolt remote that made the Pitcher route feasible.
- **[Observed] Round 2 vs Vanilla Combined:** the opposing **Achilles + Overdron + smoke + hacking** list was chosen partly as an anti-MSV counter to Qapu's Djanbazans. The presenter felt the Combined attack allocation was suboptimal: Achilles could have threatened the Iguana directly while Dartok threatened Laxmee; this remains a *postgame interpretation*.
- **[Observed] Round 3 vs Vanilla Ariadna:** multiple Camo attacks plus **Chornobog AP HMG, Vystrel/Tankhunter anti-armour and mobile Dynamo/Bront** made an unexpectedly serious non-hacking anti-TAG threat. Camo markers force deployment discipline even against ostensibly superior Qapu MSV guns.
- **[Observed] Round 4 vs White Banner:** conventional **Haetae/Jujak/Huáng TinBot + Hacking/Pitcher** infrastructure, smoke/Disco Baller, **Liang Kai/Shaolin** and a **Tiger Soldier Hacker** created multiple pressure axes; a TAG with an exposed large silhouette had trouble escaping. The narrator finished narrowly behind White Banner on points.
- **[Observed] Round 5 vs Shindenbutai:** the report describes a bloody, attritional game and mission-unit losses. **Critical rules caveat:** the players accidentally deployed/used HVTs in Capture & Protect when the mission did **not** call for them; the presenter explicitly acknowledges this and that it affected the reported 2–1 victory. Treat this round's result as **compromised for mission-performance inference**.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Player assessment] Qapu Khalqi performed better than the presenter's initial fears but struggled to achieve *large* wins** across a packet asking for fast deployment-zone penetration, long objective carries and Evacuation mobility. It cannot be labeled “bad at all mobility missions” from one event; the **list-build tax** to cover those missions is the useful insight.
- **[Inference]** Vs heavy TAG or hackable opponents, **Djanbazan smoke/AP + guarded Laxmee + Leila + flank Bashi** form complementary counter-threats. But against Combined's anti-MSV/White Noise or White Banner's mobility, *the support pieces must remain protected*. A single Djanbazan firing lane is not reliable matchup coverage.
- **[Caution]** At this event the source calls the packet **ITS16.5**, while ITS17's announcement came around the same calendar period. Avoid treating the two seasons as interchangeable. The reported Shindenbutai result includes the admitted objective error.

**Reusable lesson:** Choose *mission-delivery units* before an impressive gunline. Threatening the enemy's ability to score can beat killing a premium attack piece.

## #007 — Burn City Brawl R2, B-Pong — Bakunin vs White Banner

**Primary:** [Loss of Lieutenant full stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QM8zXEUi1Bg); [forum round index](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-burn-city-brawl-2025.43585/). **Played:** Sep 13–14, 2025. **N5.1.1 / ITS16.5.** **Source type:** actual livestream with commentary, not merely retrospective narration. **Participants:** Jacob on Bakunin, Ivory on White Banner.

### Units and combinations

- **[Observed] Bakunin's linked **Cyclone Pitcher** projected a repeater far across the board.** The casters explicitly comment on the accuracy of the linked/X-Visor shot and the way it forces White Banner to respond. It can open Spotlight/Isolation routes, even without committing the orders to build a full Guided Missile engine.
- **[Observed] Bakunin also fielded **double Stigmatons**, Fiddler and Jackbots, an **Uberfall** close-combat threat, and a complementary Hacker/control shell.** The Jackbots can physically hold or contest areas, but the forward-staged Fiddler became a highly valuable target for White Banner's close-range pressure.
- **[Observed] White Banner threatened with **Daofei** as forward gunfighter, **Liang Kai** to reach and trade into Fiddler, **Shaolin smoke**, a mobile **Tiger Soldier** entry point, and its own Pitcher/Guided/ARO infrastructure.** Liang Kai's successful trade cost a high-threat attacker but degraded Bakunin's beacon/objective mobility.
- **[Player/caster assessment] The casters say Jacob should likely have **protected Fiddler longer** because she was essential to the mission plan.** Linked repeaters and premium TAGs are not enough if the models tasked to score or secure beacons disappear.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Observed] The revised B-Pong scores movable beacons/three central objectives in a way that gives the *second* player some late-game options.** The commentators contrast the difference between moving/guarding the beacon and moving it when a trooper is touching it. Always read the exact **ITS16.5** mission text.
- **[Observed] White Banner deliberately thought about whether taking the first turn would let it act *before* a dangerous Bakunin repeater grid formed.** Conversely Bakunin used projected hacking to constrain Daofei/other Hackable advances. This is a good example of an *initiative choice made relative to networks*, not simply “always go second in B-Pong.”
- **[Inference]** Against double-Stigmaton Bakunin, White Banner can target the **enabler/scorer network** (Fiddler, Hackers, beacon controller) rather than insisting on destroying both TAGs. Against White Banner, keep valuable midfield markers and combat attackers away from linked Pitcher-supported Hacking Areas.
- **[Caution]** A lively livestream includes speculative caster opinions and rule clarifications in real time. **No categorical sectorial win-rate conclusion or exact score** is asserted here without a separately checked final official result.

**Reusable lesson:** A linked Pitcher is a strategic weapon even if the resulting hacking kills nobody; keep the mission's actual movers alive.

## #008 — Burn City Brawl R3, Evacuation — Vanilla Combined Army vs Imperial Service

**Primary:** [Loss of Lieutenant R3 stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LEHdLcZQDGI); [forum round listing](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-burn-city-brawl-2025.43585/). **Played:** Sep 13–14, 2025. **N5.1.1 / ITS16.5.** **Players:** Josh (Vanilla Combined Army), Dennis (Imperial Service).

### Units and combinations

- **[Observed] ISS fielded **Adil (Lieutenant) + Bounty Hunter Duo**, **Wu Ming AP HMG + SMG Duo**, a **Celestial Guard/Kuang Shi Core**, and a **Wu Ming TinBot (-6) + Pheasant KHD + Zhanying Pitcher Hacker Haris**. These are different, complementary tools: a durable assault gun, protected cyber team, cheap command/Orders and independent scoring.
- **[Observed]** **Zhanying Pitchers + Pheasant/Wu Ming TinBot** contest Combined's hacking game while the **Wu Ming AP HMG** can punish overextended attackers. An accompanying **Engineer/Paramedic** (when available in a legal list) supports a durable late-game gun package; don't confuse an HMG's ability to trade kills with Evacuation's actual CIVVAC scoring.
- **[Observed] Combined used **Achilles Lieutenant** as a centrally staged assault piece backed by a **Daturazi smoke approach**, powerful long-range shooter/defensive game elements, **Bit & Kiss / Base Operator support**, and a Guided-capable REM. The first-turn attack severely depleted ISS resources. Achilles' durability and placement mattered at least as much as kill count.
- **[Observed] After the alpha strike, **ISS Wu Ming AP HMG** produced a meaningful counterattack; **Adil** provided a later mission push/CIVVAC attempt.** The footage also shows a **Kunai Ninja** emerging as a hidden ARO and being defeated; this is an example of how a premium Hidden Deployment reaction can fail and leave the scoring pieces exposed.
- **[Player/caster assessment] Command tokens were contested between order stripping, moving Orders across groups, and Speedball use.** Both players afterward said they had forgotten/neglected some Speedball opportunities. Their limited order budgets were a real determinant of which evacuation objectives were reachable.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Observed] Evacuation is not merely an “alpha strike wins” mission.** A force can remove much of the enemy and still need intact specialists/Orders to approach civilians, perform the needed WIP actions and CIVVAC. Conversely a damaged defender may claw back points with one surviving mission-oriented Duo.
- **[Inference]** Versus Combined Achilles, ISS should deploy to **deny a single continuous Achilles attack path** and avoid exposing the entire cyber network + Lieutenant/Orders to the same angle; its Haris offers anti-hacking tools but doesn't make it invulnerable.
- **[Inference]** When choosing between aggressive Achilles turns and secondary-group scoring, reserve enough specialist Orders to complete the mission. The stream's closing discussion suggests that Combined's **Group 2** would have benefited from different assignment/utility.
- **[Caution]** This is one featured round on a live table, not evidence that Combined always defeats ISS or that a given TinBot package necessarily stops Achilles.

**Reusable lesson:** Separate the *attacker who breaks the board* from the *specialist and Orders that actually score the evacuation*.

## #009 — New Year Knockout 2026 — Military Orders, three-round invitational

**Primary:** [Quiet Contender full tournament recap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGZ--rtGqXA); [forum index with timecodes](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-new-year-knockout-2026.43779/). Chapters: [Briefing 00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGZ--rtGqXA), [Game 1 12:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGZ--rtGqXA&t=758s), [Game 2 21:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGZ--rtGqXA&t=1263s), [Game 3 28:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGZ--rtGqXA&t=1712s), [After-action 37:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGZ--rtGqXA&t=2225s). **Jan 2026, N5.2 / ITS17 explicitly spoken by author.** **Player:** Military Orders, **third overall** among six participants. **Opponents:** Morat Aggression Forces (Critical Intervention), USARF, Oban (Evacuation).

### Units and combinations

- **[Player assessment] The speaker deliberately brought two **HMG attack modes**:** a **Crosier MSV2 HMG + Santiago KHD + Crosier Paramedic Haris** and a **Knight Hospitaller HMG Core**. One responds to smoke/Mimetism/range exposure, the other supplies reliable long-range damage. Relying only on Spitfires leaves Military Orders struggling to reach some dangerous long-range AROs.
- **[Observed] A **Curator Engineer + Palbot**, **dual Trinitarian Forward Observer/Minelayer with deployed Repeaters**, **Crusader FO airborne specialist**, and **Knight Commander Chain of Command** addressed very different risks: Imm-B/Isolation recovery, midfield hacking/scoring, unexpected rear-angle access and active Lieutenant protection. The narrator praises the Crusader as a problem solver against an exposed hostile disruptive attacker.
- **[Observed] In Round 1 Morats, a linked **Suryat HRL and Suryat AP HMG** removed multiple key Knights/engineer pieces.** This shows why the nominally stronger heavy-infantry team can still be overwhelmed if a wide deployment permits the opponent to attack each key function in sequence.
- **[Observed] Against USARF, **Crosier MULTI Sniper** locked down approach lanes and surviving Knights kept specialists safe.** The player describes a victory aided by favorable rolls, not an automatic proof that Military Orders counters low-tech Ariadna.
- **[Observed] Against Oban, **Yojimbo** rushed toward a Santiago while **Ryuken-9 / Ninjas or shadow markers / Aragoto / Keisotsu** threatened the flanks. Layered Crosier/Hospitaller fire punished the direct assault and the **Trinitarian specialist** was then able to pursue an objective.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Player assessment]** **Critical Intervention** was chosen for defensive second-turn planning in Round 1, but an aggressive Morat strike demonstrated that *going second only helps when the first-turn defensive shell survives*. Defensive and reactive positioning must account for long-range linked templates.
- **[Observed] For **Evacuation**, the presenter emphasized **Classified-first, then CIVVAC**, assigning closer civilians to Trinitarians and a far civilian to the Crusader's unexpected arrival route.
- **[Player assessment] At the end, he recommends **low-variance Orders** after terrible dice—mines, equipment placement, repositioning and Suppressive Fire—and notes physical/mental fatigue during a very hot event.
- **[Caution]** The author repeatedly warns that the opponent's rosters are reconstructed from memory and several decisive outcomes swung on extreme dice. His **third place** is not a demonstration of sectorial dominance.

**Reusable lesson:** In Military Orders, a secondary HMG, reliable Engineer access, CoC and two independent scoring lanes often matter more than upgrading one Knight to a slightly better weapon.

## #010 — Arcfinity 2025 — White Company five-round tournament report

**Primary:** [Infinity the Dante Arcfinity WhiteCo tournament report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hiLOsN7c7zE); [forum index](https://forum.corvusbelli.com/threads/n5-tournament-arcfinity-2025.43524/). **Played by May 19, 2025. N5.1.1 / ITS16**, with event extras **Reinforced Command** (no Loss of Lieutenant in that event's format) and **Stealth Operations**. **Opponent examples:** Combined Army, Bakunin, Morats; the presenter states a **five-game win streak**, earning **best overall**, while the event also counted hobby/sportsmanship standings.

### Units and combinations

- **[Player assessment]** **Varangian + Shona Carano Duo** is a compact smoke delivery system with a dedicated dangerous CC finisher. The Fireteam bonus improves smoke reliability; Shona can leave the Duo to pursue an assassination once the line is open, so a linked smoke unit need not tether an expensive assault model forever.
- **[Observed]** **Haidao MSV2 MULTI Sniper + Hawkwood K1 Sniper** in a Core (with **Laxmee, a Rifleman, and Señor Massacre** in the narrated configuration) form a **dual-vision, different-ammunition ARO/offense package**. Haidao threatens through allied Smoke, Hawkwood can remain useful under friendly **White Noise**. The second non-visor gun is not redundant: it offsets enemy Albedo/White Noise aimed at the primary MSV shooter.
- **[Player assessment]** **Laxmee Pitcher/Hacking + Varangian Smoke + Haidao/Hawkwood guns + Thunderbolt TR REM** creates layered vision, network and ranged denial. The TRbot is valuable when it forces the opponent to spend Orders/reposition even if it ultimately falls.
- **[Observed]** **Liang Kai**, Shona and forward-borne **Tiger Soldier** give alternative access into defensive lines where the main snipers cannot find a safe shot.** The player illustrates this by sneaking mission pieces around a strong long-range ARO and by forcing expensive Combined fighters into poor ranges.
- **[Observed] Against a Morat/Combined match, a **cheap Daturazi template run killed Hawkwood** despite its premium defensive setup, demonstrating that a big gun is not protection against a close template angle.** The WhiteCo player then shifted to securing buttons/objectives and protecting the remaining denial network rather than forcing a repeat of the same gunfight.

### Matchup, mission, tactics

- **[Observed] Arcfinity played **Biotech 4, Highly Classified, Annihilation, Supremacy and custom Mega Turret**.** The player intentionally built one list for **Biotech 4 / Mega Turret** (mobility and damage), and another for **Highly Classified** (specialist diversity). The apparent two-list restriction created an opportunity-cost tension for Annihilation/Supremacy.
- **[Player assessment] White Company is better suited to missions where it can bring long-range board control, aggressive assault pieces and broad hacking/vision tools; its *specialist midfield* is less naturally dominant.** That is why he preferred this particular event packet, **not** proof that White Company is top-tier at all ITS16 missions.
- **[Observed] In one game the narrator describes the enemy spending several Orders dealing with the Thunderbolt TRbot or awkward approach rather than progressing the scoring plan.** This is an example of **ARO order-tax value**, not merely kills.
- **[Caution] Custom Mega Turret has special behavior.** The player warns that its exposure/range can sometimes let a long-range gun simply outrange and shoot it instead of pressing intended weakening buttons. This is a **scenario design/exploit warning**, not an ITS rule. The reinforced-command extra similarly invalidates generic Loss-of-Lieutenant conclusions.

**Reusable lesson:** WhiteCo can build a **Smoke + MSV sniper / White Noise + non-MSV K1 sniper** fork, but its tournament strength depends heavily on mission selection, protective Fireteam geometry and keeping a specialist plan intact.
