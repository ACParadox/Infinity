# Infinity N5 — Why games are actually won: decisive mechanisms, objective conversion and Order-budget failures

**Prepared:** 2026-11-02  
**Corpus:** [master index #001–#241](n5-battle-reports-and-tournament-reports.md), numbered [tactical analyses #001–#240](001-010-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) through [#231–#240](231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), and [partial #241](241-241-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md). Related syntheses: [unit role value](unit-value-role-effectiveness-n5.md) and [repeatable synergies](n5-repeatable-unit-synergies.md).

> **Principal finding:** The winner is often the player who converts *position, eligible task workers and remaining Orders* into OP at the packet's scoring moment—not the player who inflicts the most casualties. Killing matters insofar as it opens a scoring route, destroys an opponent's scoring capability, denies useful Orders, or itself awards OP.

**Do not interpret the percentages below as the fraction of causal success “due to” each factor.** The reports do not provide a representative set of complete turn logs, counterfactual outcomes or independent effects. This document provides (a) evidence-based causal *hypotheses*, (b) a deliberately selected **24-match, fully enumerated annotation pilot**, and (c) a protocol for a defensible future whole-corpus estimate.

## 1. The case that motivates the analysis: #199 Neoterra vs Torchlight Outbreak

[**Report #199 — NCA 6–5 Torchlight, ITS17 Outbreak**](191-200-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), [original full-game recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i9CNGTPN9G4). **Observed score:** Neoterra 6, Torchlight 5; N5.2 / ITS17 midseason probable, exact played date not established.

- **Combat picture:** Torchlight's Stormbot/Silverstar/Moonraker/other attacks removed nearly all Neoterran models. *The player winning material exchanges did not win OP.*
- **NCA's scoring pipeline:** **3 scanned infected + 2 stabilization points + 1 secured infected = 6 OP** as recounted. Early actions were already converted into scored outcomes before the final losses. The remaining forward **Sāchā** and other early-scanning/medical task pieces demonstrated the importance of keeping **an eligible path**, rather than protecting every gun.
- **Torchlight's scoring pipeline:** its scanned/stabilized/secured-infected tasks totaled **5 OP**. Late scan failures and attempts to deny NCA's already-established lead were not enough to reverse it.
- **Why the result occurred:** **Objective timing/conversion** is the strongest *observed* explanation. **Specialist access and preservation** enabled that conversion; **positioning** mattered because objectives and task-eligible bodies needed to meet. We cannot quantify exactly what proportion each contributes or predict a rerun with successful scan rolls.
- **Counterfactual question (not observed):** Could Torchlight have won by spending earlier Orders stopping the first NCA scan/stabilize sequence, or preparing more late attempts, instead of destroying more enemy material? The report strongly **suggests** this, but it was not rerun as a controlled test.

**Scope guard:** Outbreak has specialized Doctor/Paramedic/CivVac/scanning interactions; the lesson is **scoring actions are not retroactively erased by kills**, not that NCA is generically superior to Torchlight or that casualties never matter.

## 2. A five-mechanism taxonomy — what a “reason for victory” means

| Code | Winning mechanism | Directly observable evidence | Weak inference to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **K — Combat / killing** | Removing specific enemies **directly** creates the decisive scoring or action advantage; includes actual mission points **for casualties** | A decisive Lieutenant/eligible specialist death, opponent Order pool destroyed, an explicit kill-scoring category, key ARO removed *with Orders still left* | “Winner killed more models” is not enough; it may be incidental or even harmful if it triggers Retreat! at the wrong time |
| **P — Position / route control** | The winner reaches/occupies the *right scoring space* or prevents access to it at the actual scoring time | Quadrant weight, zone boundary, final objective contact, movement geometry, flank access, concealed occupying marker, safe approach lane | Owning “the middle” or having a threatening TAG does not imply occupying the exact zone, delivery circle, objective or end-of-round area |
| **T — Objective timing / conversion** | Converting available resources into **actually awarded OP**, including objective actions, timing, repeated WIP attempts, last-turn delivery, and mission-specific destruction | Button presses, collected/delivered cargo, scanned/stabilized targets, completed Classifieds, destroyed scenario hardware, pre-scored points | Treating every kill or uncompleted pickup as an achieved mission action; comparing OP types across nonidentical packets |
| **S — Specialist/recovery capacity** | Retaining, replenishing or repositioning **legally eligible and reachable** task workers, especially after alpha strikes | Surviving Hacker/Doctor/FO/Paramedic, Engineer revives critical model, backup courier makes last action, correct skill whitelist | Counting a living model as “preserved specialist value” if it cannot perform or reach the task before scoring |
| **D — Action denial / tempo** | Constraining the opponent's *usable* actions via hacking, Loss of Lieutenant, mines, forced Resets, blocked routes and expensive defensive clearance | Opponent Orders spent clearing a mine/ARO; Immobilized/Isolated scorer; possession; lost Regular Orders or thwarted objective path | Giving a passive unit fictitious “wasted Orders”; confusing Orders spent on necessary attacks with pointless expenditure |

**These dimensions overlap.** A killer can remove a Specialist (**K + D**), a hidden unit can hold a zone while denying movement (**P + D**), and an Engineer can restore a TAG so a scoring body survives (**S + P + T**). They are **coded independently**, with one **interpretive primary mechanism** per game *only for the descriptive pilot*. A primary label is a summary of the narrated turning point, **not scientifically isolated causality**.

### Objective timing subtypes to preserve

- **T1 — early banked OP:** scored early and retained despite later material collapse (#199); the objective packet must actually preserve these points.
- **T2 — last-action conversion:** a final button/zone transfer decided the result (#092, #093, #115).
- **T3 — task-chain completion:** clear lane → approach → qualify → roll/pick up → transport → deposit; half-completion does not yield the full OP (#189, #194).
- **T4 — mission-specific destruction:** anti-materiel/D-Charges/CC used on actual objectives (#216, #223, #232). **Destroying an objective is T, not an enemy troop kill K.**
- **T5 — Classified breadth / packet-specific OP:** the player performed scoring actions that a combat-heavy opponent could not (#212, #214).

**Do not call every expensive attacker bad.** Strong positive counterexample: [#216](211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), **Shona used about nine Orders and destroyed all three mission servers**, directly producing the 10–1 scoring sequence. **Same large Order outlay, opposite efficiency** because it completed the actual task. In [#181](181-190-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md), two Seraphs destroyed a Corporate Appropriation objective in a champion's winning list; attack and scoring were aligned. The question is **which objective was finished**, not just how many Orders were spent.

### Four failure classes and the better decision

- **A — Alpha sunk cost:** high-variance gunfight consumes the available first turn without creating a task advantage. **Better:** set a maximum *scoring-compatible* attack budget and reserve a second lane; if it fails, switch to the fallback scorer.
- **B — Defender fixation:** spending five-plus Orders on a dangerous ARO that can be bypassed, suppressed, blocked with Smoke/White Noise/Disco, or safely ignored. **Better:** attack only defenders covering the *required* objective route.
- **C — Incomplete scoring pipeline:** actor obtains the object or opens a console but has no Orders for delivery, placement, final WIP rerolls or scoring-area occupation. **Better:** count **all stages** before the first shot, including the final return.
- **D — Wrong threat/role coverage:** opponent can still score with cheap specialists even when heavy guns dominate. **Better:** prioritize their eligible scorers or protect your own, rather than kill the most expensive enemy model.
- **E — Harmful over-kill:** extra casualties provoke Retreat! or deny subsequent scoring turns in a packet with particular end-state rules. **Better:** check when the game/round actually ends before firing another optional shot.

## 5. An actionable *Order-to-OP* decision rule

Before committing a premium attacker, construct a **minimum remaining scoring budget** for the current combat group (and any group-transfer/command-token plan). Use *actual scenario requirements*:

\[
\text{Orders remaining after attack} \;\geq\;
\text{route Orders} +
\text{interaction Orders} +
\text{delivery/placement Orders} +
\text{reserve for likely retries or interruptions}.
\]

This is an **operational planning inequality**, *not* a formula derived statistically from the reports. If the attack has already spent that reserve, it has probably become an **attack-first trap**, unless the attack itself completes the scored objective, removes a critical opponent scoring actor, or makes the opponent unable to respond.

**Illustrative hypothetical, not a sourced match:** an army begins a turn with **10 usable Orders**. A mission route requires **2 movement + 1 console + 2 carry/return + 1 placement = 6 Orders** before contingencies. With **one retry reserved**, the discretionary attack budget is **3 Orders**, not all ten. An attacker consuming **7** to eliminate a nonessential ARO has not made progress if the courier can no longer finish.

### Measurements to extract when footage permits

| Metric | Meaning | Evidence rule |
|---|---|---|
| \`active_order_pool_start\` | Actually usable Orders for this unit's group at the beginning of the relevant active turn, plus explicit transferable special Orders | Do not treat irregular/Lt/TA/NCO Orders as freely interchangeable |
| \`attack_orders_spent\` | Orders actually spent shooting, fighting, Spotlight/Guided, moving only for offense and attempting to remove AROs | Break out **failed attacks** from **movement to objective** |
| \`attack_fraction_of_usable_turn\` | \`attack_orders_spent / active_order_pool_start\` | **Only** calculate with a fully established pool and group access; “9 Orders” alone does not prove 90% |
| \`minimum_objective_chain_orders\` | Best viable route + eligible interactions + load/carry/deploy + required contest at that point in the actual game | State whether derived from filmed position or player commentary; record uncertainty |
| \`orders_left_when_attack_ended\` | Remaining usable Orders in the **relevant group** after the attack | When a model switches groups or uses NCO/TA, reconstruct those mechanics correctly |
| \`objective_chain_complete\` | Whether required final scoring action actually completed before the scoring deadline | Separate **consoles touched**, **objects picked up**, **objects delivered** and **OP awarded** |
| \`scoring_opportunity_forgone\` | A player/narrator specifically identified a plausible scoring action that was not affordable/reachable | If speculative, label **counterfactual**, not a confirmed lost OP |
| \`opponent_order_tax\` | Explicit enemy Orders used clearing, Resetting, Discovering or bypassing the threatening unit | Never assign “would have cost 4 Orders” as observed |
| \`opponent_future_orders_denied\` | Confirmed LT / killed Regular Order engine / Isolation / possession action suppression | Keep separate from opponent Order expenditure; otherwise **double-count** denial |
| \`timing_phase\` | Early round, scoring window, active turn 3, final Order, or before early termination/Retreat! | Often the **most explanatory** field of the entire record |

**Key distinction:** \`damage_success=true\` and \`mission_conversion=false\` are entirely possible together (#231/#214/#239); likewise \`material_loss=true\` and \`mission_win=true\` (#199). Measure them separately.

## 6. Win-causation rubric for annotating additional games

**Analysis unit:** **one unique match** (\`game_id\`), not one podcast/YouTube URL or one tournament episode. A tournament retrospective may contain multiple matches; two indexes may describe the same match. Source-to-game many-to-one relationships should be stored.

| Field | Type / allowed values | Why |
|---|---|---|
| \`game_id\`, \`source_report_ids\`, \`canonical_video\` | Identifiers / URLs | Deduplicate #011/#030, #009/#028, #010/#033, #022/#026, overlapping event descriptions #161/#237 |
| \`event_id\`, \`round\`, \`players\`, \`factions\` | Provenance, may be unknown | Distinguish **independent games** from repeated players/lists/episodes |
| \`rules_revision\`, \`its_packet\`, \`mission\`, \`point_limit\` | Historical factual labels | Avoid applying N5.3 scoring or models to N5.1.1 or custom games |
| \`official_vs_custom\`, \`rewind\`, \`concession\`, \`played_to_end\` | Flags | Filter off-format/rewound/projected games before pooled estimates |
| \`winner\`, \`op_a\`, \`op_b\`, \`score_precision\` | Winner/draw/unknown; confirmed/estimated/projected | A 6–5 explicit score differs from “probably 9–1” |
| \`victory_mechanisms\` | K,P,T,S,D each **present / absent / unknown**, *with a source-action citation* | Nonexclusive mechanism prevalence, not guesswork |
| \`primary_mechanism\` | One K/P/T/S/D or \`unclear\`, **with rationale** | Transparent qualitative attribution; inter-rater agreement later |
| \`specific_turning_point\` | Factual sequence; opposing action; timing; actor | Required to move beyond “winner had better positioning” |
| \`opponent_more_casualties?\` | yes/no/unknown, source-backed | Identify **won while losing attrition** subset separately |
| \`winner_banked_op_early?\` | yes/no/unknown | Direct test of #199's central hypothesis |
| \`winner_retained_eligible_specialists?\` | count + eligibility + reachability / unknown | Distinguish life from meaningful task capability |
| \`objective_chain_failure?\` | none/approach/interaction/cargo/delivery/scoring/unknown | Identify “almost scored” traps |
| \`attack_orders_spent\`, \`order_pool\`, \`remaining_scoring_orders\` | Numeric/range/unknown; **no inferred zero** | Quantify high-priced attacker overcommitment |
| \`opponent_orders_forced\`, \`states_denied\`, \`lt_loss\` | Documented/unknown | Distinguish denial/tempo effects |
| \`luck_or_rules_caveats\` | Text or flag | Prevent unusual critical streaks, missed packet rules or variant geometries masquerading as faction balance |
| \`counterfactual_claim\` | Text **explicitly marked not tested** | Distinguish “could have ignored TAG” from action actually attempted |
| \`reviewer\`, \`review_confidence\` | high/medium/low + source type | Enable re-audit and coder agreement |

### A strict rating of causal confidence

- **High:** filmed decisive action, explicit points before/after, clear eligibility/rule conditions and no material uncertainty about whether that action scored.
- **Medium:** first-hand report or reconstructed turn where objective attribution is clear but Order count, exact point total, or mission version is uncertain.
- **Low:** commentator preference, single source without footage, disputed rules, custom scenarios, counterfactual, narrated outcome with omitted key turns. **Can teach tactics; must be excluded from strict numeric estimates.**
- **Unknown:** source does not establish a decisive action. Never convert unknown to “no kill value” or assume objective timing made the difference simply because all Infinity games award OP.

## 7. What actual *population percentages* would require

The 241-entry index **is not 241 uniformly described independent games**: it includes re-uploads, multiple entries for one event, full matches, recaps with multiple matches, custom missions, partial/transcript-inaccessible entries and non-match metadata. Do **not** write “83.3% of Infinity games are won by objective timing” from the hand-selected pilot.

To estimate reliable *observed prevalence* for the indexed population:

1. **Enumerate every identifiable distinct match**, including draws and losses, keyed by original event, players, mission and video where possible.
2. **Define inclusion rules before examining the turning point:** standard 300pt matched play, known mission and winner, finished/scorable result, sufficient narrated or video evidence, no uncorrected material rule violation; separately retain an all-format sensitivity cohort. Do **not** preferentially include dramatic mission-first outcomes.
3. **Code both players' actions** and **all five mechanisms** using a fixed rubric. Give coders the full reports *without priming for the result* if feasible. For uncertainty, use **unknown**, not absent.
4. **Review duplicates and coder disagreement.** If two independent coders disagree on the single primary cause, record \`mixed/unclear\` rather than forcing 100% certainty.
5. **Calculate** (a) **fraction of eligible wins where each mechanism was documented**, with denominators for each non-missing code; (b) **primary attribution** on games with a defensible primary; (c) results **stratified by mission type, faction archetype and patch**; and (d) a **separate Order-overrun frequency** among games with enough Order logging. Report uncertainty intervals and **selection/missingness coverage**.
6. **Do not call these causal contribution percentages.** Even a complete observational sample does not isolate how much a kill, positional move or task completion *caused* the outcome. For stronger causal claims, study alternate lines under controlled replay, compare matched mission setups, or model conditional action/OP probabilities with measured confounders.

A sound future result could say: “Among **N independently reviewed, qualifying scored matches**, the decisive episode **involved objective timing in x/N (y%)**, and an explicitly logged attacker spent at least half its usable Order pool in **a/b (c%) of adequately logged attacks**.” It should **not** assert “timing causes y% of wins” without intervention/counterfactual evidence.

## 8. Mission-specific insights and counterplay

| Mission family | Frequent failure exposed by the reports | More reliable pre-game decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Outbreak / scanning / infected** | Damage after banked scans cannot erase earlier OP (#199); an unrelated fast defender may delay but not stop task-compliant medical runners (#116) | Identify **who can perform which scan/stabilize action**, where infected begin, and which early points must be prevented **before** attacking |
| **Supremacy / Area of Interest / Battleground** | Kills but insufficient quadrant/area weight or one step across wrong line (#005, #092, #213 draw, #231, #239) | Check **end-of-round exact geometry**, last-turn zone mover, marked survivor and player having second turn |
| **Supplies / Provisioning / Data Harvest** | Remove ARO, pick up item, then discover no Orders/route for actual delivery (#189, #194) | Budget **pickup + travel + deploy** as one indivisible mission pipeline; reserve backup carrier |
| **Hardlock / consoles** | Attackers dominate firefight but cannot make enough **WIP attempts** (#096, #102, #151) | Preserve **qualified WIP actor + multiple Orders** in the right group; cyber-counter the actual terminal defender |
| **Countermeasures / Classified-heavy Firefight** | Triple TAG attacks cannot compensate for missing skill repertoire (#212, #214) | Build **redundant and broad Specialist skills**, not merely better guns; use cheap model sacrifices to open a task route |
| **Mindwipe / anti-materiel** | Opponent tries to win shooting while Shona or Seraph physically destroys targets (#216, #181) | Count all legal ways to destroy/objective-score; guard **server access and task tool**, not only long sightlines |
| **Custom / variant scenarios** | A ten-Order monster combat / unusual eligibility rule changes whole action economy (#071, #240) | Keep a separate custom-mission cohort; never pool these with standard ITS scoring percentages |

### Counterexamples that matter as much as wins

- **#216:** spending ~9 Orders can be **excellent**, if all three actual mission objectives are destroyed. Thus “Order-hungry” is not an absolute negative.
- **#137 and #004:** decisive kills, especially a Lieutenant or vulnerable scoring structure, can be the **dominant** event. Avoid the opposite overcorrection, “kills don't matter.”
- **#151 / #197:** a nonlethal disabling state can **remove a mission opportunity** as effectively as a casualty while preserving the defender's own attack options.
- **#092 / #239:** an inexpensive final zone body can outperform a strong attacker *for the last OP*, without implying the attacker was never useful.
- **#005:** a failed expensive alpha can be **recovered from**; the right unit of analysis is the *turn's attack-to-score sequence*, not a whole game's winner/loser only.

## 9. Working conclusions

1. **Victory explanations must use OP and action sequences, not casualty counts alone.** #199 is the clearest illustration: Neoterra banked six mission points while losing most of its army.
2. **Objective conversion/timing appears most often in the selected 24-match pilot**, but its 37.5% primary-code share is a **pilot narration statistic**, not a causal percentage of Infinity winning.
3. **Killing and denial are enabling mechanisms.** When they remove the exact enemy scorer, block the scoring lane or award kill OP, they can be decisive. When they consume all the active Orders and leave no task, they can be counterproductive.
4. **The useful unit is the one that is eligible, positioned and still active at the scoring moment.** #092, #093, #115, #199 and #239 demonstrate variants of this rule.
5. **Most overcommitments are diagnosable before dice are rolled** by calculating the *minimum full scoring chain* and treating the remaining Orders as the actual allowable attack budget. Exact case-level Order fractions require more complete footage.
6. **Next research milestone:** expand the pilot into a unique-game, two-sided, mission-stratified annotation dataset with explicit missing data and measured Order budgets, **then** report frequencies with known denominators and uncertainty.

Prefer adding a separate, protocol-selected full-corpus dataset instead of silently presenting convenience-sample percentages as scientifically validated effects. Preserve every original historical rules/score/misplay caveat.
