## Hardlock scoring framework (source-independent, not a transcript of #241)

For **ITS17 Hardlock at 300 points**, the documented scenario uses **two Beacons** (one per player), **three central Consoles**, and a **12-inch-wide deployment zone per side**. The relevant scoring in the [ITS17 scenario reference](https://infinity-missions.com/) and [ITS17 PDF transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/927558198/its-rules-season-17-en-v1) is:

| Scoring mechanism | Tactical impact |
|---|---|
| At the **end of each Game Round**, **Control the Enemy Beacon** | **1 OP / round**; early stealing / getting a survivor in contact yields a recurring payoff |
| At **end of game**, **your Beacon not Controlled by enemy** | **1 OP**; avoid leaving the home Beacon unprotected in round 3 |
| At **end of game**, have **more Activated Consoles** than opponent | **2 OP**; a console swing is potentially larger than one kill |
| At **end of game**, score **each Activated Console** | **1 OP per activated console** |
| Classified | **1 OP** in the ITS17 version described by source |

**Scenario rules are conditional on the played packet**. Specific words such as **“Control,” “Activated,” and “move/steal Beacon”** follow the historical ITS17 rules; recheck if the video uses ITS18. **Specialists** activate Consoles with WIP interactions; simply putting a powerful TAG next to a console cannot substitute for having the right specialist if it lacks the required scenario skill. Player must plan **console button route, Beacon access and the end-of-round/end-of-game timing** together.

## Conditional matchup analysis: Winter Force into Shindenbutai

The following is a *useful preparation checklist* for **WinterFor facing Shindenbutai on Hardlock**, **NOT a report of which units were fielded in #241**. Unit roles and compositions must be rechecked against the applicable N5 Army release. Where Shinden observations exist, they come from *other numbered reports*, identified below.

### 1. MSV fire lanes versus Shinden smoke and marker screens

- **Potential WinterFor tool — Nisse / other allowed MSV shooter:** If available in the historically legal list, a **Nisse MSV2 long-range profile** is a candidate to contest **Yojimbo smoke** and **Mimetism-mediated approach** to center. Do not take this as proof #241 used a Nisse, or that MSV automatically bypasses every visual MOD, marker, or terrain interaction.
- **Supported comparison:** Shinden players in **#224** and **#233** used **Yojimbo, Shizoku, Hatamoto and Tanuki** layers to project fire, smoke, and hacking/mines into central objectives. A **Shizoku Burst-enhanced heavy rocket launcher** in #233 was especially dangerous against exposed mid-table clumps; **Raiden HRL** provided another surprise ARO.
- **Inference:** WinterFor wants one or more **well-spaced long-range MSV/gunfighting angles** to prevent Shinden's smoke from simply granting free console touches, while protecting a **second Specialist** that can advance without sharing the main gun's route. Against **Raiden/Shizoku hidden rocket AROs**, avoid ending a Core/Haris with several members inside a single blast template.
- **Counterplay for Shinden:** Do not assume a smoke lane is safe while **Nisse MSV2** can observe it; instead isolate that shooting angle, approach through obscured terrain, use inexpensive **Koalas/markers** to tax PanO Orders, or force Nisse to fight outside its preferred range.

### 2. Expensive WinterFor armor/TAGs versus a wide specialist spread

- **Potential WinterFor tools:** **Jotum / ORC / Vargar / other durable long-gun elements** can create deterrence and eliminate threatening **Hatamoto** or **Shizoku** if legally available and appropriately positioned. **A WinterFor Machinist / Infirmarer / Blade-Ops / Fusilier-linked specialist** is a different job: actually **activate consoles and cover Beacon routes**. Do not assume all these units appear in #241 or any one Fireteam pairing is legal at all N5 dates.
- **Supported comparison:** **#233** showed how **Shindenbotai Yojimbo and an aggressive Shizoku-linked gun** can collapse a capable Torchlight defense. **#237 Hardlock** (vanilla Ariadna vs Invincible Army) showed that **scoring and protecting an enemy Beacon with mines and limited Orders** can defeat a powerful heavy-gun response.
- **Inference:** A **Jotum / Nisse gun-first plan** may win face-to-face rolls but can lose Hardlock if it cannot **activate at least two consoles** or keep the captured Beacon safe at end of each Game Round. Reserve cheap Specialists and sufficient Orders to do both. A **Machinist + TAG** is attractive for recovering the gun platform, but only if the engineer also serves a scored role / is safely positioned; don't burn all first-turn Orders pursuing opponents' far shooters.
- **Counterplay for Shinden:** Use **Hatamoto NCO / Lt-order economy, Tanuki hacking and independent Yamabushi/Specialists** to split pressure across **two distinct consoles and the enemy Beacon**. If WinterFor concentrates its surviving points in a TAG, bypass it to score rather than turning Hardlock into an unneeded armor duel.

### 3. Hacking, E/M and fast specialist counter-moves

- **Conditional Shinden threats:** **Tanuki Hacker/Pitcher**, **E/M/Koala screening**, **Raiden camo**, and fast **Yojimbo** can tax WinterFor's heavy platforms while leaving **Yamabushi Paramedic or other specialists** free to contest a console. In **#224**, Shinden used **Tanuki/Pitcher-linked long-range pressure** in a very different mission; **#233** included **Tanuki MSV2 and Hacker/Chain of Command** variants.
- **Conditional WinterFor response:** Bring a credible **anti-hacker route** (a KHD only if actually legal in the dated roster, a repeater-clearing shooter, or BTS-backed TAG protection) and keep **specialists out of chain-reaction blast lines**. If the main assault element is hackable, identify enemy repeater coverage *before* moving onto center-line consoles. A **Nøkken-type camouflaged Specialist** could provide a less predictable route **if** that exact profile is legal and in the list; do not presume the source used one.
- **Inference:** **Hacking pressure is valuable only when it converts into denied or successful mission interactions**. If it takes most of Shinden's Orders to incapacitate a WinterFor TAG while the opponent flips consoles elsewhere, the Shinden attack may be scoring-negative. Conversely, if WinterFor exposes its only Specialist to Tanuki hacking while it chases Hatamoto with a Jotum, it may be too late to win the console race.
- **Evidence limit:** The #241 source does not confirm anyone had a Nisse, Nøkken, Jotum, Tanuki, Hatamoto, Shizoku, etc. All such references are **hypothetical list-building options**.

### 4. Order and initiative planning specifically for Hardlock

- **Going first:** Can aggressively deny the enemy Beacon and bank early round scoring **only if** the force can **defend the controlled Beacon** against the reactive second player; also ideally flip one or more consoles. In **#237**, Ariadna made a **going-first Hardlock attack** work after a large attrition advantage and protected a Beacon in its deployment zone, despite that author's warning that going first can be difficult.
- **Going second:** Benefits from the **last opportunity to flip consoles and retrieve/dispute a Beacon**, but can lose the round-one OP and Order pool to a successful first-turn intrusion. **#238** demonstrated a **last-Order Taowu Specialist recovery** winning 4–1 for Imperial Service, though it was a distinct ISS-vs-Corregidor match.
- **Map test for this pairing:** Place the three consoles and both Beacons; measure how many Orders a **WinterFor Specialist** or **Shinden Yamabushi/Specialist** needs to (a) reach one console, (b) activate, (c) intercept or steal a Beacon, (d) finish **with surviving non-Null models** in the relevant contact position. Include an alternative route around **Nisse/Shizoku/Raiden** fire lanes. This can determine turn choice better than general “go second for objectives” advice.
- **Fallback:** Where a TAG or elite Hatamoto spent an opening turn shooting, proactively decide whether **one additional anti-order kill is actually worth fewer WIP attempts / one less Beacon-control OP**.
