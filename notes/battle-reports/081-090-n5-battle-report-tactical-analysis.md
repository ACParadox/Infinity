## #081 — Vanilla ALEPH vs Vanilla Haqqislam, Hardlock

**Source:** [Tabletop Throwdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mo6sw1-JiFk). **Result: Haqqislam 4–3 ALEPH**, according to the final scoring. JoshPosh played Haqq and Nighthawk ALEPH.

- **Observed:** **Saladin** order economy with **Muttawi'ah Jammer/E/M, Bashi Bazouk disguise/entry, Hidden Tuareg Hacker and other cheap specialists** gave Haqq multiple options. The players debate **HoloMask Saladin decoys**, but the precise tell through repeater measurements is an *unverified live ruling*.
- **Observed:** ALEPH's **Maximus** could dominate a lane but suffered **E/M and AP/Albedo attacks** while competing for **beacon control**. Haqq activated **two buttons and more total consoles**, reported worth **four points**; ALEPH retained **one Classified and beacon scoring/denial** worth three.
- **Inference:** **Saladin order conversion + Jammer disruption + independent button runners** is a credible Hardlock answer to an elite ALEPH anchor. More kills on Maximus would not help if the controlling model remains within the beacon radius. Preserve a second ALEPH specialist outside its main attack.
- **Caution:** The players omitted HVT models and debated exact denial/cover/HoloMask rules. This is not global Haqq-over-ALEPH matchup evidence.

## #083 — Morat Aggression Force vs Starmada, ITS17 Crossing Lines

**Source:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hom6IFqhN0k). Recorded as **new ITS17 mission testing**; app not yet current, actual played date/patch uncertain.

- **Observed:** **Starmada** (not generic O-12) used **Casanova and mobile shooting/paramedic support** to keep bodies in *dead-zone scoring areas*. Morats fought with **aggressive Fireteams and close-ranged pressure**; during turn two the host explicitly calculated a **4–1 intermediate** standing, but the exact final is not verified.
- **Observed:** Players used **rear deployment plus a special forward four-inch strip**; decisions about how much a surviving trooper was *worth inside a zone* drove activation choices. A linked **Paramedic restored an ally**, preserving scoring weight.
- **Inference:** **Starmada's fast, versatile team** suits Crossing Lines when one piece stays in each dead zone. Morats can punish a forward-deployed force if it exposes too much Order and point weight to the first attack.
- **Caution:** The video's *closing* discussion is about **Corporate Appropriation as the NEXT mission**, not a game in this entry. Don't mix prototype/ADS rules into Crossing Lines.

## #084 — Next Wave vs Imperial Service, Domination

**Source:** [Official Corvus Belli × Vanguard Tactics report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cTpOZzeFCY4). Corrected opponent: **Yu Jing Imperial Service**, not Vanilla Yu Jing. **Mission: Domination** (four quadrants, central consoles), N5.2+ era.

- **Observed:** Next Wave **Achilles Lieutenant, Juggernaut, Ironside, Harbinger Hacker, Steeljaws, Contrabandero and Cliff-Jumper Combat Jump** challenged the **Adil, Koga Ninja, Major Lunah, Crane/Pheasant/Wu Ming and Su-Jian** ISS package. A **Harbinger Hacker activated an early console** while Cliff-Jumper arrived to split defensive angles.
- **Observed:** **Juggernaut** reached **Major Lunah** on a roof, removing a strong sniper ARO, but **Adil** later removed Juggernaut. A **Pheasant melee push against a powerful close-range defender** also proved costly. **Su-Jian mode-switch/mobility** created an alternate route to contest quadrants.
- **Inference:** **Juggernaut anti-sniper leap plus Harbinger button** is a coherent Next Wave plan, but the scoring piece must survive once Juggernaut trades. **ISS Su-Jian + Ninjas** can threaten an entirely different quadrant instead of trying to beat Achilles in a fair duel.
- **Caution:** No unambiguous final numeric scoreboard in closing video. Avoid global Next Wave-over-ISS ranking.

## #085 — Bakunin vs Military Orders, ITS17 Hardlock

**Source:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTcDgXBN9Hw). N5.2 likely; **Bakunin won** per closing commentary, numeric tally not securely established.

- **Observed:** MO **De Fersen Spitfire Hacker + Hospitaller Doctor, Crosier MSV2 HMG + Mendoza + Paramedic, Teutonic/Montesa, Santiago Killer Hacker, Montesa biker and support**. Bakunin countered with **Kusanagi/Reverends, Pitcher, Zero Hacker/Camo, Orphan and TAG support**.
- **Observed:** A **Montesa** attempted to clear a Pitcher/Kusanagi attack corridor and was lost. **Zero** later reached and activated a **Hardlock button** despite elite opposition. The commentators note an extremely costly **De Fersen vs Zero Hacker** exchange; it is an individual dice/state encounter, not a permanent profile ranking.
- **Inference:** **Bakunin's protected Zero plus Kusanagi/Pitcher network** creates two scoring/denial routes. MO must retain **another safe WIP specialist** rather than commit every important model with De Fersen to one lane.
- **Caution:** Live **Classified target and Fireteam bonus** rulings are contested; verify historical ITS17 profile legality.

## #086 — Caledonia vs White Banner, ITS17 Critical Intervention

**Source:** [GMG x Tactical Awareness](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p9s9bmtYH9A). **N5.2/ITS17**, explicit practice for **Autumn Assault Nov 22–23, 2025**. Asymmetric room mission: defender Caledonia, attacker White Banner. The player reports **two attacker points**; complete defender total remains unverified.

- **Observed:** Caledonia **Wallace, McMurrough, Cameronians, Galwegians, Kaden/Wulvers, Mormaer, Isobel and Wardriver Disco** made multiple room-door barriers. White Banner brought **Shang Ji, Lei Gong, Adil, Dokkaebi, Haetae and specialist/gun Fireteams**.
- **Observed:** **Wulver/Kaden close-combat and Mormaer shooting** stopped heavy infantry; room-entry Orders were lost trying to remove Scottish fighters. Players conclude the White Banner list should have **pressed the server room earlier** rather than fight so many Warbands.
- **Inference:** **Caledonia's wall of cheap templates/Smoke and multiple hard CC models** is particularly well suited to *defending one room*. White Banner needs **a protected specialist and a separate Disco/Smoke entry plan**, using Shang Ji only to clear the blocking lane.
- **Caution:** Asymmetric attacker/defender OPs are **not directly comparable**; no verified conventional faction-wide win rate follows.

## #087 — Tartary Army Corps vs Svalarheima, Acquisition

**Source:** [Champion of Terra](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-y8pC2FDbFo). **Mission Acquisition**, two antenna specialist objectives and a central Tech-Coffin. Actual date/N5 revision unknown.

- **Observed:** One side claimed **both antennas, the Tech-Coffin and a Classified**, while the opponent reported **zero**. The auto-translated closing conversation does **not reliably assign the terminal tally to one faction**, so do not invent an exact winner/score.
- **Observed:** TAK's **Camo/Kazak shooting, Traktor artillery and area denial** faced Svalarheima **Liang Kai Super-Jump/CC, Knight of Justice and elite linked guns**. A **Knight of Justice completed an HVT Classified**, and **Liang Kai** crossed toward exposed support troops.
- **Inference:** **Liang Kai/Knight mobility** can bypass a static TAK gunline in Acquisition, but *only if* a real Specialist flips the antennas while another troop secures the coffin. TAK likewise needs more than a Tankhunter/Traktor ARO shell.
- **Caution:** The faction associated with “all points” is not verified from the extracted translation; avoid any unsupported 10–0.

## #088 — Hassassin Bahram vs Shindenbutai, Frontline

**Source:** [Robert Shepherd narrated battle report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ntnnk22Tyx8). **Frontline**, patch unconfirmed. **Confirmed narrator's score: Hassassin Bahram 8–3 Shindenbutai.**

- **Observed:** **Fiday** exploited impersonation and a **Speedball Vita Pack** to threaten **Tanuki Chain of Command/Hacker and Panguling**, eventually eliminating critical support after several Orders. **Hassassin Shakush HMG TAG** attacked **Hatamoto**, but after a Pilot/GizmoKit recovery the **surviving Japanese melee attacker** destroyed Shakush again.
- **Observed:** The **Hatamoto/Tanuki-linked shell** proved difficult to remove due to Mimetism, durable models and strong CC. Yet **Hassassin's Classified/specialist and far-zone play** earned middle and far Frontline scoring plus **three Classifieds**.
- **Inference:** **Fiday targeting Chain of Command and specialists** is more consequential than blindly attacking Hatamoto; the **Hatamoto's anti-TAG melee** is still an important JSA defense. The scenario victory does not mean Shakush efficiently counters Japanese heavy CC.
- **Player view:** Robert considers experimenting with **Hassassin HMG options**; clearly an unplayed suggestion, not proof of their efficiency.

## #089 — Next Wave vs TAK, ~200pt Crossing Lines

**Source:** [DefectiveDice episode 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DhlD-K3Rclw). **ITS17 Crossing Lines, around 200 points (slightly above)**, Next Wave Action Pack trial; exact N5.2-era patch unknown.

- **Observed:** Next Wave **Harbinger FO Core, two Raindancers, Ironside (cost-saving short-range gun), Steeljaw, Pandora, Contrabandero and Combat Jump** faced TAK **Veteran Kazak with X-Visor/Combat Instinct/Sixth Sense/Warhorse, Courier, Frontovik/launcher and Dog-Warrior/Camo**.
- **Observed:** The Raindancers were **close enough that one launcher/speculative template hit both**, scoring a critical against a supposedly safe duo. A **failed Combat Jump** removed Next Wave's backup flank; its owner wished he'd taken an **Ironside HMG** instead. TAK's **Vet Kazak** resisted the remaining direct attack routes.
- **Observed:** TAK led when Next Wave fell below the game's Retreat threshold, shortening expected scoring. The players discuss a **2–1/3–1-style score**, not a reliable conventional 300pt result.
- **Inference:** **Vet Kazak + long launcher + Camo** is an effective defensive package against **an under-gunned low-point Next Wave starter list**. More long-range active shooting and greater Raindancer spacing could change the fight.
- **Caution:** This is a **learning game**, not robust evidence of a TAK sectorial auto-counter.

## #090 — White Banner vs Nomads, ITS17 Provisioning

**Source:** [Fast Panda Gaming holiday episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rV62C7ZfEg8). Played in **Christmas 2025 context**, N5.2/ITS17. **Reported result: Nomads 10–1 White Banner**.

- **Observed:** White Banner invested heavily in **Haetae, Jujak, Dokkaebi, Liang Kai, Adil and multiple expensive Hackable troops**, with insufficient cheap independent carriers. Nomads used **Pitcher/Repeater hacking, multiple hackers, a TAG and Hellcat mobility** to attack that structure.
- **Observed:** One White Banner HI survived **roughly eleven saving rolls** before falling, yet that attrition resilience did not deliver cargo. The Nomads **Hellcat Super-Jump** collected a remaining box/present and moved it to the safe zone. White Banner earned **one HVT Espionage Classified** but little delivery scoring.
- **Player view:** White Banner deliberately used **painted/fun heavy infantry** and acknowledges being surprised by the enemy TAG and cyber pressure. This is *not* evidence Adil or Jujak are universally weak.
- **Inference:** **Nomads' protected hacker network plus a separate airborne delivery runner** can be strong against *Hackable-heavy White Banner* in **Provisioning**. A non-Hackable escort and more cheap carriers would address much of the observed problem.
- **Caution:** The Nomads' **sectorial is not confirmed from a full Army code**; label as Nomads/sectorial unknown.
