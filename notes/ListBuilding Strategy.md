# List-Building Strategy

## Core Fireteam size

Five-trooper Core Fireteams are relatively rare because they are very unwieldy to maneuver and position efficiently. The most common Core configuration is a three-trooper Core.

## Prefer Fireteam Level 2

As a general list-building principle, Fireteams should usually be built to reach at least the **Level 2 Fireteam bonus**.

That normally means either:

- a **Duo** with two troopers that share the same Unit name or Counts-as keyword; or
- a **Haris/Core** containing at least two troopers that share the same Unit name or Counts-as keyword.

This is especially important for **ARO pieces and low-Burst weapons**, because **BS Attack (+1SD)** has a disproportionately large effect when the weapon starts with a small number of dice.

There are legitimate exceptions. A Duo with no Level 2 bonus can still be worthwhile when the two models have strong complementary roles—for example, a high-Burst attack piece paired with a specialist that provides a key capability the attacker lacks. In that case, the order efficiency and role pairing can justify giving up the Level 2 bonus.

Well-rounded or individually strong troopers can also be used solo when they do not need Fireteam bonuses or a partner to perform their role, but this is generally less common than building around a useful Fireteam package.

## Weak troopers: minimize cost or create trade-up threats

As a general rule, avoid paying significant points and SWC to put conventional heavy weapons such as **HMGs, HRLs, or Missile Launchers** on low-quality troops such as basic line infantry. A weak trooper with low BS, little or no offensive MOD stacking, and low ARM is usually a poor platform for an expensive weapon: it struggles to win Face-to-Face Rolls and is easy to remove once exposed.

For weak troops, there are usually two better directions:

- keep them at **minimum cost** for cheerleader/order-generation purposes; or
- give them a cheap way to **trade up** or present a low-probability but genuinely dangerous threat to much more expensive enemies.

Preferred trade-up tools, roughly in order:

1. **Template weapons.** Usually the best option. Even if the weak trooper dies in the exchange, a successful template placement can guarantee the hit rather than asking a poor BS value to win a Face-to-Face Roll.
2. **Non-lethal disabling attacks.** Immobilization, E/M, Stun, adhesive effects, and similar tools can badly disrupt even a TAG or other premium piece. These are especially attractive when the attack uses a more favorable Attribute, attack mode, or range band than the trooper's normal weapon—for example a WIP-based attack instead of BS.
3. **Continuous Damage weapons.** Less reliable, but even a weak trooper can occasionally remove a strong multi-Wound target through a lucky sequence of failed Saves, giving the cheap model real trade-up potential.
4. **Strong Disposable weapons.** Panzerfausts, Flammenspears, and similar weapons are good ways to give an otherwise weak model a credible one-shot threat, particularly when their range bands or ammunition are better suited to the intended engagement than the trooper's normal weapon. Continuous Damage on a Disposable weapon increases the trade-up potential further.

The key principle is that a weak trooper should generally be either **cheap enough that little is invested in it**, or **dangerous in a way that does not depend on being a conventionally good gunfighter**.
