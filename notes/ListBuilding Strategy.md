# List-Building Strategy

## Core Fireteam size

Five-trooper Core Fireteams are relatively rare because they are very unwieldy to maneuver and position efficiently. The most common Core configuration is a three-trooper Core.

## Prefer Fireteam Level 2

As a general list-building principle, Fireteams should usually be built to reach at least the **Level 2 Fireteam bonus**.

That normally means either:

- a **Duo** with two troopers that share the same Unit name or Counts-as keyword; or
- a **Haris/Core** containing at least two troopers that share the same Unit name or Counts-as keyword.

This is especially important for **ARO pieces and low-Burst weapons**, because **BS Attack (+1SD)** has a disproportionately large effect when the weapon starts with a small number of dice.

There are legitimate exceptions. A Duo with no Level 2 bonus can still be worthwhile when the two models have strong complementary roles—for example, a high-Burst attack piece paired with a specialist that provides a key capability the attacker lacks. In that case, the order efficiency and role pairing can justify giving up the Level 2 bonus.

Well-rounded or individually strong troopers can also be used solo when they do not need Fireteam bonuses or a partner to perform their role, but this is generally less common than building around a useful Fireteam package.

## Weak troopers: minimize cost or create trade-up threats

As a general rule, avoid paying significant points and SWC to put conventional heavy weapons such as **HMGs, HRLs, or Missile Launchers** on low-quality troops such as basic line infantry. A weak trooper with low BS, little or no offensive MOD stacking, and low ARM is usually a poor platform for an expensive weapon: it struggles to win Face-to-Face Rolls and is easy to remove once exposed.

For weak troops, there are usually two better directions:

- keep them at **minimum cost** for cheerleader/order-generation purposes; or
- give them a cheap way to **trade up** or present a low-probability but genuinely dangerous threat to much more expensive enemies.

Preferred trade-up tools, roughly in order:

1. **Template weapons.** Usually the best option. Even if the weak trooper dies in the exchange, a successful template placement can guarantee the hit rather than asking a poor BS value to win a Face-to-Face Roll.
2. **Non-lethal disabling attacks.** Immobilization, E/M, Stun, adhesive effects, and similar tools can badly disrupt even a TAG or other premium piece. These are especially attractive when the attack uses a more favorable Attribute, attack mode, or range band than the trooper's normal weapon—for example a WIP-based attack instead of BS.
3. **Continuous Damage weapons.** Less reliable, but even a weak trooper can occasionally remove a strong multi-Wound target through a lucky sequence of failed Saves, giving the cheap model real trade-up potential.
4. **Strong Disposable weapons.** Panzerfausts, Flammenspears, and similar weapons are good ways to give an otherwise weak model a credible one-shot threat, particularly when their range bands or ammunition are better suited to the intended engagement than the trooper's normal weapon. Continuous Damage on a Disposable weapon increases the trade-up potential further.

The key principle is that a weak trooper should generally be either **cheap enough that little is invested in it**, or **dangerous in a way that does not depend on being a conventionally good gunfighter**.

## Judge profiles by functional role, not troop classification

Troop labels such as **LI, MI, SK, or WB** are not reliable strategic roles by themselves. Infinity contains cheap LI, elite LI, line-troop MI, elite MI, skirmisher-like MI, and many non-SK troops with Camouflage or advanced deployment.

Evaluate a profile primarily by what it actually contributes:

- cost and Order type;
- deployment position and mobility;
- survivability;
- Face-to-Face competency;
- specialist capability;
- passive utility;
- deployment-zone or midfield control;
- ability to trade up;
- whether it needs Orders to contribute.

The troop classification is secondary to the profile's real battlefield function.

## Cheap troops and line infantry

Cheap Regular troops are primarily part of the list's **Order economy**. The best cheap troops, however, usually contribute something useful without demanding many Orders.

A rough preference order is:

1. cheap Regular Order plus useful passive capability;
2. cheap Order plus specialist capability;
3. cheap Order plus Repeater, Sensor, Minelayer, Flash Pulse, smoke, template, or similar utility;
4. cheap model that can occasionally trade up into a much more valuable target;
5. pure minimum-cost Order generator when nothing better fits.

Cheap line troops are also useful as **Fireteam scaffolding**: inexpensive members can let a stronger attacker, specialist, or ARO piece reach useful Fireteam bonuses without paying for several premium models.

Do not overfill a list with pure cheerleaders. Cheap Orders are important, but excessive filler leaves too few models capable of actually solving problems, attacking, scoring, or controlling the table.

## Medium-cost and Medium Infantry profiles

There is no single generic "Medium Infantry strategy." Mid-priced profiles vary too much for the MI classification alone to determine their role.

The important principle is that a **medium-cost model should not merely be expensive line infantry**. Once a trooper costs enough that it is no longer an efficient cheerleader, it should justify that cost with meaningful competency or role compression.

A medium-cost profile should generally offer at least one of the following:

- strong Face-to-Face capability through BS, Mimetism, MSV, Surprise Attack, Marksmanship, or a strong weapon;
- meaningful resilience such as NWI, multiple Wounds, Immunity, strong ARM/BTS, or strong defensive MODs;
- valuable deployment such as Infiltration, Forward Deployment, Hidden Deployment, or Airborne Deployment;
- reliable mission capability with enough mobility or protection to actually reach objectives;
- multiple synergistic tools that are likely to matter from the same battlefield position.

Avoid paying a medium price for a model that is mediocre at several unrelated tasks and excellent at none of them.

## Toolbox units and multipurpose characters

Multipurpose units are strongest when their capabilities are both **competent and synergistic**.

A long list of skills is not automatically valuable. A good toolbox unit can perform several useful jobs without radically changing where it wants to be or how the list wants to spend Orders.

Examples of useful role compression include:

- competent gunfighter plus specialist;
- frontline hacker plus useful gun;
- Engineer accompanying the TAG or REMs it already wants to support;
- Sensor plus specialist plus useful deployable equipment;
- durable attacker that can also score objectives;
- attack piece that also contributes passive support or command utility.

Beware **toolbox bloat**: paying points for several mediocre or incompatible capabilities that will rarely be used in the same game plan.

Good generalists are also among the better candidates for **solo use**, because they are more likely to find a productive use for Orders without needing a Fireteam partner to make them functional.

## Skirmishers

Skirmishers are usually valuable because they create **Turn-0 and midfield value** before spending many Orders.

Typical sources of that value include:

- Infiltration or Forward Deployment;
- Camouflage or Hidden Deployment;
- Mines and other deployables;
- Repeaters;
- Specialist capability near objectives;
- short-range weapons positioned close enough to matter immediately.

Marker state itself is a resource. A Camouflage marker can force Discover attempts, constrain movement, threaten Surprise Attack, and create uncertainty. Do not reveal a skirmisher merely because it has an available ARO if remaining a marker causes the opponent more difficulty.

Skirmishers should generally **create a problem before they start consuming Orders**.

They are often too expensive to spam casually. Take enough to contest the midfield from useful angles, but avoid filling the army with 20–30 point specialists that all compete for the same active-turn Orders.

## Warbands

Warbands are primarily **trading and tempo pieces**. Their value commonly comes from some combination of:

- low cost;
- high mobility;
- Smoke or Eclipse;
- Direct Template Weapons;
- strong CC;
- Disposable weapons;
- Impetuous or Irregular Orders that let them work without consuming too much of the main Regular Order pool.

Typical jobs include:

- vision control;
- screening the deployment zone or midfield;
- forcing Dodge decisions with templates;
- trading into more expensive models;
- clearing mines and Perimeter weapons;
- attacking vulnerable enemy support pieces and cheerleaders;
- protecting expensive ranged pieces from close assault.

Do not pay premium prices for a model whose main purpose is to die while trading. The cheaper the trading piece, the more favorable the exchange can become.

## Cheerleaders

A cheerleader is not necessarily a useless model. It is a model expected to generate an Order while receiving **few or no active-turn Orders** itself.

For cheerleaders, prioritize **low cost and passive value** over active-turn combat capability.

Useful passive contributions include:

- Repeaters;
- Flash Pulse AROs;
- Sensor;
- Minelayer or deployable defence;
- Lieutenant concealment or an identical-profile Lieutenant decoy;
- Counterintelligence;
- cheap specialist capability;
- Fireteam membership that helps reach Level 2;
- Baggage or similar support functions.

Once a cheerleader starts costing significantly more than the minimum, it should usually stop being a pure cheerleader and acquire a meaningful second role.

## High-cost and apex troopers

Expensive troopers should justify their cost through **reliability, Order efficiency, or both**. A high-cost profile should normally bring several of the following:

- strong Face-to-Face odds through high BS, Mimetism, MSV, Surprise Attack, Marksmanship, strong range bands, or powerful ammunition;
- multiple Wounds/STR, NWI, strong ARM/BTS, Immunity, or strong defensive MODs;
- high mobility or advanced deployment;
- Tactical Awareness, NCO, useful Lieutenant Orders, or another source of extra Order efficiency;
- specialist capability or valuable secondary utility;
- enough weapon coverage to continue spending Orders productively after the first intended target is gone.

Do not pay premium points merely for bigger numbers or a bigger gun on a model that still has poor engagement odds.

Avoid making the whole list depend on a single superstar. A strong list usually wants **multiple models capable of productively consuming the main Order pool**, so the army still functions if the first attack piece is disabled, isolated, immobilized, hacked, or killed.

The more expensive the attack piece, the less willing it should be to accept an unnecessary fair fight. Use range control, Mimetism, MSV, Surprise Attack, Smoke/Eclipse, White Noise, hacking, vertical angles, and other asymmetric tools to make expensive pieces take favorable engagements rather than coin-flip exchanges.

## Heavy Infantry

Heavy Infantry are particularly good active-turn pieces because their durability gives them permission to take **calculated risks** that would be unacceptable for a 1-Wound trooper.

That durability should usually be used offensively rather than as an excuse to leave the model exposed in ARO.

HI also concentrate value into Hackable models, so the more points a list invests in HI, the more attractive the following become:

- TinBots and Firewalls;
- KHDs or other anti-hacker tools;
- Engineers;
- Repeater-network control;
- Fairy Dust or similar broad protection when several HI benefit from it.

One isolated cheap HI may not justify a support package. A list built around several 30–50 point HI often does.

Small Fireteams are especially useful for expensive HI because they let an attacker, specialist, and support piece reposition together for one Order. However, do not link a fast or highly mobile HI to slower partners if doing so destroys the mobility that made the expensive model valuable in the first place.

## TAGs

A TAG is usually a centerpiece rather than just another profile. The surrounding list should normally include some version of a **TAG ecosystem**:

- Engineer support;
- hacking defence;
- cheap screening troops, mines, templates, or other protection from close assault;
- mission specialists;
- at least one backup attacker that can take over if the TAG is disabled or lost.

TAGs are excellent places for **Tactical Awareness, NCO, and Lieutenant Orders** because they are already models the list expects to activate repeatedly. Extra Orders are most valuable on pieces that can convert many Orders into meaningful attacks and board position.

TAG durability should be used primarily to support active-turn aggression. Avoid treating a TAG as a static bunker that simply watches a lane in ARO, since the opponent can choose the exact counter, range band, hacking path, E/M tool, or close-assault piece to engage it.

A common sequence is:

1. attack and remove or suppress relevant threats;
2. accomplish a mission or positional objective;
3. reposition somewhere awkward to attack before the turn ends.

Keep cheap close-combat and E/M threats away from the TAG with screening pieces, mines, templates, Repeaters, and good spacing. A very cheap warband or specialist can otherwise neutralize a much larger investment.

## Vehicles (VH)

Vehicles are generally better understood as **mobile angle-generating attack platforms** than as small TAGs.

Many VH profiles combine high movement, Aerial or Super-Jump, Mimetism, decent armour, and strong weapons, but their large Silhouettes and movement restrictions make them poor candidates for simply sitting in place and armour-tanking.

Their ideal sequence is usually:

1. use speed and vertical mobility to create an unusual angle;
2. exploit Mimetism, range, or weapon quality to win the engagement;
3. continue moving or reposition to avoid an easy retaliation.

Their defence is often **Mimetism plus positioning**, not Cover plus raw armour.

Because many VH are Hackable and cost 30–40+ points, Engineer and hacking support can be worthwhile, especially when the list contains several VH or other Hackable premium models.

## Motorcycles and fast attack pieces

Mobility is effectively an **Order-economy skill**. A fast model may reach an engagement in one Order that a normal infantry model would need two or three Orders to reach, leaving more of the pool available for attacks or objectives.

Cheap motorcycles can function much like warbands or disposable flank attackers:

- move rapidly into a weak flank or rear area;
- threaten close-range targets;
- trade upward;
- force the opponent to account for large movement distances.

More expensive motorcycles and fast attackers need a meaningful task once they arrive. Mobility alone is not enough to justify a premium price; pair speed with strong shooting, specialist capability, Smoke/Eclipse, CC, Sensor, templates, mines, or another role that benefits from being delivered quickly.

Fast pieces can become **Order sinks** because they always seem to have another reachable target. Do not keep spending Orders merely because the model can continue attacking. Re-evaluate after each successful attack run whether another Order is worth more there than elsewhere in the army.

## Plan an exit for mobile attackers

For motorcycles, Super-Jump troops, fast REMs, VH, mobile HI, and other aggressive pieces, include the cost of **survival and repositioning** in the attack plan.

Do not evaluate an attack run only by how deeply the model penetrates or how many enemy pieces it can still reach. Consider where it will be when the turn ends.

A useful rule is to reserve enough Orders to:

- retreat behind Total Cover;
- reposition out of obvious counterattack lanes;
- return to a defensive Fireteam position;
- re-enter Camouflage when possible;
- deploy mines or other protection;
- occupy a mission-relevant but defensible position.

A 40-point attacker that kills 25 points and dies may be less valuable than one that kills 15 points, survives, and remains a credible Turn-2 threat.

## Airborne troops

Airborne troops are especially useful because they can attack **army structure rather than the enemy front line**.

Good targets often include:

- exposed Order generators;
- Lieutenants;
- Engineers and Doctors;
- EVOs and support hackers;
- guided-missile or other support REMs;
- vulnerable specialists;
- expensive models that are exposed from the rear.

The strongest target is not always the opponent's strongest model. Killing several cheap support pieces can cripple the Order pool or remove the infrastructure that keeps a TAG or HI core functioning.

Airborne presence also creates value before deployment by forcing the opponent to protect table edges, rear arcs, landing zones, cheerleaders, and support pieces.

### Combat Group placement for airborne troops

Because an undeployed airborne trooper does not contribute its Order normally but can use its own Order to enter the table, Combat Group placement should reflect **expected post-entry Order use**.

A useful heuristic is:

- **short, expendable attack run:** Group 2 is often ideal;
- **major sustained attack expected to use many Orders after landing:** Group 1 is usually better.

The important question is not simply whether the model has Airborne Deployment, but **how many Orders it is expected to consume after entering**.

### Combat Jump versus safe entry

Use Combat Jump primarily for **positional freedom and attack-vector selection**, not for unnecessarily risky stunt landings.

A safe landing out of sight followed by a strong attack angle is usually preferable to attempting to land directly among enemies unless the payoff clearly justifies the risk.

When several valuable Combat Jump troops are present, an EVO becomes more attractive because Controlled Jump can make the whole airborne plan more reliable. The EVO is even easier to justify if the same list also benefits from other Supportware or a TR REM.

### Airborne troops as pressure pieces

Even if an airborne model never deploys, it can influence enemy deployment and facing. The opponent must respect the possibility of attacks from the rear or table edges.

Use that pressure deliberately. An airborne threat can make the opponent cluster support pieces, spend Orders guarding the deployment zone, or deploy important models in less efficient positions.
