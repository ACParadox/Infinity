# Core Fireteams: when to field three, four or five troopers (N5.3)

**Purpose:** Choose a Core's *physical size*, qualifying bonus level, and supporting army architecture based on what it actually accomplishes. This guide distills [the battle-report extraction of four-/five-model Fireteams](../battle-reports/n5-four-and-five-model-fireteams-vs-three-model-cores.md) from the #001–#241 dossiers. Read it alongside [Fireteams and profile evaluation](fireteams-and-profile-evaluation.md).

**Evidence limitation:** The archive contains duplicate reports, tournament retrospectives, narrative/intro games, different N5 rules eras and uncertain lineup reconstructions. It is **not** a statistical sample of Fireteam usage or a controlled 3-versus-5 Core win-rate comparison. A narrated starting Fireteam is historical evidence, **not proof of current N5.3 legality**; confirm each new list in its current `sectorials/` Fireteams chart.

## What the extra models actually provide

| Matching/counts-as Fireteam members | Cumulative N5.3 benefit | What matters when building a list |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Linked activation for the legal Fireteam | Several models can move together with one Regular Order |
| 2 | BS Attack **+1 Special Die (SD)** | A better result distribution, **not +1 Burst**; applies in Reactive Turn as specified |
| 3 | +3 Discover, +1 Dodge | Useful anti-marker detection and defensive repositioning |
| 4 | +1 BS when declaring BS Attack | Genuine shooting improvement, including relevant BS Weapon (PH/WIP) attacks |
| 5 | Sixth Sense | Strong reactive defense, but lost as soon as qualifying membership drops below five |

The table summarizes [N5.3 Fireteam Bonuses](../../rules/6_Command%20and%20Fireteams/Fireteam_Bonuses.md). **Four physical troopers are not necessarily Level 4:** profiles must be the same unit or specifically count as one another for the bonus. A five-body mixed Core can likewise be below Level 5. Always calculate the *actual* bonus level, not the visible team size. Creating a legal Core also depends on its sectorial chart, minimum/maximum, FTO and Wildcard rules.

**The right comparison is not "Does five beat three in a gunfight?" It is "What does the fourth or fifth body contribute compared with leaving that model free?"** A Core of three and a Haris of three can have different chart permissions/formation slots. Battle-report examples with a Haris illustrate the *army architecture* trade-off but are not direct evidence that a three-member Core itself outperforms a five-member Core.

## When to choose a three-member Core

Prefer **three** when the team has a clear route and job with three members and the extra models would be more useful **independently**:

- One strong **gunfighter**, one **protection/vision** piece (TinBot, Smoke/Disco, Hacker) and one **specialist/recovery** model cover the planned approach.
- The list needs separate scorers for **multiple consoles, carrying/extraction, or end-of-round zones**; pulling them into the Core can make scoring slower and more fragile.
- Dense **templates, mines, repeater networks** or vertical terrain make a large coherency footprint costly.
- A fast or special-movement profile cannot efficiently drag four teammates across the same route.
- The fourth body is costly, duplicates a gun or specialist, and does not actually raise the qualifying Fireteam level.

**Evidence:**
- [#199 — NCA versus Torchlight](../battle-reports/191-200-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md): NCA **Bolt/Hannibal/Shona Haris 3**, separate Maximus/Pathfinder Duo and inexpensive objective workers defeated Torchlight's **four-member SilverStar/Wave Rider/Kappa hacking Core** 6–5 in Outbreak. The result hinged on scans/stabilization and surviving mission workers, not a proven inherent superiority of Haris size.
- [#227 — NCA versus Ramah](../battle-reports/221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md): NCA's **Aquila/Bolt/Shona three-model link** with independent mission actors defeated an **approximately five-model Tarik/Khawarij Core** in an eight-model Ramah list, 7–1. The expensive Ramah roster lacked enough surviving independent civilian carriers.
- [#230 — Kosmoflot versus Nomads](../battle-reports/221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md): Kosmoflot fielded an explicitly **three-model Scots Guard/Volkolak Core**, plus a separate **Kosmosoldat/Strannik Haris** and SAS skirmishers. The 3–3 Uplink Center outcome demonstrates spread-out specialists and midfield denial; it does not establish a Core-size optimum.

A three-member Core is a strong **starting point**, not a universal endpoint. If a fourth cheap qualifying model changes the attacker's BS while preserving independent mission capacity, it may be worth adding.

## When a four-member Core is worth the extra trooper

A **fourth** is most convincing when it either **earns Level 4 (+1 BS)** on the decisive piece *or* contributes a necessary role that the three existing members cannot cover. Ask whether that model would be purchased anyway.

|---|---|---|---|

| [#019 Varuna](../battle-reports/011-020-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Kamau HRL *or* HMG + 2 Montesa FTO + Pathfinder | Gun and multiple mobile specialist/repeater paths traveling together | Two **separate submitted** lists; no guarantee everyone wants the same route |
| [#054/#059/#060 Bakunin](../battle-reports/051-060-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Cenobite gun + Custodier Hacker + Healer + Moira | Offensive/reaction gun, hacking, recovery and a fourth useful body | Premium models can create an exposed, expensive single-area commitment |
| [#088 Hassassin](../battle-reports/081-090-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | 2 Asawira + Ghulam NCO/Smoke + Barid KHD/Pitcher | Fighting, vision control and cyber protection in one route | Independent Fidays/Ghazi retain the alternative angles; do not sacrifice them for link size |
| [#108 Tunguska](../battle-reports/101-110-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Perseus + Hollow Man Hacker + ML + Pitcher | Durable ARO, hacker, forward repeater and assault in one package | Support Clockmaker is **independent**; preserve mobility and avoid clustered hacking/templates |
| [#231 Torchlight](../battle-reports/231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Jackboot HMG/NCO + Hellblazer + Wave Rider + Kludger | Gun, cyber, Engineer and visual-denial tools with linked travel | **Independent** Jamie/Moonraker penetration won key ground; 10–0 cannot be credited to Core size |
| [#122 Neoterra](../battle-reports/121-130-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | ORC HMG + Blocker + Fusilier Paramedic + Pathfinder | Utility/support is real despite having four bodies | **Only Level 2** due to composition; no +1 BS just for four models |
| [#137 Tunguska](../battle-reports/131-140-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | 2 Securitate + Interventor + Grenzer | Backfield Hacker/specialist/sniper role compression | Four bodies but **only Level 2**; 7–0 Firefight win does not prove team-size benefit |

**Four versus three marginal test:** What would the *fourth model* do this game? Specifically: improve the decisive shooter via an **actual** Level 4; carry a specialist needed on the same route; cover a complementary range; provide Firewall/repair/medical access; or contribute an inexpensive Order and useful defense. If none applies, use three and keep the fourth independent.

## When a five-member Core justifies itself

A **fifth** is more specialized: the team must reach **five qualifying members** to gain Level 5 **Sixth Sense**. A fifth inexpensive line troop may still be worth bringing for its **Regular Order** even if it does not increase bonus level, but then the rationale is Order economy and useful positioning, not Sixth Sense.

| Battle-report example | Five-member package | Why five can make sense | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| [#175 Kestrel CanCon winner](../battle-reports/171-180-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Griffin Feuerbach + Bipandra + 3 Fennecs | Defensive anchor, Doctor, cheap Fennec Order bodies; separate Hetkari/Firefly attack Duo and independent mission pieces | Same event also appears in #176; Day 2 involved a **different, retrospectively identified illegal two-Haris configuration** |
| [#203 Varuna](../battle-reports/201-210-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Fusilier Hacker + Paramedic + Quinn + 2 Fusiliers | Inexpensive utility Core protects Orders while **Cutter and Squalos** fight independently | Two TAGs and free-ranging scorers, not the Core's fifth body, enable broad coverage |
| [#017 Military Orders](../battle-reports/011-020-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Four Crosiers (HMG, MULTI Sniper, 2 Blitzen) + Knight Commander | Long-lane defense and command backed by cheaper troopers | Core should not pull costly mobile Knights away from actual objectives |
| [#087 TAK](../battle-reports/081-090-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Four Line Kazaks + Frontovik AP Sniper | One powerful long gun supported by cheap orders, not five expensive attack pieces | Stationary lane defense can be bypassed or flanked |
| [#100 Invincible Army](../battle-reports/091-100-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Shang Ji + Bixi + Hulang + 2 Haidao | Start as one group, then **deliberately split** into two-Haidao Core and three-member attack Haris | Historical demonstration in a **teaching game**; a Command Token and current chart legality matter |
| [#112 White Company](../battle-reports/111-120-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Hawkwood + Emily + 3 Fusiliers | Powerful defensive multi-gun/command group when intact | Loss of Fusilier Hacker dropped **Sixth Sense**; opposing Charontid exploited it to kill Hawkwood |
| [#238 Imperial Service](../battle-reports/231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Celestial Guard Monitor + 4 Kuang Shi | Economy of disposable bodies and Regular Order support; separate Wu Ming Haris | A special order engine, not a universal template for elite Core play |
| [#227 Ramah](../battle-reports/221-230-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) | Tarik + 4 Khawarij (probable) | Exceptional concentration of attack, Doctor, NCO and other skills | Eight-model army lost 7–1; weak redundancy and civilian scoring after casualties |

**Five versus four marginal test:** The fifth should be **cheap, useful and qualifying**, or Sixth Sense must be worth its points *and* its vulnerability to losing one member. Do not pay for a fifth expensive hero to protect an ARO if it leaves no independent scorer or flank threat. A five-model Core does not grant the team five separate actions on each Order.

## Comparison at a glance

| Decision axis | Three physical members | Four physical members | Five physical members |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical role | Mobile specialist/gun/protection package | Combined arms or Level 4 shooter | Cheap defensive backbone, zone fortress or dedicated Sixth Sense shell |
| Incremental payoff | Compact linked mobility and Level 2–3 | Utility **or actual Level 4 +1 BS** | Cheap Order capacity **or actual Level 5 Sixth Sense** |
| Maneuver and ARO footprint | Easiest to keep safe | More exposed bases | Hardest to manage in dense terrain |
| Redundancy | Other models stay independent | One more capability within team | More within-team jobs, potentially less army-wide independence |
| Best supporting army | Separate scorers and alternate attacker | At least one independent scoring route | Independent serious attacker *and* resilient mission scorers |
| Principal failure | Too few roles or losing a key member | Paying for a nonqualifying fourth just for +1 BS | Threshold loss and excessive budget concentration |

## Practical build and play sequence

1. **Define the job first:** defensive sniper/ARO, attacking gun+specialist, infiltration delivery, room holding, anti-hacker utility or multi-turn recovery.
2. **Build the best legal three-member Core** for that job; calculate qualifying members, not just physical membership.
3. **Test the fourth member:** its full points/SWC and mission purpose, whether it actually unlocks +1 BS, and whether an independent specialist would be better.
4. **Test the fifth member separately:** does it unlock Sixth Sense and is its continued survival realistic? Is it otherwise a cheap useful Order?
5. **Audit the rest of the army:** independent mobile scorer, alternate attack angle, cheap Orders, mine/repeater defense, medical/engineering capacity, and actual mission Classified coverage.
6. **Plan the link's break point:** the optimal Turn 1 defensive Core need not remain intact on Turn 2/3. Use legal reformations/Command Tokens when smaller formations score more efficiently, as in [#100](../battle-reports/091-100-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md).
7. **Reassess every movement Order:** multiple activated models can invite additional AROs, splash/template attacks, hackers and coherency problems. A protected Core that never advances can be right; forcing five models into a single gap can be wrong.
8. **Track outcomes by mechanism, not win rate:** did the fourth/fifth actually shoot, heal, discover, block or score? Or did an independent specialist and an unrelated attack decide the game?

## Recommended baseline

**Start with three. Upgrade to four because of a real extra capability or confirmed Level 4 benefit. Upgrade to five primarily when inexpensive, useful members bring a defensible Level 5 Sixth Sense package or a valuable backfield Order engine.** The strongest reported larger Cores are usually one component of an army that retains independent pressure and scoring.
