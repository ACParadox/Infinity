# Endgame, Retreat!, Loss of Lieutenant, and Playing While Broken

Infinity endgames often stop rewarding the same decisions that were correct on Turns 1–2. Once an army is badly damaged, in Loss of Lieutenant, near Retreat!, or entering its final Active Turn, the value of Orders, survival, Command Tokens, and individual models changes sharply.

The central principle is:

> **A broken army should not try to behave like a healthy army with fewer Orders. Revalue every surviving model according to what it can still change before final scoring.**

This note focuses on operating after command failure, exploiting or avoiding Retreat!, converting late-game resources into score, and recognizing when survival has no remaining strategic value.

## 1. Endgame value is different from normal value

During most of the game, a model has several kinds of value:

- its current position;
- combat or Specialist capability;
- future Orders;
- future AROs;
- future scoring potential;
- surviving Victory Points or tiebreak value;
- support it provides to other models.

As the game approaches its end, some of those values disappear.

A useful endgame question is:

> **What can this model still change before the final score is calculated?**

A model's survival matters only if it still contributes to one or more of:

1. scoring;
2. denying enemy scoring;
3. carrying or contesting something;
4. killing or disabling something that affects scoring;
5. presenting an ARO or movement tax during an enemy Turn that still remains;
6. surviving Victory Points or tiebreakers;
7. keeping the army above the Retreat! threshold;
8. enabling another model.

If none of those apply, preserving the model has little or no strategic value.

## 2. No future friendly Turn: Order generators lose much of their value

Orders are counted during the Tactical Phase at the beginning of the Active Turn.

Therefore, once a model has already contributed its Order on the last friendly Active Turn, its death cannot reduce a future Order Pool if no future friendly Tactical Phase will occur.

This creates a major terminal-turn principle:

> **After final-turn Order Count, cheap Order generators can be liquidated aggressively if their survival no longer matters for scoring, AROs, Retreat!, or Victory Points.**

Possible uses include:

- Move-Move into a scoring zone;
- trigger or clear a Mine;
- Discover a Marker;
- block a doorway or movement route;
- force an enemy template or ARO choice;
- Engage an enemy;
- enter Repeater range;
- screen a more important model;
- contest an objective;
- make a low-probability attack on a scoring model.

The normal concern that "this model is worth an Order next Turn" has vanished.

However:

> **No future friendly Turn is not the same as no future game state.**

If the opponent still has an Active Turn, the model may still be valuable as an ARO piece, zone contender, blocker, objective guard, or source of surviving Victory Points.

## 3. Retreat! is a threshold checked at the start of the Active Turn

At the beginning of the Active Turn, during the Tactical Phase, an army whose surviving Victory Points are equal to or less than 25% of its starting Army Points enters Retreat!.

For common game sizes:

| Army Points | Retreat! threshold |
| ---: | ---: |
| 400 | 100 or less |
| 350 | 88 or less |
| 300 | 75 or less |
| 250 | 63 or less |
| 200 | 50 or less |
| 150 | 38 or less |

Troopers not yet placed on the table through abilities such as Airborne Deployment or Hidden Deployment still count as surviving for this check.

The timing matters enormously:

> **You do not enter Retreat! halfway through your own Active Turn just because casualties reduce you below 25%.**

If a 300-point army begins Turn 3 with 83 surviving points, it passes the Retreat! check. It can then lose 50 points during that Turn without suddenly entering Retreat!.

If there is no later friendly Turn, preserving models merely to stay above 75 points may no longer matter except for surviving Victory Points or scenario-specific scoring.

## 4. Avoiding Retreat! can revolve around a single expensive survivor

Retreat! is based on surviving **points**, not number of surviving models.

At 300 points:

- 76 surviving points avoids Retreat!;
- 75 surviving points enters Retreat!.

This makes some defensive priorities nonlinear.

A 45-point Heavy Infantry surviving alongside 35 other points keeps the army functional next Turn. Losing that model may collapse the army into Retreat! even if several cheap models remain alive.

Late in the opponent's Turn, explicitly calculate:

> **Which surviving model or combination of models keeps me above the Retreat! line?**

Sometimes an otherwise mediocre expensive piece is the most important model to preserve simply because it anchors the army above the threshold.

Hidden Deployment and undeployed Airborne troops can also function as concealed Retreat! insurance because they count toward surviving points even though they may not currently provide Orders.

## 5. Deliberately entering Retreat! is mission-dependent

Do not treat "enter Retreat! to end the game" as a universal trick.

In a Standard Game, if a player starts their Active Turn in Retreat!, the game ends at the end of that Turn. Missions and scenarios may use different end-game conditions.

Therefore intentionally crossing the 25% line can occasionally be useful when:

- you are already ahead;
- the applicable mission will end after your Retreat! Turn;
- the opponent would otherwise receive another chance to score;
- your remaining models can still secure or preserve the current result.

Because Retreat! is checked at the beginning of your Active Turn, deliberately entering it normally means accepting casualties during the preceding Reactive Turn rather than sacrificing models during the same Active Turn.

Before considering this:

1. read the mission's exact end-game clause;
2. confirm when scoring occurs;
3. determine whether the opponent receives another Turn;
4. determine what your remaining Retreat-resistant pieces can still do.

> **Never deliberately enter Retreat! based on a generic assumption about how the game ends.**

## 6. Retreat! and Loss of Lieutenant are related but not identical

A Retreat! situation automatically causes Loss of Lieutenant.

Loss of Lieutenant by itself is less restrictive.

### Loss of Lieutenant

Normally:

- all Troopers become Irregular for the Turn;
- there is no shared Regular Order Pool;
- each Trooper can still perform normal Skills using its own Irregular Order;
- Tactical Orders and other personal Order sources can still matter;
- the player appoints a new Lieutenant at the end of the Turn if an eligible Trooper remains.

### Retreat!

In addition to Loss of Lieutenant:

- normal Troopers enter Retreat! State;
- Troopers in Retreat! State are heavily restricted in what they can declare;
- the Impetuous Phase is not applied;
- eligible troops may leave via the broad Deployment Zone edge and count as surviving;
- special skills such as Courage, Religious Troop, Warhorse, or Inspiring Leadership can alter these effects.

The practical distinction is:

> **LoL asks how to operate with decentralized Orders. Retreat! additionally asks which survivors are still allowed to operate normally.**

## 7. Playing a Loss of Lieutenant Turn: think "one useful Order each"

Do not mentally concede a Turn merely because the army is in Loss of Lieutenant.

Instead of attempting a conventional six-Order attack sequence, scan the table model by model and ask what each piece can accomplish with exactly one personal activation.

Useful one-Order actions include:

- attack an already-exposed enemy;
- Move + place a Mine or other deployable;
- Move + interact with a nearby objective;
- enter or contest a zone;
- Discover;
- Spotlight or perform another immediate hacking action;
- Move into an ARO position;
- Dodge into safety or toward an objective;
- reposition a Repeater;
- trade a warband into an enemy Specialist;
- move toward a Turn-3 scoring route;
- reload or support another model when immediately relevant.

LoL resilience therefore rises sharply in lists containing:

- Warhorse;
- troops that are already Irregular;
- Tactical Awareness;
- useful Minelayers or deployable carriers;
- midfield skirmishers;
- warbands;
- independent Specialists;
- pieces that can accomplish real work with one activation.

A useful list-building question is:

> **If my Lieutenant dies before my next Turn, how many models can still do something genuinely useful with only their own Order?**

## 8. Spend natural Irregular Orders before converting them

A common LoL mistake is spending Command Tokens simply to convert as many Irregular Orders as possible.

That is usually inefficient.

If a model's personal Irregular Order can already accomplish something useful, spend it normally.

Examples:

- a Specialist already beside a Console;
- a warband already near a viable trade;
- a Minelayer that can reposition and place a Mine;
- a shooter with a target already in LoF;
- a model that only needs one Move-Move to reach a scoring area.

Use Irregular-to-Regular conversion when the real need is **concentration**:

- one TAG needs multiple activations;
- one Specialist must cross several movement segments;
- one attacker is the only model capable of breaking an ARO;
- a Fireteam needs a Regular Order;
- a Coordinated Order is strategically valuable.

The principle is:

> **Spend local Orders locally. Convert only the Orders that need to become flexible.**

## 9. Command Tokens are emergency command infrastructure

Command Tokens become disproportionately valuable in broken-army endgames.

Important uses include:

- convert an Irregular Order into a Regular Order;
- make one Trooper ignore Retreat! until the end of the game;
- restructure Combat Groups before Order Count;
- rebuild a Fireteam when relevant;
- Doctor or Engineer rerolls on eligible targets;
- cancel Possessed;
- Coordinated Orders;
- force or fail Guts when position matters.

### Retreat! immunity via Command Token

One Command Token can make one Trooper unaffected by Retreat! until the end of the game.

This can effectively create a functioning closer out of an otherwise restricted survivor.

A strong sequence can be:

1. choose the critical Specialist or attacker;
2. spend one Command Token so it ignores Retreat!;
3. spend its own Irregular Order on a normal action;
4. convert another survivor's otherwise-poor Irregular Order to Regular;
5. spend that new Regular Order on the critical model.

A supposedly crippled army can therefore still concentrate several activations into one important piece.

### Preserve late Command Tokens when collapse is plausible

This does **not** mean automatically hoarding two Tokens every game.

Early Doctor/Engineer rerolls, Fireteam repairs, or coordinated plays may be more valuable.

But before spending the final Command Token on Turn 2, ask:

> **If my Lieutenant dies or I enter Retreat! before Turn 3, what would this Token buy me?**

In lists vulnerable to command collapse, entering Turn 3 with one flexible Command Token can be strategically significant.

## 10. Fireteams in LoL and Retreat!

### Loss of Lieutenant only

Loss of Lieutenant does not automatically cancel an intact Fireteam.

However, members normally become Irregular, so personal Irregular Orders do not function like the shared Regular Orders that normally drive a Fireteam.

Useful emergency options include:

- Tactical Awareness on an eligible Team Leader;
- convert an Irregular Order into a Regular Order with a Command Token;
- use the resulting Regular Order to activate the Fireteam;
- break individual members out with their own Irregular Orders when the team no longer matters.

One Irregular-to-Regular conversion can therefore sometimes unlock several models worth of Fireteam movement or attacking.

### Retreat!

Entering Retreat! cancels Fireteams.

Do not build a Retreat! plan around preserving a Core or Haris as a functioning team unless a specific rule explicitly overrides this.

## 11. Retreat-resistant troops have different levels of resilience

Do not lump Courage, Religious Troop, and Warhorse together.

| Troop type | Loss of Lieutenant | Retreat! |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | becomes Irregular | becomes Irregular and enters Retreat! State |
| Courage | becomes Irregular | ignores Retreat! State and acts normally |
| Religious Troop | becomes Irregular | ignores Retreat! State and acts normally |
| **Warhorse** | **remains Regular** | **remains Regular and acts normally** |

### Courage and Religious Troop

These models are excellent Retreat! survivors because they can still use normal Skills.

However, because the army is still in Loss of Lieutenant, they normally remain Irregular.

Treat them as strong **one-Order closers**:

- nearby Specialist presses the final button;
- midfield attacker makes one decisive attack;
- Engineer repairs the key model;
- hacker disables a scoring piece;
- deployable carrier seals a route.

Pre-positioning such troops before the army collapses greatly increases their value.

### Warhorse

Warhorse is substantially stronger.

The user:

- is unaffected by Loss of Lieutenant;
- remains Regular;
- is unaffected by Retreat!;
- does not enter Retreat! State;
- acts normally;
- cannot enter Isolated State.

This makes Warhorse a true **broken-army anchor**.

A Warhorse Specialist, attacker, or mobile scoring piece can continue functioning as part of a Regular Order economy even when the rest of the army has collapsed.

List-building implication:

> **Warhorse is not merely anti-Isolation tech. It is late-game command and Retreat! insurance.**

## 12. Inspiring Leadership removes the Retreat! cliff

When the Inspiring Leadership user is:

- the Lieutenant;
- on the table;
- not in a Null State;

its army is not affected by Retreat! and never enters a Retreat! situation.

This fundamentally changes the endgame.

An army reduced below the normal 25% threshold can continue operating without Retreat! as long as the Inspiring Leadership Lieutenant remains active.

Qualifying Order-providing Troopers also become Regular and gain Courage, further stabilizing a damaged army.

This makes the Lieutenant unusually important late:

> **Killing an Inspiring Leadership Lieutenant can simultaneously remove a model, collapse command, and restore the army's vulnerability to Retreat!.**

Conversely, if an Inspiring Leadership model becomes Lieutenant later through Chain of Command or normal Lieutenant appointment, its effects apply once it becomes the Lieutenant.

## 13. Last-turn suicide plays use different probability thresholds

Normal attack evaluation includes the future value of the attacker.

Terminal-turn evaluation often does not.

Suppose a Specialist has a 35% chance to cross an exposed lane and press the winning Console.

If:

- success wins the game;
- failure loses the game;
- the Specialist has no useful alternative contribution;

then the fact that the run is dangerous is largely irrelevant.

The same applies to denial.

A cheap troop with a low chance to kill or Engage the enemy's final scoring Specialist may be worth sacrificing because failure costs nothing strategically while success changes the match result.

Use this framework:

> **Chance the play changes the result × value of the changed result.**

When failure is strategically free, low-probability plays can become correct.

## 14. Sacrifice Order generators when there is no next Turn

Once the final friendly Order Count has happened, cheap backfield troops should stop being treated automatically as protected cheerleaders.

They can be converted into:

- zone points;
- bodies blocking paths;
- Discover attempts;
- Mine-clearing sacrifices;
- ARO taxes;
- Engage attempts;
- template trades;
- objective contesters;
- emergency screens.

The important distinction is:

> **Past Orders are sunk. Future Orders matter only if another friendly Tactical Phase will occur.**

Turn 3 therefore often rewards plays that would be bad on Turn 1.

## 15. Deny scoring rather than trying to rebuild a normal attack plan

A badly damaged army may no longer have the resources for a conventional offensive sequence.

It can still destroy the opponent's scoring sequence.

Prioritize targets according to what the opponent still needs to score:

1. Specialist capable of completing the decisive objective;
2. objective carrier;
3. high-mobility model capable of reaching the scoring area;
4. Engineer/Doctor required to restore a scoring piece;
5. hacker, Repeater, or support model enabling the final play;
6. generic combat pieces only when they directly prevent scoring.

You do not always need to kill the target.

States and positional effects such as:

- Immobilized;
- Isolated;
- Engaged;
- Unconscious;
- forced displacement;
- blocked movement;
- loss of Repeater/support infrastructure;

may be enough to make the opponent incapable of completing the mission.

Late-game target priority should therefore be:

> **Kill or disable the enemy's remaining scoring path, not necessarily their strongest model.**

## 16. Retreat! evacuation: preserve Victory Points or stay and deny

In Retreat!, troops that exit the table through the broad side of their Deployment Zone are considered to have survived and count toward Victory Points.

This creates a real choice.

### Evacuate when

- surviving Victory Points are the critical tiebreaker;
- the model has no meaningful scoring or denial role;
- leaving it on the table only gives the opponent a chance to kill those points.

### Stay when

- its ARO matters;
- it contests a zone;
- it blocks a route;
- it threatens an objective;
- it forces the opponent to spend Orders clearing it;
- its physical position matters more than the Victory Points saved by evacuation.

Do not automatically flee merely because Retreat! permits it.

Compare:

> **surviving VP value vs board-denial value.**

## 17. Endgame survival can reach zero strategic value

The most important psychological adjustment is recognizing when "keep the model alive" is no longer a useful goal.

Survival has effectively zero remaining value when all of the following are true:

- no later friendly Turn exists;
- the model's surviving Victory Points do not affect the result or tiebreaker;
- it does not need to remain in a zone or carry an objective;
- it has no meaningful future ARO;
- its death cannot trigger a relevant Retreat! check before another friendly Turn;
- it does not protect or enable another scoring piece.

At that point, preserving it is usually dominated by any action with a nonzero chance of improving the result.

This is where intentional sacrifices become correct rather than reckless.

## 18. Broken-army endgame sequence

When the army is badly damaged, use this sequence before spending Orders.

### Step 1 — Check the score

Determine the exact result you need:

- preserve a lead;
- force a draw;
- flip one objective;
- dominate one zone;
- kill or disable one scoring piece;
- protect a carrier;
- preserve Victory Points for a tiebreak.

Do not start by asking what you can kill.

### Step 2 — Check Retreat! timing and mission end conditions

Determine:

- whether you are already in Retreat!;
- whether crossing the threshold matters before another friendly Turn;
- whether the mission ends on Retreat!;
- when scoring occurs;
- whether the opponent still receives another Active Turn.

### Step 3 — Identify functional survivors

Prioritize:

1. Warhorse;
2. Courage / Religious Troop;
3. Tactical Awareness;
4. nearby Specialists;
5. disposable attackers;
6. deployable carriers;
7. models whose current position already matters.

### Step 4 — Spend natural personal Orders first

Extract useful actions from Irregular and Tactical Orders before spending Command Tokens to create flexibility.

### Step 5 — Pick the one model worth concentrating Orders into

Usually this is:

- the final scorer;
- objective carrier;
- mobile attacker capable of denying enemy scoring;
- Warhorse anchor;
- Specialist that can still reach the decisive objective.

### Step 6 — Spend Command Tokens aggressively when there is no future value in saving them

Possible uses:

- Retreat! immunity;
- Irregular-to-Regular conversion;
- Fireteam activation/rebuild;
- Coordinated movement or attack;
- critical recovery.

Unused Command Tokens have zero post-game value.

### Step 7 — Liquidate future Order generators

Once future Orders cease to matter, turn cheap support bodies into movement, blocking, Discover attempts, zone points, trades, or ARO taxes.

### Step 8 — Attack the enemy's scoring plan

Remove the exact model, state, route, or support system the opponent requires to score.

### Step 9 — End with denial

Whenever an enemy Active Turn remains, finish with:

- Mines and deployables;
- blocking bodies;
- Engage threats;
- useful AROs;
- Suppressive Fire when practical;
- zone presence;
- objective guards;
- awkward movement geometry.

### Step 10 — Stop protecting models whose survival no longer changes the score

Once survival is strategically irrelevant, take any reasonable play that can improve the final result.

## 19. List-building implications

Endgame resilience should be evaluated during list construction, not only after collapse occurs.

Useful features include:

- Warhorse;
- Courage or Religious Troop on useful scorers;
- Chain of Command;
- Inspiring Leadership;
- multiple plausible Lieutenant candidates;
- Tactical Awareness;
- decentralized midfield pieces;
- Specialists that can operate with one Order;
- Command Token efficiency or extra Command Tokens;
- cheap useful Irregular troops;
- deployables that remain relevant without further Orders;
- at least one late-game piece that can still function after the main Order Pool collapses.

A useful robustness question is:

> **If I start Turn 3 in Loss of Lieutenant or Retreat!, what is my actual scoring plan?**

If the answer is "I probably cannot do anything," the list may be more fragile than its raw combat power suggests.

## 20. Core principles

1. **Revalue models when the game approaches its end.** Future Orders and future survival may disappear as sources of value.
2. **Retreat! is a start-of-Turn threshold.** Crossing it during your own Turn does not immediately cripple that Turn.
3. **Count points, not bodies.** One expensive survivor may keep the entire army out of Retreat!.
4. **Loss of Lieutenant is playable.** Extract one useful action from each independent model before trying to recreate a normal Order pool.
5. **Spend personal Orders locally; convert only where concentration matters.**
6. **Command Tokens become emergency command infrastructure.** One Token can create Retreat! immunity or one flexible Regular Order at exactly the moment it matters most.
7. **Courage and Religious Troop are one-Order Retreat! closers; Warhorse is a true broken-army anchor.**
8. **Inspiring Leadership removes the Retreat! cliff entirely while the Lieutenant remains active.**
9. **On the final friendly Turn, Order generators can become disposable once their future Orders no longer exist.**
10. **When failure is strategically free, low-probability plays can be correct.**
11. **Deny the opponent's scoring path rather than attacking irrelevant models.**
12. **Survival is not intrinsically valuable. Preserve only what can still change the final result.**
