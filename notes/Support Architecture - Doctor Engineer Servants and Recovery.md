# Support Architecture — Doctor, Engineer, Servants, and Recovery

Support should be treated as **board infrastructure**, not merely as “bringing a Doctor” or “bringing an Engineer.”

The central question is:

> **When an important model goes down, can I actually recover it for roughly 1–3 Orders without exposing another valuable model?**

A support piece that cannot reach its patient cheaply is often wasted. A well-positioned Servant can make one Doctor or Engineer cover a large part of the table.

---

## 1. Build a recovery network, not a recovery model

The basic architecture is:

**valuable piece → likely fighting position → nearby safe corner → Servant → Doctor/Engineer in safety**

Do not normally park the Servant directly beside the model it supports. Put it **one movement away behind Total Cover**.

If the attack piece loses a fight and falls Unconscious, the Servant moves around the corner and performs the recovery through its Controller.

This is particularly useful for pieces that repeatedly fight from predictable locations:

- TAGs;
- REM gunfighters;
- long-range ARO pieces;
- HI attack pieces;
- Fireteam shooters;
- midfield specialists defending an objective.

Think of every valuable model as having a **recovery radius**.

If it runs several Orders deep into the enemy DZ, it may have effectively left that recovery network. That is acceptable, but understand that the model has changed from a **reusable asset** into a **trading piece**.

---

## 2. Servants are positional infrastructure

A Peripheral (Servant) lets a Doctor or Engineer perform their Skill through the Servant when the Servant is in Silhouette contact with the target.

Important implications:

- the **Doctor/Engineer makes the roll**, not the Servant;
- the Controller can remain hidden in the rear;
- there is no maximum operating distance between Servant and Controller;
- a Controller may have up to two Servants;
- if there are two, only one is activated at a time.

The lack of a control-distance limit does **not** remove movement costs. A Servant on the wrong side of the table may still require several Orders to reach a casualty.

Therefore, pre-position Servants before they are needed.

---

## 3. Two Servants should usually cover two different recovery zones

Do not treat the second Servant merely as a spare standing beside the first.

Use two Servants to create two recovery stations:

**left flank ← Servant A — Doctor/Engineer — Servant B → right flank**

Possible assignments:

- one Servant behind the main long-range ARO position;
- one behind the primary attack route;
- one on each flank;
- one near a TAG route and one near midfield specialists.

A Turn 1 development Order spent moving a Servant into a future recovery position can save several Orders later.

---

## 4. Put the support Orders where recovery will actually happen

A support specialist in a nearly empty Combat Group may be unable to do anything useful.

Conversely, a secondary Combat Group can be an excellent place for support if it retains enough Orders to perform recovery actions.

A useful architecture is:

| Combat Group | Main job |
|---|---|
| **Group 1** | primary attacks and mission push |
| **Group 2** | support, recovery, defensive development, cheap independent pieces |

Group 1 can attack while Group 2 pays for:

**Move → Move → Doctor/Engineer**

This prevents recovery from consuming the remaining Orders of the main attack group.

When constructing the list, ask whether the support group will realistically have **2–4 usable Orders** when the recovery is needed.

---

# Doctor strategy

Doctor is primarily used to recover **VITA** troops.

A successful Doctor roll removes a Wound and can cancel Unconscious. A failed roll kills the patient.

Doctors can also remove **Stunned** from VITA troops.

If the target has a Cube, Command Tokens may be used to reroll failed Doctor rolls.

## Best Doctor targets

Do not simply revive the most expensive model.

Revive the model that gives the most useful **future value**.

Early game priorities often include:

- primary attack pieces;
- critical ARO pieces;
- important Fireteam members.

Mid-game priorities often include:

- attack pieces that can make another run;
- surviving specialists;
- valuable Order generators.

Late-game priorities often change to:

- specialists capable of scoring;
- zone-control bodies;
- models required for Classified Objectives;
- units needed to deny or contest an objective.

A 15-point Specialist may be worth more than a 40-point gunfighter on Turn 3.

---

## Doctor (2W) greatly improves recovery value

Doctor (2W) removes two Wounds when successfully cancelling Unconscious.

This is especially strong with multi-Wound troops because one support Order can return a major piece with meaningful durability instead of merely restoring it with one fragile Wound.

Premium HI and characters become substantially more recoverable when paired with Doctor (2W).

---

## Do not automatically activate Dogged

Dogged creates an important recovery decision.

Once Dogged is activated:

- the model continues functioning for that Player Turn;
- it cannot be healed;
- it dies in the States Phase.

Therefore ask:

> **Do I need this troop to act immediately, or is it more valuable to leave it recoverable?**

Good Dogged use:

- warband already committed deep into enemy territory;
- model needs one final attack;
- model must score or contest immediately;
- there is no practical recovery route.

Reasons **not** to activate Dogged:

- a Servant is nearby;
- the model remains strategically valuable next Turn;
- the unit is an important Specialist;
- the unit is lying somewhere safe enough to recover.

---

## NWI can be healed

A troop using No Wound Incapacitation can be healed. Successful healing removes a Wound and returns the troop to Normal State.

This creates an important pattern:

**enter NWI → finish the immediate job → retreat toward support → heal → regain durability**

NWI therefore synergizes strongly with Doctors when the unit can disengage rather than being forced into a suicide run.

---

# Engineer strategy

Engineer is essential infrastructure for lists containing:

- TAGs;
- REM gunfighters;
- Remote Presence pieces;
- expensive STR units;
- hacking-vulnerable attack pieces.

Engineer has two separate strategic functions.

## Function 1 — repair STR

A successful Engineer roll removes a Wound from a STR model and can cancel Unconscious.

A failed repair roll causes a Wound instead.

This makes Engineers particularly valuable for expensive multi-STR pieces.

## Function 2 — clear disabling States

Engineer can remove important States including:

- Immobilized-A;
- Immobilized-B;
- Isolated;
- Targeted;
- Stunned;
- Disconnected;
- Unconscious, where applicable.

When attempting to remove these non-Unconscious States, failure has no damaging consequence and the attempt can be repeated later.

This makes Engineer part of your **anti-hacking architecture**, not merely a repair piece.

A common sequence is:

**enemy Repeater → Oblivion / Carbonite / Spotlight → expensive attacker disabled → Servant reaches target → Engineer clears State → attacker returns to operation**

Against hacking-heavy armies, this can matter more than repairing lost STR.

---

## TAG + Engineer should be considered a package

If a large percentage of the army's points are concentrated in a TAG, the Engineer is part of the TAG's defensive infrastructure.

Think in terms of:

**TAG + Engineer + Servant + hacking defence**

The Servant should usually follow the TAG's **route**, not physically follow immediately behind it.

For example:

**TAG → corner A → corner B → midfield**

Position the Servant around corner A or B, where it remains protected but can reach the likely casualty location quickly.

Do not put the Servant beside the TAG if a single template, E/M attack, Mine, shotgun blast, or enemy assault can hit both.

Remote Presence makes Engineer support especially reliable because Command Tokens may reroll failed Engineer rolls against Remote Presence targets.

---

# Recovery economy

Before attempting recovery, count the entire operation:

**travel + recovery + likely retry + useful repositioning afterward**

A 40-point attack piece requiring one Servant Move and one Doctor roll is often an excellent recovery target.

A 10-point Line Infantry model requiring four Orders to reach usually is not.

But do not judge recovery only by points.

A cheap troop may justify several Orders if it is:

- the last surviving Specialist;
- needed for a Classified;
- the Lieutenant or part of a Lieutenant backup plan;
- necessary to contest a scoring zone;
- going to restore an Order next Tactical Phase;
- critical to preserving Fireteam function.

Conversely, recovering an expensive attacker may be poor if it simply stands back up in the same exposed position and immediately dies again.

The correct question is not:

> “Can I heal this?”

It is:

> **“What do I gain after it stands back up?”**

---

# The ARO resurrection pattern

One of the strongest recovery plays is supporting a serious ARO piece.

1. Place a threatening ARO piece in a position the opponent must solve.
2. Hide a Servant nearby behind Total Cover.
3. Force the opponent to spend Orders defeating the ARO piece.
4. On your Active Turn, move the Servant into contact.
5. Doctor or Engineer the piece back up.
6. Re-establish the defensive position if worthwhile.

The opponent may now need to defeat essentially the same obstacle again.

This converts a defensive troop into a **recurring Order tax**.

The threat of recovery can also force an opponent to spend another Order finishing an Unconscious model. In that case, the support architecture generated value even though no recovery roll was ever made.

---

# Servant positioning principles

## Keep Servants behind the fighting line

The Servant should normally be:

- behind Total Cover;
- close enough to reach important positions in one or two Orders;
- outside obvious template zones;
- outside common attack lanes.

Do not expose it unnecessarily merely because it is cheap.

## Avoid clustering support assets

Do not place:

**attack piece + Servant + Doctor/Engineer**

all within the same blast/template/attack zone.

The opponent should have to choose between attacking the combat piece and hunting the support network.

## Protect the recovery route

A Servant may technically be close to a casualty but still be unable to reach it efficiently because of:

- Mines;
- Perimeter weapons;
- direct-template AROs;
- hacking zones where relevant;
- enemy ZoC;
- exposed firelanes.

Deployment and defensive architecture should preserve **safe recovery corridors**.

---

# Doctor/Engineer and Fireteams

Standard Peripheral rules prevent a Controller with active Peripherals from simply operating as a normal Fireteam member.

N5.3 also clarifies Fireteam integrity interactions when a Fireteam member becomes a Peripheral Controller.

This creates two different support models.

| Architecture | Strength |
|---|---|
| **Doctor/Engineer integrated with Fireteam** | support moves naturally with the team |
| **Solo Doctor/Engineer + Servants** | wide table coverage while Controller remains safe |

Integrated support is useful for a compact team expected to remain together.

Solo support plus Servants is usually more flexible for armies using several independent attack pieces.

---

# Strong support-package patterns

## TAG package

**TAG + Engineer + Servant + hacking protection**

Use the Servant to shadow the TAG's intended route from protected positions.

## Two-flank insurance

**central Doctor/Engineer + Servant left + Servant right**

Provides broad recovery coverage without moving the Controller.

## ARO resurrection

Put a Servant near a valuable defensive shooter and force the opponent to defeat the same obstacle twice.

## Group-2 ambulance

Keep Doctor/Engineer and support Orders in a secondary Combat Group so recovery does not consume the primary attack group's remaining Orders.

## Doctor + premium HI

Especially attractive with:

- multiple Wounds;
- Cube;
- NWI;
- Doctor (2W);
- Doctor rerolls.

## Engineer + hacking defence

Do not assume every enemy hack must be prevented.

Sometimes it is more Order-efficient to:

**survive hack → clear State → continue**

rather than build the entire list around preventing every hacking attempt.

## Combined Doctor/Engineer profile

When available, one model with both skills can be exceptionally efficient because the same Servant network supports both VITA and STR components of the army.

## Late-game ambulance

Do not automatically sacrifice Servants early.

A surviving Servant on Turn 3 can turn an Unconscious Specialist, zone-control body, or attack piece back into a scorer.

---

# Recovery denial and counterplay

An opponent can attack the recovery architecture rather than the combat model.

Common counterplay includes:

- Shock or other effects that bypass useful Unconscious recovery;
- spending an extra Order to finish Unconscious troops;
- killing Servants;
- killing or Isolating the Controller;
- placing Mines or Perimeter weapons over casualties;
- standing near bodies to make recovery dangerous;
- forcing attack pieces to die too far forward for efficient recovery;
- pushing opponents into activating Dogged instead of remaining recoverable.

Conversely, when facing enemy Doctors or Engineers, ask whether it is worth spending one extra Order now to prevent the opponent from recovering a major asset next Turn.

---

# When support is not worth the investment

Do not automatically include heavy recovery infrastructure.

Support has lower value when most of the force consists of:

- cheap warbands;
- disposable trading pieces;
- forward-deployed troops expected to die deep in enemy territory;
- models with Dogged that routinely activate it;
- pieces whose normal operating positions are impossible to reach safely.

A highly disposable army may gain more from another attacker or Order generator than from an expensive support package.

---

# Core strategic principle

Doctor and Engineer do more than restore Wounds.

They change the **risk profile** of the models operating inside their recovery network.

A TAG, HI, REM, or major ARO piece supported by a properly positioned recovery network may require the opponent to:

1. defeat the combat piece;
2. prevent or survive its recovery;
3. hunt the Servant or Controller;
4. or spend additional Orders finishing the body.

That means support architecture contributes to **attrition, Order efficiency, defensive architecture, hacking defence, and mission resilience** even when no healing roll is ultimately made.
