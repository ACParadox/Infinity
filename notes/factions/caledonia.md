# Caledonia — List-Building and Tactical Note

## Scope

## Core identity

Current Caledonia is best understood as a **combined-arms aggression faction with unusually cheap violent pieces, strong camouflaged midfield scoring, and two very different command/economy architectures**.

A common successful structure is:

> **Ranged backbone + Uxía/SAS midfield + cheap violence/control + enough surviving specialists to score after the fight.**

The faction has no shortage of ways to kill models. The central list-building problem is avoiding a roster where every useful piece is also expected to die attacking.

The most important strategic fork is:

1. **Wallace Lieutenant:** deliberately exploit Caledonia's underpriced Irregular profiles by converting their Order contribution to Regular.
2. **Mormaer / conventional Lieutenant:** keep the army natively Regular, use Mormaer extra-order efficiency, and take Irregular pieces more selectively.

That fork should usually be decided before the rest of the list is built.

## Architecture A — Wallace as an Irregular-economy engine

### Do not frame Wallace as “good if you already have Irregulars”

The stronger construction is the reverse:

> **Choose Wallace specifically so the list can deliberately buy more of Caledonia's efficient Irregular troops without accepting their normal Order-pool drawback.**

The local `Inspiring_Leadership.md` rules reference states that while the Inspiring Leadership user is the Lieutenant, is on the table, and is not Null, **all Troopers in the Army List that provide Orders possess Regular Training**, except for the listed Religious Troop exception. The army also ignores Retreat!, and Wallace may declare a Coordinated Order including himself by spending only the Lieutenant Special Order.

This means Wallace is not merely an expensive combat Lieutenant. He changes how the army prices Irregular models.

### Best profiles to exploit with Wallace

| Unit | Current cost / AVA | Why Wallace changes its value |
|---|---|---|
| **Cameronian** | 18 pts, AVA 3 | Already one of the faction's most efficient attack pieces; Wallace turns its Order contribution Regular while it retains its Impetuous activation. |
| **45th Highlander / Galwegian Chain Rifle** | 5 pts, AVA TOTAL | Dirt-cheap smoke/trading body becomes real Order-pool infrastructure instead of a self-contained Irregular piece. |
| **Cateran** | 21–23 pts, AVA 4 | Cheap camouflaged sniper/ARO piece can sit passively and still contribute a Regular Order. |
| **McMurrough** | 23 pts, AVA 1 | Premium fast smoke/template/CC attacker no longer strands its Order on itself. |
| **Beasthunter** | 9 pts FTO or 15–17 pts camo, AVA 1 | Extremely compressed disposable/area-control profile; Wallace regularizes the Order on either the cheap FTO or camouflaged version. |
| **Warcor** | 3 pts, AVA 1 | Not a build-around, but even the cheapest Irregular utility slot can feed the normal pool. |

**Denma Connolly is not a Wallace-economy beneficiary.** Denma is already Regular. He is still excellent at 12 points, but his value is independent of Inspiring Leadership.

### Why the economy is stronger than “+N Regular Orders”

The Irregular pieces above often partly self-fund their battlefield role:

- Galwegians and Cameronians receive Impetuous activity.
- Caterans can create long-range pressure without spending Orders at all.
- Beasthunters can tax movement through mines, Panzerfaust/HFT threat, or camouflage without being the main attacker.
- McMurrough/Cameronians become the pieces that consume the main pool only when their attack window opens.

So a Wallace list can contain several cheap models that **create pressure on their own while still feeding the flexible Regular pool**.

That is the real list-building advantage.

### Good Wallace starting shell

A serious Wallace build can begin aggressively with something like:

- Wallace Lieutenant.
- 2 Cameronians.
- 2–4 five-point Galwegians.
- 1 Cateran, possibly 2 on suitable tables/missions.
- Beasthunter if the slot/role fits.
- McMurrough if the list wants another premium independent missile.

Then add:

- Uxía and/or SAS for scoring and midfield work.
- A proper ranged solution such as a Grey link, Mormaer, or Wulver AP Spitfire.
- Specialist/recovery support.

This is not “too many Irregulars rescued by Wallace.” It is **Wallace being used correctly as the reason to purchase those discounted profiles in the first place**.

### Wallace constraints

#### 1. Fifteen slots become the real resource

Five-point Highlanders and 18-point Cameronians fill the army quickly. The Wallace economy stops being useful when cheap bodies crowd out:

- specialists;
- a real long-range opener;
- redundancy;
- recovery/support;
- a Turn 3 scoring plan.

The optimization question is therefore not “how many Irregulars can I fit?” but:

> **How many discounted Irregular profiles can I exploit before the next slot is worth more as a specialist or premium gun?**

#### 2. Wallace is a single point of failure

The current Caledonia reference has **no Isobel Chain of Command profile**. Isobel's current profiles are the 25-point standard Wildcard and 25-point Hacker.

Therefore do **not** import old/legacy advice that treats `Wallace + Isobel CoC` as the current default safety package.

When Wallace goes Null, the list loses the active Inspiring Leadership economy. A Wallace list should therefore care about:

- deployment safety;
- not exposing Wallace merely because he is a competent fighter;
- whether his Fireteam position can be attacked through templates or pie-slicing;
- preserving enough functional Orders if the Wallace engine disappears.

#### 3. Wallace's combat ability is secondary to the economy

Wallace is good enough to fight, carry smoke, and join Fireteams as a Wildcard counting as Highlander/Wulver, but a Wallace list should not casually risk him for a merely efficient trade. He is supporting the entire Order architecture.

#### 4. Use the Lieutenant-only Coordinated Order

Inspiring Leadership also gives a Coordinated Order that includes Wallace for only his Special Lieutenant Order, without the normal Command Token/extra-Order expense. This is another reason to think of Wallace as an economy piece rather than only as a combat model.

Fireteam restrictions still apply; do not assume Fireteam members can participate merely because Wallace pays for the Coordinated Order.

## Architecture B — Mormaer / conventional Regular economy

The alternative is to avoid making the army dependent on Wallace and use Caledonia's strong native Regular pieces.

### Mormaer Lieutenant

Current options include:

- **41 pts / 0 SWC:** T2 Rifle Lieutenant (+1 Lt Order).
- **49 pts / 1 SWC:** HMG Lieutenant (+1 Lt Order).

The Lieutenant version gives up the non-Lieutenant Mormaer's Tactical Awareness but gains a second Lieutenant Order.

This is the natural command package when the list wants:

- a hard-to-remove Lieutenant rather than an Inspiring Leadership engine;
- more native Regular pieces;
- fewer cheap Irregulars;
- a premium heavy infantry anchor that can also fight.

The T2 Rifle Lieutenant is the safer baseline when another model is already the primary gun. The HMG Lieutenant is more aggressive and should be selected because the list genuinely wants the Lieutenant itself to be a major ranged attacker.

### Non-Lieutenant Mormaer HMG

The **49-point HMG with Tactical Awareness** is one of the defining current Caledonia gunfighters.

It provides:

- BS13 HMG reach;
- ARM5 body;
- X-Visor;
- a Tactical Awareness Order;
- Heavy Caledonian Fireteam access.

Use it when the list wants the Mormaer to attack repeatedly without also tying the entire Lieutenant plan to that attack piece.

### Other Lieutenant options

| Lieutenant | Use case |
|---|---|
| **Volunteer, 8 pts** | Cheapest passive Lieutenant when the list wants points elsewhere and can tolerate an obvious/fragile command model. |
| **Wallace, 38 pts** | Deliberate Irregular-economy architecture; not merely a combat LT. |
| **Mormaer T2, 41 pts** | Durable command piece with +1 Lt Order; strong default conventional LT. |
| **Mormaer HMG, 49 pts** | Aggressive heavy-gun LT when the list wants to spend LT resources directly on the Mormaer. |
| **Highlander Grey T2, 25 pts** | Cheaper fighting LT and link-compatible option; more exposed than a passive Volunteer and lacks Wallace/Mormaer economy effects. |
| **Highlander Grey HMG, 28 pts** | Very aggressive LT choice; mainly for lists comfortable revealing/using the Lieutenant as a gunfighter. |

## Uxía + SAS — the recurring midfield package

The strongest repeated current pattern is not simply “take some camo.” It is specifically that **Uxía and SAS solve the mission after the violent pieces have opened the board**.

### Uxía

Current Uxía is 25 points and brings a very compressed package:

- Camouflage / infiltration role.
- MSV1.
- close-range firepower.
- smoke.
- serious close combat.
- mission utility.

She should normally be evaluated as both a threat and a scoring piece, not as a one-use assassin.

### SAS

Current useful profiles include:

- **21 pts Forward Observer** — straightforward concealed specialist.
- **22 pts / 0.5 SWC Minelayer** — AP Mine plus midfield marker/control pressure.
- **25 pts E/M Mine profile** — more expensive, but can add anti-heavy state pressure.

The common division of labour is:

> **Violent pieces clear the lane; Uxía/SAS survive to take the objective.**

One Uxía + one SAS is an easy starting package. A second SAS is reasonable in missions where midfield scoring/control matters more than another premium attacker.

## Go-to Fireteam structures

The current legal chart supports several distinct approaches. None is mandatory in every list.

### 1. Grey + Volunteer Core/Haris — efficient ranged infrastructure

The Volunteer Fireteam can include:

- Volunteers;
- up to 3 Highlander Greys;
- 112 FTO;
- Beasthunter FTO;
- Denma;
- optional Spec-Ops content.

A common practical use is to make the **Grey HMG** the real gun and use cheap Volunteers to provide bodies/orders/team bonuses.

This is attractive when the rest of the list is spending heavily on:

- Uxía/SAS;
- dogs;
- Caterans;
- Wulver;
- independent midfield pieces.

Do not overinvest in the Volunteer shell merely to have five models. Build the smallest team that gives the shooter/support package real value.

### 2. Heavy Caledonian Haris/Duo — premium combat team

The Heavy Caledonian formation is built from the required-choice Mormaer/Grey foundation and may add one Scot Guard FTO.

A highly natural three-model package is:

- **Mormaer HMG** — long-range active gun.
- **Highlander Grey T2 Boarding Shotgun** — smoke, close-range killing, durable short-range coverage.
- **Scot Guard FTO Paramedic** — specialist/recovery and Panzerfaust/AP-SMG utility.

This is one of the strongest current “quality Haris” templates because each model fills a different range/utility job.

Isobel is also a Wildcard counting as Mormaer and can alter the composition when the list wants her specialist/hacker tools.

### 3. Wulver Core/Haris/Duo — aggressive medium-range package

Wulvers are strong enough that the **AP Spitfire can simply operate solo**; do not force a Wulver Fireteam just because it is available.

Build the Wulver team when the list explicitly wants:

- multiple 2-Wound assault bodies;
- fast mid-board fighting;
- Discover pressure;
- a coherent short/mid-range advance package.

A Scot Guard FTO may join, and Wallace/Cadin can enter through Wildcard status/counts-as relationships.

The main cost is concentration: several expensive models move together and solve similar brawling problems.

### 4. Scots Guards Fireteam — utility/gun platform

Scot Guard FTO profiles can form Core/Haris/Duo and add one Wardriver.

Reasons to use this formation include:

- AP Spitfire / Missile Launcher / T2 Marksman / HRL options;
- Paramedic specialist;
- Wardriver hacking pocket;
- keeping more expensive Mormaer/Wulver pieces independent.

The non-FTO camouflaged Scots Guard profiles **cannot** join these Fireteams.

### 5. Galwegian Core — specialized warband formation

Galwegians can form a Core, with Denma as an additional member. Wallace is a Wildcard counting as Highlander and may join otherwise legal teams.

This is not the default list backbone merely because five-point bodies exist. A Galwegian Core should be chosen because moving the warband package together and exploiting the Fireteam structure is worth the opportunity cost versus simply scattering cheap Highlanders around the table.

## Go-to units

### Near-default considerations

- **Uxía** — midfield attacker/scorer and one of the faction's best role-compression pieces.
- **SAS** — concealed specialist/control piece; FO and Minelayer are especially easy to justify.
- **Cameronian** — 18-point high-speed attack/trade missile; even better in Wallace economy lists.
- **Mormaer HMG** — premium ranged anchor, especially when the list needs a serious active gun.
- **Highlander Grey HMG / T2 BSG** — efficient linked gun and close-range complement.
- **Cateran** — cheap camouflaged long-range pressure; particularly attractive under Wallace.

### Excellent but architecture-dependent

- **Wallace** — excellent when intentionally exploiting Irregular pricing; not an automatic LT in mostly-Regular lists.
- **McMurrough** — exceptional independent attacker, but overlaps with cheap Cameronians and is not mandatory.
- **Wulver AP Spitfire** — strong solo gunfighter or Fireteam member; does not require a full Wulver team.
- **Galwegian Chain Rifle** — exceptional at 5 points in Wallace builds; more self-contained/awkward without him.
- **Beasthunter** — very strong role compression but AVA 1, so choose FTO cheap defence or camo midfield based on what the list lacks.

## Lesser-used / underappreciated tools

### Beasthunter FTO — 9 points

One of the clearest sleepers.

The FTO profile brings:

- Heavy Flamethrower;
- Panzerfaust;
- AP Mine;
- DA CC Weapon;
- Volunteer-team access.

At 9 points it can defend, trade, carry a disposable long-range threat, and add a mine while barely affecting the point budget. In a Wallace list its Irregular drawback also disappears from the Order economy.

### Denma Connolly — 12 points

Denma is already Regular and Impetuous, so he does not need Wallace to function.

He adds:

- smoke;
- Chain Rifle;
- Heavy Pistol;
- serious AP+DA close combat;
- Volunteer and Galwegian Fireteam access.

Use him when the army wants a cheap Regular attack/trade piece but another Galwegian/Cameronian would duplicate too much Irregular pressure.

### Motorized 112 — 19 points

The motorcycle doctor adds fast recovery plus smoke equipment. It is useful when the list contains valuable 2-Wound infantry or a flank-oriented attack group that a deployment-zone doctor cannot efficiently reach.

### Wardriver — 14 or 16 points

The Wardriver is not a reason to turn CHA into a hacking army. It is a cheap way to make a specific route or hackable attacker respect:

- Cybermine;
- Hacking Device;
- Sixth Sense;
- optional Disco Baller on the 16-point profile;
- Scots Guard Fireteam access.

The value is often deterrence rather than winning a broad hacking war.

### Traktor Mul Guided package

The 9-point Katyusha and 14-point Uragan provide Guided attack options. Caledonia can support them through Forward Observers/hacking, but this should be a deliberate alternate attack vector rather than a tax included automatically.

Use it when the list already wants the relevant targeting pieces and benefits from forcing opponents to respect Guided even while hiding from Mormaer/Grey/Cateran guns.

### Camouflaged Scots Guard

The non-FTO HRL/AP-SMG profiles trade Fireteam access for camouflage/forward-deployment utility. They are useful when the list wants another marker/ambush layer rather than another visible linked gun.

### Armand 'Le Muet'

Armand is the premium sniper alternative. His advantage is a more substantial independent sniper package; his problem is that Caledonia already has very efficient Caterans.

Take Armand because the specific durable/MSV/Minelayer sniper profile solves a matchup problem, not because the faction needs “a sniper.”

## Repeated useful combinations

### Wallace bargain package

**Wallace + 2 Cameronians + 2–4 Galwegians + Cateran**

Purpose: deliberately turn multiple discounted Irregular profiles into a Regular-feeding combat group while retaining Impetuous/self-funding pressure.

Add Beasthunter or McMurrough only if the list still has specialist/ranged/scoring slots available.

### Grey Core + independent premium pieces

**Grey HMG + cheap Volunteer shell**

Purpose: obtain an efficient primary gun/order structure while leaving points for SAS/Uxía, dogs, Caterans, Wulver, etc.

### Heavy Haris

**Mormaer HMG + Grey T2 BSG + Scot Guard Paramedic**

Purpose: long-range breaker + smoke/close-range brawler + specialist/recovery in one compact package.

### Midfield scoring pair

**Uxía + SAS FO** or **Uxía + SAS Minelayer**

Purpose: keep concealed/scoring capability alive after the army's disposable violence opens the board.

### Cateran ambiguity

**Cateran(s) + SAS/Beasthunter/other marker pressure**

Purpose: make deployment and long-lane movement harder to read. The opponent should not be able to identify every marker's function before committing movement.

Double Cateran is much more attractive under Wallace because both snipers can remain mostly passive while still contributing Regular Orders.

### Wulver solo + cheap backbone

**Wulver AP Spitfire + Volunteer/Grey shell**

Purpose: avoid paying for a full Wulver team when only one mobile AP gun is needed.

### Small hacking pocket

**Wardriver + Isobel Hacker**

Purpose: create a limited cyber-threat/defensive zone in a faction opponents may otherwise treat as hacking-light. This is niche; do not pay for both unless the matchup/mission/network plan makes the investment useful.

## What is not mandatory

### Wallace

Wallace is not a universal tax. He is excellent when the army is intentionally exploiting Irregular profiles. If the list is mostly Mormaers, Greys, Wulvers, Volunteers, SAS and other Regular pieces, his economy effect may not justify replacing a cheaper or more directly useful Lieutenant.

### McMurrough

Still excellent, but current **18-point Cameronians** make it much easier to skip him. McMurrough should be taken because his superior independent attack package is specifically useful, not simply because “Caledonia takes McMurrough.”

### Full Wulver Fireteam

A single AP Spitfire Wulver can solve the mobile mid-range gun job without committing 90+ points to a brawling team. Build the Wulver Fireteam only when its multiple bodies/team bonuses are part of the actual plan.

### Volunteers

Volunteers remain highly useful as a Grey-link/order shell, but CHA no longer needs every list to start from a cheap five-Volunteer block.

### Armand

Caterans cover cheap long-range pressure so efficiently that Armand should have a specific purpose rather than being included as the default sniper.

## Common list-building traps

### 1. Too many models with the same job

Cameronians, McMurrough, Galwegians, Denma and Wulvers can all become “run forward and kill/trade” pieces. Taking too many produces a list that is terrifying but redundant.

Ask whether the next violent slot should instead become:

- a specialist;
- a long-range gun;
- a doctor;
- a marker/minelayer;
- a second scoring piece.

### 2. Using Wallace without exploiting him

Wallace plus one Irregular model is usually not an economy architecture. If Wallace is selected, deliberately test whether additional Cameronians/Galwegians/Caterans/Beasthunter/McMurrough produce more value than conventional Regular filler.

### 3. Exploiting Wallace so hard that the list stops scoring

The opposite failure also exists. A 15-slot army full of underpriced Irregular violence can run out of actual button pushers and late-game pieces.

### 4. Risking Wallace for an ordinary trade

Wallace's death can change the Order character of a large portion of the list. His battlefield value is therefore larger than his personal combat profile suggests.

### 5. Assuming old Isobel Chain of Command advice still applies

It does not under the current repository reference. Current Isobel has standard and Hacker profiles, not Chain of Command.

### 6. Overattacking instead of scoring

The Open Barcelona experience is a useful warning: CHA is capable of spending a whole turn destroying enemy pieces while losing sight of the mission.

Before beginning a long attack run, identify which surviving model will perform the required Turn 2/3 objective work and reserve Orders accordingly.

## Two practical build orders

### Wallace build

1. **Take Wallace LT because you intend to exploit Inspiring Leadership.**
2. Select the Irregular bargain package: usually 1–2 Cameronians, 2–4 Galwegians, Cateran(s), Beasthunter/McMurrough as appropriate.
3. Add **Uxía/SAS** until the mission has enough real specialist coverage.
4. Add one dependable ranged backbone: Grey link, Mormaer, Wulver, etc.
5. Add recovery/utility only where it protects something worth recovering.
6. Check the **15-slot ceiling**.
7. Ask what remains functional if Wallace dies.
8. Remove redundant attackers before removing scoring pieces.

### Mormaer / conventional build

1. Choose the LT: Mormaer T2 is the durable baseline; Volunteer/Grey are cheaper alternatives.
2. Choose the primary gun: Mormaer HMG, Grey HMG, Wulver AP Spitfire, or a specific Fireteam package.
3. Add Uxía + at least one SAS unless the mission gives a strong reason not to.
4. Add 1–2 independent pressure pieces: Cameronian, McMurrough, Denma, Cateran, Beasthunter.
5. Add the Fireteam only if it improves actual range/scoring/order efficiency.
6. Ensure a Turn 3 specialist survives outside the main attack route.

## Video/community consensus vs disagreements

### Strong convergence

Across the current faction reviews, sample list construction, forum testing and tournament evidence, the recurring ideas are:

- Caledonia should exploit **cheap violence without letting it replace the mission plan**.
- **Uxía/SAS** are among the most natural midfield/scoring tools.
- **Cameronians** are exceptionally efficient at current pricing.
- **Mormaers** are now serious gun/command pieces rather than old-edition curiosities.
- **Grey + cheap support** remains a strong efficient link concept.
- The new **Heavy Caledonian** structure gives the faction a real premium Haris/Duo option.
- Full Wulver teams are good but not required; solo Wulver use is credible.

### Main strategic disagreement / fork

The key disagreement is less “which unit is good?” and more **how much of the list should be built around Wallace's Inspiring Leadership**.

One school leans toward the classic/current Wallace economy: deliberately maximize Cameronians, Highlanders, Caterans and other discounted Irregular bodies.

The other leans toward a more conventional modern combined-arms list: Mormaer/Greys/Wulver plus a smaller number of Irregular missiles and more native Regular specialists/support.

Both are coherent. What is inefficient is sitting in the middle with Wallace while barely exploiting the Irregular conversion, or taking so many cheap irregular-derived pieces that the list loses the ability to score.

## Working conclusions

Current starting assumptions for future Caledonia analysis:

- **Wallace is a proactive list-building engine, not a patch for a list that accidentally contains Irregulars.**
- In a Wallace list, deliberately test **Cameronians, 5-point Galwegians, Caterans, McMurrough and Beasthunter** as discounted Order-generating value pieces.
- **Isobel Chain of Command is not a current option**; old advice relying on that package must be discarded.
- **Uxía + SAS** is the strongest default midfield/scoring starting point.
- **Mormaer HMG** and **linked Grey HMG** are the cleanest primary ranged starting points.
- **Mormaer + Grey + Scot Guard FTO** is a strong premium Haris template.
- **Solo Wulver AP Spitfire** deserves consideration before automatically buying a full Wulver Fireteam.
- **Beasthunter FTO at 9 points** is one of the faction's clearest underused efficiency pieces.
- **Caterans become substantially better in Wallace architecture** because their passive ARO role no longer comes with an Irregular Order-pool penalty.
- Caledonia's recurring practical failure mode is **spending too many Orders on killing and not enough on scoring**.
