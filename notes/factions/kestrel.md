## Strategic identity: good guns plus unusually useful infrastructure

Recurring themes from the video-indexed discussions and the concrete 2026 sample lists:

- **One or two decisive gunfighters with different attack bands:** Griffin, Hetkari, Tikbalang or Squalos; less conventional choices include Redeye, Bulleteer, Maximus, Crux and airborne Banshee. Do not buy several expensive attackers that all solve the same lane.
- **Cheap and multifunctional command/mission shell:** Fennecs, Magistrate, Pathfinder, Fireflies, Mulebots, Fugazi, Tech-Bee. Several are both order contributors and genuine defensive or specialist tools.
- **Layered midfield uncertainty:** Scarecrow hidden deployment/mines/hackers, Blink hidden sniper/rocket threats, Banshee entry, Dart/Beasthunter, Peacemaker/repeater and Firefly mines/turrets. It is much harder to counter a Kestrel list if the opponent cannot infer every live threat from the visible deployment.
- **Strong flexible small links:** a FENNECS Core/Haris for value, KESTREL Haris/Duo for heavy infantry and Firefly support, ARMORED Duos for a TAG with a tailored partner, COLCORP Duo/Haris for Magistrate plus cheap Jackals. **One Core, one Haris, unlimited Duos** in the current chart; do not assume the sectorial must always take a full five-model Core.
- **Activation efficiency beyond the ten-Regular-Order shorthand:** Tactical Awareness on a Pathfinder, TAG, Bipandra, Harper or Agent Maximus; NCO on Fireflies and Sforza; affordable low-order support. Extra special Orders only provide their rules-defined uses; they are not automatically interchangeable generic Orders.
- **Resilience needs planning:** premium HI/TAGs invite hacking, E/M, AP, isolated flanks and attrition of the surrounding support pieces. Have a matching Engineer/Doctor, safe Repeater approach, and a route to the mission after the primary gunfighter is gone.

**Practical list skeleton:** command shell + long-range problem solver + close/midrange exploitation piece + 2–4 mission/board-control pieces + hacking/utility network + recovery. Decide which model actually wins the opponent's first exposed FtF and which model scores while they react.

## Common viable architectures

### A. FENNECS Core + separate heavy threat (general-purpose / video example)

- Start with at least **one actual Fennec**; its Fireteam needs that native minimum. A cheap Fennec is 10 points, but profiles such as **13-point FO/Sensor/Disco Baller**, **14-point Thunderbolt (+1B)/mine/Triangulated Fire**, **16-point Hacker/FastPanda/E/Mitter**, or **12-point Paramedic** often deliver substantially more value than another bare rifle.
- A **Griffin HMG (37 pts/1.5 SWC)** or Feuerbach can contribute substantial shooting while counting as Fennec in the FENNECS row; other FENNECS inclusions include up to two **Black A.I.R. FTO**, one **Magistrate**, and the permitted **Agent Maximus**. Appropriate Wildcards include Quinn, Emily, Bipandra, Harper FTO, Sforza FTO, Pathfinder and Crux FTO, but never ignore the native Fennec minimum.
- Keep a separate specialist/short-range team, a TAG or infiltrating control layer rather than using all your list points to embellish the Core. Even the 2026 entry-level lists are **not** simply a five-Fennec brick: they add Magistrate/Jackal and Firefly elements plus hidden threats.
- **Trade-off:** long-range link bonuses and cheap shared mobility versus concentration of investment and vulnerability to one template/attack lane. Use Core when it is the right tool, not because it is compulsory.

### B. Griffin / Hetkari / Firefly KESTREL Haris or Duo

- **Griffin:** BS14/MSV1, two wounds, HMG or Feuerbach. Good reliable linked or solo attacker, and a bridge between FENNECS and KESTREL formations.
- **Hetkari:** BS14, Marksmanship, Combat Instinct, ARM5; **MULTI Red Fury 48 pts/1.5 SWC** or Missile Launcher 47 pts/1.5 SWC. Pick when its attack/defence profile is meaningfully different from the TAG or Griffin you already bought. The missile can be a difficult ARO problem, but standing exposed is still a calculated sacrifice, not automatic safety.
- **Firefly:** every current profile has **NCO** and **Albedo**; useful partners are CoC, Engineer, Hacker, HRL, repeater carrier or armed-turret Minelayer. The **19-point repeater** is a particularly economical companion; the **23-point Engineer/CoC** variants each have substantial additional utility.
- **Quinn** and **Bipandra** count as Kestrel in a legal link; Pathfinder or other eligible Wildcards can round out a Duo/Haris for a distinct role. The native KESTREL chart allows **Duo/Haris, not Core**; it requires an eligible required-choice Griffin, Firefly or Hetkari.
- **Trade-off:** very punchy mobile Haris, but two- and three-model HI teams expose a predictable hacking route. Use separate positioning and defensive mines/repeaters to make that route expensive.

### C. Tikbalang or Squalos ARMORED Duo (mobile TAG battery)

- **Tikbalang 69 pts/1.5 SWC:** BS15 HMG, Mimetism (-3), **BS Attack (SR-1)**, Climbing Plus, Heavy Flamethrower, TacAware. Fast board access and efficient fights across awkward geometry. The **73-point LT (+1 Order)** is a distinct, riskier plan.
- **Squalos Mk-II:** 55-point MULTI Marksman Rifle, 62-point HMG/Grenade Launcher or 66-point MULTI HMG/NCO; all current variants have **TacAware** and **BS Attack (+1SD)**. Choose **62-point GL** when the extra angle/indirect threat matters, **66-point NCO MULTI HMG** when you want the main direct-fire hammer and can exploit LT Orders, or **55-point MMR** for a lower-cost durable midfield TAG. Do not evaluate them as interchangeable guns.
- **17-point Pathfinder TacAware Wildcard** is a standout TAG partner: fast 15-10 movement, FO/Sensor/Repeater, a separate special activation and objective capability. The **14-point normal Pathfinder** is preferable when points, not the extra special activation, are the binding constraint.
- **Firefly NCO** can be the ARMORED Duo partner to give the TAG a nearby support/mission piece and potentially convert an otherwise unused LT Order into useful team tempo, subject to the current Fireteam/order rules. A Firefly Engineer can instead make the Duo more resilient. Other permitted optional members are Bulleteer or TAG Maximus.
- **Mandatory constraint:** an ARMORED Duo needs at least one **Tikbalang or Squalos Mk-II**. Pathfinder is a **Wildcard**, not a standalone native ARMORED anchor. The chart is for a **Duo**, not a TAG-Haris.
- **Watch the LT interaction:** a Tikbalang LT's personal LT Order is not necessarily a second free Fireteam Order. Explicitly budget which special Orders move a Fireteam and which would require independent activation; using an LT Order directly can also reveal the LT. An NCO Firefly can mitigate the mismatch.
- **CoC architecture:** if fielding Tikbalang LT, keep a CoC Firefly or Magistrate as succession insurance **outside the exposed TAG Duo**; do not put LT and CoC into the same vulnerable two-model link.
- **Trade-off:** TAG + Pathfinder is one of the cleanest mobile TAG/specialist bundles. TAG + Firefly is more about NCO/Engineering/CoC utility. Two TAGs in a Duo consume points and concentrate anti-TAG exposure; the second TAG does not automatically make it efficient.

### D. Magistrate + Jackals COLCORP Duo/Haris (cheap utility screen)

- A **Magistrate is mandatory (min 1)**. Good options: **19-point Strategos L1 Lieutenant**, **21-point CoC Magistrate**, 17-point basic Specialist or 21-point MULTI Marksman specialist. All have Counterintelligence.
- **6-point Jackal FTO Panzerfaust or Light Rocket Launcher**: cheap **Irregular** models that can be included in COLCORP. They add expendable weapons/ARO and close protection without pretending to be full-value Regular order batteries. N5 order behaviour and link activation must be budgeted correctly.
- Beasthunter FTO can serve as a close-range template/anti-armour threat, but only the **9-point FTO** has the COLCORP chart access; its camouflaged forward-deployed profiles do not.
- A 10-point infiltrating Jackal or the **11-point E/M GL Minelayer** works separately as midfield denial, not as a COLCORP FTO.
- **Trade-off:** very efficient zone control and command shell; do not put the LT, CoC and all command security together in the same cluster.

### E. Spread-out solo pieces / no Core

A fully populated Core is optional. Spend its points on a **Tik/Squalos/Redeye**, **hidden Scarecrow/Blink**, **Peacemaker/Bulleteer**, an **airborne Banshee**, and a mission-ready utility/doctor/engineer shell. A KESTREL Haris plus an ARMORED Duo, or even independent attackers with limited Duos, is a legitimate list shape. Best when the table rewards multiple independent attack angles rather than a five-model formation.

## Lieutenant and command choices

| Plan | Reason to choose it | Exposure and pairing |
|---|---|---|
| **BCA Magistrate LT, Strategos L1 (19/23 pts)** | Efficient central command, Counterintelligence and an ordinary specialist body; excellent conservative default in many lists | Protect him: do not send the LT to trade. CoC, if purchased, is a separate insurance investment |
| **Fennec LT (10 pts)** | Lowest-friction cheap LT; can fit a FENNECS shell | Predictable line-unit LT and relatively low personal durability; do not assume a Decoy on the current Fennec LT |
| **Griffin LT HMG (37 pts)** | Offensive LT on a multiwound MSV gunfighter | Uses the piece opponents want to kill; consider safer NCO/CoC structure |
| **Hetkari LT (47/48 pts)** | Strong shooting in an HQ slot | Exposed heavy-infantry LT is attractive to hacking and E/M |
| **Tikbalang LT (+1 Order, 73 pts)** | High-tempo TAG plan: TacAware and extra LT Order access | Strong but high-stakes, especially in a visible Duo; CoC elsewhere and useful NCO are deliberate mitigations |
| **Agent Maximus LT (+1 SWC profile)** | Tactical Awareness and a premium all-round specialist tied to a Fennec structure | Concentrated premium target and significant opportunity cost |
| **TAG Maximus LT (82 pts/0 SWC)** | Another very high-investment attacking command piece | Preserve mission Orders/support and succession plan |

**NCO outlets:** Fireflies have NCO natively; **Sforza** also has NCO, but its FTO lacks the solo version's Holoprojector/Forward Deployment. The **66-point Squalos** carries NCO itself. Plan the LT-order recipient before purchasing an offensive LT. **CoC vs NCO:** one spends special orders efficiently; the other insures succession. Those roles are complementary but do not require the same team.

## Role-by-role troop selection

### Fennec choice: Disco Baller vs Hacker vs Thunderbolt/Minelayer

| Profile | Best reason | Not interchangeable with |
|---|---|---|
| **FO, Sensor, Disco Baller (13)** | Missions, Sensor, and Disco Baller utility from a very cheap regular model. Valuable even when not firing. One of the underused options specifically mentioned in the January 2026 video discussion | The **Hacker** is the profile with **E/Mitter/FastPanda**; the current FO/Disco Baller does **not** carry E/Mitter |
| **Hacking Device, FastPanda/E/Mitter (16 / 0.5 SWC)** | Extend hacking area / local electronic pressure, plus cheap specialist in a Fennec fireteam | Not as universal a safe button pusher as FO; vulnerable to enemy hacker quality |
| **Thunderbolt (+1B), Shock Mine, Triangulated Fire, Minelayer (14)** | Excellent cost-to-defence ratio, mine lane denial and shooting from a long defensive angle; especially rewarding as FENNECS filler | Does not provide the first two profiles' mission/hacking functionality |
| **Paramedic (12)** | Cheap objective and link recovery | No Sensor/Disco or hacking |
| **Commlink +1 Specialist (12)** | Budget specialist / comms tool | Less board denial |

Often **take two different Fennec utilities**, not three copies of “cheap Fennec”. The January 2026 sample lists both illustrate Hacker + Paramedic + Thunderbolt/Minelayer.

### Which ARO / defensive pieces?

- **Fennec Thunderbolt/Minelayer:** cheap continuous value; is often better concealed than an expensive exposed model.
- **Black A.I.R. FTO MULTI Sniper / Neurocinetics (33/1.5):** MSV2, Mimetism (-3), Number 2, high reactive threat in FENNECS; judge by actual sight lines and ability to remove it after a bad FtF.
- **Blink HRL / WildParrot or MULTI Sniper:** Hidden Deployment and Mimetism (-6) create deployment uncertainty. Treat as a surprise ARO/ambush, not a guaranteed durable defence.
- **Firefly armed turret / AP mine / HRL:** a movable, rebuildable defensive layer around the mission and the Fireteam.
- **Sierra Total Reaction HMG 23/1 SWC or Thunderbolt (+1B) 18/0:** choose when sustained reactive B and coverage justify its opportunity cost. This is *not* the same niche as a 7-point flash Fugazi.
- **Fugazi 7/0:** Repeater, Mimetism (-3), Flash Pulse and a cheap regular Order; often excellent filler, but **AVA 1**. Do not claim “always mandatory” without a particular list reason.

### Remote and support choices

- **Pathfinder normal (14):** Sensor/FO/Repeater; one of the cleanest utility purchases. **TacAware (17)** when a Fireteam/TAG plan actually spends that special activation. A **non-FT Pathfinder** remains useful on its own.
- **Peacemaker:** forward-deployed Repeater plus Auxbot and short-range template/shotgun angles. A threat that can take space while big guns remove AROs.
- **Bulleteer:** **19-point Heavy Shotgun (+1B)** for close-range burst and attack geometry, or **22/1 SWC Spitfire** for mobile Mimetism (-6) work. Can be the optional body in a legal ARMORED Duo; compare to a Pathfinder for mission utility.
- **Tech-Bee 9:** TAGCom **Dodge (PH+3), GizmoKit (PH+1)** in the current reference, plus repeater/adhesive utility. Verify the current TAGCom/group interaction from rules; **do not copy the archived January summary's claim of a general +1 BS bonus**.
- **Mulebot 8:** Baggage/Repeater/deactivation and cheap Regular body; **EVO 14/.5** if the list needs its programs; **TR combi 17** for a different defensive role. Cheap does not mean disposable if it is essential to your order economy.
- **Machinist/Palbot or Firefly Engineer:** purchase recovery in relation to TAG/REM placement rather than as a decorative checkbox. A repairer hidden in the same fatal blast zone as the TAG may not be a useful repairer.

### Less conventional or situational units

- **Drummer + Drumbot:** a legitimate multi-body shooting and board-control alternative. The **21-point FO controller** offers an unusually low entry point for a Peripheral package; the heavier Drumbot HMG/HRMC/Feuerbach combinations cost much more. Evaluate the controller's hiding position, Peripheral attack vectors and vulnerability to templates, not only total gun Burst.
- **Redeye:** extremely fast Mimetism (-6) aerial vehicle with BS14; flexible Spitfire/Blitzen versus continuous-damage GL. It cannot claim Cover and is a conspicuous S7 piece. Take when access/speed wins an angle that a TAG cannot.
- **Scarecrow:** Hidden Deployment/Infiltration/Mimetism (-6) specialist, repeater, KHD, hacking or AP-mine Minelayer. Excellent complement to visible PanO firepower because opponent must respect *unseen* midfield threats.
- **Banshee:** Combat Jump/Parachutist with Pulzar (+1B), WildParrot or Hacker/KHD. Not just an assault gun: can create scoring or defensive problems behind enemy ARO lines.
- **Crux Knight:** expensive but capable CC/assault specialist; non-FTO Hidden/FD builds versus **FTO Wildcard** firefight/scoring variants. Do not silently link a Hidden Deployment profile.
- **Dart / Beasthunter:** camouflage / close-range angle control with dangerous asymmetric tools. Beasthunter's cheap COLCORP FTO and solo camo variants are entirely different listbuilding decisions.
- **Emily Handelman:** E/M GL and FO, FENNECS or COLCORP equivalence, no SWC; can force a stationary/hackable enemy to react to speculative/indirect electronic threat. Confirm trajectory, target and orders before making the grenade plan the whole list.
- **Quinn:** FO/MSV1 with **26-point MULTI Marksman** or **26-point MULTI Rifle + Disco Baller**. One of the most role-dense eligible Wildcards; choose gun range versus Disco utility.
- **Bipandra:** an actual Doctor with a **25-point TacAware** option, unlike a mere Fennec Paramedic. Choose for a costly multiwound HI cluster; do not simply compare gun costs.
- **Dr Harper FTO:** specialist with NWI, Nanoscreen, **TacAware** and a **+1 SWC** loadout. A notable way to fund SWC-intensive builds, but not interchangeable with the non-FTO forward-deployed version.
- **Knauf:** independently useful MSV1/Mimetism sniper with Decoy; choose when a solo rifle with concealed target information is worth more than another linked ARO.
- **Sforza:** **23-point FTO NCO Viral Rifle/adhesive launcher** or **26-point FTO Albedo** can convert unused LT tempo into threat projection. His **unlinked** Holoprojector/FD profile is strategically different.

## What the January 2026 example lists actually demonstrate

The archive supplies **full Army strings** and point allocations; these are historical *illustrations*, **not guaranteed current N5.3 legal exports**:

- **“Expanded” (300 points / 5.5 SWC):** Griffin HMG; Fennec Hacker, Paramedic and Thunderbolt/Minelayer; Magistrate Strategos LT; two 6-point Jackal FTO; Hetkari MULTI Red Fury; Firefly Engineer and Paramedic; Group 2 Banshee KHD, Blink HRL, Scarecrow Minelayer, two Mulebots.
- **“Heavy Hitter” (299 points / 5 SWC):** same Fennec/Griffin/Magistrate/Jackal base, the same two Fireflies and a **Tikbalang HMG** instead of Hetkari; second group Blink HRL, Scarecrow KHD, Tech-Bee and a Mulebot.

What changes between the lists is instructive: keep a **cheap functional shell** and switch the central premium attacker while retaining hidden midfield/ARO problems. These are also reminders to check **Group placement** for any group-specific support bonus, and to match **Engineer support** to the TAG. The archived comment summary discusses the risks of **Tikbalang LT**; it does **not** establish that Tik LT is always wrong, only that offensive LT requires a deliberate plan.

## Sequencing and counters (reverse-engineering the opponent's plan)

The Infinity Dante anti-Kestrel video summary describes the recurring counters as **hacking/E/M pressure against heavy attackers, long firing lanes to force movement, exposing the weak command shell, and solving hidden pieces**. Convert that warning into a Kestrel deployment checklist:

1. **Before deployment:** identify enemy primary ranged ARO, MSV, hacking repeaters, warband/template entry, AD threat, and the lane the opponent wants your TAG/Griffin to traverse.
2. **First active turn:** use the best gun for *that* lane to remove an ARO or repeater; use Sensor/Disco/deployables to protect the crossing; do not advance premium models deep into unaddressed hacking coverage.
3. **Follow through:** spend the cheaper pieces' Orders on positioning, buttons, mines/turrets and denial. Preserve Banshee/Scarecrow/Blink threat as long as concealment is itself pressuring enemy decisions.
4. **Opponent's turn:** force several different expensive answers rather than one obvious target. Separate Engineer/Doctor/LT/CoC from the TAG's danger zone.
5. **Turn 3:** prioritize objective positions and surviving specialist access; a 70-point gun that does not score can be worth less than a 13-point Fennec on the right objective.

## Common mistakes / stale-video traps

- **Treating an FTO name as automatic Fireteam access:** Kestrel's canonical chart excludes the **Clipper FTO** and **Yamabushi FTO** from its Fireteam rows. Likewise only **Black A.I.R. FTO / Sforza FTO / Crux FTO / Harper FTO** link where their row requires FTO.
- **Adding arbitrary models to a Core/Haris:** FENNECS requires a Fennec; COLCORP requires a Magistrate; KESTREL needs its required-choice body; ARMORED Duo needs Tikbalang or Squalos. Pathfinder Wildcard does not remove these.
- **Assuming “Jackal FTO” supplies a normal Regular order:** Jackals are **Irregular**, including their 6-point FTO choices. The group can still be efficient, but count it honestly.
- **Bundling all insurance with the exposed LT:** CoC in the same endangered Fireteam as the LT is generally bad insurance.
- **Taking an LT TAG just because it has +1 Order:** build an actual LT-order/NCO/CoC and retreat plan first.
- **Treating the Video's 2025/early-2026 Army costs and skills as current:** reconcile with the pinned N5.3 reference. In particular, **Tech-Bee TAGCom is Dodge/GizmoKit**, **Fennec FO Disco Baller is not the Hacker E/Mitter**, and current **Fennec LT has no listed Decoy**.
- **Copying unreviewed machine summaries literally:** archived text occasionally misidentifies units, weapon traits and links (for instance describing a TAG/Griffin–Squalos formation that does not match the canonical current chart). Use it for *themes*, not list legality.

## Quick build checklist

1. Pick **primary attack lane**: Griffin/Hetkari, Tikbalang, Squalos, Redeye, Bulleteer, Crux or Maximus.
2. Pick **complementary second vector**, not a duplicate heavy gun: AD Banshee, hidden Scarecrow/Blink, fast REM, Firefly area-control or a second gun in a different range band.
3. Choose **LT** (default safe Magistrate or Fennec versus deliberate offensive LT) and decide whether **NCO** and **CoC** have distinct jobs.
4. Choose **one meaningful Fireteam architecture** and validate native minima; add Duo(s) if their movement/special-order savings outweigh formation constraints.
5. Budget **button pressers**, **Sensor/FO**, **Repeaters/hacking**, **mines/turrets**, **Doctor/Engineer**, and a reserve or hidden response to Turn 1 pressure.
6. Check **SWC**, Combat Groups and each **TacAware/LT/NCO** order's actual permitted destination. Check the enemy's easiest hacking/AP/E/M route before finishing deployment.
