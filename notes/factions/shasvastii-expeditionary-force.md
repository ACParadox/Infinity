## Core identity: make the opponent spend Orders solving uncertainty

Shasvastii win by **turning hidden information into tempo**, then exploiting the gap with a small number of lethal pieces. They are **not** a faction that must table the enemy.

- **Marker saturation:** Camo, Hidden Deployment, Impersonation, decoys, Mines, and seed-state uncertainties force Discover, Sensor, cautious movement, and inconvenient lines of approach. A model in marker state may do more work by *remaining ambiguous* than by shooting.
- **Late-game scoring:** infiltrating specialists and Shasvastii Embryos can preserve scenario-relevant presence. In N5, Shasvastii Embryos have *specific timing and mission-scoring rules*; **do not assume dead/Unconscious models count for every objective**. Check the mission.
- **Skewed fighting:** Speculo, Noctifer, Caliban, Sheskiin, and occasionally Sphinx can attack from positions that bypass opponents' preferred gunfights. Preserve your expensive gun until that lane exists.
- **Fragile once revealed:** many important skirmishers are one-wound/low-ARM. Direct templates, Sensor, Discover, strong MSV and concentrated hacking can collapse the marker game quickly.
- **Economy constraint:** the best pieces are expensive, and Irregular Taighas or unrevealed Hidden Deployment cannot substitute blindly for active-turn Regular Orders. A list with too many specialists/ambushes and too little ordinary infrastructure can run out of Orders before scoring.
- **Vanilla Combined Army competition:** Vanilla takes many Shasvastii stars while offering other inexpensive orders, Smoke, heavy weapons and premium hackers. SEF should deliberately exploit **higher availability, specific Fireteams, marker density, or unique profiles**, not merely replicate a Vanilla roster.

> **Default plan:** protect an efficient command shell → secure 1 genuine active-turn gun → conceal 1–2 credible asymmetric attack pieces → buy enough cheap Regular Orders and scoring specialists → add ARO/recovery/REM support → reserve Orders for the final scoring turn.

### Recommended first-pass architecture

1. **Command:** Mentor LT for safety or Sheskiin LT for tempo with meaningful CoC/contingency support. Choose deliberately; there is no universal LT.
2. **One central fighting route:** Sheskiin **or** Gwailo team **or** Sphinx, plus one inexpensive alternative. Three expensive centerpieces starve scenario support.
3. **A hidden tax:** Speculo *or* Noctifer (sometimes both). Build for the mission, not for maximizing scary markers.
4. **A scoring shell:** Shrouded / Caliban specialists, Nox/Tensho / Victor where the particular mission needs them.
5. **Keep the gears running:** Ikadron, R-Drone, Q-Drone as appropriate and accessible Engineer/Doctor support (Caliban or Med-Tech); enough Regular Orders for a planned round-three push.

## 2026 tournament evidence: two different lists, both valid

**MetaChemistry E139 (Apr 2026), Brian “Nazeen,” winner of Battle by the Beach satellite:** one of the strongest **post-N5.2 played-event** perspectives. These are patterns he reported, not universal prescriptions.

- **“Face-down” / sneak list:** **2 Speculo + 3 Caliban + Shrouded + economical order bots**. Add a **solo Jayth with disco balls** kept for the late-game run. Why: make the opponent burn its first turns attacking uncertain markers, then reveal/stage specialists to win late.
- **“Face-up” / mission list:** **Sheskiin + Nox/Tensho specialists**, **Gwailo with Victor** and supporting utility. Why: some missions require repeated box retrieval, destruction, or long pushes into defended positions. A specialist-dense attack package is better than trying to score with one surviving Camo operative.
- **Defensive innovation:** with **R-Drone repeaters + Victor + cyber mines**, the deployment zone becomes inconvenient for opposing hackable attackers. This protects Sheskiin/Gwailo without overextending a fragile hacking network.
- **Against flyers and Sensor:** he deliberately returned to **Caliban MSV Spitfire** and Victor defense to answer the new aerial/vertical approach problem. A flyer/Detector can rapidly turn a field of anonymous markers into exposed one-wound troops; terrain height and roof access strongly affect that risk.
- **Actual game lesson:** Dukash entered from the flank, killed critical targets and forced Loss of Lieutenant in one match; in other missions **disco-ball coverage** let surviving Caliban and specialists reach turn-three objectives. This is the core tactical loop: **delay early, trade only when necessary, spend decisive Orders scoring last**.
- **Mission caveat:** Brian specifically singled out **Provisioning** as awkward because moving multiple boxes from secure areas takes real Orders and specialists; marker tricks alone do not solve its delivery requirements. Check the exact current mission text.

**Opposing view:** early N5 Tactical Awareness described SEF as weakened because much of its original identity is also available in Vanilla Combined. Nazeen's 2026 result shows the sectorial is **capable of winning**, but does not establish that SEF is always equal to Vanilla or that all missions favor the same skew.

## Opposing assessments (preserve both)

### Gwailo: expensive trap or tournament workhorse?

- **Skeptic (Robert Shepherd, 2024):** costly heavy link, middling damage and awkward escort movement; Albedo may solve MSV gunfights for the Gwailo but **not** necessarily for its companions, so the actual team still gets pinned. Prefer a Sheskiin/Victor/Nox Haris.
- **Advocate (Nazeen, cited by Robert and demonstrated again Apr 2026):** **MSV2 Heavy Rocket Launcher Gwailo can deliberately stand and deny the attack route**. Even if it eventually falls, an attacker that spends most of a turn clearing it is losing scenario tempo. Combined with Victor and linked specialists it scores as well as fights.
- **Choice:** take Gwailo when you need sustained lane pressure against specific opponents and have a mission specialist team supporting it; avoid if it is merely an expensive source of a gun.

### Sphinx: elite predator or 93-point AP problem?

- **Skeptics (Loss of Lieutenant E142, 2025; WIP12 Vanilla Combined E176):** extremely expensive, typically lacks AP, specializes in killing soft/medium targets rather than punching into TAG-grade armor; hacking exposure and climbing/movement rule changes make its opportunity cost severe. Competes with cheaper active guns + more Orders.
- **Advocates (Robert 2024 and opposing TAG-tier-list voices):** **Hidden Deployment + high speed + Climbing Plus + Mimetism (-6)** produce an attack angle that a normal TAG or Sheskiin cannot duplicate. It can prey on exposed order generators and reach defended rear lines without an open-field gunfight.
- **Decision:** select **for terrain and matchup**, not as an automatic SEF staple. Ask if it can reach soft targets and safely retreat/re-camouflage; on open tables or into repeater nets/AP-heavy opposition, choose a more distributed list. Some April 2025 climbing interactions were subsequently revised; use current N5.3 rules.

### Noctifer missile vs Spitfire

- **Missile:** punishes grouping, advancing linked teams and overconfidence. The *possibility* of its reveal taxes opponents, even when not taken.
- **Spitfire:** credible active-turn hidden gun that can exploit a side angle repeatedly; the 2026 tournament winner explicitly found it worthwhile. If clustered linked targets are scarce, Spitfire may generate more value.
- **Cost:** any Hidden Deployment piece contributes less to on-table activation until revealed. Budget actual regular orders.

### Shrouded vs Caliban vs Malignos

- **Shrouded** for repeatable button pressing, mines and economic marker pressure.
- **Caliban** for more aggressive protected specialist work and Protheion-based late-game resilience; can bring Engineer/CoC or MSV.
- **Malignos** when **Hidden Deployment** materially protects a high-value objective/ambush; don't pay for premium stealth if the mission only requires a cheap specialist.

### Jayth / Taigha / Fireteams

- **For Jayth:** EM/disco balls and compact multi-role teams solve situations a normal rifle cannot; useful to shepherd a late scoring run.
- **Against Jayth:** elite-price single-wound/low-ARM attacker can disappear to a template; a solo utility profile or other coverage may be safer.
- **For Taigha:** inexpensive disposable melee disruption and excellent orders *in their own lane*.
- **Against overloading Taighas:** the Irregular budget makes expensive guns and specialists underactivated; don't fill slots merely to reach a model cap.

## TAG, Vehicle and REM coverage (including Vanilla/Onyx research)

### Sphinx — Shasvastii's defining TAG option

**Primary task:** exploit a hidden, mobile predator route to destroy *accessible* soft targets, unlock movement for specialists, then return to cover/marker safety.

- **Strength:** unlike a visible standard gun TAG, the Sphinx taxes the opponent's deployment *before it appears*. Its 6-6 movement, Climbing Plus, Hidden Deployment and Mimetism give high vertical/flank reach on appropriate tables.
- **Liability:** high opportunity cost and no easy AP solution to heavy armor; dangerous enemy repeater nets/hackers and direct-template counters. Protect your path with clearance, short-range engagements and deliberate movement.
- **Support:** Caliban Engineer or Med-Tech with correct access can preserve an expensive TAG; defend its near routes against hackers and close-range attacks. No amount of support rescues a TAG whose mission can't justify its Orders.
- **Cross-sectorial lesson:** compare the Sphinx in **Vanilla Combined** against non-Shas support packages (e.g., other smoke or hacker options) and in **Onyx** against its own TAGs; **never assume the Onyx Fireteams or TAG catalog are available to SEF**.

**Vehicle distinction:** the sources reliably identify the **Sphinx as a TAG**, not a generic Vehicle. Do not import Vehicle keywords or profile availability from other Combined Army factions. No separate Shasvastii-specific Vehicle is confirmed in the sources used for this guide; check current Army before claiming one.

- **Vanilla Combined WIP12 E176 (Jan 2025):** cheap Q-Drone/Ikadron/Doctor Worm defensive backbone is genuinely useful; also contains a blunt **anti-Sphinx** assessment because Vanilla has stronger alternative premium threats. SEF must justify its fireteams/extra AVA/marker plan.

## Practical mission and opponent-specific playbook

- **Going second in zone-control or final-turn-objective missions:** start with Camo/Hidden barriers, force the opponent to spend Orders revealing/removing them, then emerge for late scoring. The 2026 event winner used this repeatedly.
- **Provisioning / multiple mobile boxes / destruction missions:** choose more actual specialists, robust transports/assault pieces and enough Orders to move objectives. Do **not** expect a marker-only force to retrieve everything from the enemy's safe zone.
- **Versus airborne/flyer attack:** Cyber Mines + Victor/R-Drone + prepared MSV and roofs/vertical coverage. The 2026 winner explicitly changed his toolbox after seeing this repeated threat.
- **Versus high-quality Sensor:** use actual distance, cover and layering rather than assuming marker state lasts forever. Do not cluster all key specialists within one Sensor activation.
- **Versus MSV and gun castles:** White Noise through Victor, Jayth visibility tools, Noctifer timed reveal, impersonated Speculo access, or Sphinx flanks. **No single visibility trick answers every sensor/MSV/360-vis ARO.**
- **Versus armor/TAGs:** Speculo and Caliban close/short-range plays, suitable AP/EM tools and mission denial, not a frontal Sphinx Spitfire duel. Set up **redundant anti-armor** because the first assassin may fail.
- **Always preserve a scoring branch:** even if the initial assassination fails and Sheskiin/Gwailo is taken out, still have a Shrouded or Caliban and safe Orders for the last turn.
