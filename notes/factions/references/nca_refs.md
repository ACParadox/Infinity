### vidIQ transcript findings — concrete NCA listbuilding takeaways

*Transcript source A:* [Robert Shepherd — N5 NCA Faction Focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axa19k7qE1E) (2026-01-18, English). The following are **his recommendations and reasoning**, not an official consensus list:

1. **Plan for PanO lockout, not just your first gunfight.** NCA often buys only **two or three genuinely suitable premium guns** after SWC and points commitments. Since its best pieces are specialized, losing an Uhlan/Swiss and a second long-range answer can leave a Redeye or close-range tool forced into a disastrous long-range engagement. Before deployment, designate **primary ARO solver, alternative solver, third-turn objective piece, and a realistic Engineer/Doctor recovery route**. Keep expensive shooters out of unnecessary reactive engagements; preserve one independent threat to open a lane after bad dice.
2. **Treat cheap troops as real capabilities, not filler.** Shepherd particularly values **Fugazi AVA 3**, the **9-point Tech-Bee**, and **one or two Auxilia**. An illustrative 60-point infrastructure shell is **3 × 7-point Fugazi + 9-point Tech-Bee + 2 × 15-point basic Auxilia = 6 Regular Orders**; the two disposable Auxbots also screen approaches, and the package supplies repeater/flash/support capabilities. This is a *possible* starting module, not a six-unit mandatory tax. The transcript explicitly contrasts NCA's unusually broad cheap support with sectorials forced to choose between elite units and order count.
3. **Auxilia are a defining NCA tool.** Their Auxbots offer 360° defensive coverage, Mimetism (-3), and Heavy Flamethrower corner denial while the *Regular Order* remains on the controller if the Peripheral dies. The **15-point SMG Forward Observer Auxilia + Auxbot_2** is an especially efficient mission/network pick: both profiles are Forward Observers, and both carry Deployable Repeaters (the Auxilia controller carries one; Auxbot_2 carries another). Because synchronized Peripherals activate with their controller, there are opportunities to move/plant repeaters or attempt suitable objectives with both bodies in one Order, subject to coherency, AROs and mission wording.
4. **Conditional two-check objective probability:** if a scenario allows the Auxilia FO and Auxbot_2 FO to *each* perform an independent unmodified WIP roll on the same objective, the nominal success probability is **1 − (8/20 × 9/20) = 82%**, from **WIP 12 and WIP 11**. This is *not* a universal claim that all missions permit two activations of a console, nor that the models can ignore simultaneous-peripheral restrictions. Check the scenario's requirements. More broadly, dual access is a substantive reason to buy an Auxilia specialist instead of a plain cheerleader.
5. **Cheap passive Lieutenant shell is his baseline.** Shepherd argues that many lists do not need an expensive active LT or Chain of Command insurance: one **Fusilier LT plus a separate basic Fusilier decoy**, or an **Auxilia LT** when already purchasing Auxilia, often protects Orders and frees points. He is distinctly skeptical of routine purchases of the **Aquila Disco Baller LT** and **Richard Quinn CoC**, because their cost and role can be weak relative to simpler support. This is an important **difference in evaluation** from the playable Aquila Strategos/Quinn build-around suggestions elsewhere in this note. The best option depends on whether the premium LT meaningfully uses its extra capabilities and whether CoC is separated from the risky LT attack group.
6. **Avoid the automatic five-Fusilier Core.** His opening profile discussion describes generic Fusilier combat loadouts as mostly crowded out by better specialists or guns. Fusilier LT/decoy, and occasionally an affordable Hacker/Paramedic, are the main budget roles. Spend Fireteam slots on a **small purposeful team** if it solves a matchup, not solely to field five bodies.
7. **Limited smoke/visibility control is a real design cost.** He identifies expensive Aquila and Richard Quinn Disco Baller options as relatively awkward ways to obtain a safety valve. The best countermeasures are *not* to claim the army has plentiful smoke: select good attack angles, use camouflage/Hidden Deployment and Surprise Attack, preserve recovery, and plan for target-suppression failure.

*Transcript source B:* [Gravitypool Orbital Operations — Neoterra Is Back!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B4G8-VVj8yo) (2025-10-03, Spanish-language transcript). **These were launch/rework observations; validate profiles against the September 2026 Army snapshot:**

- **Flexible small Fireteams are a genuine advantage:** the presenter specifically explores **Fusilier + 2 Bulleteers** and **Fusilier/Blade Engineer/Bulleteer** constructions; the engineer brings Tactical Awareness and repair potential but **10-10 MOV can constrain a 15-10 REM/TAG's movement**. Determine whether the extra team utility and order are worth slowing the attack vector.
- **Ultima Ratio double Squalos:** a Squalos NCO and lower-cost Squalos create an optional two-TAG attack module with multiple self-funded activations. This is **not the default**: it concentrates points and still needs mission specialists and repair capacity. Spending the Lieutenant Order through NCO should be rules-checked for the intended Fireteam activation rather than assumed to move the whole Duo.
- **Hidden Hexa Counterintelligence:** Hexa's **Counterintelligence + Hidden Deployment** imposes an information dilemma. If you use its Counterintelligence protection when an opponent tries to strip Orders, you may reveal the *existence* of a Hexa while not disclosing its position/loadout. Its KHD/E/Marat provides a separate hidden threat against a hacking-enabled advance.
- **Do not copy the presenter's old Blocker loadout verbatim:** the transcript discusses a **Pitcher on the Firewall Hacker**. That is **inconsistent with current N5.3**: the **15-point Specialist Operative** has the Pitcher; the **19-point Hacker** has Firewall (-3) and **no Pitcher**. The underlying **network projector + defended hacker** plan remains good, but now needs the correct profiles.
- **Aquila FTO assessment differs by plan:** Gravitypool sees the linked Aquila FTO Disco Baller as a credible advancing spearhead; Shepherd questions its cost efficiency as a standard LT/visibility-control pick. **Keep both positions visible** instead of calling it a mandatory or universally poor inclusion.

**Synthesized construction choice (analysis, not a quote):** choose between (A) **support-dense 13–15 troop combined arms**, using inexpensive Regular Orders, 2–3 distinct guns, specialists and optional small Fireteams; and (B) **Tactical Awareness TAG compression**, accepting fewer Regular Orders but investing in Squalos/Uhlan/Pathfinder self-funded activity. Both need explicit objective access and an emergency lane-opening plan. Avoid treating TAG compression as proof that ordinary NCA should always aim for low model count.

### What the video descriptions genuinely reinforce

- **NCA is about highly specific tools, not a uniform premium gunline.** Robert Shepherd's public synopsis singles out marksmanship, fast exploitation, mechanical midfield control, hacking and support as distinct jobs. The practical build test is whether each expensive pick adds a *new job/angle* instead of merely a second BS15 gun.
- **A sample list is more informative than isolated unit ratings.** Loss of Lieutenant explicitly includes example-list discussion; when reviewing it, compare the attacker's fuel (Tactical Awareness/NCO), objective runners, LT protection, anti-camo infrastructure and recovery before adopting a favorite profile.
- **Do not turn a tier-list format into alleged tier rankings.** Swiss Miss's video establishes the scoring framework but its public description does not expose who received S/A/etc. Current choice comparisons in this note are our analysis, not quotations from that video.
- **Launch-era recommendations must be rechecked profile-by-profile.** For example, the current 15-point **Blocker Specialist** has the Pitcher; the 19-point **Hacker** has Firewall (-3) but **no Pitcher**. Likewise use the pinned current Fireteam chart for Wildcard/FTO access.

### Independent battle-video accounts: a useful failure test

The [IGL video battle-report index](https://www.bureau-aegis.org/forum/index.php?topic=17992.20) records current-era NCA appearances and secondary written summaries. These write-ups are **not verified transcripts**, so treat individual turn details/results as reported by the recapper rather than independently observed fact.

- [NCA vs Military Orders — Area of Interest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HdMKL4uj5qw): a listed NCA force built around **Spitfire/HRL Uhlan**, **Redeye**, **Hexa Hacker**, **Blocker Hacker**, **Auxilia FO** and an Engineer lost a reported **1–5**. The account connects a costly Uhlan attack, loss of the primary attacker and later difficulty recovering zone control. Practical correction: plan who takes zones/objectives **if the Uhlan achieves nothing**, and do not spend every useful early Order on one difficult engagement.
- [NCA vs White Banner — Hardlock](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oIYgorD6Nh8): a complementary match report with specific deployment/turn timestamps and an NCA list; use it to study *when* attacking gives way to objective runs, rather than assuming the highest-quality gun determines the winner.

**Concrete pregame stress test for NCA:** (1) name the first ARO/problem and a fallback if the favored Uhlan/Swiss/Aquila cannot remove it; (2) identify two independent scoring paths (e.g., Pathfinder/Blocker/FO Auxilia versus a hidden/advanced specialist); (3) verify a backup network/hacker if the first one is shut down; (4) budget Orders for objective movement **before** launching a premium attacker; (5) put the repair/revive solution where it can actually reach the threatened unit. This preserves the existing premium-attack + cheap-support philosophy while avoiding an army that only functions when its Turn-1 gunfight succeeds.

#### Worst-value and rare/circumstantial picks — proxy-before-buy guide (2026-10-30)

**Question answered:** Which *entire miniatures* deserve no immediate purchase, which **specific profiles** should be avoided despite the unit having good uses, and which useful-but-rare units are best represented by an existing model? This expands — and does **not** overturn — the whole-unit purchasing audit above.

**Decision labels (not measured pick rates):**
- **Avoid profile** = sourced criticism of a particular loadout or application; **do not infer that the whole sculpt is bad**.
- **Proxy first** = *inference* from costly/redundant/narrow role, limited list demand or explicit published criticism. Not an assertion that a video presenter called every profile terrible.
- **One is enough** = a duplicate miniature is lower priority than its AVA suggests.
- **Specialist build** = deliberately purchase only for its unique tool or a list actually tested. **Strong / contested** identifies disagreement; never equate a rare pick with an unusable one.
