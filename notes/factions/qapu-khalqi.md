# Qapu Khalqi — N5 listbuilding and profile selection

_Original research review: 2026-10-17 (post-N5.2 tactical material). Merged against the repository's N5.3 Army snapshot on 2026-10-23. This is a strategy note, not an Army legality export._

**Rules / roster authority:** [Current Qapu Khalqi N5.3 roster, loadouts and Fireteams](../../sectorials/haqqislam/qapu-khalqi.md) (Army **7.26246.158**, pinned 2026-09-03, verified 2026-10-15), then [local N5.3 rules](../../rules/README.md). QK is marked *discontinued* in Army metadata but remains represented by a full selectable 38-unit roster and Fireteam chart. **Core 1, Haris 2, Duo unlimited.** The imported video/forum claims are research leads and dated opinions; transcripts have **not** been newly retrieved for this merge. Where the upload disagrees with the pinned source, the explicit notes below govern.

**Critical N5.3 reconciliation:** Contrary to the uploaded post-N5.2 chronology, **Rafiq Remote FTO (19/0.5, Red Fury) is currently a legal QK Wildcard (max 1)**; the 13-point non-FTO Rafiq has no Fireteam access. The pinned Odalisque entries have **15-5 MOV, Sixth Sense, NWI and Combat Instinct**, with individual Paramedic, +1B, Viral MMR/Marksmanship and Disco Baller choices. **Discover (+6), Natural Born Warrior, Stealth and “MediKit PH15” are not confirmed on the pinned Odalisque profile** and are not relied upon in the tactical recommendations. Sekban's Doctor option is **Doctor (+1)**, not Doctor (+3).

## Core identity

Qapu Khalqi (QK) is best approached as a **deception / asymmetric toolbox sectorial** that uses inexpensive regular infrastructure, Hafza identity games, compact mixed Fireteams, mobile specialists, mercenary attack vectors, and one or two substantial gunfighters.

The practical high-level structure is:

> **safe command + NCO outlet + flexible small teams + smoke / flank pressure + a real primary gun + mission and recovery coverage**

Do **not** assume the best QK list is a five-person Core marching forward. QK has many attractive ways to spend 25–45 points, and it is easy to turn a potentially flexible army into one costly, hackable Fireteam with too few independent threats.

A strong default list tends toward **15 troopers** when points and mission allow, with one main attack engine, an independent secondary attack route, and enough specialists and cheap Orders to act in later rounds. Fifteen is an architectural target, not a mandatory rule.

What defines QK compared with another Haqq sectorial:

- **Hafza / Holomask:** uncertainty over which model is the LT, CoC, Forward Observer or unexpected weapon.
- **Hafza LT (+1 Order) + Sekban NCO:** an efficient and distinctive command-order engine added by N5.2.
- **Odalisques:** unusually flexible, mobile link participants and close/mid-range utility after N5.2.
- **Bekçileri:** purpose-built Janissary / Kaplan / Odalisque teams, including economical Duos or Haris.
- **Yuan Yuan + Djanbazan:** cheap smoke and asymmetric arrival backed by MSV2.
- **Laxmee / Druze / Al Hawwa:** serious hacking, repeater and midfield-specialist options.
- **Iguana / Janissary / Azra'il:** alternative primary combat engines; none is obligatory.
- **Bashi and Korsan:** real pressure from unexpected access lanes instead of only frontal fighting.

### Evidence and edition warning

Most substantial N5 QK faction-focus videos were published in **August–September 2025**, *before* the 15 October 2025 balance pass. Their positioning, efficiency and matchup advice is still useful, but **do not copy their exact Fireteams without an N5.2 legality check**.

Confirmed N5.2 changes particularly relevant to building:

- **Hafza** gained a Lieutenant **(+1 Order)** option.
- **Odalisques** were reworked historically. The pinned N5.3 data supports MOV 15-5, CC19, Sixth Sense, NWI, Combat Instinct and +1B, Marksmanship, Paramedic and Disco Baller loadout choices; **the cited Discover+6/NBW/Stealth/MediKit-PH15 package is absent from that current data**.
- The separate **Kaplan Fireteam** was removed; current **Bekçileri** teams use **Janissary or Kaplan** as the required-choice foundation, with one eligible Odalisque/Sekban and Wildcards within their chart limits.
- Odalisques gained a **(Ghulam)** role in Ghulam Fireteams.
- **Rafiq FTO:** a post-N5.2 removal is reported by older sources, **but the authoritative current N5.3 chart explicitly retains the 19-point Red Fury Rafiq FTO as a Wildcard**. Treat removal claims as historical, not current legality.
- The initially published claim that **Djanbazans gained the (Qapu Khalqi) tag was corrected as an error**. They did *not* gain that tag.

This note distinguishes **reported post-N5.2 testing**, **older N5 discussion**, and **listbuilding synthesis** where possible. The linked videos are primary viewing leads; their full transcripts were not independently extracted for this note.

# Command architecture: Hafza and Sekban

## Hafza LT (+1 Order) + Sekban NCO

This is one of QK's clearest **post-N5.2 default starting packages**.

Roles:

- **Hafza LT (+1 Order):** safe WIP 14 Lieutenant hidden behind Holomask information, with two LT Orders available.
- **Sekban NCO:** spends LT resources on activations that should advance QK's plan instead of forcing the LT to leave safety.
- **The rest of the list:** does not need to pay for an aggressive LT merely to consume LT Orders.

Use the package when:

- Sekban has actual active-turn, specialist or movement work to do;
- the list wants a stable command model instead of a fighting Lieutenant;
- there is enough normal Order support to make the NCO's activations productive.

Do not buy an NCO just to say you have one. If the NCO is deployed too far from relevant tasks, or its Fireteam remains passive, the second LT Order is not effectively contributing.

**Failure mode:** recent QK playtesting reported losing the **only** Sekban NCO early as a major setback. If much of your list economics rely on two LT Orders being spent through NCO, consider a redundant NCO or a plan that still functions without it. Two NCOs are a list choice, not an automatic inclusion.

**Profile warning:** Sekban NCO, Doctor +3 and particular weapon combinations need to be checked at the *exact* current Army profile level; do not treat all of these benefits as automatically available on one model.

## Hafza Holomask and the shell game

Holomask in QK should create **wrong decisions and delayed certainty**, not simply a funny reveal.

Useful identities include:

- a hidden **Hafza LT** posing as a plausible non-LT;
- a **Hafza CoC** that cannot easily be identified or assassinated;
- a **Hafza Forward Observer** disguised as a model that does not appear to fulfil a classified / specialist objective;
- an **HRL Hafza** presented as a less threatening model;
- cheap Hafza Fireteam filler that protects points for the rest of the list.

A current post-N5.2 report specifically described a **Hafza FO disguised as a Janissary Doctor**, misleading an opponent about the list's available classified capability.

Useful discipline:

1. Decide what conclusion you want the opponent to draw from the apparent model.
2. Decide what they will do if they believe it.
3. Decide whether exposing the real Hafza at that point is still profitable.
4. Do not confuse disguising a vulnerable LT with protecting its physical position.

**Trap:** disguising an HRL Hafza as the apparent obvious LT can merely persuade the opponent to hunt and kill an expensive special-weapon piece they might otherwise have ignored.

## Lieutenant options

### Hafza LT (+1 Order) — preferred general starting point

Choose this when the list has a valuable Sekban NCO outlet and wants safe, cheap command. The LT itself should not be required to expose itself to make the roster work.

### Ordinary Hafza LT

Use when points are tight or the extra LT resource would go largely unused. It still offers the Holomask command shell.

### Ghulam LT

Simple low-cost option, especially if several Ghulams already make the true identity hard to identify. Useful for a cheap backfield shell, although normally less attractive than a good Hafza command package.

### Janissary LT

A much tougher combat LT option. Consider when you already intend to buy the Janissary as a serious attacker and the rest of the list accepts aggressive-LT risk.

**Hafza CoC** becomes significantly more attractive here. Keep the succession model **outside the same likely lethal attack footprint**; putting LT and CoC next to each other or in the same exposed Fireteam undermines the insurance.

### Sekban LT

A functional combat / utility LT, but it exposes command when used to solve midfield problems. Buy it for an intentional aggressive plan, not because the profile seems generically efficient.

### Other cheap or exotic LTs

Consider alternate available cheap LTs only for a specific roster construction, points break or command deception plan. Check current Army availability and actual profiles before selecting them. No strong evidence of a universal alternative LT consensus.

**Overall rule:** a passive LT + NCO and an aggressive LT + protected CoC are **different packages**. A passive Hafza LT normally does not require CoC merely for the sake of redundancy.

# Laxmee and the hacking network

Laxmee is a high-priority QK toolkit piece:

- Hacking Device Plus and **White Noise**;
- access to a broader repeater / Pitcher network;
- defensive support such as **Fairy Dust** when relevant;
- hacking-based control of Hackable targets;
- specialist capability.

The point is not simply “QK should have a Hacker.” It is that a good QK list can exploit **multiple forward hacking access points** while its actual gunfighter attacks from a different position.

## Laxmee + Odalisque

A particularly coherent **small-team / escort concept**, subject to the current Fireteam chart and selected profiles.

- **Laxmee:** hacking, White Noise, specialist work, repeater play.
- **Odalisque:** mobile non-HI escort, close-range protection, active shooting and objective work depending on variant.

This remains attractive even when the final legal formation is different from an older posted Duo recipe. The idea is that Laxmee is easier to project toward useful midfield positions when another model can clear the danger.

## Druze Pitcher + Laxmee / Al Hawwa

A classic combined-arms QK sequence:

1. Deploy or shoot a **Pitcher** from a favourable angle.
2. Use a Hacker to threaten the newly covered area.
3. Force a vulnerable target to spend Orders Resetting / repositioning or accept states.
4. Use a Janissary, Iguana, Odalisque or Azra'il to exploit the resulting opening.

**Druze is often best as a capability purchase, not a compulsory Druze Core.** Paying for one useful Hacker / KHD or Pitcher platform can be much more efficient than making five Druze the principal attack element.

## Iguana / Laxmee network-support concept (verify exact Repeater source)

An advancing Iguana can benefit from Laxmee's Pitcher and other actual Repeater coverage while contributing AP-HMG pressure. **Do not assume the Iguana itself automatically has a Repeater: the pinned QK loadout does not explicitly list one on the TAG.** Identify which equipped trooper/deployable is providing coverage before counting this as a network engine. The standard caveat remains: a Repeater helps only when Hackers can exploit its area and can also expose your network to enemy interference.

## Hacker redundancy

Al Hawwa can contribute forward specialist / hacking presence. Druze or other hackers expand the network. Do not overinvest in hacking if the mission, terrain and expected opponents mostly reward conventional gunfighting; do not stop at one expensive Hacker if the whole battle plan requires projected repeater coverage.

# Yuan Yuan and asymmetric Order economy

The QK habit worth testing first is **two Yuan Yuan**, not because two are mathematically compulsory but because they perform several distinct jobs:

- smoke access for Djanbazan / movement;
- cheap flanking or rear-area pressure;
- unexpected template / CC trade;
- forcing the opponent to respect off-axis threats;
- inexpensive, largely self-funded Irregular participation.

A useful deployment thought experiment:

> **one Yuan Yuan to provide smoke / table-side nuisance, another threatening an unexpected entry vector**

This is a meaningful alternative to deploying both for immediate suicide attacks.

Recent QK testing explicitly cautioned **against throwing Yuan Yuan away just to template the maximum number of models**. Killing one vulnerable 1W model and then remaining alive to consume future enemy Orders can provide more total value than attempting an ambitious trade against several tough targets.

### Impetuous and Irregular sequencing

- Check whether the Impetuous activation is safe or worth declining.
- Put the Yuan Yuan where its own Irregular Order can genuinely produce smoke, movement or an attack.
- Use Command Token conversions only where they unlock a valuable follow-up.
- Place self-funding warbands in a secondary group when they don't compete for normal Regular Orders needed by the main attack engine.
- Budget Orders and arrival rolls for airborne or lateral deployment realistically rather than assuming an ideal alpha strike.

### Yuan Yuan + Djanbazan

One of QK's most iconic packages:

> **Yuan Yuan supplies Smoke → Djanbazan with MSV2 attacks through it**

What makes it attractive is the opponent's uncomfortable reactive choice, not the mere presence of Smoke.

Plan the engagement geometry:

- can the Djanbazan reach a good shooting position without wasting too many Orders?
- does the Smoke meaningfully limit the opponent's visual response?
- does another, non-MSV enemy piece cover the same route?
- is there a better use for the Yuan Yuan's Order?

Don't take Djanbazan solely because “QK is supposed to smoke-shoot”; take it when it fixes a real matchup or table problem.

# Odalisques: N5.2's standout winners

Odalisques gained enough value in N5.2 to move from supporting role to **high-priority building block**.

Relevant **pinned N5.3** strengths:

- **15-5 MOV** (the metric equivalent of 6-2 inches), giving practical mobility;
- **Sixth Sense, NWI and Combat Instinct**, which distinguish their survivability;
- **CC19**, while the older-source claims of Discover+6 / Stealth / NBW are **not substantiated by the pinned profile**;
- **Paramedic / MediKit on its specific option**, not a confirmed PH15 universal skill;
- **BS Attack (+1B)** variants;
- **Viral Marksman Rifle + Marksmanship** variant;
- **Paramedic** specialist;
- **Disco Baller** tool.

## Viral MMR + Marksmanship

One of the clearest post-N5.2 recommendations. The positive field report singled out this profile as reliable and useful even as a **midfield reactive piece** in a Duo.

Prefer when:

- you want a ranged contributor but not another expensive HI;
- the team needs a valuable Turn 2 / Turn 3 fighter;
- Viral ammunition and Marksmanship attack a relevant target set;
- the army has enough close-range safety elsewhere.

## +1B short-range profiles

Use when:

- clearing and contesting a Camo / marker-heavy midfield **once targets are revealed** (do **not** assume a Discover+6 bonus);
- threatening opponents around corners;
- direct close-range fire is preferable to shooting from the deployment zone;
- you're purchasing a bodyguard for a Hacker / specialist.

Useful Burst options plus Sixth Sense, NWI and Combat Instinct make these a different unit from an ordinary cheap rifle carrier; do not budget an absent Discover+6 bonus.

## Paramedic

Often very efficient when:

- a Fireteam needs a specialist that can also fight;
- the mission rewards advancing to objectives;
- MediKit utility matters;
- you want a second scoring piece rather than another dedicated gun.

## Disco Baller

This is an **enabling utility purchase**. It becomes valuable when the visual-control / support effect actually enables an expensive team to move, fight or claim an objective. Do not pay for novelty without a table plan.

### Odalisque principle

> **In modern QK, ask whether an Odalisque can fill the team's missing role before buying a much more expensive third elite model.**

# Main attack engines — choose one first

## Iguana: TAG / hacking / Engineer architecture

One of QK's most coherent signature aggressive plans, especially after its 2025 improvement.

Good shell:

> **Iguana + Laxmee + appropriate Engineer (often Burkut) + cheaper orders / specialists**

Roles:

- Iguana opens lanes and pushes repeater reach;
- Laxmee supports cyber and visual-control play;
- Engineer makes preserving / recovering the investment feasible;
- cheap orders sustain its actual active-turn work;
- Yuan Yuan / Bashi give the opponent problems away from the TAG.

The Iguana's ejectable operator contributes a different endgame / attrition profile from a conventional TAG. Do not assume the operator will always save the plan, and do not overexpose the TAG just because it has a secondary phase.

**When to choose Iguana:** you want one mobile centerpiece that pressures through guns *and* hacking network geometry without buying a full premium HI Fireteam.

The September 2025 Burn City Brawl QK tournament report is the best linked practical viewing lead; its list discussion starts at **2:55**. It predates N5.2.

---

## Janissary: conventional tough gunfighter

Janissaries offer:

- **BS 14**;
- two Wounds, ARM 4 / BTS 6;
- **BS Attack (-3)**;
- strong HMG / ML and other options;
- Doctor / specialist or LT choices depending on profile.

N5.2 **Bekçileri** means a Janissary need not be part of an old-style costly pure HI Core.

A post-update report found even a **Janissary ML in a Duo** surprisingly useful in the active turn, not just as an ARO trap.

**When to choose Janissary:** you want robust, high-quality conventional ranged combat in a two- or three-person team while retaining points for asymmetric pieces elsewhere.

---

## Azra'il: major heavy-weapon threat

Azra'il is the threat you select when its heavy weapon can make a key hard target very expensive to keep exposed.

Its challenges:

- size, terrain and mobility / exposure;
- hacking vulnerability;
- dependency on access to useful rangebands;
- opportunity cost of buying too many escorts.

Useful **support package**, subject to current Fireteam legality:

> **Azra'il as gunfighter + Sekban NCO / specialist + Engineer or cheap supporting body as needed**

That describes complementary roles, **not** a claim that every listed model can join one legal Fireteam.

**When to choose Azra'il:** you need a specific punishing weapon and can realistically arrange the shooting lanes. Avoid buying a huge hackable brick whose protection costs more than a second threat.

# Fireteam architectures

**Validate the exact composition in live Army.** The N5.2 Bekçileri restructure and corrections invalidate a number of older printed and video lists.

The guiding rule is:

> **Start with what the team must do; build the smallest legal team that improves its job.**

## Bekçileri Janissary + Odalisque / Kaplan Duo

One of the cleanest post-N5.2 constructions:

- a Janissary provides the main gun or resilient front model;
- Odalisque provides mobility, specialist or efficient secondary fight;
- Kaplan can provide alternate support / Engineer utility where legal.

Use to give a premium gunfighter Fireteam movement and support without committing five models.

## Bekçileri Haris / compact Core

Buy a third model when it adds a *different* job:

- specialist / Doctor / Engineer;
- close-range protection for the main ranged gun;
- alternate rangeband;
- meaningful link benefit.

Avoid paying 100+ points for three similar guns that all want the same Orders.

## Laxmee + Odalisque small-team / escort package

A good hacking + close/mid-range escort architecture. In any particular list verify whether the chosen Laxmee and Odalisque entries share a valid Fireteam; otherwise keep the same complementary roles without declaring a link.

## Sekban-focused Duo / Haris

Good all-rounder team when the list benefits from an **NCO activator** that can also fight or contribute specialist and recovery work.

Profile selection should answer:

- is the NCO advancing or mostly protecting the rear?
- is a Doctor more valuable than another gun?
- does a heavy rocket launcher cover a useful reactive lane?
- is the team actually worth spending the two LT Orders through?

Don't conflate mutually exclusive Sekban profiles.

## Ghulam shell / Core

Still useful as **cheap Regular Orders, Lieutenant ambiguity, specialist support and defensive friction**. Odalisque integration after N5.2 makes some Ghulam-based packages more mobile / functional.

A cheap Core is sound when you are spending heavily on Iguana / Azra'il / Janissary plus Laxmee and infiltration; it is less sound if its members become so passive that no one can score.

## Djanbazan ranged / MSV package

The reason to include Djanbazan is usually **MSV2 combined with the list's Smoke and visual-control plan**.

- **AP Sniper / long gun:** support a lane or counter visual modifiers.
- **Hacker:** flexible specialist / control if the list values MSV2 and hacking utility in one profile.
- Keep the team size economical; beware copying older Djanbazan Fireteam tags and links.

Do **not** assume the corrected-away (Qapu Khalqi) tag exists.

## Druze package

A small Druze presence for Pitchers, KHD / Hacker and E/M is often more compelling than making Druze the main combat block. A Druze team can be fine when the mission and ranges reward it, but do not treat it as obligatory.

## Large five-person combat Core

Take only when it has a measurable return from the specific team bonuses, terrain and mission.

Ask:

- how many Orders can this team spend before it reaches an objective?
- how many expensive / Hackable models can a single template, repeater or flank attack threaten?
- what independent unit survives if the team loses its point model?
- is the SWC and points cost denying you Yuan Yuan, Al Hawwa, an Engineer or a secondary attacker?

Modern QK looks unusually able to gain most of the practical value from smaller teams.

# Djanbazan: the MSV2 problem-solver

The unit is not mandatory on raw profile efficiency alone; it is strategically special because it provides an answer to **Smoke and strong visual MODs**.

Choose a Djanbazan if the alternative list would struggle to approach an MSV-relevant gunfight.

- Long-range MSV package: watch a lane or exploit the Yuan Yuan smoke angle.
- Hacker / utility profile: broader specialist toolset, valuable if the list cannot fit a dedicated extra Hacker.
- Regeneration is a useful possibility, not a guarantee that a failed ARO survives.

**Common mistake:** buying it and smoke because the combo is famous, then discovering the table has no lane on which it earns its cost.

# Secondary and lesser-used useful pieces

## Al Hawwa

One of QK's most important **midfield deployment answers**.

Use Hacker / Forward Observer / mine-oriented variants to:

- start closer to objectives;
- lay a threat across an approach;
- extend hacking;
- score without spending several Orders merely walking forward.

Recent player evidence included a Forward Observer *posing strategically as a likely Hacker* to influence opposing movement. The model does not need to kill anything for its position to alter the game.

## Bashi Bazouk

A genuinely interesting, easily underrated **scalpel** rather than a default suicide unit.

Use when:

- the enemy supports / specialists are protected from the front but exposed laterally;
- you want flexible ammunition and controllable damage;
- an independent attacker creates pressure without moving your main Fireteam.

A recent play report specifically valued its ability to **injure rather than necessarily overkill**, including leaving a target Unconscious when that is tactically useful.

## Korsan

Provides an unusual off-axis / deployment-zone pressure vector. It becomes much more threatening when the opponent also needs to respect a Yuan Yuan and your conventional on-table attack.

Do not take merely to attempt a spectacular first-turn arrival. Sometimes holding an arrival threat or using it for a late objective angle is more productive.

## Burkut

A high-value **combat Engineer**, not simply the cheapest repair tax.

Advantages highlighted in current testing:

- can support a forward TAG / REM or HI-heavy plan;
- has useful weapons and D-Charges / mission utility;
- can work toward classifieds or objectives instead of waiting in the DZ;
- can make an Iguana-centric list more resilient.

A field report explicitly praised Burkut but noted a weapon-choice issue: the LRL profile could be vulnerable at close range without a template, prompting consideration of an HFT loadout.

Prefer Burkut when the Engineer itself must advance and fight. Prefer a cheaper dedicated Engineer / Servant arrangement if it accomplishes the repair task without consuming vital attack points.

## Kaplan

The post-N5.2 Bekçileri integration matters more than imagining a self-contained old Kaplan Fireteam.

Kaplan's Mimetism and role diversity remain useful, but some players now prefer Burkut for combat-Engineer/classified duty.

Consider Kaplan when:

- a specific Bekçileri role fits its equipment;
- Mimetism / shooting matters more than Burkut's different battlefield profile;
- the team needs repair capability without a completely separate attack piece.

Avoid choosing it merely from old KTS nostalgia.

## Hafza Forward Observer / HRL

These are exceptional *information value* profiles.

- FO lets an apparently non-specialist accomplish an objective.
- HRL creates a surprise ranged / template threat.
- Their value comes from the opponent's false deployment or threat evaluation.

## Zeybek and other post-update specialty profiles

The N5.2 update improved Zeybek and its operator. Interesting to test as a different gun / mission package, but the retrieved QK evidence is too thin to rank it with Hafza, Odalisque or Laxmee.

## Djanbazan Hacker

An under-discussed hybrid: MSV2 plus hacking / specialist utility can compress two match-relevant roles into one trooper. Don't buy it if both roles are already covered more efficiently.

# REMs, cheap Orders and recovery

QK can easily run out of **roster space** before it runs out of possible good units.

## Fanous

Cheap Regular support with Flash Pulse / Repeater utility; strong candidate to fill an otherwise weak Order slot. Not mandatory if the slot would be better spent on mission reach.

## Rafiq

Fast utility / Sensor / specialist and repeater contributions. Buy for its actual on-table role; **do not rely on pre-N5.2 Rafiq FTO Fireteam templates**.

## Shihab

TR-style reactive lane holder. Valuable when the main list lacks a credible stop sign, but easy to overpay for additional static ARO in an army already buying Djanbazan, Janissary ML or disposable interference.

## Kameel and other utility REMs

Can improve support / baggage and Order economy. Their value depends on mission, available points and how the rest of the list handles defense and repeaters.

## Engineer / Doctor selection

A repairable centerpiece (Iguana, other TAG, valuable REM) sharply improves the value of a nearby Engineer and Servant plan. An infantry-dominant list may care more about a **Sekban Doctor / other Doctor** and objective capability.

Do not pay for multiple redundant support models without enough valuable targets to recover. Conversely, do not buy an expensive TAG and leave its repair plan impossible because the Engineer is stranded across the table.

# Matchup and deployment planning

## Versus strong visual modifiers / Smoke

- Test whether **Djanbazan MSV2** earns its cost.
- Test whether Laxmee's **White Noise** is relevant against enemy MSV pieces.
- Avoid building a plan that assumes Smoke alone neutralizes *all* lines of attack.

## Versus heavy hacking

- Do not concentrate Iguana / Azra'il / Janissary and their Engineer in one hackable choke point.
- Use Stealth, White Noise, short non-Hackable escorts and alternate routes as allowed by individual profiles.
- Consider disabling opposing repeaters / Hackers before taking an expensive HI team forward.

## Versus strong midfield Camo

- Odalisque's **Sixth Sense, NWI and close-range profiles** matter; **do not presume Discover+6** in the pinned N5.3 profile.
- Al Hawwa and mines create friction.
- Sensor / Rafiq can be worth more than a redundant extra HMG.

## Versus dense DZ defense or weak rear protection

- Yuan Yuan, Bashi and Korsan present multiple different angles.
- One off-axis attacker arriving at the right time can be better than exhausting the main Fireteam attacking a protected front.
- Do not spend Orders through every model: choose the attack vector with the lowest expected Order cost.

## Going second

- Protect Hafza LT and CoC from the same attack footprint.
- Present **layered cheap AROs**, not one exposed premium sniper to be picked off.
- Position midfield markers / specialists to survive the initial attack and take over once a lane is opened.
- ARO pieces succeed when they cost enemy Orders; killing is a bonus.

# Traps and misleading advice

1. **Old Fireteam guides copied into N5.2:** Kaplan / Bekçileri changed; Rafiq FTO removal and the Djanbazan tag correction matter.
2. **Overbuying a full combat Core:** multiple costly bodies competing to deliver one gun.
3. **LT + CoC in the same lethal footprint:** a single raid / template can destroy both command layers.
4. **Hafza Holomask as decoration:** a disguise should provoke a wrong decision, not merely surprise the opponent when it is revealed.
5. **Single fragile NCO without fallback:** if all LT Orders rely on it, losing the Sekban early can seriously hurt.
6. **Two disposable Yuan Yuan suicides by default:** cheap smoke and persistent nuisance may create more value.
7. **Djanbazan because the smoke combo is iconic:** only worth it when the lanes and matchup reward MSV2.
8. **Hacking network without forward projection:** Laxmee alone in the DZ with no relevant repeater geometry may be underused.
9. **Druze Core by habit:** often a Druze Pitcher / Hacker is the important capability, not five Druze.
10. **Engineer divorced from attack route:** repair capability that cannot reach the TAG is functionally absent.
11. **Three expensive active attackers and no Order support:** one will typically spend most of the turn watching.
12. **All defense and no Turn 2 / 3 scoring:** the mission decides games, not merely preserving ARO pieces.
13. **Assuming each Sekban profile combines NCO, Doctor and chosen weapons:** check individual loadouts.
14. **Treating source rankings as statistics:** community recommendations and isolated battle reports are evidence of useful archetypes, not proof of universal optimum.

# Practical starting list architectures

These are **role templates, not exact legal Army exports**.

## A. Generic 15-trooper QK toolbox

Start with:

- Hafza LT (+1 Order).
- Sekban NCO with an actual job.
- Laxmee.
- One useful Odalisque (Viral MMR, Paramedic or appropriate close-range profile).
- One or two Yuan Yuan.

- One asymmetric secondary attack, often Bashi or Korsan.
- Remaining slots for rangeband, ARO, specialists and a fallback scoring line.

# Practical QK playbook: how the packages operate together

These are **sequencing templates**, not rigid turn scripts. They combine the role conclusions above into practical decisions for deployment and game turns. None of the examples implies an unverified Fireteam membership.

## Deployment: identify the attack path before placing the pieces

Begin with four questions:

1. **Which lane needs to be opened?** Identify the opponent's likely reserve gun, the relevant ARO, the hidden repeater / midfield hazard, and which of your weapons has the best credible matchup.
2. **What must survive Turn 1?** Hafza LT, succession backup (if any), second attacker, late-game specialist, and Engineer / Doctor need different protection depending on whether you go first.
3. **Which threat should the opponent see?** A visible Janissary / Azra'il / Iguana can attract deployment toward a strong lane, while Al Hawwa, Hafza disguise and arrival threats punish overcommitting to that lane.
4. **Where will the support piece actually finish its activation?** Place the Engineer, Sekban NCO and Laxmee relative to likely routes, rather than only near their nominal partners in the DZ.

**Going first:** reserve a primary attack piece or deployment-sensitive flanker if its placement can target the revealed defense. Keep enough normal Orders in its group to achieve the *first actual objective*, not merely reach the fight. Consider leaving an exposed Yuan Yuan arrival until after your conventional gun opens a lane.

**Going second:** hide the premium attackers more aggressively. Cheap AROs, templates, mines and uncertainty should cause enemy Orders to be spent before they can threaten the command and scoring models. Do not expose the Hafza LT just because Holomask obscures its identity.

## Order groups: buy activations, not just trooper count

A 10-Regular-Order primary group plus a secondary group with disposable or self-funding pieces can be a productive starting point, but don't force a particular split without checking the mission.

- Place the **primary attacker** in the group that can deliver the full opening attack, reposition and still let a specialist capitalize.
- Keep the **Sekban NCO** in a useful activation position. An LT (+1 Order) is only an order-economy benefit when someone has productive work to spend those Orders on; check the current NCO/Fireteam Order rules when allocating groups.
- **Yuan Yuan** can be efficient secondary-group models when their Irregular / Impetuous resources are useful on their own. A defensive smoke provider that needs extra movement or throws may require Regular support instead.
- **Bashi / Korsan** arrival attacks can be starved by too few follow-up Orders. Budget the cost of appearing, clearing the first corner and reaching the target; otherwise they become expensive threats that never finish their job.
- Do not overfill the main group with several active attackers competing for the same eight or nine usable Orders after casualties.

**Practical test:** write down your probable Turn 1 first six activations. If four different premium units are all expecting to attack, the list may need either consolidation or better staged roles.

## Three sample attack sequences

### A. Conventional gun opens; asymmetry finishes

1. Use **Iguana / Janissary / Azra'il** to remove or bypass the defender preventing access to an important area.
2. Check whether an enemy reactive model or mine is still covering the route. Do not advance the scoring specialist into the first available ARO.
3. Threaten a newly vulnerable flank with **Bashi / Korsan / Yuan Yuan**, preferably against support, order generators or a mission-critical specialist.
4. Move **Al Hawwa / Odalisque / other specialist** only after the necessary space is created.
5. End with primary attacker out of free retaliation lanes and support close enough to make repairs or recovery plausible.

**When this fails:** a strong initial gunfight consumes too many Orders. Switch to the alternate attack angle instead of reflexively repeating low-probability FtF rolls.

### B. Hacking network forces a bad choice

1. Identify a Hackable enemy piece that must cross a specific corridor or controls the objective.
2. Project coverage with **Druze Pitcher**, **Iguana repeater**, or the forward position of **Al Hawwa** when the actual profiles allow.
3. Use **Laxmee / another Hacker** to threaten a control state or compel Reset / route changes.
4. Capitalize with the primary gunfighter or take the newly uncovered objective.
5. Remove unnecessary exposure of the Hacker; avoid leaving the network's one indispensable piece accessible to a Killer Hacker or direct attack.

**When this is preferable to shooting:** the enemy would otherwise receive a favorable direct gunfight, but cannot ignore a hacking corridor without giving up ground.

### C. Smoke plus Djanbazan

1. Identify a **genuine long-range MSV2 payoff** before spending an Order on smoke.
2. Put the Yuan Yuan where its smoke can be deployed without immediately trading away the smoke source.
3. Throw smoke to block the particular target's sightline while leaving the Djanbazan's intended angle.
4. Shoot with the Djanbazan while the matchup remains favorable, then choose whether to leave the shooter in ARO or withdraw.
5. Preserve the Yuan Yuan when its next-turn smoke or nuisance value is greater than a marginal extra attack.

**When not to do it:** the board has no profitable firing lane, the important target is protected by another response, or an independent close-range attacker can solve the position for fewer Orders.

## Midgame and Turn 3 handoff

**Turn 1 — change the board:** trade Orders for access, remove the opponent's primary threat where feasible, and establish an awkward repeater / template / arrival problem. Keeping your main attacker alive for another turn is often better than taking one final uncertain shot.

**Turn 2 — exploit the damaged board:** allow a previously protected **Odalisque, Al Hawwa, Laxmee or Sekban** to shift from support into objective work. If the first gunfighter is gone, the surviving small-team structure should still be able to attack; that is the real reward of QK's distributed threat model.

**Turn 3 — score and deny:** let expendable warbands or flank attackers die if that is the cheapest way to remove an ARO or prevent enemy scoring. Don't protect a remaining Order generator purely for a future turn that no longer exists. Preserve a specialist only if it has a scoring task left.

## Quick selection: what should the next 20–35 points do?

| Existing weakness | Preferred thing to examine | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No independent midfield scorer | **Al Hawwa** | Starts closer to objectives and contributes mines or hacking |
| Command Orders wasted | **Sekban NCO** | Adds real productivity to Hafza LT (+1) |
| Ranged piece needs mobile escort / late-game mission utility | **Odalisque** | Flexible combat/specialist role after N5.2 |
| List cannot answer Smoke / visual MODs | **Djanbazan** | MSV2 is a matchup tool, not a mandatory tax |
| Strong gun but no side attack | **Bashi / Yuan Yuan / Korsan** | Attacks the protection of the target instead of its best facing |
| Primary attacker vulnerable to Hackers | **Laxmee / Druze / better repeater and movement plan** | Fix the attack geometry, not only raw gun quality |
| Expensive repairable piece with no recovery path | **Burkut / other Engineer and appropriate Servant** | One repair may preserve the whole center of the list |
| Too many premium pieces, too few Orders | **Ghulam / Fanous / appropriate cheap support** | Give the pieces you already bought enough activations |
| Too much passive defense | **Mobile specialist or second active attack vector** | Converts reactive advantage into mission progress |

The right choice is the **first missing job**, not the most impressive remaining datasheet.

# Compact listbuilding audit

Before locking the list, answer:

1. **Primary attack:** which model opens a protected lane against an enemy real gunfighter?
2. **Second attack vector:** how do I attack somewhere that primary weapon cannot?
3. **Command:** where is the LT, and what spends the two LT Orders if using Hafza (+1)?
4. **Succession:** if my LT fights, can the Hafza CoC survive the same attack?
5. **Mission:** who is already near the midfield and who scores on Turn 3?
6. **Visuals:** what handles Smoke / Mimetism? Do I need Djanbazan or White Noise?
7. **Hacking:** are there useful repeaters / Pitchers, or am I paying for disconnected Hacker profiles?
8. **Recovery:** can my Engineer / Doctor actually reach the valuable pieces?
9. **ARO:** what costs the enemy Orders without giving them a free primary-target kill?
10. **Order economy:** are my Irregulars self-funding, and are the important attackers in a group with enough Regular Orders?
11. **Fireteam legality:** did I recheck post-N5.2 Army combinations?
12. **Resilience:** if the first attacker or NCO dies, what is the fallback plan?

## Overall takeaway

> **Qapu Khalqi is strongest when the opponent has to solve several small, different problems—not when the entire army is one visible Janissary / Azra'il / Iguana problem.**

Its strong form is **Hafza command deception; LT-to-NCO economy; Odalisque and Bekçileri efficiency; Laxmee / Druze control; Yuan Yuan / Bashi / Korsan alternate angles; and a deliberately chosen heavy hitter.**
