# Trinitarian Tertiaries — Military Orders (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: S-tier independent midfield scoring/ambush unit; one is close to a default inclusion, two are often excellent, and three are a deliberate mission/midfield skew.** Prefer the **24/0 Boarding Shotgun Killer Hacker** as the general-purpose first pick when another Hacker-specialist is useful; the **22/0 Boarding Shotgun Forward Observer** when FO-specific objectives, Targeted support, or saving 2pts matter. With **De Fersen** and an intentional offensive hacking plan, the **23/1 FO + Deployable Repeater Minelayer** rises to **S / near-must**. The **Shock Mine Minelayer (23/1)**, **MULTI Sniper (25/1.5)** and **SMG FO (23/0)** are increasingly situational, not equal default alternatives.

## Why a Trinitarian is valuable in MO

MO has multiple costly, capable Knight gunfighters but fewer cheap independent **Infiltration + Hidden Deployment + Camouflage** specialists. Trinitarians let the list score at a distant console, cover a second route if a main Fireteam stalls, punish isolated units at close range, or introduce an invisible threat without demanding a 35–50pt combat model. **One Trinitarian is nearly an automatic first look, not a compulsory inclusion**: a mission or roster can instead use Montesa, Peacemaker, Dart or an airborne specialist.

The tradeoff is fragility: **BS12, ARM1, BTS0, one Wound**. These are positioning/scoring/ambush assets, **not** durable gunfighters or good substitutes for a long-range opener. Do not reveal one merely to take a mediocre Face-to-Face Roll; often its greatest contribution is surviving until the mission's decisive turn.

### Deployment: Hidden or Camouflaged?

- **Hidden Deployment:** genuinely absent from table and marker information. Best for an unexpected sniper ARO, hidden KHD ambush or last-turn console scoring. **The Hidden trooper does not contribute its Regular Order to the Order Pool while Hidden.** The tactic has a real early-turn Order cost, especially if hiding two or three Trinitarians.
- **Camouflaged State:** a visible Camo marker, but still protected by marker restrictions and Mimetism when applicable. **Contributes its ordinary Regular Order** and can move/screen an objective. Opponent knows a marker is there, but may not know its identity. If it moves, its position is no longer a secret even though marker protection persists.
- **After reveal:** Camouflage remains useful where legal because the model may re-enter the Camouflaged State under the usual conditions; Hidden Deployment is not a reusable in-game 'go Hidden again' button.
- **Minelayer while Hidden:** the official rule explicitly permits a Hidden, infiltrating Minelayer to place its eligible deployable equipment during deployment, within the requisite placement area/ZoC. **The placed mine or Deployable Repeater remains on the table**, however, and can reveal broad clues about the hidden trooper's whereabouts. If attempting an opponent-half Infiltration roll, follow the failure/removal restrictions; do not assume the infiltrated hidden position is automatic.
- **Surprise Attack (−3)** is an *active-turn* benefit when revealing from the appropriate state, including eligible shooting/Comms Attacks; **not a reactive-turn −3** on a sniper's surprise ARO.

**Deployment recommendation:** In a thin-order MO list, deploy at least one Trinitarian as a Camouflage marker unless its hidden reveal has a concrete purpose. A truly Hidden second piece can be excellent late-turn insurance.

## Profiles: why and when

### 1. KHD / Boarding Shotgun — 24/0 (S, default general-purpose first pick)

Only **+2pts** versus the 22pt FO while retaining the same **BSG, D-Charges and Shock Mine**. Gains a **Killer Hacking Device**, which is both a Hacker-specific mission tool and an offensive anti-Hacker threat. The two ordinary KHD programs are **Trinity** (against enemy Hackers) and **Cybermask** (enter IMP-2). It remains an eligible Specialist where missions accept Hackers or any Specialist.

**When to prefer:** enemy Hackers likely to contest midfield; a hacking-heavy MO roster needs an independently positioned cyber killer; Hacker-designated mission requirements; a hidden turn-2/3 counterattack and scoring pivot. The BSG + Shock Mine ensure the model is not useless when the enemy has no Hackers.

**Limits:** the basic KHD **does not** get standard Hacking Device **Oblivion, Carbonite, Spotlight or Total Control**. It is not an anti-TAG controller just because it is a Hacker. **WIP13/BTS0/W1** means it should pick carefully advantageous exchanges and avoid a fair duel through a hostile network. Becoming a Hacker also creates new hacking exposure that the non-Hacker FO does not have.

**Cybermask versus Camouflage:** Cybermask is a **Long Skill** used to enter **IMP-2** without a WIP roll; not a free activation. It is an alternate marker/repositioning tool particularly after exposure, **not a second Mimetism layer**. Its **NFB** prevents stacking Mimetism (−6) while in IMP-2. A Trinitarian already has reusable Camouflage, so Cybermask is *useful insurance* rather than the primary reason to pay +2pts. An IMP-2 marker also cannot be treated as an indefinitely invisible off-table piece; it is still a marker with movement/discovery limitations.

**Why it outranks FO generally:** the functional Hacker mission and Trinity option are often worth +2pts, with little loss of weaponry. However, in **FO-specific missions**, for **Targeted support without Comms exposure**, or when saving exactly 2pts buys an extra Order elsewhere, the **22pt FO can be more efficient**. Do not universally substitute KHD for FO without reading the mission.

### 2. FO + Deployable Repeater Minelayer — 23/1 (S with De Fersen)

This version is the exceptional **turn-zero network** option: **Infiltration + optional Hidden Deployment + Minelayer + Deployable Repeater + FO**, for only **23pts**. Put a Repeater into a valuable midfield location in deployment, without spending a Turn-1 Order. The parent Trinitarian can remain Hidden; later it can reveal to score or fight with a BSG. The price is **1 SWC** and **losing the ordinary FO/KHD's Shock Mine**.

**With De Fersen:** generally **near-mandatory when De Fersen is chosen as a serious offensive network Hacker**, rather than merely as a good gunfighter in a Knight Fireteam. It can expose enemy Hackers to his upgraded **Trinity (+1SD)** and hackable HI/TAG/REMs to **Carbonite, Oblivion and Spotlight**, without moving the valuable 42–46pt Knight into immediate danger. A linked **Order Sergeant FTO Pitcher**, native **Peacemaker Repeater** or **Pathfinder** can supplement or, in some lists, replace this delivery; if you already cover the key angles, do **not** pay 1SWC just to claim a mandatory synergy.

**Placement test:** Which concrete enemy route/console is in the projected Repeater's Zone of Control? Will your live Hackers have a good activation and follow-up against targets there? Can the opponent remove or ignore the node? If the answer is vague, the 22/0 FO or 24/0 KHD is safer value.

**Cyber risk:** a friendly Repeater is not an unrestricted gift of your network to opposing Hackers, but hostile Hackers can exploit an **enemy Repeater against its owner's Hackers** under the Repeater and Firewall rules, with relevant Firewall MODs. This is a managed exposure, not a reason to forgo projected hacking in every matchup. Do not confuse the preplaced Deployable Repeater with a Shock Mine or a full-time native Repeater on the Trinitarian.

**Guided Clipper caveat:** useful only if the army can apply **Targeted**, can exploit Guided fire, and has SWC/Orders to spare. DR Minelayer **1 SWC** + Guided Clipper **1.5 SWC** + other network/guns can create serious opportunity cost. De Fersen + DR does **not** require buying a Clipper.

### 3. FO Boarding Shotgun — 22/0 (S−; best budget FO)

The **lowest-cost** Trinitarian preserves the great defensive package — **Hidden Deployment, Camouflage, Mimetism (−6), Infiltration, Shock Mine, D-Charges, Flash Pulse** — plus a **BSG** and **Forward Observer**. It is an excellent independent console runner, close-range AP threat and mission-specific FO. FO can apply **Targeted** without relying on a Hacking Device/network, although it still needs an eligible target and positioning.

**Why not rank above KHD by default?** The KHD has essentially the same BSG/mine/ambush profile, paying 2pts to add Hacker mission eligibility and Trinity/Cybermask. Thus FO should lead only if the mission explicitly favors FO, a non-Hacker specialist matters for security, the hacking tools are redundant, or the points really matter.

**When to take two ordinary FOs:** multiple distant consoles or FO-defined objectives, no real enemy-Hacker problem, and 0SWC Specialist saturation.

### 4. FO Shock Mine Minelayer — 23/1 (B+; optional)

For **+1pt and +1SWC** versus the 22/0 FO, it **pre-places one Shock Mine at deployment** instead of spending an Order later. The model carries the same basic FO weapon package. This does not mean a second *free ammunition supply*; Minelayer consumes the selected mine's disposable allocation as usual.

Its value is **turn-zero lane/console denial**, especially when going second and the enemy needs to cross a predictable chokepoint. Hidden Deployment can make the counterattack hard to predict even if the mine itself is visible. However, the enemy can read the mine's marker characteristics, and **1 SWC for that deployment benefit is significant** in a 300pt MO roster.

**Take it when:** 1 SWC is unspent, the scenario creates predictable contested approaches, and you have **no De Fersen-centered Repeater use** (or you specifically want to avoid hostile hacking exposure through a forward node). Otherwise prefer **FO22/0**, or the **DR Minelayer** in a dedicated hacking architecture.

### 5. MULTI Sniper Rifle — 25/1.5 (B+; conditional hidden ARO)

This is a **non-Specialist**, so it does not replace the FO/KHD's central mission job. Its attraction is **an entirely Hidden Deployment sniper with Mimetism (−6)**: the opponent can commit a valuable model to a sightline without knowing the Trinitarian has that lane covered, then face a surprise *position*. **Surprise Attack (−3) does not apply in ARO**; its reactive lethality is the rifle, range, visual MODs and correct engagement geometry.

**The awkward range bands are real.** A MULTI Sniper Rifle has **−3 at 0–8in, 0 at 8–16in, +3 at 16–48in, −3 at 48–96in**. Infiltration is **optional forward positioning**, not a requirement to crouch next to an objective. It is perfectly valid to hide the sniper **back in your own half**, off the expected DZ roofline, covering a long lateral or diagonal corridor; a **diagonal deployment map**, elevated flank position, or unusual cross-table lane increases this value. In a dense midfield with only sub-16in exchanges it is a poor use of 1.5 SWC.

**Use actively** only to exploit a favorable off-angle and **Surprise Attack (−3)**, ideally against a target without MSV; after revealing, the W1/ARM1 model is quite vulnerable. Its MULTI ammo modes add flexibility against target defenses.

**Competitive context:** the MO archive cites Sneaky Snake's July 2025 IGL example with **two hidden Trinitarian MSRs**, and a counterplay incident involving **Holoprojector + Triangulated Fire**. Interesting evidence that the tactic is playable, not a current N5.3 automatic double-sniper prescription. **Two MSRs cost 50pts/3 SWC** and consume two of AVA3 while providing **zero Specialists**.

### 6. FO Submachine Gun (PS=6) — 23/0 (B; narrow alternative)

**Explicit correction:** this profile has a **Submachine Gun (PS=6), not (+1SD)**. Do not give it extra Success Dice. Relative to the BSG FO **22/0**, it adds a point, replaces the **AP B2 PS6 BSG** with a **Normal-ammo B3 PS6 SMG** on this printed profile, and keeps Flash Pulse, Shock Mine, D-Charges, FO and the common skills.

| Range | Boarding Shotgun | Printed Trinitarian SMG |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8in | **+6, B2, PS6 AP** | +3, B3, PS6 Normal |
| 8–16in | 0, B2, PS6 AP | 0, **B3**, PS6 Normal |
| 16–24in | −3, B2, PS6 AP | −3, B3, PS6 Normal |
| Reactive use | B1 ordinary ARO | **Suppressive Fire option** |

Its honest niche is **more Burst in the 8–16in band** against lightly armored targets, or **Suppressive Fire** on a position after scoring. Suppressive Fire provides **B3 ARO** within its legal range and a **−3 opponent MOD** to relevant Face-to-Face Rolls; it can combine with **Mimetism (−6)** against opponents lacking a suitable visor. But entering Suppressive Fire means **giving up Camouflaged State**, so a W1 trooper becomes an identifiable defender. The BSG remains generally preferred for **+6 up close, AP ammo, lower cost and attack-from-marker tactics**.

**Default:** skip SMG unless there is a deliberate plan to fight at 8–16in or go into Suppressive Fire. It is not an upgrade for generic scoring.

## Sample selection: one, two, or three?

| Package | Total | Best use |
|---|---:|---|
| **One KHD** | **24pts / 0 SWC** | General-purpose hidden/camo Hacker specialist, close-range BSG/anti-Hacker tool |
| **One FO BSG** | **22 / 0** | Cheaper independent FO and Targeted option |
| **FO BSG + KHD** | **46 / 0** | **Default broad mission flexibility:** independent FO and Hacker, two axes |
| **FO DR Minelayer + KHD** | **47 / 1** | **De Fersen network plan:** turn-zero node plus backup cyber assassin |
| **FO BSG + FO DR Minelayer** | **45 / 1** | De Fersen network plus two separate FO specialists, no KHD |
| **FO BSG + MSR** | **47 / 1.5** | Scoring plus a genuine hidden diagonal/long-lane threat |
| **FO BSG + KHD + FO DR Minelayer** | **69 / 1** | Three-axis mission/hacking saturation; high investment in W1 midfield assets |
| **Two MSRs** | **50 / 3** | Very specialized hidden long-range ARO plan; no scoring specialists from these slots |

**Quantity recommendation:**

1. **One** is a strong default in an otherwise complete MO roster. If the mission rewards FO or cost is tight, take **FO22**; otherwise start **KHD24**.
2. **Two** is often the best balance at 300pts: **KHD24 + FO22**, or **KHD24 + DR FO23/1** when De Fersen is an offensive cyber centerpiece. Use **different corridors** and vary Camo versus truly Hidden deployment so the early Order pool does not collapse.
3. **Three** can be reasonable for specialist-heavy missions or dedicated hacking/area denial; 67–75pts is an expensive share of a sectorial already buying elite Knights and limited cheap Orders. Avoid automatic AVA3 just because each profile is individually efficient.

## Final decision rule

- **No De Fersen and broad objectives:** **KHD24** as first look, **FO BSG22** as second.
- **De Fersen as premium network Hacker:** **FO Deployable Repeater Minelayer23/1** becomes a **near-must**, then add **KHD24** if affordable. The 1 SWC is justified when it really extends his hacking threat; if a Peacemaker/Pitcher already covers the same geometry, reassess.
- **Extra SWC + chokepoint + minimal hacking:** **FO Shock Mine Minelayer23/1** is permissible but not the best starting profile.
- **Diagonal deployment or exceptional hidden long-range lane:** consider **MSR25/1.5** instead of (not automatically in addition to) a second scoring Specialist.
- **SMG23/0:** take only for planned B3 midrange or Suppressive Fire defense; **not** an alleged +1SD gun.

**Bottom line:** Trinitarians are close to MO's most economical concealed mission pieces. Their strongest competitive proposition is **KHD/FO mission versatility plus genuine Hidden Deployment**, with the **predeployed Repeater + De Fersen** as the standout faction-specific force multiplier. Preserve the rest of the list's long-range opener, ordinary Orders and SWC before buying the third.
