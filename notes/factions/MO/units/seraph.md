# Seraph — Military Orders (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: MULTI HMG 74/1.5 — A+ as an MO centerpiece; AP Spitfire + Auxbot 72/1.5 — A, situational A+ for dense-table assault/forks. Neither is mandatory.** The Seraph is a **BS15, ARM7, STR3, S7** main-battle TAG with **Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion, +3"), NCO and Tactical Awareness**. The MULTI HMG gives Military Orders a scarce full-Burst, 16–32-inch **AP** gun; the AP Spitfire trades that reach for a **separately positioned, synchronized Heavy Flamethrower Auxbot** capable of real shooting/template forks and close-combat Burst support. Both can use the two Lieutenant Orders from a Knight Commander LT (+1) through NCO. Unlike the Seraph, the **MO Tikbalang has no NCO**.

**Recommendation:** Usually start with **MULTI HMG** because the rest of MO already has abundant Spitfire/shotgun-range attackers, but use **AP Spitfire + Auxbot** deliberately for midfield/DZ clearing with the correct approach geometry. Do not judge it as merely a two-point-cheaper, short-range MULTI HMG.

## 1. Current profiles and priority

**Seraphs: ID 34 · Regular TAG · AVA 2 · no Military Orders Fireteam access.**

**Shared attributes:** MOV **15–10 cm (6–4")**, CC23, BS15, PH16, WIP13, ARM7, BTS6, **STR3**, **S7**.

**Shared equipment/skills:** ECM: Guided (-6), Martial Arts L1, **BS Attack (SR-1)**, Remote Presence, **Tactical Awareness**, **NCO**, Religious Troop, **Dodge (PH=12), Dodge (+2")**, GizmoKit (PH=13), **Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion), Super-Jump (+3")**, EXP CC Weapon (PS=4), Crabbot.

| Pinned option | Loadout | Pts / SWC | Rating | Primary job |
|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| **2** | **MULTI HMG, Pulzar (+1B)**, EXP CCW, Crabbot | **74 / 1.5** | **A+** | Main 16–32" long-range opener; repeated AP shooting; jumping to create new attack angles |
| **1** | **AP Spitfire, Pulzar, AUXBOT_2**, EXP CCW, Crabbot | **72 / 1.5** | **A**, situational **A+** | Dense-board midrange assault, simultaneous AP gun + Heavy Flamethrower forks, enemy-DZ clearing and CC support |

**Do not invent a third option:** no Seraph Lieutenant profile, no Seraph Fireteam, and **the MULTI HMG profile has no Auxbot**. The **AP Spitfire profile does**. Both have an Ancillary Crabbot, which is a different Peripheral type and is not a second ordinary Regular Order.

**AUXBOT_2 (AP Spitfire option only):** MOV 6–4", CC13, BS10, PH10, WIP11, ARM0, BTS3, STR1, S1; **Heavy Flamethrower**, PARA CC Weapon (-3); **Peripheral (Synchronized)**, Courage, **Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion, +3")**. It shares the Seraph's super-jumping movement package but must maintain **Coherency**.

**Crabbot (both profiles):** S1, MOV 6–4", WIP12, STR1, **Specialist Operative**, Mimetism (-3), **Peripheral (Ancillary)**, Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion, +3"). It **is not on the table by default**: the Seraph must use **Place Deployable** to deploy it in Silhouette contact. Once deployed, it activates according to Peripheral rules, with no Ancillary operating-distance limit; it does not generate an Order.

## 2. What actually distinguishes the Seraph?

1. **Super-Jump attack geometry:** 6" base first MOV plus **Super-Jump (+3")** enables unusually long vertical Jump movement with Jet Propulsion, and a legal Jump + BS Attack can threaten angles unavailable from the starting position. Super-Jump is not free concealment: being exposed during the Jump creates ARO opportunities, and jumping does **not** automatically grant Partial Cover. An exposed S7 model without Mimetism can lose to competent reactive guns.
2. **NCO + Tactical Awareness:** unlike the 68-point MO Tikbalang, the Seraph can spend Lieutenant Special Orders via NCO, making it a strong recipient of **Knight Commander LT (+1)**. Its own Tactical Awareness gives one more restricted activation, independently of NCO.
3. **Actual long-range AP option:** the MULTI HMG uses **Burst 4 AP at PS5** in its AP mode, not a lower-Burst anti-armour compromise. Shock mode remains B4; EXP anti-materiel mode is B1. The AP Spitfire offers **Burst 4 AP at PS6** and strong 8–24" bands, but has poor reach beyond 24".
4. **Threatening melee, not a default melee specialist:** CC23 + MA1 + **EXP CCW (PS4)** makes it costly to challenge in CC. The EXP weapon imposes three saving rolls on a successful hit, but **does not halve ARM**; positioning and opponent CC skills still decide whether the charge is worth it.
5. **ARM7/STR3/Remote Presence:** robust as an active-turn attacker and repairable by an Engineer, yet still very Hackable and vulnerable to E/M, hostile Repeater grids, immobilization and anti-TAG AROs. Unlike Tik's Mimetism (-3), it has no persistent visual MOD to help win every Face-to-Face gunfight.

## 3. MULTI HMG versus AP Spitfire: real weapon and mission trade-offs

| Question | MULTI HMG **74/1.5** | AP Spitfire + Auxbot **72/1.5** |
|---|---|---|
| Best primary shooting distance | **16–32"** | **8–24"** |
| Repeated anti-ARM fire | B4, **AP**, PS5 | B4, **AP**, PS6 |
| Additional long-range mode | Shock B4; EXP B1 anti-materiel | None; same AP Spitfire |
| Close-range attack options | **Pulzar (+1B)** from the Seraph's position | Seraph **Pulzar** + independently positioned **Auxbot HFT** |
| Simultaneous two-body attack origins | **No Auxbot** | **Yes**, legal synchronized attacks from separate positions |
| Close-combat Burst helper | Deployed Crabbot / other allied models conditionally | **Auxbot**, plus deployed Crabbot conditionally |
| Favourable geometry | Open/medium boards, long diagonal lanes, elevated 24–32" threats | Dense midfield, corners, DZ clusters, routes where Auxbot can reach its own target |
| Main listbuilding risk | Spends 74 points on a big no-Mimetism TAG | Spends 72 points on a TAG whose primary gun may be outranged; Auxbot can be killed or lose Coherency |

**Weapon-range consequence:** the MULTI HMG's +3 range band through 32" frequently allows a Super-Jump peek without first advancing under opposing AROs. The AP Spitfire is excellent once delivered into its preferred distances, but jumping at a model 28–32" away can mean shooting at -3 instead of the HMG's +3. On dense tables where most decisive engagements are inside 24", this matters much less.

### Decision rule

- **Take MULTI HMG** if the Seraph must be the army's main long-range opener, if opposing TAGs/HI must be fought at distance, or if you lack another AP HMG/long-range gun.
- **Take AP Spitfire + Auxbot** if the table gives repeated safe routes into the 8–24" band and you have a planned synchronized template/fork attack; ideally another unit handles the longest ARO lanes.
- Do **not** pay for the Spitfire variant on the vague premise that “Auxbot adds Burst to the gun.” It does **not**: its value is **a separate attack from a separate position**, or **+1B in close combat** under the multiple-trooper rule.

## 4. The AP Spitfire + Heavy Flamethrower fork — why enthusiasts like this profile

This is the **explicit podcast-supported combo**, not a theoretical invented interaction.

**Setup:** Seraph and AUXBOT_2 remain in Coherency. The Seraph has a legal AP Spitfire attack against enemy A, while the Auxbot has a legal Heavy Flamethrower template that covers A (or a second enemy B). Both are activated by the **same Order** and declare the applicable same Skills, but **may choose different targets**.

**Against one enemy A**, consider the defender's response:

| Defender's response | Why it is awkward |
|---|---|
| **Shoot the Seraph** | Opposes the **AP Spitfire** if legal, but cannot Face-to-Face cancel the **Auxbot's direct template**. The HFT hit is resolved independently. |
| **Shoot the Auxbot** | Cannot negate the Seraph's AP Spitfire with that shot; the Seraph can obtain Normal Rolls against that defender while the Auxbot's template remains a danger. |
| **Dodge** | Can avoid **both** attacks if it successfully handles the relevant opposing BS rolls and template Dodge; failure may leave it taking Spitfire hits and the template. |
| **Reset** | Does not oppose conventional Spitfire/HFT fire. |

A direct template normally requires **no BS roll**; shooting its bearer can kill it, but does not retroactively cancel the template if the attack was legally declared and resolved simultaneously. Template placement may not include friendly/neutral targets illegally; be careful with overlapping Seraph/Auxbot bases.

**Against two different enemies**, the Seraph can shoot A while the Auxbot templates B around a different corner, as in the official **Peripheral (Synchronized) example**. Both activate together and grant their own possible AROs, so this is **not immunity to return fire**. It is compression of two useful attacks into a single activation and creates multiple defensive dilemmas.

**Practical sequence:**
1. Use alternate forces, hacking, or a good firing lane to remove enemy HMG/MSR/ML pieces that would dominate a 24–32" approach.
2. Advance Seraph + Auxbot behind terrain with their **matching Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion, +3")** where useful; check **Coherency at the start and end of Orders**.
3. Approach the last corner with the Seraph's AP gun covering the defender and Auxbot threatening its exposed position with the HFT. Do not accidentally leave the fragile S1 Auxbot visible to multiple extra AROs.
4. Declare a legal synchronized attack **only after** verifying both models' range/LoF/template placement, keeping other allied models outside the template.
5. After trading up, return to a protected end position and protect the TAG against hacking/reprisal instead of leaving S7 in an obvious open lane.

**Why it can be worth losing HMG range:** a normal AP HMG can defeat a single defender in a conventional Face-to-Face duel; **HMG + no Peripheral cannot create this same multi-origin template/gun fork**. If the HFT bypasses a defender's best shooting MODs or catches a second model, the close-assault package becomes especially efficient.

**Limits:** Auxbot is **not a deployable mine** and does not remain as an independent attack battery. A disconnected or destroyed Auxbot cannot provide the synchronized attack. The Seraph cannot bring the Auxbot to a different Combat Group or join a Fireteam.

## 5. Other AP Spitfire combinations

### A. Seraph + Auxbot close-combat B2; potentially B3 with Crabbot

The N5.3 **Close Combat with Multiple Troopers** rule grants the chosen attacking allied Trooper/Peripheral **+1B for each additional eligible allied model/Peripheral in Silhouette contact with the enemy**.

- **Seraph alone:** B1 **EXP CCW**, CC23/MA1.
- **Seraph + Auxbot both touching the same enemy:** **B2** Seraph EXP CCW, *provided both are eligible and the Seraph performs the CC Attack*.
- **Seraph + Auxbot + deployed Crabbot touching the same enemy:** theoretically **B3** EXP CCW under the same rule. The Crabbot must first be **deployed using Place Deployable**; it does not appear automatically, and arranging legal base contact with all three bodies is expensive/risky.

This is an **actual rules interaction**, not a reason to rush the Seraph blindly into enemy melee. A competent enemy CC specialist, E/M effect, or a bad engagement lane can still stop it. If using Crabbot for mission scoring, also account for the Order needed to deploy it and its limited standalone resilience.

### B. Seraph + De Fersen cyber-cover

**Tactical Awareness's MO Redux discussion** explicitly likes the Seraph AP Spitfire assault package while describing **De Fersen's offensive anti-hacker threat** as a way to discourage enemy Pitcher/Repeater abuse near the Hackable TAG.

This is a **combined-arms/ARO deterrence plan, not a synchronized Seraph + De Fersen single-Order fork**. De Fersen must have a **legal hacking target and network/range**, and the opponent can still exploit hacking paths before the response. De Fersen's **Trinity (+1 SD)** targets enemy hackers; his ordinary HD programs provide other control options. The package becomes more coherent when an existing **Trinitarian deployable Repeater, Order Sergeant Pitcher or Peacemaker/Pathfinder** already forms a useful network.

A **De Fersen Lieutenant** configuration may be considered for an active fighting/hacking LT strategy, but **do not combine that with Knight Commander LT (+1)**: a normal list has only one active Lieutenant, and Seraph NCO benefits from **available** LT Orders rather than generating any itself.

### C. Seraph + Knight Commander LT (+1) — NCO action engine

| Source | Can the Seraph spend it? | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Seraph's own Tactical Awareness | **Yes — 1** | Restricted personal Tactical Order each eligible active turn |
| Knight Commander Lieutenant (+1) | **Yes — 2 via NCO** | They remain two Lieutenant Special Orders; NCO permits Seraph activations |
| Seraph's own Regular Order contribution | **Yes, as part of its Combat Group's pool** | Not permanently attached to Seraph; could activate a different eligible unit |
| All other Regular Orders in its Combat Group | **Yes** | Normal pool rules, not an NCO bonus |

**Three potential restricted Seraph activations** (1 TacAware + 2 NCO) before spending the group's Regular pool make the 72/74-point model unusually self-funding. The same engine is **not** available to the MO Tikbalang because it has **no NCO**.

**Seraph MULTI HMG 74 + Commander LT (+1) 24 + Tech-Bee 9 = 107 pts / 1.5 SWC**; the AP Spitfire variant is **105 / 1.5** for the same support shell. Add actual Engineer/Palbot access and objectives to turn this into a playable plan, not just a TAG attack chain. A 20-point ordinary Knight Commander or 18-point Teutonic Lieutenant may be more economical when the extra LT Order cannot be used repeatedly.

## 6. Seraph versus MO Tikbalang — corrected differences

| | **Seraph MULTI HMG** | **Seraph AP Spitfire** | **Tikbalang of Montesa** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | **74 / 1.5** | **72 / 1.5** | **68 / 1.5** |
| BS / ARM / STR / S | 15 / 7 / 3 / **S7** | Same | 15 / 6 / 3 / **S6** |
| NCO | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** |
| Tactical Awareness | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Mimetism (-3) | No | No | **Yes** |
| Special movement | Super-Jump +3", Jet Propulsion | Same | **Climbing Plus** |
| Main gun | **B4 AP MULTI HMG**, 16–32" | **B4 AP Spitfire**, 8–24" | **B4 normal HMG**, 16–32" |
| Other ranged threat | Pulzar (+1B) | Pulzar **and Auxbot Heavy Flamethrower** | Heavy Flamethrower |
| CC | CC23 MA1, EXP (PS4) | Same; Auxbot can add +1B | CC17, **CC Attack (-3)**, DA CCW (PS5) |
| Special weakness | No Mim, exposed S7, high budget | Shorter range, lost Auxbot vulnerable | **No AP HMG, NCO, Shock Mines or MO Fireteam** |

**Why Mimetism still matters:** Tik's -3 penalizes enemy return shooting in repeated conventional Face-to-Face fights, which may reduce the number of shots reaching the TAG more than ARM7 saves. It has a smaller footprint and routes around buildings through Climbing Plus. Choose Tik when **attack-angle control and persistence** matter more than AP or a Commander LT-order engine.

**Why Seraph is often the stronger MO centerpiece:** For **6 points more** than Tik, the MULTI HMG gains NCO, a full-Burst AP long gun, +1 ARM, serious CC, and a different class of vertical attack. The AP Spitfire costs **only 4 points more** than Tik and adds the independently positioned Auxbot fork; it is *not* a cheaper long-range substitute.

**Versus Knight of the Holy Sepulchre NCO AP HMG 53/1:** Sepulchre is **21 points and 0.5 SWC cheaper than Seraph HMG**, has Holoprojector/Stealth/BTS9 and NCO, and is often the better budget-efficient AP gun when many specialists/Knights are already wanted. Seraph buys **TacAware, BS15, ARM7/STR3, Super-Jump mobility** and TAG-scale pressure. No automatic winner, but Sepulchre is a valuable opportunity-cost check.

## 7. When to take / when to skip

**Take the MULTI HMG when:**
- The list lacks any serious long-range AP gun and needs to clear enemy 24–32" AROs itself.
- You can afford a **Knight Commander LT (+1) + NCO** engine, or just want TacAware-powered BS15 long-range shooting without the extra Commander's premium.
- The battlefield supports Jump angles that isolate targets without exposing the Seraph to several dangerous AROs.
- You have genuine **Engineer/Palbot recovery** and independent specialists left after buying it.

**Take AP Spitfire + Auxbot when:**
- Dense terrain gives repeated **8–24"** approaches and reachable corners with legal Peripheral Coherency.
- You want **AP Spitfire + HFT simultaneously**, potentially into **different targets**, instead of another standalone midrange gun.
- The mission rewards driving an aggressive attacker into the enemy DZ or flushing clustered troops, while another shooter handles long lanes.
- You have real hacking cover and can protect the S1 Auxbot on the approach; a conditional B2 EXP CCW also adds upside.

**Skip both when:**
- The 72–74-point cost and support compress the rest of the army below a reliable Regular Order and mission-specialist floor.
- Enemy repeaters, E/M, anti-TAG tech and open long-range AROs are numerous enough that no safe approach exists.
- An **AP HMG Sepulchre 53**, Tikbalang 68, or cheaper linked gun leaves substantially better objective coverage and a second attack plan.
- You want the TAG as a passive exposed ARO sentry. Both profiles are designed mainly for **active-turn maneuver and attacks**, not to stand visible against opposing active Burst 4 guns.

## 8. Support, recovery and listbuilding

- **Tech-Bee 9/0:** highly efficient Regular Specialist and TAGCom support **only for same-Combat-Group TAGs**. Seraph's printed **Dodge PH12 becomes PH15**, not PH19; printed **GizmoKit PH13 becomes PH14**. Place her somewhere safe. She is **not** an Engineer.
- **Military Order Curator Engineer 17–20:** actual MO Engineer for STR restoration and Engineer-removable states. **Curator 17 + Palbot 3** is a practical independent recovery route; a Curator controlling Palbot **cannot simultaneously remain in a Fireteam**. No standard PanO Machinist in the pinned MO roster.
- **De Fersen / Santiago KHD / Trinitarian KHD / legal Pitcher or Repeater users:** protect assault paths through offensive hacking, active hunter-killer response and scenario control. A TinBot in a separate Fireteam **does not magically protect** the independent Seraph.
- **EVO/Fairy Dust:** conditional Firewall protection against **Comms Attacks**, not a universal counter to direct E/M weapons, and may require an Order to establish the buff. The program's benefit versus its SWC/points cost must be justified by the matchup and other Hackable troops.
- **Trinitarian FO/KHD, foot/motorized Montesa Paramedic, inexpensive Crosiers/Raveneye/Fugazi/Mulebot:** provide separate objective scoring, defence and actual Orders. The TAG is not an unrestricted button-pushing Specialist; its **deployed Crabbot is** a specialist but takes setup and can be removed.
- **Secondary long-range plan for AP Spitfire:** particularly important. Consider Sepulchre AP HMG (expensive dual-gun approach), an appropriate linked long-range weapon, or a controlled bypass route. **Do not automatically solve range shortcomings by paying for a second huge main gun.**

**Illustrative package, not a legal Army export in isolation:** AP Seraph **72** + Knight Commander LT(+1) **24** + Tech-Bee **9** + Curator basic **17** + Palbot **3** = **125 points / 1.5 SWC**, leaving 175 points for Order infrastructure, midfield specialists, hacking protection and AROs. **MULTI HMG version = 127 / 1.5**, leaving 173. Those totals include one Palbot as a Peripheral, *not* another independent Regular Order.

### Final priority

**Seraph MULTI HMG: A+ in TAG-centred MO, 0–1 ordinarily.** It provides the army's rare long-range B4 AP gun, Super-Jump angle selection, TacAware + NCO engine and serious durability. **Seraph AP Spitfire + Auxbot: A / conditional A+, 0–1 when the fork is the plan.** It is a distinct **multi-body assault threat** with actual simultaneous template/gun and CC Burst opportunities. The choice is **reliable ranged opening** versus **optimized close-assault geometry and fork potential**, not a blanket verdict that the cheaper option is bad.

Do not buy or proxy a second Seraph automatically merely because AVA is 2. Test the twin-TAG strategy on the appropriate missions, with **independent objective and hacker/Engineer support**; double Seraph has actual tournament precedent but a severe concentration risk.
