# Raveneye Officer — Military Orders (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: A+ for the 12-point E/M Mine profile; A for Cybermine. Strong default and close to an auto-include in many 300-point MO lists, but not mandatory.** Raveneye is an unusually efficient **Regular Order + Forward Observer + Minelayer + E/Marat + Flash Pulse** package. It primarily buys *predeployed defense* without demanding activation Orders, which matters in MO's expensive Knight/TAG lists. Prefer **E/M Mine** for general-purpose denial; take **Cybermine** if its forced Reset interaction specifically defeats the expected opponent or route.

## Why a 12-point Raveneye is unusually good in MO

A basic **11pt Crosier** gives a Regular Order and can fill a Fireteam seat; Raveneye costs only **one more point** yet has several independent jobs:

1. **Predeployed mine from Minelayer:** begins taxing a route during deployment rather than spending an active-turn Order first. It may force enemy detection/clearance, a changed route, a risky movement, or a mine-trigger trade.
2. **E/Marat:** credible short-range **Large Teardrop E/M** punishment for an HI/TAG/REM assault or DZ parachutist that closes in. A BS11 SMG is not the reason to put him in the list.
3. **Forward Observer (WIP13):** real mission specialist, plus the possibility of Targeted/Guided support if the army deliberately includes such a package. It is not a reason to run a Guided Clipper automatically.
4. **Flash Pulse (WIP13):** nonlethal ranged ARO against a vulnerable approach; potentially stalls an attacker by inflicting Stunned.
5. **Deactivator:** helps clear revealed enemy deployables when he can reach a suitable angle. Ancillary tool, not a reason to send the cheap defender into midfield.
6. **Native pooled Regular Order:** no Joan LT or link needed. Compared to an Irregular Warcor, its Order can fuel another unit **in the same Combat Group**.

That is an extremely high ratio of useful *independent functions* to price, particularly in MO where Knights, TAGs, premium guns and linked specialists consume most of the budget.

## E/M Mine vs Cybermine: choose by what you want to punish

### Option 1 — E/M Mine (12/0), default A+

- E/M ammunition forces **two Saving Rolls against half BTS** on a hit. Failure causes **Isolated**; HI, TAGs, REMs and vehicles additionally enter **Immobilized-B**.
- **Isolated is a severe attack-plan disruption:** the trooper cannot use ordinary pooled Orders, and Hacking Devices become unusable while Isolated. An affected valuable attacker may require multiple Orders or an Engineer to recover.
- Protects against both advanced expensive threats and a broad range of ordinary troops. Excellent opposite a corridor to your Lieutenant, expensive Hacker, TAG engineer or a rear objective.
- It is not an automatic stop: enemies can detect/remove a mine, avoid the template/trigger path, Dodge when legally able, or successfully pass Saving Rolls. Do not assume the opponent must enter the trap.

**Reason to default to it:** its worst outcomes are strategically costly for the attacker, even if the Raveneye itself never fights.

### Option 2 — Cybermine (12/0), situational A

- Cybermines are **Comms Attacks**, so the defense against their attack is **Reset at -3 rather than Dodge**. They cause **two BTS Saving Rolls** using their special PS.
- A failed roll **Stuns ordinary troopers** or **Immobilizes-B Hackable troopers/Hackers instead**. A Cybermine does *not* impose Isolated, and it does not inherit the E/M mine's half-BTS Saving Roll mechanic.
- The enforced Reset makes it especially awkward for some fast/high-PH, relatively low-WIP units that would be happier Dodging a physical mine.
- Its main downside is often less punishing consequences: Stunned expires under its state rules; Immobilized-B is an obstacle but **does not stop a unit from generating an Order for its pool**, unlike Isolated.

**When to prefer Cybermine:** a specific lane/matchup where **forcing the Reset** is more reliable or valuable than an E/M Mine's harsher but conventionally Dodgeable punishment. It is not a universally superior anti-Hacker choice just because of its name.

## Practical deployment and defense plan

### 1. Protected DZ choke point — main use

Place the Raveneye behind a wall, building corner or offset from a key route rather than as a long-range ARO gun. Put the **Minelayer mine at a legal position within his Zone of Control**, respecting the actual Minelayer and deployment requirements, to threaten the *approach toward* him or toward important support personnel.

Useful targets of the trap include:

- A Combat Jump/Parachutist or flank attacker seeking a Lieutenant, Hacker, Curator or Tech-Bee.
- An HI/TAG advancing from an exposed access route to reach your expensive backline.
- An infiltrator entering a rear corridor or mission path.
- An attacker hoping to dispose of cheap Orders after your main gun has advanced.

**Make the opponent choose** between a risky mine route, an alternate approach, and investing Orders in discovery/clearance. The mine is an obstacle and information problem, not a guaranteed kill. A mine also does **not** automatically reveal an Impersonator or defeat all Impersonation/camouflage interactions; follow the marker and mine trigger rules.

### 2. E/Marat as the second layer

The mine and Raveneye should cover *different but related* positions where possible:

- If the enemy clears the mine, entering the Raveneye's **E/Marat template** may still be unpleasant.
- If they try to snipe the Raveneye safely, the mine can remain relevant along the route to more valuable targets.
- A 12pt Raveneye trading with a 30–80pt attack piece is excellent, but **avoid gratuitous exposed AROs** against superior long-range guns.

E/Marat is a template threat rather than a BS11 Face-to-Face gunfighter plan. The Raveneye is **ARM1/W1**, even with Immunity (Shock). Keep him alive while he is denying the route.

### 3. Backup specialist and reactive utility

When the deployment threat has passed, he can move out to press a nearby objective, Forward Observe/Spotlight a target when legal, Discover, place another mine or Deactivate a deployable. His **WIP13** is a reasonable specialist stat, but **MOV 4–4 and no forward deployment** make him a *local or late-game* scorer, not a Trinitarian/Montesa replacement for distant midfield objectives.

Likewise, WIP13 Flash Pulse can force a dangerous attacker to respect even a weak ARO angle, but exposed Flash Pulse against a serious attack is still fragile. Don't confuse potential value with a reliable main defensive piece.

### 4. Combat Group allocation — do not strand the Regular Order

Raveneye is **Regular**, not Irregular. His Regular Order joins the **pool of whichever Combat Group he belongs to**. Group 2 is reasonable when its own specialists/defenders need Orders or Group 1 is full; it is **not inherently preferable** and a Group 2 Raveneye **does not provide a pooled Order to Group 1's TAG or Knights**.

For a list based on a single main attack group, put him in that group if space exists and his Order will fuel the attacker. If he must go in Group 2, make sure the auxiliary group has useful ways to spend that Order.

## Compared with MO's competing cheap pieces

These options are **complementary**, not strict either/or substitutions. A well-built list may contain Raveneye **and** Tech-Bee, Warcor, Fugazi, Mulebot or Curator.

| MO unit | Cost | Advantage over Raveneye / reason to choose |
|---|---:|---|
| **Fugazi** | **7 / 0** | Cheaper Regular Order, fast mobile Repeater, Mimetism/Flash Pulse. AVA 1, no predeployed mine/E/Marat/FO specialist package |
| **Mulebot basic** | **8 / 0** | Cheapest other Regular Order/Baggage/Repeater infrastructure; bulky S4, no equivalent point-defense attack |
| **Tech-Bee** | **9 / 0** | Cheaper Regular **specialist**, Flash/Adhesive, deployable Repeater/GizmoKit and TAGCom for eligible same-group TAGs; no Minelayer |
| **Crosier basic** | **11 / 0** | Only one point cheaper but meaningful if filling a required Fireteam seat or achieving a team-level bonus |
| **Raveneye E/M** | **12 / 0** | **Predeployed E/M mine + E/Marat + FO + Flash** in one independent Regular body |
| **Crosier MSR E/M Minelayer** | **19 / 1** | More costly SWC/points but brings a proper sniper ARO and can contribute to a qualified Fireteam; different job |
| **Curator Engineer** | **17–20 / 0** | Actual Engineer/repair access, mine/turret depending on profile; better support when your TAG/REM must have a repair plan |
| **Warcor 360º** | **3 / 0** | Inexpensive rear/flank Flash Pulse sentry; normally **Irregular**, but may become Regular via **Joan Lieutenant's Inspiring Leadership**; no mine or specialist |

**Practical purchasing hierarchy:** do not cut a high-value **Tech-Bee/Fugazi** to take Raveneye by reflex; start with a list's required Order engine, qualified Fireteam members and mission coverage. After that, Raveneye is usually one of the highest-value 12 points you can spend for protection. Warcor and Tech-Bee can be taken **alongside** it.

For a Seraph/Tikbalang or Holy Sepulchre list, an **E/M Raveneye guarding the TAG's support corridor** is especially worthwhile. In Joan-based lists, a **360º Warcor may become a Regular 3pt rear sentry**, but Raveneye still offers a distinct, stronger *mine-based* defensive role.

## When to take and when to skip

**Take one E/M Raveneye by default when:**

- MO's primary investment is an expensive TAG, Lieutenant, Hacker or Knight attack group vulnerable to DZ flanking.
- You want your defense to consume *enemy* Orders rather than spend *your own* Orders on setup.
- A 12pt Regular Order with a genuine backup specialist function fits the Combat Group.
- Existing long-range AROs cover open lanes but not corners/short-range infiltration or aerial entry routes.

**Consider Cybermine instead when:** you have a well-identified Reset-vs-Dodge trap against an expected threat and accept the less punishing state result.

**Skip Raveneye when:**

- A needed Fireteam body/team-level breakpoint or critical attacker/specialist requires those exact points. Raveneye cannot join teams.
- A different cheap Regular Order is necessary to hit list/order milestones; the mission and table have adequate defensive coverage from Trinitarian, Crosier Minelayer, Curator, Escort/Auxbot, etc.
- Most threatening approaches are already effectively denied by several other tools, so a further DZ mine is redundant.
- You have no valuable Order consumer in his Combat Group and his cheap support role is not needed.

## Final rating

- **E/M Mine: A+ / default 0–1, usually 1 in a balanced 300pt MO list.** Borderline auto-include based on marginal points efficiency, not a true mandatory tax.
- **Cybermine: A / alternate 0–1.** Selected for an explicit Reset-vs-Dodge matchup, not merely because it sounds more anti-hacking.
- **Overall MO roster priority:** high-value *independent* cheap support; not a Fireteam member, not a primary ARO, and not a replacement for a mobile midfield specialist.

**One-line listbuilding test:** *If my 12pt Raveneye never spends an Order, does a predeployed E/M trap still make reaching my Lieutenant/TAG/Engineer more expensive?* If yes, he has probably earned his slot.
