# Knight Commander — Military Orders (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: A+ for Lieutenant (+1 Order) with an active NCO plan; A / situational A+ for Chain of Command behind an aggressive Lieutenant; C for the ordinary Lieutenant.** This is a **defensive command and Fireteam-support piece**, not an assault Knight. The ideal use is to **remain protected in a backfield Military Orders Core** while contributing a Regular Order, Crosier-equivalent Fireteam level, Holomask deception and either (a) **two Lieutenant Orders for NCO elsewhere** or (b) **succession insurance for a fighting Lieutenant elsewhere**. The two strong profiles solve different listbuilding problems; they cannot be taken simultaneously (AVA 1).

## Profiles and priority

**ID 1561 · AVA 1 · Regular MI · MOV 10–10 cm (4–4 in) · CC20 · BS13 · PH12 · WIP13 · ARM2 · BTS6 · W1 · S2.** All profiles have **Holomask, Martial Arts L2, Immunity (Shock), Religious Troop**, and enhanced Dodge / BS Attack as recorded in the pinned roster. All join only the **Military Orders** Fireteam family, where they **count as Crosier**; none is a universal Wildcard.

| Opt | Profile | Pts / SWC | Weapons | Rating | Use |
|---:|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **3** | **Lieutenant (+1 Order)** | **24 / 0** | Boarding Shotgun, Pulzar, Pistol, Shock CCW | **A+ with NCO** | Default Commander Lieutenant when a real NCO attacker can exploit both Special Lieutenant Orders |
| **1** | **Chain of Command** | **23 / 0** | Light Shotgun, Pulzar, Pistol, Shock CCW | **A; A+ with aggressive LT** | Safe successor behind Joan or a fighting Santiago/Justice/Hospitaller/Teutonic Lieutenant |
| **2** | **Lieutenant** | **20 / 0** | Boarding Shotgun, Pulzar, Pistol, Shock CCW | **C; situational B−** | Mostly skip; niche backfield Holomask/Fireteam Lieutenant without NCO |

The extra Lieutenant Order and Chain of Command **are never on the same profile**. All options contribute one ordinary Regular Order to their own Combat Group while alive and eligible. A Commander is **not** automatically a mission Specialist; scenario wording may treat Chain of Command as qualifying for particular objectives, but do not assume universal Specialist Operative.

## 1. Lieutenant (+1 Order): the NCO economy engine — A+

The point is **four additional points over the 20pt basic Lieutenant buys one additional Special Lieutenant Order**, giving the army **two LT Orders** each turn while the Commander remains a valid Lieutenant. These are **not ordinary transferable Regular Orders**. They become valuable when NCO converts them into Tactical Orders that an NCO gunfighter can spend.

**Best recipients (the MO Tikbalang is **not** one of them; Infinity Army screenshot verified 23 November 2026):**

| NCO attacker | Why Commander LT (+1) fits | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Seraph MULTI HMG 74 / AP Spitfire + Auxbot 72** | NCO + Tactical Awareness can concentrate activations on a high-impact Super-Jump TAG | Expensive centerpiece; position, hacking defense and a backup scoring route still matter |
| **Knight of the Holy Sepulchre NCO AP HMG 53** | Strong mid-cost independent AP HMG gets two restricted attack Orders without occupying the Core/Haris | No Tactical Awareness on this AP HMG option; not equivalent to a Seraph in durability or reach |
| **Teutonic Knight NCO Spitfire 29** | Particularly economical: when the NCO is the legal Fireteam Leader and spends its Tactical Order, **its whole attacking Fireteam activates** | Haris/Core must be chart-legal, and the NCO may be unable to use both Orders every turn |

**Order accounting:** With a Seraph, the Commander contributes **one pooled Regular Order**, plus **two Special Lieutenant Orders available to NCO**; the Seraph independently has its **own pooled Regular Order and one Tactical Awareness Order**. Thus the pair can potentially support **five activations in total attributable to their own Orders** (two pooled Regulars, two LT-via-NCO, one TAG Tactical Awareness), before counting the rest of the group's pooled Orders. **Do not call those five transferable Orders**. NCO and Commander need not be in the same Combat Group to access the Special Lieutenant Orders under the current NCO rule, but ordinary pooled Orders remain group-specific.

**Important distinction:** The Knight Commander himself is not NCO and should almost never spend his Lieutenant Orders to attack or move merely to avoid wasting them; doing so exposes the command piece and using the Lieutenant's own Special Order as Fireteam Leader can disrupt Fireteam Integrity. Keep the Commander safe; let the *separate* NCO attack. A Teutonic NCO leading an **attacking** Haris can use its converted Tactical Orders to activate that whole team while the Commander stays in a **different defensive Core**.

**Failure mode:** If the NCO is lost, Isolated, in a bad position or has no useful target/objective, the LT (+1) upgrade can lose much of its practical value. Build around an NCO expected to spend Orders productively in Turns 1–3; do not confuse paper Order count with flexible pool depth.

## 2. Chain of Command: protect an aggressive Lieutenant — A to A+

At **23 / 0**, the CoC Commander provides succession without committing the successor to the same risk as the fighting Lieutenant. The principal use is **Joan v2 LT** or another Knight Lieutenant that actively advances or exposes itself to shoot.

| Fighting Lieutenant | CoC synergy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Joan v2 AP Spitfire LT** | **A+** | Joan can commit Tactical Awareness and her combat kit without making the entire army's command survival depend on her living |
| **Knight of Justice Spitfire LT** | **A** | High-value combat Lieutenant may need to take unfavorable-but-necessary engagements |
| **Santiago LT** | **A** | Supports an aggressive Knight or eligible combat team |
| **Hospitaller LT** | **A− / A** | Protects a high-value advancing assault piece |
| **Teutonic Lieutenant 18** | **B / situational A−** | Insurance is useful, but a 23pt successor is more expensive than the cheap LT; reassess whether the original point-saving strategy still makes sense |

Chain of Command operates at the **start of the Loss of Lieutenant check**, subject to its conditions: the successor must still be deployed on the table and eligible. It prevents the normal Loss of Lieutenant penalty when it takes over; it **does not restore the dead Lieutenant's special abilities or previous offensive presence**. In particular, if Joan dies, her **Inspiring Leadership and Tactical Awareness are lost**; the Commander does not inherit them. The successor's own profile is unchanged apart from becoming the Lieutenant.

**Deployment:** Put CoC in a protected backfield support Core, **away from the fighting Lieutenant's attack route**. Avoid a single template, infiltration lane, airborne strike or hacking chain being able to neutralize both. Holomask makes locating the successor harder, but it is not an unconditional guarantee of anonymity or safety.

**When to skip CoC:** Your LT already hides effectively, your main attack piece is not your LT, or the 23 points are more valuable as a Trinitarian, Raveneye, extra gun/support or necessary scoring option. CoC is *insurance*, not an automatic tax on Joan or every Knight.

## 3. Basic Lieutenant: mostly skip — C

The **20 / 0** Lieutenant offers the same backfield Holomask and Crosier-equivalent link contribution, but only one Special Lieutenant Order. If the list already contains a **productive NCO**, the **24pt LT (+1)** upgrade is normally a very favorable four-point spend, potentially one more impactful attack or complete attacking-Fireteam activation every turn.

**Narrow case to use basic LT:** No NCO is available or expected to spend the bonus Order, yet you specifically want a hidden, non-Impetuous Lieutenant contributing to a cheap defensive Crosier Core. Then the 20pt option saves four points versus LT (+1). Compare against an **18pt Teutonic Lieutenant**, which has a different risk profile (more obvious, Impetuous, potentially involved in combat), and against whether those points should support better mission tools instead.

Do not buy the basic Lieutenant merely as a cheap generic trooper: the 11pt Crosier, 12pt Raveneye, 9pt Tech-Bee and cheap REMs perform ordinary Regular-order/support jobs for substantially less. For the Commander, you are primarily paying for **command function plus link equivalence**, not raw combat strength.

## 4. Preferred placement: defensive/support Fireteam, not an advancing assault link

This is the critical usage preference. **If taking a Knight Commander in a Fireteam, favor a static or lightly repositioning Military Orders Core that stays in or near the DZ.** He then contributes:

- **One native Regular Order**, while remaining safely positioned.
- **Crosier Counts-as** for the qualifying Military Orders Fireteam bonus level, without purchasing another basic Crosier merely to raise the level.
- **Lieutenant-order generation for external NCO** *or* **CoC succession insurance for an external fighting LT**.
- **Holomask deception** and modest close-range emergency defense, rather than deliberately hunting targets.
- A defensive/support unit alongside a **Crosier MSV2 HMG, Black Friar, defensive specialist and/or Order Sergeant FTO Hacker/Pitcher**, provided the formation meets the chart's required-choice and membership rules.

### Concrete defensive command Cores

| Construction | Points / SWC | FT level | Good use |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Crosier MSV2 HMG 24 + Crosier Commlink Specialist 13 + Crosier Paramedic 13 + Crosier basic 11 + Commander LT(+1) 24** | **85 / 1** | **FT5** | Command stays protected while the HMG performs the shooting; two cheap scoring/support bodies |
| **Same Core, Commander CoC 23** | **84 / 1** | **FT5** | Same shell, but protects Joan or another fighting Lieutenant operating elsewhere |
| **Crosier Commlink 13 + Crosier Paramedic 13 + Crosier basic 11 + Commander LT(+1) 24** | **61 / 0** | **FT4** | Low-SWC defensive command Core when ranged AROs are provided elsewhere |
| **Same compact Core, Commander CoC 23** | **60 / 0** | **FT4** | Economical succession-insurance Core with three other Crosier-equivalent members |

These examples include **actual Crosiers** to satisfy the pinned Military Orders chart's required-choice condition; **Knight Commander counts as Crosier** for the FT level but should **not be assumed to waive native required-choice requirements**. The Commander can only join the **Military Orders** Fireteam family (Core/Haris/Duo where legal), **not Order Sergeants, Crusade or Velox by implication**. Do not count a Black Friar as an extra Crosier for FT levels; check each member's real unit/counts-as equivalence.

**Useful architecture:** Commander LT (+1) in a defensive FT5 Crosier Core **plus** a Teutonic NCO Spitfire in a *separate advancing Military Orders Haris*. The Commander stays out of danger; the Teutonic converts both LT Orders and can activate its own Fireteam. Alternatively use a **solo Seraph or Holy Sepulchre NCO**, leaving the Haris slot open for other midfield demands. A **solo Tikbalang is still a strong shooter, but it has no NCO and cannot use the Commander LT Orders**.

**CoC architecture:** Joan LT in a legal advancing combat formation (or acting independently) while Commander CoC sits **in a separate protective Crosier Core**. The defensive team provides passive support and controlled AROs; Joan uses her combat tools more assertively.

### What not to do

- **Do not attach Commander to the primary advancing Knight Haris solely to fill a slot.** The one-Wound MI command piece is comparatively fragile, slower in practice than mobile Knights, and wants to remain hidden.
- **Do not put both the fighting LT and only CoC backup in the same vulnerable group.** A shared attack vector can remove the reason to pay for CoC.
- **Do not expose Commander as a routine ARO shooter to get value out of the model.** He already provides his primary value passively; choose Crosier MSV2 HMG, Black Friar or other purpose-built ranged AROs for those jobs.
- **Do not assume the five-member Core automatically has FT5 if its unit/counts-as composition differs.** Check the current chart's same-unit levels, required choice and any restrictions.

## 5. Emergency combat and defensive utility

The Commander is **not defenseless**, but that is a secondary benefit. BS13, a shotgun, Pulzar, CC20/MA2, Shock Immunity and BTS6 can punish a close-range attacker, especially one arriving in the DZ. His enhanced BS Attack makes the shotgun/Pulzar more substantial in his **active turn** than a basic Crosier's shooting. **This does not make him a safe forward gunfighter**: ARM2, W1 and no durable negative shooting MOD mean a bad exchange can cost the entire command plan. Prefer keeping LoF hidden, preserving Holomask when useful, and letting a purpose-built body watch the main lane.

As a backfield defense component, he complements—not replaces—**Raveneye E/M Minelayer, Tech-Bee, Warcor, Fugazi, a defensive Crosier ARO or a Curator**. Those inexpensive pieces buy different tools and can protect the Commander from infiltrators, parachutists and short-range breach attacks.

## Decision guide and rating

| List context | Best Commander choice | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Seraph TAG centerpiece with NCO | **24pt Lieutenant (+1 Order)**, ideally in a defensive/support Core | **A+** |
| Tikbalang centerpiece **without another NCO** | Prefer a **20pt basic Commander LT** or cheaper safe LT rather than paying for unused +1 LT Order | **Conditional; +1 not justified by Tik alone** |
| Holy Sepulchre AP HMG NCO | **24pt Lieutenant (+1 Order)** | **A+** |
| Teutonic NCO Spitfire advancing Fireteam | **24pt Lieutenant (+1 Order)** in a *separate* defensive Core | **A+** |
| Aggressive Joan v2 Lieutenant | **23pt Chain of Command** protected in separate defensive Core | **A+ conditional** |
| Aggressive Justice/Santiago/Hospitaller Lieutenant | **23pt Chain of Command** | **A** |
| Passive, protected LT without a relevant NCO | Consider **20pt basic Lieutenant**, but compare to a cheaper LT | **B− niche / C generally** |
| Commander in a primary forward assault Fireteam | Usually use a safer backfield link or leave him independent | **C** |

**Bottom line:** Take the Knight Commander for the **command engine**, then exploit the fact that he **counts as a Crosier in a cheap defensive/support Military Orders Fireteam**. **LT (+1) + NCO** is the favored economy build; **CoC behind Joan or another fighting LT** is the favored risk-management build. Skip the **ordinary Lieutenant** unless the list lacks an NCO and the particular hidden, linked backfield LT role is worth its premium over other Lieutenant options.
