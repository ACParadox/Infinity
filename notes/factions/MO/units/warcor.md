# Warcor — Military Orders (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: A+ / near-auto-include with Joan of Arc Lieutenant; B / optional 3pt ARO filler otherwise. Always select the 3pt 360º Visor profile in normal MO listbuilding.** At three points, Warcor covers side and rear deployment-zone angles against Parachutists, Combat Jump attackers and unusual enemy-DZ incursions. When Joan v1 or v2 is the active Lieutenant, **Inspiring Leadership upgrades the Warcor's personal Irregular Order into a pooled Regular Order**. That makes the Warcor an exceptional cheap cheerleader *and* a useful 360º Flash Pulse ARO.

This is the [Kestrel Warcor analysis](../../kestrel/units/Warcor.md) adapted for MO. In Kestrel, a Warcor is an inexpensive defensive nuisance with an Irregular Order. **In Joan-led MO, it is first a three-point Regular Order and second an independent backfield sentry.** It is not a must-take when all 15 trooper slots are doing more important jobs or its Order is stranded in an inactive group.

## Exact profiles

**ID 50 · AVA 1 · Irregular LI · no Fireteam access.** MOV 10–10 cm (4–4"), CC11, BS9, PH11, **WIP13**, ARM0, BTS0, W1, S2; **Journalist**. Neither version is a Specialist.

| Profile | Points / SWC | Weapons | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Option 1 — 360º Visor** | **3 / 0** | Flash Pulse, Stun Pistol, PARA CCW (-3) | **Default, virtually always**: true rear/side vision and Flash Pulse ARO against visible movement from any angle |
| Option 2 — Sixth Sense | 3 / 0 | Same | **Skip**: offers reactive defenses in narrower cases, but loses the independent 360º lookout role |

The profiles are mutually exclusive: Sixth Sense does **not** accompany 360º Visor.

## Why 360º Visor is superior in MO

**360º is the reason to buy a defensive Warcor.** With a normal 180º arc, a cheap model guarding the front often leaves a wide blind rear sector; 360º removes that *facing* vulnerability and can threaten enemies moving or attacking in visible lanes from behind or across the flank.

- **Parachutist on a deployment-zone side/back edge:** A model advancing from an unexpected side may expose itself to the Warcor's Flash Pulse even if it never enters the normal front arc. Place the Warcor so the flank/edge actually crosses its visible lanes.
- **Combat Jump or other backline incursion:** After a legal arrival, the attacker may need to move between cover, shoot support models, or pass into a protected route. The 360º Warcor watches those exposed traversals, including rear angles. It is not an automatic interdiction of a landing in Total Cover.
- **Odd-angle attacks into the DZ:** A flank attacker trying to bypass a Crosier Core, linked launcher, Sierra or other forward-facing defense may still encounter a separate Warcor's ARO.
- **Scattered rear and side threats:** The Warcor can cover more than one approach without committing a substantial defensive model or changing its facing.

**360º Visor grants vision, not omniscience:** buildings and Total Cover still block LoF; a Parachutist can select a safe arrival/route, and a Warcor does not automatically stop an airborne deployment. Use complementary, *offset* sightlines rather than parking it on an exposed rooftop opposite the enemy's strongest long-range gun.

**Sixth Sense does something else, but is not an equivalent rear sentry:** it can enable reactions to attacks targeting the Warcor from outside its LoF, counter Stealth and provide defensive MOD benefits, but does not grant general 360º LoF to react to ordinary enemy movement behind it. It does not provide Combat Instinct's immunity to Surprise Attack penalties in N5. An exotic table/matchup might favor Sixth Sense, but **do not choose it for ordinary MO listbuilding**. The lost 360º vision is a much larger practical sacrifice than its situational protections are worth.

## Flash Pulse: credible nuisance, not reliable hard ARO

Flash Pulse attacks on **WIP13**, not BS9. It is B1 in ARO, inflicts Stunned after an unsuccessful BTS Saving Roll, and does not deal normal lethal damage.

| Distance | Range MOD | Base target number |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0–8" | 0 | 13 |
| 8–24" | +3 | **16** |
| 24–48" | -3 | 10 |
| 48–96" | -6 | 7 |

A Flash Pulse win and failed BTS save can disrupt an expensive attack run: Stunned prevents declaring Attacks and imposes -3 on other Rolls other than Saving Rolls. **It does not retroactively cancel the action that caused the Stun**, and Stunned is not permanent. A W1/ARM0/BTS0 Warcor will often die to a dedicated multi-Burst shooter; its benefit is the threat of losing momentum, accepting a risky roll or spending an extra Order to clear a **three-point** defender.

**Journalist** provides +3 to allied Guts Rolls and -3 to enemy Guts Rolls within the Warcor's ZoC. Treat this as incidental positional utility, not a reason to expose it. Existing Courage and Religious interactions may reduce its impact.

## The decisive synergy: Joan of Arc Lieutenant / Inspiring Leadership

Both **Joan v1** and **Joan v2** have Inspiring Leadership. When the skill's LT condition is fulfilled—Joan is the Army Lieutenant, on the table, and not in a Null State—troopers that provide Orders gain **Regular Training and Courage**, **excluding Religious Troops**. Warcor is Irregular but *not* Religious, so **its one Order becomes a pooled Regular Order in its own Combat Group**.

| Scenario | Warcor's Order | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Joan v1 LT active** | **Regular** | Can fuel Joan, another attacker, a Fireteam or specialist in same group |
| **Joan v2 LT active** | **Regular** | Same; independent of Joan's version or weapons |
| Joan chosen as **Chain of Command**, but not Lieutenant | Irregular | Not activated merely by Joan being on the roster |
| Knight Commander / Teutonic / other LT, with no active Joan LT | Irregular | Ordinary Kestrel-like ARO filler economics |
| Joan LT off-table / in a Null State at relevant Order Count | Not converted | Do not assume the Regular conversion persists without the requirements |

**No extra Order is generated**: the original Irregular Order becomes Regular. No automatic transfer between Combat Groups occurs. The Religious exclusion is important for MO's many Knights and Crosiers; Inspiring Leadership does not convert their training merely because they are Religious, but they are already Regular on their printed profiles.

Joan has other separate Order mechanics, including Tactical Awareness. **Do not count the Warcor's converted Regular Order as an additional Lieutenant Order, Tactical Order or Command Token.** Neither Joan's LT(+1 Command Token) profile nor Strategic Deployment changes the Warcor conversion itself.

### Group placement with Joan

**Prefer the main Joan/attack Combat Group if there is room.** The Warcor contributes a pooled Order to that group while still watching the DZ; it does not have to move or expose itself to provide useful fuel. An illustration: nine otherwise Regular order-providing troopers in Group 1 plus the Warcor yield ten Regular Orders there while Joan's Inspiring Leadership is active (assuming all provide Orders at Order Count). This is a **one-Order increase to that group** over omitting the Warcor, not an extra Order beyond its own contribution.

**Group 2 is still valid**, particularly if it has its own worthwhile active-turn specialist or scoring threat. But a Group 2 Warcor does **not** feed a Group 1 Joan, TAG or attack Fireteam. Unlike the Kestrel analysis, which often favors Group 2 because of Irregular training, **Joan-led MO should place Warcor based on where its converted Regular Order is most productive**.

When Joan is not LT, the Warcor's self-only Irregular Order can occasionally reposition it, attempt Discover or perform a sacrificial probe, but it cannot freely fuel Knights or a TAG.

### Coordinated Order with Joan — minor extra

Inspiring Leadership also permits a Coordinated Order that **includes Joan**, paid with her Special Lieutenant Order and without a Command Token or additional Orders. An independent Warcor in the same Combat Group can join that Coordinated Order if the normal participant rules are met; this can reposition a backline defender while moving Joan and other unlinked troops. **Members of a Fireteam cannot participate in a Coordinated Order**, so do not claim the trick for Joan while she remains a linked team member. This is opportunistic utility, not the primary Warcor justification.

### Fragility of the conversion

Joan is an expensive, potentially exposed fighting LT. If the Inspiring Leadership LT/on-table/non-Null conditions cease to hold, future Order generation is not safely assumed to retain the Warcor's converted status. Avoid treating the 3pt Warcor as a guaranteed Regular for the whole game in an aggressive Joan plan. **This risk is smaller than the massive three-point upside while Joan's conditions are met**.

## Positioning in an MO deployment zone

The best location is **behind or offset from the primary defenses**, partly protected by Total Cover, with visibility into at least one side, rear or diagonal route an attacker would use to reach vulnerable models.

1. **Watch an AD arrival route or DZ edge**, rather than duplicating a long front-facing HMG lane. The Warcor reacts to *visible enemy actions*, not the mere fact that an enemy possesses Parachutist/Combat Jump.
2. **Offset it from the high-value pieces**—Joan's nearby support, an Engineer/Curator, Tech-Bee, Fugazi, a hacker or a linked rear Core. Ideally the attacker clearing a primary ARO must expose itself to the Warcor at a different angle/order.
3. **Protect the Order generator.** With Joan LT it is no longer just a dispensable nuisance: preserving three points also preserves one unusually cheap pooled Order at later Order Counts.
4. **Do not treat Flash as perimeter lockdown.** Add Raveneye E/M Mine/E/Marat, a linked HRL, a template defender or a Sierra where the threat requires actual damage. A determined attacker can shoot the Warcor or use unseen cover.
5. **Do not rely on only one angle.** A 360º arc addresses facing; it does not see through walls or across blocked elevation changes.

In a typical Joan/Crusade attack list, the Warcor is often best **left in the DZ**, providing the Order to the advancing team while threatening backline breaches. An offensive Warcor activation is rarely preferable to spending that Regular Order on Joan or a specialist.

## Warcor versus MO's low-cost alternatives

All costs are pinned N5.3 MO roster values, 0 SWC for the listed basic profiles.

| Trooper | Pts | Order with active Joan LT | Why choose it instead / alongside |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Warcor 360º** | **3** | **Regular** | Absolute cheapest transferable Order **with Joan**, and unusual side/rear LoF |
| Fugazi | 7 | Regular | WIP13 Flash Pulse, Mimetism (-3), mobile Repeater; excellent with MO hackers |
| Mulebot (basic) | 8 | Regular | Baggage, Repeater, Remote Presence |
| Tech-Bee | 9 | Regular | Specialist Operative, Deployable Repeater, TAGCom/GizmoKit for appropriate TAG plans |
| Crosier (basic) | 11 | Regular | Fireteam eligibility, rifle and Religious training; native Regular regardless of Joan |
| Raveneye FO/Minelayer | 12 | Regular | Specialist and preplaced E/M Mine/Cybermine, E/Marat for real area denial |

**With Joan, Warcor versus Fugazi is not mainly an Irregular-versus-Regular debate**—both supply a pooled Order. Fugazi's extra four points buy a useful Repeater, Mimetism and movement; take it for those jobs, or take both if the hacker plan and trooper slots warrant. **Without Joan**, Fugazi is usually a much stronger generic Order battery because its Order is naturally Regular.

A Raveneye has more stopping power and mission value in the DZ but costs four times as much; it is a complement or a mission-driven alternative, not a reason the 3pt Warcor's 360º guard is obsolete.

## When to include or omit

**Joan LT list: near-auto-include** if a trooper slot exists and the converted Regular Order can contribute to a productive Combat Group. It is hard to beat a **three-point pooled Regular Order with a useful 360º ARO** elsewhere in MO.

**Without Joan LT: optional**. Select it when three points buy meaningful rear/flank coverage, not if it prevents upgrading to a Fugazi, completing a legal Fireteam or buying needed specialist/Repeater coverage.

**Skip even with Joan** if the 15-trooper cap or 10-trooper group layout would displace a more useful piece; if it would be trapped in a Group 2 with no real spending opportunities; or if the list critically needs a specialist, template, mine or stronger ARO instead. Do not buy Joan *solely* to regularize a Warcor—Joan's substantial LT/attack cost needs to pay off independently.

## Ratings and final selection

| Role / configuration | Grade |
|---|---|
| **Warcor 360º with active Joan v1/v2 LT** | **A+; near-auto-include** |
| **Points per Regular Order with Joan** | **S-value** |
| 360º protection against DZ Parachutists/flank attack routes | **A+ per point** |
| Independent secondary Flash Pulse ARO | **A** |
| 360º Warcor without Joan LT | **B; filler, not a must** |
| Sixth Sense Warcor | **C / skip** |
| Surviving committed active-turn gunfire | **Poor** |

**Default: Warcor option 1 (3pt 360º Visor), preferably in Joan LT's attack Combat Group.** The 360º profile is effectively always better for the actual MO Warcor job: its rear/flank LoF gives the army an inexpensive answer to Parachutists and attacks from strange DZ angles that Sixth Sense does not replicate. With Joan Lieutenant, that defense is attached to the cheapest pooled Regular Order in the sectorial.
