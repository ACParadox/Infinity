# Crux Father-Knight — Military Orders (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: A-tier inclusion priority in MO, S-tier individual capability; preferred profile is the 46/0 FTO MULTI Marksman Rifle, usually solo.** Crux is a powerful self-sufficient midfield gunfighter, generic specialist and area-denial piece, but **less distinctive and less necessary in Military Orders than in Kestrel**. MO already has numerous excellent close/midrange Knights and stronger matching Fireteam synergies. Include Crux for its exceptional PS5 AP MMR, native +1SD, Albedo (-6), Drop Bears and Suppressive Fire—not merely because it is another elite Knight. When building a substantial linked assault team, often prefer a **41/0 Knight of Justice Forward Observer** for five fewer points, a true FO and Hospitaller Counts-as.

## Why MMR 46/0 remains excellent

Lower **PS** is better. The MMR's **PS5** AP/Shock modes supply unusual armor conversion and mission utility without spending any SWC; DA mode is B1 for heavier per-hit saves. In the normal active turn, its MULTI MMR is **B3 +1SD**: roll four dice and keep up to three. An actual **FT2** shooting bonus adds another Special Die, for five rolled and up to three kept. SD increases reliability, **not** possible successful hits or Burst.

| Gun | Active B | PS | Important distinction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Crux MULTI MMR** | **3 +1SD** | **5** | AP/Shock, DA B1; best at roughly **8–24in** |
| Crux Spitfire | 4 +1SD | 6 | Normal ammo; SWC cost; loses MMR modes |
| Knight of Justice FO MMR | 3 | 6 | True FO, Flash Pulse, BTS9; Counts-as Hospitaller |
| Hospitaller HMG | 4 | standard HMG | Much better long-range reach, cheaper model; 1.5 SWC |

**Albedo (-6)** penalizes opponents that have MSV or Marksmanship when applicable; it does **not** penalize ordinary shooters and is **not Mimetism**. Crux can counter specialized gunfighters hard, but it can still lose exchanges to ordinary shooters, Mimetism, unexpected templates and weapons outside its good range band.

Crux is unusually complete: **two Wounds / ARM5**, **D-Charges**, **Specialist Operative**, powerful **CC22/MA2/NBW/Berserk/DA PS5**, and **Drop Bears**. Nevertheless, it is Hackable HI. Albedo does not solve enemy Repeaters, hacking, E/M or direct templates.

### Solo MMR — default in Military Orders

The strongest MO use is a **second-stage independent attacker and midfield occupier**:

1. Open long lanes with an HMG, AP HMG, Tikbalang or Seraph, rather than asking the MMR to fight outside 24in.
2. Advance Crux through a safe route; remove medium-range defenders, punish armored targets with PS5 AP or close combat, and push generic specialist objectives.
3. Lay Drop Bears to constrain flank/approach routes.
4. Enter Suppressive Fire **when position and remaining Orders justify it**, covering approaches to objectives with a strong counterattack threat.

**Suppressive Fire:** using the MMR in this state gives **B3 + native 1SD**, PS5, AP or Shock, within the suppression range; it does not retain the ordinary +3 MMR range band (0 MOD up to 16in, -3 at 16–24in, none beyond 24in). It adds the normal suppressive penalty to incoming qualifying shooting. **Entering the state removes Crux from a Fireteam.** This is a principal reason not to buy two or three escorts just to make its gun better for a few Orders. Suppression also ends under the usual cancellation conditions; it is not an automatic safe turn-end state.

**Do not confuse Drop Bears with direct templates.** They place or throw deployable mines, and thrown mines do not detonate in the placement Order. Plan a screen before suppression; enemies can still use templates, non-LoF tools and flanks.

**Complementary MO packages:** Holy Sepulchre **NCO AP HMG 53/1** + Knight Commander **LT(+1) 24/0** can open lanes and efficiently spend special LT Orders, while Crux **46/0** independently occupies the midfield. An alternative long-range opener is a Tikbalang or Seraph. This is a **123-point/1 SWC command-and-attack module**, not a full list; preserve points/Orders for Trinitarians, repair/hacking protection, and other specialists. Crux has **no NCO or Tactical Awareness** itself.

## Fireteam access in MO: useful, but not automatic

**Only FTO options 4/5/6 can link.** Crux is **Wildcard, max one**, with **no Counts-as equivalence** to Crosier, Teutonic, Santiago, Order Sergeant or Hospitaller. A legal host/required-choice member is still needed:

- **Military Orders** Core/Haris/Duo: eligible Crosier, Teutonic or Santiago FTO required-choice anchor.
- **Order Sergeants** Haris/Duo: at least **one actual Order Sergeant FTO**.
- **Crusade** Core/Haris/Duo: at least **one actual Hospitaller FTO**.
- **Velox** Duo: its specified required-choice model; do not assume Crux can create a link alone.

Crux generally provides its **own +1SD**, but a mixed two-person Fireteam with one differently named anchor is normally **FT1**, not FT2. For an FT2 bonus the team needs **two qualifying matching/Counts-as members**. This matters especially when comparing KoJ FO, who **counts as Hospitaller**, with Crux, who does not. Limits are one Core, one Haris and unlimited Duos. Always validate the exact composition in Army.

|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Crux MMR + Teutonic TinBot** | **65 / 0** | **FT1** | **Best simple Duo option**: Firewall(-3), Panzerfaust, movement together; no linked SD |
| Crux MMR + Teutonic TinBot + Teutonic Specialist | 84 / 0 | FT2 | Good compact attacking Haris; two Teutonics grant linked +1SD |
| Crux MMR + two cheap Crosiers (11 + 13 Paramedic) | 70 / 0 | FT2 | Cheap FT2 but fragile/slower escorts |

### Preferred linked choice: a small Duo

**Crux 46 + Teutonic TinBot 19 = 65/0**, a two-Wound gunfighter alongside a cheap two-Wound Firewall(-3) guard. Both have 15–5 cm movement. Crux remains the main attacker; Teutonic supplies a Panzerfaust, Light Shotgun, an order/body and hacking protection.

**Caveat:** the Teutonic is Impetuous with Limited Cover. Using its independent Impetuous activation affects Fireteam membership, so do not budget the free advance *and* an intact Duo simultaneously. Crux gains **no extra SD** from this FT1 Duo, so linking is principally for safe joint movement and TinBot support. If Crux needs to use Suppressive Fire or solo movement, split intentionally.

### A three-Knight Haris is viable, but expensive

**Crux 46 + TinBot Teutonic 19 + Specialist Teutonic 19 = 84/0, FT2.** The two Teutonics supply the FT2 shooting bonus, Firewall, two Panzerfausts and another generic specialist. Crux then fires MMR **B3 +2SD**.

This is a meaningful offensive unit, not an automatic upgrade. It clusters three Hackable HI models, consumes the sole Haris, and the Teutonics' Impetuous/Limited Cover issues remain. Often a solo Crux with a cheaper independent objective unit offers better distribution of risk.

### Larger Fireteams: usually favor KoJ FO

A full assault Core or premium Crusade Haris already has good shooters and scoring options. Crux does **not** raise Hospitaller matching bonuses, and its independent SD makes some linked enhancement redundant. A **KoJ FO 41/0** instead counts as Hospitaller, is a real FO and can increase Fireteam level. In a Crusade Duo:

- **Hospitaller TinBot 27 + KoJ FO 41 = 68/0, FT2**, with the KoJ gaining the team +1SD.
- **Hospitaller TinBot 27 + Crux MMR 46 = 73/0, FT1**, with Crux using its own +1SD.

The KoJ gains stronger team synergy; Crux still carries the better gun and Albedo. With more Hospitaller-equivalent members, KoJ may help reach FT3/FT4/FT5 while Crux does not.

## Crux versus Knight of Justice FO: the key MO decision

| Characteristic | **Crux FTO MMR** | **KoJ FO MMR** |
|---|---|---|
| Points / SWC | **46/0** | **41/0** |
| BS / MOV / Wounds / ARM | 14 / 15–5 / 2 / 5 | Same |
| BTS | 6 | **9** |
| MMR PS | **5** | 6 |
| Native +1SD | **Yes** | No |
| Albedo (-6) | **Yes** | No |
| Specialist | Generic Specialist Operative | **Forward Observer**, Flash Pulse |
| Area control | **Drop Bears**, Suppressive Fire | Flash Pulse and suppression; no Drop Bears |
| CC | CC22 MA2, **NBW/Berserk**, DA PS5 | CC23 MA2, DA PS5 |
| Fireteam equivalence | Wildcard only | **Wildcard, counts as Hospitaller** |
| Extra Fireteam resilience | None specific | **Number 2** |
| D-Charges | Yes | Yes |

**When solo / gunfighting is the goal, prefer Crux.** PS5 AP, extra Special Die, Albedo and Drop Bears are a substantial advantage, particularly against armored targets and MSV/Marksmanship gunfighters. KoJ's better BTS and its five-point discount do not make it the stronger standalone shooting piece.

**When linking or completing FO objectives matters, prefer KoJ FO.** It costs five less, has BTS9, is a *true* FO (Flash Pulse, mission requirements and Targeted opportunities) and improves matching Hospitaller Fireteams. The 41/0 profile makes a more efficient **linked supporting specialist** even though it is less imposing solo.

**Other MO substitutes:** A **19/0 Teutonic Specialist** or **20/0 Montesa FTO Paramedic** offers much cheaper two-Wound scoring; **22/0 Trinitarian FO** gives infiltrating Camo; **40/0 Mendoza FTO** gives close pressure, Mimetism(-6), TinBot and Number 2. Crux should not be a 46-point purchase merely to push buttons. These pieces do not reproduce its PS5/SD shooting, but the list may need their distinct mission capability more.

## Strategic Deployment, forward MMR and Hidden Deployment

### Strategic Deployment FTO (50/0)

**Conditional team-first upgrade, not the default.** Four extra points grant the qualifying Fireteam **FD (+10 cm / ~4in)** if Crux deploys first as Fireteam Leader. Mission-granted FD may improve this benefit according to the N5.3 Strategic Deployment rule. A team with two Teutonics has compatible 15–5 cm movement and can exploit safe advanced starting positions well.

Buy this when a planned attacking Haris genuinely saves approach movement or finds better first-turn angles. Do **not** buy it for solo Crux or for a defensive Core, and do not assume an extra full Order is always saved. **Crux + two 19pt Teutonics changes from 84/0 to 88/0.**

### FD (+20 cm / ~8in) solo MMR (54/0)

Same good MMR, but costs **eight more points** than the 46/0 FTO and replaces Drop Bears with CrazyKoala. It cannot link. Worth considering for an immediate forward objective/attack geometry that saves enough Orders to justify the eight-point premium. Otherwise the 46/0 solo is simply more efficient, especially in MO's tight Order economy.

### Hidden AP SMG options (47/0 or 48/0.5)

Both have **FD (+20 cm), Hidden Deployment and Surprise Attack (-3)**, but trade the excellent PS5 MMR for **AP SMG PS7** and lose Fireteam access. Prefer **47/0 CrazyKoala** for efficiency; the **48/0.5 Armed Turret** is a separate fixed fire-angle tool, *not* a second CrazyKoala.

A hidden Crux can avoid direct opening-turn counterdeployment/attack and emerge as a durable forward threat. While Hidden at Order Count, its Regular Order is not pooled but is reserved for its revealing activation. **Reveal and act early** if possible; keeping 47–48 points and a flexible pooled Order unavailable for multiple turns is especially costly in MO.

MO also has **Trinitarians (FO 22, KHD 24)**, **Konstantinos Hidden 26** and cheaper advanced/airborne scoring paths, so Hidden Crux is much less necessary as a specialist than its price suggests. Take it only when the mission wants a particularly resilient surprise attacker, not merely a concealed console-pusher.

### Spitfire FTO (49/1.5)

**Normally skip.** The MMR is 3pts and 1.5 SWC cheaper, has the exceptional PS5/AP/DA/Shock modes and native +1SD, and covers more useful distance. MO already offers **Joan v2 AP Spitfire, Mendoza, Teutonic Spitfire NCO, KoJ Spitfires**, and strong cheaper linked guns. This is almost never where the list needs to spend SWC.

## Close combat and support cautions

Crux is a credible close-range heavy-target finisher with **DA CCW PS5**, CC22, MA2, Berserk and Natural Born Warrior. Its CC is unusually good for an otherwise complete rifle specialist. **N5.3 Berserk remains a face-to-face interaction** where relevant attacks and Dodges can oppose Crux; it is not an automatic older-edition Normal Roll trade. The Long Skill moves at most the sum of its MOV values before CC, requires a visible target at declaration, and fails if the route cannot reach contact. It is often easier to exploit **while solo** than when dragging teammates.

Avoid exposing a 46pt Hackable HI to repeaters without a plan. A protected Fireteam can use a **Teutonic / Hospitaller / Santiago TinBot Firewall(-3)** where legal, but MO can also provide **De Fersen/other hacking pressure, Trinitarian midfield Repeaters and cheap screening** outside Crux's own activation path. A two-Wound attacker is still vulnerable to coordinated templates, E/M and isolation. A Doctor can revive it, but do not automatically buy a 30pt Hospitaller Doctor plus linked escorts if the actual plan is to use Crux independently.

## Final MO selection rules

1. **Start at zero Crux**, then add the **46/0 MMR** when a self-sufficient elite **solo** shooter/specialist/area-control piece improves the army's attack plan.
2. **Pair with a long-range opener.** Sepulchre NCO AP HMG, Tikbalang, Seraph or a linked HMG covers the range where Crux is weaker; do not spend everything on 8–24in combatants.
3. **In Fireteams, prefer a simple purposeful Duo** (especially TinBot Teutonic) rather than funding a large escort solely for one more SD. A real forward Haris is viable but must justify the expense.
4. **Prefer KoJ FO in larger linked Knight teams** when FO capability, points saving and **Counts-as Hospitaller** actually raise team bonuses. Crux remains stronger when the gun itself is the reason for the slot.
5. **Take Strategic Deployment only for a coordinated turn-one forward team.** Regular FD MMR and Hidden SMG are mission-dependent experiments.
6. **Skip Spitfire by default.** The 0-SWC PS5 MMR is Crux's signature advantage.
7. **Do not import Kestrel's S-tier inclusion priority unchanged.** In MO, Crux is a powerful but optional luxury in a crowded close/midrange gunfighter category.

**Bottom line:** **S-tier profile, A-tier MO inclusion, preferred as solo 46/0.** In a Fireteam, a good Duo is reasonable; a **KoJ FO** often gives the team more for less.
