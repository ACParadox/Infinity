# Ōban — List-Building and Tactical Note

**Reference archive:** [Ōban sources, battle-report cross-references and purchasing audit](references/oban_refs.md). **Authoritative roster and Fireteams:** [Ōban N5.3 sectorial reference](../../sectorials/jsa/oban.md).

### Overview

**What Ōban does exceptionally well:** deliver high-end close-combat threats and resilient objective specialists *through* dangerous firing lanes, then keep the midfield difficult to reclaim. Its signature toolkit includes:

- **Tankō Disco Baller:** projected Eclipse to create assault routes.
- **Shikami:** self-carried Eclipse for mobile scoring and vertical access.
- **Ordinary Smoke support:** a separate way to manage visibility and enable MSV shooting.
- **Hidden Deployment/Infiltration assassins:** concealed threats that punish exposed targets.
- **Linkable durable mission pieces:** two-/three-STR specialists able to occupy and hold objectives.

This is an army for **route creation → selective assassination or scoring → durable position holding**, not a faction that must win every fight in melee.

**Concealment caveat:** These Ōban assassins have **Hidden Deployment + Infiltration, not Impersonation**:

- **Shinobu Kitsune**
- **Oniwaban**
- **Saito Tōgan**
- **Ninja**

Ōban has **no ordinary-roster Impersonation assassin** analogous to the following outside options:

- **Fiday**
- **Speculo Killer**

Ōban's assassins threaten from concealed, pre-positioned starting points; they do not bypass deployment screening with the Impersonation State.

#### Particularly strong Fireteam combinations

- **Domaru Haris — Domaru LT (+1 Order, 23/0) + Daiyōkai HMG NCO (50/1) + Tankō Disco (19/0):** unusually clean **LT-order economy → NCO gun → projected Eclipse → serious close combat**. Potent but expensive and vulnerable to hacking; not an automatic default.
- **Ōban Haris — Shikami + Karakuri + Karakuri:** unusual combination of **mobile Mimetism(-6)/Eclipse vertical scorer** with **two STR3, 0-SWC specialist anchors**. A scoring/holding package, rather than a conventional long-range gun link. One Shikami + one Karakuri as Duo is a leaner alternative.
- **Aragoto Haris — Aragoto + Kuroshi Rider + Rui Shi MSV2 Spitfire:** fast **Smoke Grenade Launcher + MSV2 shoot-through-normal-Smoke** offensive package, with a mobile FO and hacker/shotgun choices on the Aragoto slot. Large motorcycle/REM bases and terrain can be a real limitation.
- **Banzai Duo/Haris — Tankō + Miyamoto Mushashi *FTO* (optional additional Tankō):** projected Eclipse helps **deliver MA5/EXP** to the relevant target. **Only the 25/.5 Regular Mushashi FTO** is legal in this family; his cheaper Irregular profile is not.
- **Keisotsu Core/Haris with Daiyōkai HMG + Kempeitai Wildcard:** a cost-controlled **ranged anchor plus command/MSV/utility** shell that leaves the more aggressive samurai or Shikami module independent.

All five are **legal construction concepts, not free-form mix-and-match**: Ōban allows **1 Core, 1 Haris, and multiple Duos**; check each family, required source member, FTO and maximum count in the [canonical Fireteam chart](../../sectorials/jsa/oban.md#canonical-fireteams).

#### Unusual access, gaps and trade-offs

- **Eclipse and Disco:** exceptional visibility-control access for a JSA assault army:
  - **Tankō Disco Baller 19/0:** projects Eclipse with limited-use deployed equipment and possible Deactivator reactivation, **not ordinary Smoke**.
  - **Shikami:** carries Eclipse Grenades for its own movement.
  - **Rui Shi MSV2:** cannot shoot through the Eclipse created by either source.

  **Rules distinction:** Eclipse blocks even normal MSV sight.
- **Ordinary Smoke and visors:** unlike Eclipse, this supports Smoke/MSV fire:
  - **Kuroshi Rider:** Smoke GL.
  - **Garon:** Smoke GL.
  - **Yojimbo:** Smoke.
  - **Shinobu Kitsune:** Smoke.
  - **Saito Tōgan:** Smoke.
  - **Yuan Yuan:** Smoke.
  - **Rui Shi MSV2 Spitfire 23/1:** can exploit *ordinary* Smoke.
  - **Kempeitai MSV2 Shock Marksman 22/1:** can exploit *ordinary* Smoke.
  - **Lù Duān MSV1:** a separate Holoprojector/anti-Mimetism angle.

  **Rules distinction:** shooting through ordinary Smoke and blocking all sight with Eclipse are **two different game plans**.
- **Chain of Command and LT-order engine:**
  - **Kempeitai CoC 17/0:** unusually affordable LT insurance.
  - **Domaru LT (+1 Order):** supplies extra LT-order economy.
  - **Daiyōkai NCO:** can convert those command resources into attack Orders when paired with that Domaru.
  - **Keisotsu LT:** cheap alternative if the premium command shell is unnecessary.
- **Defensive tricks and speed:**
  - **Ryūken HRL:** Mimetism(-6), Decoy, Minelayer, one-use Camo and HRL for awkward ARO lanes.
  - **Aragoto BSG 13pt:** unusually cheap motorcycle mobility.
  - **Yuan Yuan:** low-cost Parachutist/Combat Jump pressure and Smoke.
  - **Garon:** Total Reaction/360º, without implying an unlimited pool of premium ranged ARO bodies.
- **Structural limits:** no standard **Impersonation**; relatively limited **premium long-/medium-range gunfighter** selection compared with shooting-focused sectorials; many of the best HI/REM/TAG pieces are **hackable**; and multiple impressive assassins/mobility specialists compete for the same Orders. Ōban wins by coordinating *one* decisive route with enough Regular Orders left to score.

**Practical identity:** start with a **vision-control plan**, **one reliable conventional shooter**, **one or two mission routes**, and **a cheap command backbone**; then add the assassin or deluxe Fireteam that serves the mission.

### Core identity — controlled aggression, not melee spam

The strongest repeated current lesson is:

> **Ōban is an aggressive midfield-control faction that uses visibility denial, durable brawlers, Hidden Deployment threats and disposable trades to manufacture favourable short-range interactions.**

The army has exceptional close combat, but melee is usually the **punishment** for reaching the right position rather than the primary plan from deployment.

A good Ōban turn often looks like:

> **open a route → deny the key firing lane → move the expensive piece into a difficult position → complete the mission / remove one key obstacle → stop before overextending.**

This is important because many Ōban pieces tempt the player to keep attacking until they die. Current experienced-player commentary repeatedly warns that the faction rewards **measured aggression**, not simply maximum aggression.

The best current lists also tend to be **less ninja-heavy than the faction fantasy suggests**. One Hidden Deployment specialist or assassin is common. Three expensive hidden assassins is a deliberate skew archetype and can starve the visible army of Orders.

## Architecture A — Domaru LT / Daiyōkai NCO assault engine

The most obvious Ōban command package is:

> **Domaru LT (+1 Order) + Daiyōkai HMG NCO + Tankō Disco Baller**

Current profiles:

- Domaru LT +1 Order: 23 / 0.
- Daiyōkai HMG NCO: 50 / 1.
- Tankō Disco Baller: 19 / 0.

All are legal in a Domaru Haris.

The interaction is simple:

1. Domaru supplies extra Lieutenant-order economy.
2. Daiyōkai NCO turns those Orders into useful HMG activity.
3. Tankō provides a way to deny dangerous LoF rather than forcing every advance through a normal gunfight.
4. Once the team reaches useful distance, its close-range fighters become more dangerous than their conventional shooting suggests:
   - **Domaru:** threatening melee/Berserk follow-through.
   - **Tankō:** dangerous monofilament counter-charge/trading.

#### Important qualification — this is an architecture, not an automatic default

Current player discussion contains a real disagreement about this package.

The pro side values:

- extra LT Orders;
- NCO conversion;
- a coherent all-HI attack team;
- Daiyōkai as the ranged opener before Domaru/Tankō take over at closer range.

The critical side points out:

- Domaru are only BS12;
- Daiyōkai is BS13;
- the package is expensive;
- buying the Domaru LT specifically pressures the list to exploit those LT Orders;
- moving the HMG forward with the close-range team can pull it out of its preferred range bands.

Therefore:

> **Use Domaru LT + Daiyōkai when you deliberately want an NCO assault engine. Do not assume it is the universally best LT shell.**

### Daiyōkai in Keisotsu — the important alternative

The Daiyōkai is also legal in a Keisotsu Fireteam.

This creates a different role:

> **Daiyōkai HMG + cheap Keisotsu utility bodies**

Advantages:

- HMG remains closer to useful firing lanes rather than being dragged deep into the midfield.
- Keisotsu provide inexpensive structure.
- the team can include Hacker / Thunderbolt / FO / Paramedic roles.
- the Domaru/Tankō assault element can remain a separate, cheaper trading package.

A useful conceptual split is:

- **Daiyōkai in Keisotsu:** ranged anchor.
- **Daiyōkai in Domaru:** NCO assault-engine gun.

## Architecture B — cheap Domaru attack package

Current Domaru Fireteams can include these members:

- **Domaru:** the required family.
- **Oyama:** eligible addition.
- **Tankō:** eligible addition.
- **Daiyōkai:** eligible addition.
- **Wildcards:** where permitted by the canonical chart.

You do **not** need to build a deluxe samurai death-star.

A cheap team such as:

> **Domaru + Disco Tankō + cheap Tankō**

already provides:

- multiple 2W HI bodies;
- visibility denial;
- Blitzen/Flammenspeer/Panzerfaust style disposable threats depending on profile;
- E/M;
- monofilament;
- strong CC.

This package is allowed to trade upward and die after doing something important.

That can be strategically better than risking 100–130 points in one Fireteam.

## Architecture C — “Shika-kuri” midfield fortress

The IGL 2025/26 “Shika-kuri” list provides the strongest evidence for a second major competitive Ōban architecture.

Its main elements were:

- Shikami;
- Karakuri Mk12;
- Karakuri E/Mitter;
- Daiyōkai HMG;
- Kempeitai CoC;
- cheap Keisotsu support;
- **2× Tankō Disco Baller**;
- **2× Ryūken HRL**.

The important lesson is not simply that those are good units.

It demonstrates that Ōban can play as a **midfield fortress**:

> **take the centre → block key LoF → survive → force the opponent to spend Orders digging out durable specialists.**

The IGL Shika-kuri list uses two complementary jobs:

- **Shikami:** need not make a heroic Turn-1 CC attack.
- **Karakuri:** need not sprint into the enemy deployment zone.

Instead, the opponent must solve multiple durable, specialist-capable bodies under difficult visibility and ARO conditions.

### Shikami + Karakuri division of labour

#### Shikami

Current strengths:

- Mimetism -6;
- Forward Deployment;
- Eclipse Grenades;
- Specialist Operative;
- Super-Jump;
- Climbing Plus;
- serious close combat;
- Assault Pistol / MULTI Rifle depending on profile.

Its primary job is to **make inaccessible positions accessible**:

- Eclipse across the dangerous lane;
- climb/jump around geometry;
- reach the objective;
- force a short-range interaction only when needed.

**Toolbox and scoring trade-off:**

- **Shikami 42/0:** exceptional self-contained scorer and CC/route-bypass piece with Forward Deployment, Eclipse, Climbing Plus, Super-Jump, Mimetism(-6), 2W and Martial Arts. Prefer it when *getting to* a dangerous, elevated objective is the problem.
- **Shikami 47/0 MULTI Rifle:** adds 5 points for a credible gun and D-Charges.
- **Karakuri 31–38/0:** prefer these when *surviving on* the objective matters more.

[Cross-faction toolbox apex/value and opposing-transcript guide](../list-building/jack-of-all-trades-toolbox-units-apex-value.md).

#### Karakuri

Current strengths:

- STR3;
- Remote Presence;
- Enhanced Immunity;
- FO on every profile;
- Panzerfaust;
- Chain Rifle;
- Mk12 / MULTI Rifle / Heavy Shotgun options.

The Karakuri are the actual **anchor** of the package.

A better framing than “Shikami with durable escorts” is:

> **Karakuri establish and hold the position; Shikami creates the movement and visibility conditions that let the team get there.**

#### Double Karakuri is legitimate

The IGL list uses multiple Karakuri, confirming that duplication is not wasted simply because both are durable specialists.

Useful pairing:

- **Karakuri Mk12** — main conventional midrange gun.
- **Karakuri E/Mitter / cheaper profile** — durable specialist and heavy-unit control piece.

## Shikami Duo / double Shikami

Current Ōban Fireteams allow up to two Shikami in an Ōban Duo/Haris.

Double Shikami gives:

- two Mimetism -6 bodies;
- two specialists;
- Eclipse;
- extreme vertical mobility;
- strong close-range attack capability.

It is legitimate, but very Order-hungry.

> **Double Shikami is a list architecture, not two good profiles casually added to a normal list.**

When comparing a costly mobility Duo to a leaner route-creation package:

- **Double Shikami:** build the main combat group to feed both; duplication adds redundancy but concentrates 84–94 points and activations in the same mission role.
- **Single Shikami:** leaves more points and Orders for other jobs.
- **Tankō Disco 19/0:** can support a single Shikami with projected Eclipse rather than a second expensive scorer.

**Shepherd and Tactical Awareness differ on when double Shikami pays**; compare these structures before committing to the Duo.

## Combat-group architecture — particularly important in Ōban

A repeated current lesson is that Ōban's best models are often **Order hungry**.

Therefore:

> **Group 1 should contain the pieces that actually need to move. Group 2 should contain pieces that create value without demanding many Regular Orders.**

#### Good Group 1 pieces

- Shikami;
- Karakuri;
- Daiyōkai;
- Domaru assault team;
- O-Yoroi;
- active Rui Shi/Aragoto package;
- main mission specialists.

#### Good Group 2 pieces

- Ryūken HRL;
- Warcor;
- Robio / cheap REMs;
- Pangguling;
- Yuan Yuan, which partly self-fund through Impetuous/Irregular structure;
- possibly Yojimbo when the pool is designed around his Irregular/Impetuous economy.

The IGL Shika-kuri list demonstrates the combat-group split: the 10-Regular main group contains the expensive active pieces, while the small secondary group contains:

- **2× Ryūken HRL:** low-Order reactive pressure.
- **Warcor:** inexpensive secondary-group support.

### Redundancy versus Order economy

Current lists demonstrate that duplication is normal:

- 2× Disco Tankō;
- 2× Ryūken HRL;
- multiple Karakuri;
- sometimes 2× Shikami.

But current list critique also repeatedly cuts the **second** duplicate when the list becomes too elite and the Order pool is too small.

A useful rule:

> **Duplicate pieces that remain valuable without consuming many Orders more freely than active attackers.**

Good low-Order duplicates:

- Ryūken HRL;
- Disco Tankō in separate useful structures.

More demanding duplicates:

- Shikami;
- expensive Karakuri attack profiles;
- Oniwaban;
- premium bike modules.

## Visibility control — the faction's real delivery system

Ōban has several different visibility tools:

- Tankō Disco Baller;
- Shikami Eclipse Grenades;
- Smoke from Yojimbo;
- Smoke from Kuroshi;
- Smoke from Yuan Yuan;
- Garon Smoke Grenade Launcher.

The main tactical use is **delivery**, not only shooting through Smoke with an MSV piece.

Typical sequence:

1. deny or block the important LoF;
2. move HI/specialist safely into the midfield;
3. reach the objective/corner/room;
4. Force interactions at uncomfortable ranges for the opponent, using whichever threat holds the relevant ground:
   - **Domaru:** short-range assault and close-combat pressure.
   - **Tankō:** short-range trades and monofilament deterrence.
   - **Shikami:** mobile Eclipse-backed approach and objective pressure.
   - **Karakuri:** durable specialist presence and midrange control.

Ōban has several distinct visibility plans:

- **Rui Shi:** a classic Smoke/MSV ranged attacker.
- **Tankō Disco:** projected Eclipse that denies sight even to normal MSV.
- **Shikami:** personal Eclipse that also denies normal MSV.
- **Yojimbo:** ordinary Smoke source that can support MSV2 shooting.
- **Kuroshi Rider:** another ordinary Smoke source that can support Rui Shi MSV2.

A screen that enables an objective or CC delivery is often more valuable than a smoke-shooting duel. [Global N5.3 vision-control tiers and counterarguments](../list-building/vision-control-units-apex-and-value.md).

## Domaru threat range — use Dodge and positioning, not only Berserk

Current player discussion emphasizes that Domaru control more space than their direct Berserk range suggests.

A common sequence is:

1. use Dodge movement to improve position;
2. preserve the model behind a corner/covering piece;
3. threaten Berserk or direct CC on the next Order/turn.

Therefore a Domaru standing near an objective can be valuable even if it never immediately charges.

The opponent must respect the **future cost** of entering that area.

## Tankō Disco Baller — closest thing to structural glue

Current Tankō profiles are cheap 2W HI with strong disposable weapons and monofilament melee.

The 19-point Disco profile is especially useful because it compresses:

- a durable Fireteam body;
- visibility control;
- Blitzen;
- close-range lethal threat;
- Deactivator.

Useful roles:

- escort Domaru/Daiyōkai;
- deliver Musashi in Banzai;
- add a second visibility-control axis elsewhere in the list;
- sit cheaply in a team without requiring Orders itself.

Taking **two Disco Tankō** is a legitimate current pattern when the rest of the list still has enough Orders.

**N5.3 vision-control evidence and cost:** A legal **19/0 Tankō Disco Baller + Deactivator** Fireteam can project an Eclipse screen over a gunline, enabling different jobs:

- **Domaru:** approach for a close-combat assault.
- **Miyamoto Mushashi:** approach for a close-combat assault.
- **Karakuri:** reach and hold a scoring position.

Shepherd argues that projected Eclipse solves the hardest part of melee delivery. The **MetaChemistry #153 (October 2026) winner, playing Vanilla JSA**, credits the Disco but reports substantial Orders spent reloading it. This is a powerful route-building tool, **not free/repeatable Eclipse every Order**. [Global apex/value comparison](../list-building/vision-control-units-apex-and-value.md).

## Banzai — Musashi delivery package

Current Banzai:

- requires Tankō;
- may include **Miyamoto Mushashi FTO only**;
- FTO Musashi counts as Tankō.

A compact package is:

> **Tankō Disco Baller + Musashi FTO**

The point is not Fireteam shooting bonuses.

It is a delivery system:

1. Tankō blocks/denies the dangerous lane.
2. team advances.
3. Musashi reaches the position where CC25 / MA5 / NWI actually matters.

A third Tankō can create a Haris and add another disposable ranged threat.

Do not use the normal 21-point Irregular Mushashi profile for this; it is not Fireteam legal in current Ōban.

## Aragoto / Kuroshi / Rui Shi speed module

Current Aragoto Fireteams may contain:

- Aragoto;
- Kuroshi Rider;
- one Rui Shi.

### Full tool Haris

> **Kuroshi + Aragoto KHD + Rui Shi MSV2**

This gives:

- Smoke GL;
- MSV2;
- fast Repeater projection;
- KHD;
- specialist coverage;
- extreme movement.

A tactically clever hacking sequence is:

1. activate Aragoto KHD + Rui Shi.
2. Rui Shi moves its Repeater into the enemy hacker's ZoC.
3. Aragoto remains in a safer position.
4. Aragoto attacks through the Rui Shi's Repeater.

The main limitation is **table geometry**. Three large motorcycle/REM silhouettes can be extremely awkward on dense tables.

Treat this as a terrain-selected package.

### Cleaner bike option

> **Kuroshi + Aragoto Spitfire Duo**

This retains speed, Smoke and useful shooting while being much easier to maneuver.

### 13-point Aragoto Boarding Shotgun — sleeper profile

This profile is useful as:

- a cheap Regular Order;
- fast flank attacker;
- disposable close-range piece;
- corner guard;
- mobile alternative to another static Keisotsu.

It is one of the faction's better low-cost profiles that is easy to overlook.

## Kuroshi versus Yojimbo

Both provide speed and visibility control, but they solve different problems.

### Motorized Yojimbo

Best when the list wants:

- Koala pressure;
- disposable close assault;
- fast Smoke;
- aggressive trading.

His major strategic risk is **overspending Orders** after he has already accomplished enough.

### Foot Yojimbo

More table-dependent:

- smaller silhouette;
- Stealth;
- can go Prone;
- easier to maneuver through cramped terrain.

But slower and lacking the bike's defensive benefits.

### Kuroshi

Best when the list wants:

- Smoke GL;
- WIP14 Forward Observer;
- D-Charges;
- rapid scoring;
- Dogged mission pressure.

Decision rule:

> **Need disposable violence/Koala → Yojimbo. Need fast mission work + Smoke → Kuroshi.**

## Hidden Deployment — one scalpel is often enough

Current competitive/player material cautions against automatically stacking all the costly Hidden Deployment assassins:

- **Shinobu Kitsune**
- **Oniwaban**
- **Ninja**

The visible Order pool still needs to function.

Too many Hidden Deployment pieces can create an apparently high model count but a weak visible Order pool.

### Ninja KHD — generic competitive hidden piece

A strong default hidden role because it can:

- remain Hidden until needed;
- score;
- punish enemy hackers that commit into the HI plan;
- protect expensive hackable troops indirectly;
- lay a mine;
- appear late without consuming early Orders.

For a generic tournament list, this may be the best **single hidden utility piece**.

### Oniwaban — assassination / latent threat

The normal Oniwaban is still excellent.

Do not evaluate it only by Turn-1 kills.

An unrevealed Oniwaban can:

- force cautious deployment;
- make a TAG/HI/LT preserve escape routes;
- threaten a late-game assassination;
- spend zero Orders until the attack window appears.

Sometimes remaining Hidden for two turns is correct.

### Shinobu — premium specialist assassin, not an automatic upgrade

Shinobu offers exceptional infiltration reliability, MA5 and Smoke, but costs significantly more.

Use when the mission/matchup rewards:

- premium assassination;
- Smoke on the infiltrating piece;
- extremely reliable infiltration;
- CC25/MA5 quality.

Do not take her merely because she is “the best ninja.”

### Hidden Lieutenant options

Hidden Lieutenant options are expert- or matchup-specific rather than generic defaults:

- **Shinobu LT**
- **Oniwaban LT**

They can be attractive in missions where hiding the Lieutenant is unusually valuable, but create command-economy and Loss-of-Lieutenant risks.

## Ryūken Unit-9

### HRL — first defensive copy is easy to justify

Current profile combines:

- HRL;
- Mimetism -6;
- Minelayer;
- Decoy;
- one-use Camouflage;
- mine pressure.

The key advantage is not only the gun.

It creates **deployment ambiguity** and forces the opponent to solve marker/mine positions before freely moving.

### Two HRLs

The IGL list uses two, confirming that duplication is legitimate.

Double Ryūken works particularly well in Group 2 because they can influence the table without consuming many Orders.

However, the second Ryūken is a common cut when the main active group is underfunded.

Decision rule:

> **First Ryūken HRL: easy inclusion. Second: excellent if the main Order economy is already healthy.**

### Ryūken KHD

Less universally attractive than the HRL, but useful when the list needs another midfield specialist/hacker rather than more passive defence.

**Decoy/Minelayer as a force multiplier:** Ryūken markers can tax enemy Discover and protect a genuine HRL ARO, but **fake Decoy markers provide neither independent AROs nor Orders** and are not a way to add a Fireteam member. [Global force-multiplier tiers and practical counters](../list-building/force-multipliers-special-abilities-apex-value.md).

## Karakuri — stronger than the profile initially looks

Karakuri are not flashy conventional attackers, but the combination of:

- STR3;
- Enhanced Immunity;
- Remote Presence;
- FO on every profile;
- multiple direct/disposable weapons;

makes them unusually expensive to remove efficiently.

They are especially useful when the expected opponent relies on:

- Guided;
- MSV shooting;
- conventional damage trading;
- trying to clear the midfield with a limited number of premium attacks.

The practical value is **Orders-to-remove**, not only raw shooting.

Current useful profiles include:

- 9-point AP Rifle LT;
- 10-point Combi LT;
- Hacker with CrazyKoala / ECM Hacker;
- FO;
- Paramedic;
- Commlink specialist;

## Lieutenant hierarchy

### 1. Keisotsu LT — safest cheap baseline

Current options include a 9-point AP Rifle LT and a 10-point Combi LT.

Take this when the Lieutenant does not need to participate in the plan.

The saved points are often more valuable than a premium command piece.

An identical or similar Keisotsu shell also prevents the LT from being completely trivial to identify.

### 2. Domaru LT +1 Order — NCO economy LT

23 points.

Take when:

- Daiyōkai NCO is central;
- the Domaru team itself is expected to advance;
- extra LT Orders will reliably become useful actions.

Do not take merely because +1 Order is intrinsically good.

### 3. Oyama LT — active combat command

28 points.

Oyama provides:

- Tactical Awareness;
- WIP14;
- 2W;
- strong E/M/CC capability;
- Domaru/Ōban/Keisotsu access.

Use when Oyama is already part of the active package and the list wants the LT itself to contribute materially.

### 4. Kuroshi LT — mobile mission command

26 points, no SWC.

Useful when:

- mission scoring rewards a fast specialist;
- the list already wants Kuroshi;
- the player accepts a mobile/exposed LT.

### 5. O-Yoroi LT — TAG architecture

Same 78-point chassis as the non-LT O-Yoroi.

Use when the list is already built around the TAG.

Pair naturally with:

- Kempeitai CoC;
- Tokusetsu Engineer + Yáozăo;
- cheap support/order infrastructure;
- visibility-control pieces.

Important current rule: O-Yoroi itself does **not** have normal Ōban Fireteam access.

### 6. Shinobu / Oniwaban LT — mission-specific expert tricks

Use only when the mission specifically rewards an exceptionally difficult-to-locate LT and the list can tolerate the hidden command structure.

## Units that are easy to overvalue

### Saito Tōgan

Still a capable assassin and Smoke source, but squeezed by:

- cheaper Oniwaban for killing;
- Ninja for mission/hacking utility;
- other Ōban Smoke/visibility sources.

Take when his specific combination of Hidden Deployment + Smoke + EXP CC is needed.

### Too many Hidden Deployment assassins

A thematic but potentially Order-starved Hidden Deployment package stacks:

- **Shinobu Kitsune**
- **Oniwaban**
- **Multiple Ninjas**

That combination risks leaving a weak visible Order pool.

One hidden piece is often enough unless the mission/list is specifically built around information asymmetry.

### Deluxe all-samurai Fireteam

A 100+ point all-samurai Fireteam can combine:

- **Domaru**
- **Oyama**
- **Daiyōkai**
- **Tankō**

It can be powerful, but it also:

- exposes multiple expensive models to the same hacking/area-control problem;
- ties too many points to one movement vector;
- can force the HMG forward out of its ideal role;
- makes a single bad approach disproportionately costly.

Small teams and independent modules are often healthier.

#### Disagreements to preserve, not flatten

- **Rui Shi versus Daiyōkai as main gun:**
  - **Rui Shi:** Shepherd prefers its Smoke-shooting versus Mimetism/TR targets because MSV2 changes the FtF odds.
  - **Daiyōkai:** greater durability and an NCO engine; more suitable when extra LT Orders and armor-fighting are priorities.
- **Shikami single versus Duo:**
  - **Single Shikami 42/0:** preferred by several hosts to keep 84–94pts of mobility from consuming the same Order pool.
  - **Double Shikami:** Dante calls the Duo *promising* under revised link rules, but had not committed to using it in tournaments. It remains a deliberate skew, not an auto-include.
- **Musashi Banzai versus other samurai:** melee damage is rarely the limiting factor; **delivery** is.
  - **Miyamoto Mushashi FTO:** Shepherd/Jordan view it as impressive but sometimes redundant. The Krug 2026 winner instead used Musashi for scenario objectives behind Disco, not just kills; the example is **Vanilla JSA**, so translate only the role to legal Ōban Banzai.
  - **Tankō 19pt Disco:** the route-creation partner making Musashi's delivery viable.
  - **Domaru:** an alternate close-range trading body, reducing the need to pay for deluxe CC.
- **Ōban versus Shindenbutai:** 2025 Shepherd/Jordan call them **very close**, with Ōban better able to dodge fixed hacking routes using Hidden threats and more convincing static ARO, Shinden with more modular high-tech tools. Both factions still field hackable REM/HI; **Ōban is not hacking-immune**.
- **Static ARO investment:**
  - **Garon 16pt Smoke-GL TR:** Tactical Awareness likes it as a cheap emergency trick, but BS11/STR1 cannot reliably hold elite attackers.
  - **Ryūken HRL:** a second copy can be overkill if it displaces needed mission specialists.
- **Two O-Yoroi or a midfield center:**
  - **O-Yoroi TAG(s):** straightforward MULTI HMG pressure and Tactical Awareness.
  - **Shikami:** a mobile scorer in the alternative route-denial army.
  - **Karakuri:** its durable objective-holder.
  - **Ryūken:** its defensive ARO/area-denial piece.
  Neither style dominates all missions, and neither is a roster requirement.

## Three strong starting archetypes

### A. Cheap backbone + independent monsters

Start with:

- cheap Keisotsu LT;
- useful Keisotsu Hacker/Thunderbolt/FO/Paramedic mix;
- possibly Daiyōkai HMG in the Keisotsu team;
- one Shikami;
- one Hidden piece;
- one Ryūken HRL;
- Yojimbo or Kuroshi depending mission.

This is the least structurally fragile way to learn current Ōban.

### B. Domaru NCO assault engine

Start with:

- Domaru LT +1;
- Daiyōkai HMG NCO;
- Disco Tankō;
- Kempeitai CoC;
- cheap support/order infrastructure;
- one Hidden mission/assassination piece.

This is the cleanest “samurai assault” architecture.

### C. Midfield fortress / Shika-kuri

Start with:

- Shikami;
- Karakuri Mk12;
- second Karakuri;
- 1–2 Disco Tankō;
- 1–2 Ryūken HRL;
- cheap LT/backbone;
- enough Regular Orders in Group 1 to feed the elite midfield block.

Play to **occupy and survive**, not to alpha-strike everything.

### Priority army-list configurations

**A. Balanced three-module team (best complete linked example — #109).** Start from **Keisotsu LT/Hacker/Thunderbolt/Yuriko FTO Core**, **two Karakuri Duo** (Mk12 gun + cheaper E/Mitter/FO body), and **Kuroshi FO + Aragoto + Rui Shi MSV2 Haris**. Assign their jobs *before* purchasing every expensive model: **Core protects order/specialist economy**, **Karakuri hold a central objective**, **bike Haris crosses a flank through ordinary Smoke and gives MSV2 fire**, while a **Ryūken/Hidden piece** taxes opponents. This is an **evidence-backed shell**, not a claim that those units alone form a legal 300-point list without an Army export/check. On dense tables substitute a **Kuroshi/Aragoto Duo** and spend the freed Haris/points on another objective route.

**B. Cheap four- or five-model Keisotsu shell + autonomous specialists.** Use the **#236 four Keisotsu** or **#069 five Keisotsu/Kempeitai** model examples as two **different** configurations. Add **Shikami**, **Ninja KHD**, **Kuroshi**, **Lù Duān** and optionally **Tankō Disco** where the mission needs vertical scoring, hacker access, mine-screening or projected Eclipse. #236's **3–2** is a single player's tournament account, not proof that these choices outperform every samurai Core.

**C. Elite midfield fortress is a *legal hypothesis*, not a confirmed linked deployment.** The **Shikami + two Karakuri Ōban Haris** is legal and strategically credible, but [#099](../battle-reports/091-100-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) only shows the three models and a commentator saying **“Haris or Duo plus single—we'll see”**; it does **not prove the Haris was formed**. [#148](../battle-reports/141-150-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) likewise discusses **two Shikami** and possible Tankō/Musashi or Tankō/Domaru links *as guesses*, not confirmed simultaneous teams. Retain the fortress/double-Shikami options as **test ideas**, not battle-tested exact team memberships.

### Legality and mission guardrails from the reports

- [#105](../battle-reports/101-110-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) includes **two O-Yoroi TAGs** and a **probable two Tankō + Yojimbo** linked movement; **two TAGs are not a certified Duo**, and **Yojimbo cannot join Banzai under the current chart**. Do **not** recommend Tankō + Yojimbo as an N5.3 Fireteam on this evidence. The actual **Tankō + Mushashi FTO Banzai** remains a separate *legal* delivery concept.
- [#148](../battle-reports/141-150-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) reports **Ōban 8–2 Qapu Khalqi** in Area of Interest against a **QK list with no Hacker**. Attribute the lesson to **specialist/hacking access and mission context**, not to a confirmed double-Shikami link or universal matchup superiority.
- [#236](../battle-reports/231-240-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) also documents **Hardlock failure vs Shinden's Hatamoto/Raiden** and a severely unfavorable **open-table Zeta/sniper Cutthroat** game. A fast melee/skirmish list still needs a **long-range/anti-ARO lane answer, redundant WIP specialists and enough Regular Orders**. Prioritize those ahead of an extra premium assassin.
- [#164](../battle-reports/161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md) reports an **opposing** JSA **Domaru five-person Core, double Shikami and Musashi + two Tankō** in a Haqq tournament retrospective. These are **Ōban-compatible families**, but the opponent's exact team declarations and game-era profiles are not independently confirmed. Do not rebrand them a Vanilla JSA Core or use them as proof that all combinations started linked.
