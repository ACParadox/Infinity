## Overview

**What Vanilla Nomads does exceptionally well:** it can combine elite hackers, immediate midfield control, extremely cheap Smoke/Eclipse and trading packages, dangerous independent shooting, and a wide choice of specialists in the *same* roster. The payoff is breadth and redundancy, **not** the full linked-gun bonuses or AVA of every Nomad sectorial.

### Particularly strong or unusual Fireteams

**Vanilla has no Core, only one Haris and two Duos.** That is a major constraint compared with the three Nomad sectorials; **FTO labels alone do not create a Fireteam**.

- **Nomads Fireteam I — Gecko + Evader Duo:** a self-funding Tactical Awareness TAG with a repairing/specialist partner; select a legal Evader Engineer and confirm the profile.
- **Nomads Fireteam I — Taskmaster + Morlock(s):** a compact FT Master/warband Order-conversion package when the current chart and chosen profiles qualify; it cannot borrow Bakunin's unrestricted Taskmaster team composition.
- **Nomads Fireteam II — Mobile Brigada + Marspider:** mixes a tough fighting/command anchor and Engineer/specialist support without assuming a Marspider can join Gecko in Fireteam I.
- **Compact alternatives:** a **Gecko + Gecko** Duo or other eligible Fireteam I gun/support pairing can be worth more than using the one Haris slot simply to maximize body count.

### Unusual access, gaps and trade-offs

- **Very broad sight-line toolkit:** **Morlock Smoke + Intruder MSV2** for one-sided shooting, **Chimera Eclipse** for blind assault, **Jazz/Interventor HD+ White Noise** against visors and a legally equipped **Kulak/Lobo/Prowler/Shifta Disco Baller** for projected Eclipse. These are distinct setups, not one combined profile.
- **Command resilience:** **Kulak or Shifta Chain of Command**, with cheap **Puppet Master Counterintelligence** if chosen. The same role on several profiles is not an instruction to buy all of them.
- **Heavy options:** standard **Szalamandra, Gator and Gecko** TAG choices plus **Go-Pod VH**; **Lizard** belongs to the separate Vipera Reinforcement chart, while **Lizard and Iguana are not standard vanilla TAGs**.
- **Sectorial access does not transfer:** **only one Moran**; no Corregidor two-Moran start, no Tunguska Hollow Men Core, and no automatic **Vostok/Tsyklon FTO Fireteam**. Check the [vanilla Fireteams I/II and limits](../../sectorials/nomads/nomads.md#canonical-vanilla-fireteam-chart) before assuming synergy.

## One-minute verdict

**Identity:** Combine **elite hackers + repeaters + control tools + a competent direct gun** across three Nomad ships. Pick one main way to win fights; use hacking to clear lanes, force awkward routes and deny counterattack—not as a substitute for scoring or every form of killing.

- **Strengths:** Interventors, Mary, Jazz, Zoe/Π-Well; Moran/Zero/Spektr and Pitcher/Disco Baller projection; White Noise and smoke/Eclipse options; durable or efficient TAG and REM guns; cheap screening orders; many mission specialists.
- **Constraints:** one standard Moran (not two); scarce long-range burst and high-SWC firepower if Guided consumes the budget; no vanilla Core; 2026 Fireteam membership is selective; concentrated hackers may share one vulnerability to enemy killer hackers, stealth/non-hackables or broken repeaters.
- **Practical rule:** *build the objective plan and ordinary gunfighting answer first; then buy sufficient hacking network to improve those answers*. You can pivot into full hacking, but it requires an explicit anti-Stealth and non-hackable plan.

**Different vision-control paths:**

- **Morlock Smoke + Intruder MSV2:** engage through normal Smoke.
- **Jazz or Interventor HD+ + Repeaters:** project White Noise against visors and Marksmanship.
- **Chimera + Pupniks:** deliver close-combat pressure with Eclipse.
- **Kulak, Lobo, Prowler or Shifta Disco Baller profile:** cross a protected lane without winning the gunfight.

Do not assume one smoke/White Noise/Eclipse template enables all the others; choose the one that lets your actual scorer act. Hacking grids cost setup Orders, and cheap crossings can sometimes score more efficiently. [Whole-game vision-control analysis](../list-building/vision-control-units-apex-and-value.md).

## Hacking and board-control sequencing

1. **Before deployment:** specify which enemy activation path your repeater/Koala/mine controls; do not scatter every tool simply because it can be placed. Screen the deployment zone against airborne or impersonation threats.
2. **Establish a safe first foothold:** a *single* Moran AVA1 is strong but no longer covers two flanks by himself. Fill the other side with Zero mine/repeater, Spektr hidden deployment, a mobile Disco Baller/Pitcher, Pupniks, RacerBot, or a conventional guard. **Moran has Repeater options; choose that profile deliberately.**
3. **Clear the repeater approach:** direct shooting, smoke/Eclipse, White Noise or a sacrificial attack can protect the unit placing a repeater. A pitcher that costs two risky Orders to deploy may be worse than moving a forward-deployed asset already nearby.
4. **Select the correct effect:** Oblivion / isolation can stop a high-value operative; immobilization can set up a safe shot; Spotlight can improve subsequent direct BS attacks **even if a missile is never launched**. Do not automatically Guided every target.
5. **End each turn defensively:** move exposed hackers away from easy KHD lines, mind friendly Repeater exposure, and ensure opponents cannot eliminate all controllers with one flank penetration.
6. **Plan for Stealth and non-hackables:** a 2025 Tactical Awareness speaker said vanilla lacked Combat Instinct. **That statement is now obsolete:** N5.3 Vanilla **Territorials** carry Combat Instinct and are non-hackable (see current roster). They give an alternative screen/gun/specialist without assuming an entire repeater network can force AROs through Stealth. Check precise rules interactions rather than inferring automatic hacking AROs.

**Useful pairing decisions:**
- **Interventor + Jazz:** WIP15/BTS9 strategic hacker backed by Pitcher-equipped Jazz; Interventor LT is efficient but risks commander exposure or hacking; protect against Loss of Lieutenant. Interventor KHD/FastPanda is another tool, not a replacement for long-range guns.
- **Mary + Zoë/Π-Well:** Mary forward presence, Mimetism and climbing create projection angles; Zoe provides Engineer/hacker and Π-Well another active piece. Expensive together; only take both if their different jobs are needed.
- **Kulak KHD + Disco Baller:** forward deployable-hacking reach on a legal Duo/Haris-compatible body; Kulak **CoC** is insurance if investing in a vulnerable Lieutenant.
- **Cheap Morlocks / Chimera:** smoke or Eclipse, contact trading, lane denial and distraction. Irregular/Impetuous Orders are **not freely fungible Regular Orders**; tally usable orders by combat group.

**Vertigo is a conditional Guided payoff, not a compulsory hacking centerpiece:**

- **Vertigo Guided 16/1.5:** converts marked targets into missile pressure.
- **Interventor, Jazz and Mary:** hackers; **Pitchers, Zero and one vanilla Moran (AVA1):** network delivery, unlike Corregidor's two Morans.
- **Tactical Awareness S3E26:** contrasts missile-first hacking saturation with diversified direct REM/gun plans.

A network can shape the enemy's movement and enable ordinary Targeted attacks even if no missile fires. Preserve an independent gun and scoring plan if the network is shut down. [Indirect-fire analysis](../list-building/indirect-fire-speculative-guided-apex-value.md).

**Engineer architecture by legal role:**

- **Clockmaker WIP15 + Zondbot:** compact long-reach repair insurance.

Repair positioning and a safe retreat matter more than merely including an Engineer.

- **Lizard and Iguana:** not standard Vanilla TAG choices.
- **Lizard in Vipera Reinforcement:** available only through that separate chart/mode.
- **TAG-Treiber pilots:** separate purchases, not free extra pilots or automatic TAG loadouts.
- **Other CJC/Bakunin tactics:** useful comparisons, but do not import their legality.

## Vehicle — Go-Pod

**Current vanilla Go-Pod (AVA1) alternatives:**

- **33pt/0SWC MULTI Rifle + E/Mitter with Go-Bot.**
- **32pt/1.5SWC Heavy Rocket Launcher + AP SMG / BS Attack (Shock) with Go-Bot.**

Both are fast aerial VH but differ in weapon and SWC commitment; neither avoids the chassis's movement/cover exposure.

- **Why take it:** high mobility, Super-Jump angles and a secondary Go-Bot body allow fast objective flanking, asymmetric attack lanes and forked short-range threats. The Dante transcript highlights the speed and defensive modifier package but warns not to mistake it for a durable TAG.
- **HRL variant:** ranged threat/area template, but 1.5 SWC competes directly with Vertigo and main long-range guns; if it only gets one exposed attack, points are wasted.
- **MULTI Rifle + E/Mitter:** no SWC and more useful to pressure hackable armor or attack a vulnerable flank at practical ranges; must survive the approach, where it cannot rely on a conventional defensive fireteam.
- **Opposing advice — strongly negative (Robert Shepherd's [Tunguska video, ~60–69 min](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A2eKdenbcsw&t=3600s), transcript verified):** The same spectacular jump movement gives an **extremely high ceiling but a poor floor**. No Cover + ARM2 + S7 makes return fire lethal and concealment difficult; enemy repeaters can Isolate/Immobilize it, and a Go-Pod stranded in the DZ contributes little. It is a **finisher after other pieces remove AROs and hostile hacking**, not a reliable Turn-1 hard-ARO suppressor. Shepherd questioned whether that setup cost makes it unnecessary once your real attackers have won. Contrast this with its enthusiasts, who value its unexpected angles, Go-Bot forks, and potential late-turn sweep. **Decision:** pick it only if your list already contains an anti-hacking plan and another first-contact gun. Compare to an inexpensive mobile specialist if it merely cleans up a won flank.
- **Unresolved:** Shepherd was discussing Tunguska, where linked Hollow Men pitchering can help neutralize opposing hackers before the Go-Pod attacks; **Vanilla cannot import that sectorial Core or its bonuses**. A vanilla setup is therefore no easier, although Mary/Interventor/Jazz/Zero can replace parts of the protection package.

## Flexible specialists and safety nets

- **Moran** — best early midfield disruption, but only **AVA1** in vanilla. Pick Repeater vs other variants consciously; plan the uncovered flank.
- **Zeros (AVA2) / Spektr (AVA1)** — hidden/camo mines, sensors/targets and objectives; **Spektr's Hidden Deployment** gives a last-resort revelation/mission tool and a surprise network anchor, while Zero is the cheaper repeatable midfield screen.
- **Territorials** — **N5.3-era non-hackable, Tactical Awareness, Combat Instinct** Engineer/gun/specialist options. Strong antidote to building a list where *everything* is vulnerable to the same hacking counterplay. Tactical Awareness gives a separate way to spend activation resources.
- **Vigilantes** — non-hackable, Camouflage, Counterintelligence and forward-deployed surveillance/technical profiles; useful if the opponent's first-turn plan exploits your vulnerable hacker castle.
- **Shifta Chain of Command** / **Kulak CoC** — lieutenant continuity with alternative utility; choose based on positioning/Fireteam and SWC. Do not pay twice for identical backup without a reason.
- **Puppetactica** — Multiple remote bodies, FO option and resilient trading/control depending on target selection; useful when the attacker roster is gun-light. **Robert Shepherd's transcript (~54–55 min)** particularly values cheap two-structure FO/shotgun PuppetBots as **ablative DZ pickets**: enemy Orders are consumed killing them while the PuppetMaster stays safe, and a surviving bot can still attack or push buttons. A cheap PuppetMaster also supplies Counterintelligence. PuppetBot coordinated movement **is not a Fireteam bonus**; avoid treating PuppetBot activations as new independent Regular Orders. Their degraded state matters after taking damage.
- **Intruder MSV2** — a conventional gun answer to optical opposition, complemented by Morlock smoke; expensive enough to compete with a TAG/REM attack plan.
- **Hellcat/Bearcat** — air arrival or unexpected lane pressure; bring when the opponent can hide from the hacking net or cannot adequately screen its DZ.

**High-efficiency specialist toolboxes:**

- **Mary Problems 27/.5:** HD+KHD, Pitcher, Forward Deployment, Mimetism and Climbing Plus provide cyber versatility, not an elite direct gun.
- **Evader 35/1.5:** frontline Engineer plus AP offense where the selected team/profile is legal.
- **Moran 15/.5:** cheap FO/Repeater/Koala coverage, but **vanilla AVA1** differs from Corregidor AVA2.

Several separate specialists with different Orders/routes can beat one expensive multifunction model. [Toolbox comparison and opposing transcripts](../list-building/jack-of-all-trades-toolbox-units-apex-value.md).

## Explicit disagreements and tests

1. **Is vanilla stronger than the sectorials?** *For:* take the best hacking personalities, Moran, smoke, TAGs and unique Corregidor/Tunguska active pieces together (Tactical Awareness E26). *Against:* you lose specialized Fireteams, Bakunin Observance, Riot Grrls and other sectorial-exclusive tools. **Test:** name one mission job achieved only by this exact mixed roster; otherwise consider the sectorial bonuses.
2. **Guided as win condition vs deterrent?** *For:* excellent board control when the opponent cannot avoid your repeaters. *Against:* 1.5SWC missile + deployment setup + multiple hacks often underperform one efficient direct attack; stealth/non-hackables resist the intended plan. **Test:** tally expected Orders per killed/isolated target, not just theoretical hacker coverage.
3. **Szalamandra vs Gator?** One efficient HRMC long gun and BTS9 vs Gator’s TacAware/NCO, AP mine, chain templates and 0.5 SWC saving. Podcast panel disagreed on whether Gator extras are useful enough. **Test:** write which specific turn uses NCO and mines; if none, the Szalamandra may be cleaner.
4. **Gecko duo vs single TAG + support?** Double-Gecko fans value independent Tactical Awareness Orders and two armored threats; critics see significant point investment in BS13 midrange bodies with shared positioning tax. **Test:** map two separate objective approaches; if both TAGs must take the same path, value may fall.
5. **All-REM vs two conventional attack types?** Synergies from EVO/White Noise/Engineer can multiply Vostok; opponents can kill repeaters, bring KHDs, use non-hackable attacks and deny REM mobility. **Test:** remove your primary hacker hypothetically—can you still clear one ARO and score?
6. **Moran dependence vs diversified defense?** One Moran is still efficient (Dante), but older “double Moran” board denial is **illegal at AVA1**. **Test:** identify second flank's actual controlling unit before deployment.
7. **Corregidor-link Vostok vs vanilla solo Vostok:** 2026 WIP12 improved appraisal relies substantially on **CJC Haris + Evader/TinBot keyword arrangements**; do not import bonus dice, native team safety or legality into vanilla. **Test:** recalculate solo Vostok with available EVO only.
8. **Go-Pod mobility vs consistency:** The Dice Gods / initial reviews value aerial forks and extra angles, while [Shepherd's full Tunguska transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A2eKdenbcsw&t=3600s) argues it **cannot safely remove the ARO and repeater threats that prevent it from reaching those angles**, and questions its win contribution after others already did the work. **Test:** sketch the turn-one path against a live TR bot + one hidden KHD without assuming those threats disappear first.
9. **Sectorial TAG/REM hype vs vanilla legality:** The [Dice Gods Corregidor transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mvsu9ISjV4U&t=3679s) praises CJC multiple Geckos and Gators, Iguana and Lunokhod. The pinned vanilla reference allows **Gecko AVA2, Gator AVA1** and excludes ordinary Iguana/Lunokhod. **Test:** verify every profile and Fireteam against the vanilla source, not CJC footage.

## Practical 300-point list audit

- [ ] Can my **main active piece** reliably win a 24–32″ or 32″+ gunfight (or do I deliberately win by avoiding that lane)?
- [ ] Is there a **separate** short/midrange attack vector for the target that cannot be hacked?
- [ ] Which **one flank** does my Moran cover, and which Zero/Spektr/Morlock/RacerBot covers the other?
- [ ] Does my hacker package have an actual **deployment method** (starting Repeater, Pitcher, Disco Baller, mobile REM) rather than theoretical programs?
- [ ] If I buy **Vertigo 1.5 SWC**, what direct gun/SWC option have I sacrificed? Can it still play without Guided?
- [ ] If I buy a TAG/REM, who **repairs it**, where will that specialist start, and is retreat physically possible?
- [ ] Are **vanilla Fireteams truly legal** (Core 0, Haris 1, Duo 2; FTO-only Jazz in Fireteams I; *no Vostok/Tsyklon FTO links*)?
- [ ] Have I budgeted **Regular, Irregular, Impetuous, Tactical Awareness, NCO and Lieutenant** Orders separately and preserved objective specialists for Turn 3?
- [ ] Does the list survive enemy **Stealth, non-hackable rush, killer hackers, smoke/MSV, templates, airborne DZ entry and lost network**?
