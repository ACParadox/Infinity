# Kosmoflot — listbuilding and profile selection

## Overview

**What Kosmoflot does exceptionally well:** it combines **fast conventional heavy shooting, very cheap Regular Smoke, dangerous close assault and Mirage-5 airborne pressure** in one army. Its best lists force a front-lane, a midfield and an enemy-DZ problem at once rather than just building a bigger gun team.

### Particularly useful Fireteams

- **Varangian Guard Core/Haris/Duo — unusually flexible close-range family:**
  - **Varangian Guard** is the mandatory native anchor and provides inexpensive Regular Smoke/CC pressure.
  - **Wallace / Denma Connolly / Wolfgang / Ioann Bann** count as Varangians in the chart, adding distinct fighting or command jobs.
  - This can support a cheap screen or an aggressive close-assault team; the extra heroes are not automatic value.
- **Rokots Core/Haris — budget utility shell:**
  - **Rokot** anchors the team.
  - **Patcher** Wildcards count as Rokots; eligible **Frontovik/Mekhanik/Kazak Doktor/Wardriver** choices supply a gun, repair, medical support or hacking.
  - Keeps the main attack Order pool available for Mirage-5 or the TAG.
- **Kosmosoldat Duo/Haris:** **one or two Kosmosoldats** with a permitted **Frontovik** is a heavy direct-attack package. **Chernobog Duo** also exists, but the mere ability to field two TAGs is not itself an exceptional advantage.

### Unusual access, gaps and trade-offs

- **Chernobog TAG, Polaris and Mirage-5** give unusual diversity for Ariadna, backed by very cheap normal Smoke. **Wardriver Disco Baller 16/1** adds limited projected Eclipse coverage; normal Smoke cannot always bypass enemy MSV.
- **Wallace LT Inspiring Leadership** regularizes *genuinely Irregular* troopers (for example Irmandinhos). **Varangians and Denma are already Regular**; do not count an extra Order from them. Wallace also has a separate CoC option.
- **Scots Guard FTO has no current NCO**; do not claim Lieutenant Orders can be routed through it. Hacking/Repeater depth and high-end MSV2 remain significant gaps.

**Three crucial roster corrections:**

1. **Current Scots Guard FTO does NOT list NCO** in the pinned Army profile, contrary to repeated NCO claims in the uploaded material. **Do not count a Wallace or Kosmosoldat Lieutenant Order as transferable through a Scots Guard NCO.** The LT Order belongs to its LT under current rules unless another *actually legal* mechanism applies.
2. **Kosmoflot Varangian Guard and Denma Connolly are already Regular** (Varangians/Denma also Impetuous). **Wallace's Inspiring Leadership does not newly regularize their Orders.** Its regularization benefit applies to genuinely **Irregular** troopers, such as Irmandinhos, eligible Ioann Bann/Caterans/Warcor (subject to that skill's exceptions and list membership).
3. **Kibervolk Patrol is a bundled two-model team: one Kibervolk + one Dozer KVP**, same Combat Group; it is **not** two independently selectable options or a normal Fireteam. This matters when evaluating the two-Kibervolk/two-Dozer example roster.

## Core identity

Kosmoflot is strongest when it combines:

- one credible frontal hammer;
- cheap smoke and short-range aggression;
- asymmetric pressure from midfield or table edges;
- economical Regular-order infrastructure;
- specialists and defensive utility that do not compete with the main attackers for Orders.

The recurring successful pattern is **not** “build the best possible Fireteam and push it.” The exact Fireteam varies enormously between players. What repeats much more reliably is the role structure:

> **frontal threat + asymmetric threat + smoke/control + cheap mission infrastructure**

The biggest listbuilding trap is buying several expensive frontal gunfighters that all want the same pooled Orders.

A better default sequence is:

1. choose the Lieutenant / command architecture;
2. choose one primary ranged hammer;
3. add one attack vector that approaches the opponent differently;
4. add cheap smoke / close-range violence;
5. build economical Fireteam and specialist infrastructure;
6. add midfield / defensive friction;
7. only then add another premium attacker if it solves a genuinely different problem.

---

**Smoke and route control:** choose the carrier whose destination serves the mission.

- **Regular Varangian (10/0):** low-cost Smoke and close defence; its Order is already Regular.
- **Duroc (Mirage-5):** Smoke on a potentially distinct airborne attack axis.
- **Irmandinho:** Smoke/trading; Wallace can regularize its genuinely Irregular Order when the rules apply.
- **Other fast assault pieces:** redundancy only when they solve another job; extra Smoke sources consume slots.

Check approach squares and enemy MSV first. See the [vision-control guide](../list-building/vision-control-units-apex-and-value.md).

## Video-derived listbuilding patterns

The most useful early-N5 video material includes Tactical Awareness's **Zeroes & Heroes: Kosmoflot**, Ryan4043's **Kosmoflot: The Good and The Bad** and purchase guide, and the Counterintelligence Kosmoflot deep dive.

The Tactical Awareness episode is particularly valuable because it contains **three independently built sample lists**.

Those three lists differ dramatically in Fireteam construction, expensive pieces and support architecture, but several role patterns repeat.

### Mirage-5 is the clearest recurring premium piece

All three Tactical Awareness hosts independently included **Mirage-5**.

That is stronger evidence than any single presenter's unit rating because the rest of their lists were very different:

- one list used double Kosmosoldat, Bear, double Kibervolk and double Dozer KVP;
- one used a much more conventional Rokot / Patcher / Mekhanik / Frontovik support shell;
- one used Wallace, several Irregulars and two Chernobogs.

Despite those differences, Mirage-5 survived all three builds.

This makes Mirage-5 one of the closest things Kosmoflot has to a go-to premium package.

The strategic reason is more important than the raw profile:

> **Mirage-5 attacks from an axis that the opponent cannot solve merely by defending the frontal firelanes.**

A common Kosmoflot structure is therefore:

> **visible frontal hammer + Mirage-5 threatening side/rear access**

Margot contributes gunfighting and specialist utility; Duroc contributes disposable violence, Smoke and board disruption.

Do not evaluate Mirage-5 as “58 points of guns.” Evaluate it as a deployment and defensive-tax package.

---

### Smoke redundancy is a structural pattern

The sample lists repeatedly include multiple independent Smoke sources rather than relying on one model.

Examples across the three Tactical Awareness lists include combinations of:

- Polaris Bear;
- Denma Connolly;
- Duroc;
- Irmandinho;
- Wallace;
- Varangian.

The strategic lesson is:

> **Kosmoflot wants distributed Smoke access so losing one model does not turn off the movement plan.**

This matters because the army often has several different attack vectors:

- a heavy gun leaving the DZ;
- Mirage-5 arriving from an edge;
- a Bear or warband pushing close;
- midfield Camo already near the objectives.

Each of those can benefit from Smoke without requiring the same support model to follow all of them.

---

### Utility redundancy is common

The Tactical Awareness lists also show deliberate duplication of utility pieces.

Examples include:

- 2× Patchers;
- 2× Mekhaniks;
- 2× Rokot Paramedics;
- 2× **Kibervolk Patrols**, each automatically consisting of **1 Kibervolk + 1 Dozer KVP**, same Combat Group;
- 2× Kosmosoldats.

This is a useful Kosmoflot principle because the faction can afford redundancy without sacrificing all of its premium threats.

Redundancy means:

- one Engineer dying does not end your repair plan;
- a broken Fireteam can still contain useful bodies;
- a defensive area-control layer can remain after one piece is removed;
- losing one specialist does not force a premium attacker to abandon its job and press buttons.

The list should not depend on one unique character doing every support task.

### Community tactical field guide — N5 transcript synthesis (2026-10-19)

> **Opinion/analysis, not Army data.** The profile, point, SWC, AVA, Fireteam and rules facts **above** remain authoritative under the pinned N5.3 source. The analyses below come from recorded community discussions, chiefly January–July 2025 N5, and tournament retrospectives from N4. Verify any exact older rules/list suggestions against the current reference; do not copy podcast Army codes as current legal N5.3 lists. Speaker disagreements are retained.

#### Fast evaluation

- **Identity:** an aggressive, flexible Ariadnan force built around cheap Rokot orders, unusually broad smoke/close-assault tools, infiltration, airborne flank attacks, and a few substantial long-range guns. Its price-efficient *breadth* lets it threaten several routes at once; it does not automatically win direct face-to-face shooting.
- **Main weaknesses:** defensive ARO depth, scarce elite BS/visual mods, inconsistent Fireteam bonuses, limited hacking, and too many expensive *effectively ~1.5-Wound* gunfighters. An opponent can win by controlling approach lanes, layering repeaters against the TAG/HI, and trading for the few mission specialists.
- **Practical principle:** first budget reliable button pushers, then one long-range problem-solver, one midfield attack, and one flank/close-assault plan. Several 2025 lists stacked bears, TAGs and Mirage-5 but had too little specialist coverage; the opponent need only protect objectives rather than kill everything.

#### TAG, vehicle, REM and support architecture

- **TAG — Chernobog (AVA 2):** current N5.3 **63-pt AP HMG + Heavy Flamethrower** or **70-pt AP HMG + Heavy Rocket Launcher (+1B)**, both 1.5 SWC, chest mine, **Tactical Awareness**, ancillary Samodelkin specialist, and an available **Chernobog Duo**. BS14/ARM8 is a rare Kosmoflot long-range answer. **Transmutation (2)** reduces BS14→13, ARM8→6 and BTS6→3 after damage; **Dodge PH=9** and low repair PH make reactive escape/recovery unreliable. Prefer lane-clearing from cover and avoid exposing it to hostile repeater webs without first removing the hacker/relay. Bring a suitable Engineer or accept that lost structure may not be recoverable. A 9-pt **Mekhamobilya** pilot/support specialist exists but is not automatically worth its cost; the Samodelkin already adds a mission-capable ancillary. **Older spoken claims that the TAG is unhackable or a brokenly efficient TAG are not rules evidence.**
- **REM — Traktor Mul (AVA 2):** **5-pt Regular Baggage + Deactivator** is an outstanding order/area-support purchase; Baggage can restock eligible Disposable assets and helps order economy. **9-pt/1 SWC Katyusha** is a cheap **Guided** indirect-pressure option only if the list provides spotting/targeting and orders; do not overpay in orders for BS10 speculative guesses. **14-pt/1 SWC Uragan** has **Total Reaction + Guided** in the *current reference*, giving inexpensive ranged deterrence, but BS10 and S4 exposure are poor against dedicated gunfighters. *Counterintelligence/Yellow Snake* often prefer the cheap baggage order or other defenses over committing to a shaky artillery plan. **Disagreement check:** Yellow Snake's 2025 translated commentary describes an Uragan without Guided, **contrary to the current N5.3 profile above**—follow current data.
- **REM — Remontnikbot (AVA 2):** **2-pt Peripheral (Servant)** for an eligible Doctor/Engineer: extends repair/heal reach without exposing the controller, screens narrow approaches at low cost, and **generates no Regular order of its own**. In a Chernobog list, plan an Engineer's path in advance; a bot alone does not repair. A 2025 Yellow Snake speaker likes it as nearly free physical screening, while questioning how often the TAG will be worth risky repair.
- **Vehicle / motorcycle:** no separate **VH-classified troop** appears in this pinned **Kosmoflot** main roster. **Motorized 112** is a **19-pt LI Doctor with motorcycle, Mimetism (-3), smoke grenades/launcher and 20–10 MOV**. Its job is speedy healing, smoking an approach, and late objective play; its S4 base and weak direct firepower mean it is not a tank. *Tactical Awareness TAK/Vanilla Ariadna* compares faster/more specialized **Dynamo bikes** favorably, but those are **not** automatically Kosmoflot-legal.
- **Adjacent “robotic” pieces:** **Kibervolk Patrol is a two-member compound team**, not a REM; the **Chernobog Samodelkin is Ancillary**, and the **Mekhamobilya is an LI pilot**, not another TAG/vehicle. This distinction prevents imaginary order pools or Fireteam access.
- **Shared Ariadna research:** *Tactical Awareness: Vanilla Ariadna SOUP* explicitly discusses **Traktor Mul Katyusha Guided with Chasseur FO**, 5-pt Baggage deactivation/reload utility, and Chernobog Duo ideas; **Chasseur and TAK Dynamo synergies may not be imported into Kosmoflot** unless they are on its roster. *Tactical Awareness: TAK* independently praises the **5-pt Traktor** as a cost-efficient Regular Baggage order and gives motorcycle benchmarks.

1. **Balanced pressure:** a multi-vector list with scoring redundancy:
   - **Rokot:** Order economy.

   - **Chernobog:** AP HMG/long-range attack.

   - **Mirage-5:** off-table attack.

## Strong recurring building blocks

### Mirage-5

**Very high priority consideration.**

Take when you want:

- a threat from a different table edge / attack vector;
- a specialist that does not begin trapped in the DZ;
- a disposable close-range assault element in Duroc;
- an opponent to spend deployment resources protecting areas away from your main force.

Mirage-5 combines particularly well with an obvious frontal threat such as:

- Kosmosoldat;
- Chernobog;
- Frontovik / mixed Fireteam gun;
- Bear / warband rush.

The opponent then has to defend both the front and the side/rear.

---

### Patcher

Patchers appear repeatedly enough in sample and community lists to treat them as one of Kosmoflot's main **glue units**.

Their value is not that they are the best attacker in the faction.

They are good because they can accompany expensive pieces while contributing:

- specialist utility;
- durability;
- close-range/control capability;
- Fireteam body count;
- useful positioning without monopolizing active-turn Orders.

A good principle is:

> **one expensive shooter + Patchers / economical specialists is usually healthier than several premium models all competing to be Team Leader.**

A compact **Kosmosoldat + 1–2 Patchers** package is particularly coherent.

---

### Rokots

Rokots are not merely dead Fireteam filler.

They are useful because they can cheaply carry:

- Lieutenant duty;
- Paramedic / specialist roles;
- Regular Orders;
- Fireteam bonuses.

That lets the expensive members focus on fighting.

The 9-point Rokot Lieutenant architecture is strategically simple:

> **the Lieutenant costs almost nothing; the other 291 points are the actual plan.**

A Rokot-heavy support shell can reserve points for one deliberately chosen premium threat:

- **Mirage-5:** external entry and rear-angle attack.
- **Kosmosoldat:** forward heavy-infantry hammer.
- **Polaris Bear:** close-range pressure.
- **Chernobog:** TAG attack anchor.

Do not treat the list as requiring all four.

---

### Irmandinho

Irmandinho deserves a high-priority utility rating.

Without Wallace, it is already:

- cheap;
- self-funded by its Irregular Order;
- Engineer;
- Smoke source;
- D-Charge carrier;
- expendable close-range piece.

With Wallace LT, its value rises sharply because Inspiring Leadership can convert **Irmandinho's actual Irregular Order** into the Regular economy (subject to normal rules). This does **not** upgrade already-Regular Varangian/Denma Orders.

This creates two distinct uses:

#### Non-Wallace list

Treat the Irmandinho as a cheap autonomous utility model that largely pays for its own activations.

#### Wallace list

Treat Irmandinho as one of the main reasons to take Wallace at all.

Do not merely notice after list construction that you happen to have some Irregular troops.

If taking Wallace, actively shop for the best Irregular profiles.

---

### Varangian

Varangians are another excellent Wallace target but remain useful without him.

They provide:

- cheap Smoke;
- direct-template pressure;
- close-combat threat;
- disposable trading ability;
- a **Regular Order already**, whether or not Wallace is Lieutenant; Wallace does not add a regularization benefit for Varangians.

The correct use is not necessarily to run every Varangian forward immediately.

A cheap Varangian can also:

- protect a corner;
- Smoke a critical crossing;
- trade into an expensive attacker entering your DZ;
- preserve its Order until late game.

---

### Kosmosoldat

Current Kosmosoldat is significantly better than the version criticized in early-2025 videos.

The October 2025 update added / improved major elements including:

- universal MSV1;
- BS Attack(-3);
- additional profile utility;
- a Kosmoflot-only Lieutenant(+1 Order) profile.

Therefore older video criticism should be tagged mentally as **pre-buff**.

Current Kosmosoldat is one of the faction's clearest frontal hammers.

Important jobs include:

- long-range pressure;
- solving durable targets;
- providing a serious threat that forces the opponent to respect frontal lanes;
- forming the expensive center of a compact Patcher-supported team;
- serving as an aggressive Lieutenant architecture.

#### Double Kosmosoldat

One Tactical Awareness sample already used **two Portable Autocannon Kosmosoldats**, one as Lieutenant, before the later buffs.

That architecture has two useful effects:

- two serious ranged threats;
- Lieutenant ambiguity when both models look equally plausible.

Do not automatically duplicate the same premium gun, but double Kosmosoldat is a real tested concept rather than pure theorycrafting.

## Lieutenant architectures

Kosmoflot's Lieutenant choice should materially shape the army.

### Rokot LT

Use when:

- you want the cheapest possible command shell;
- you do not need a command-engine gimmick;
- points are better spent on threats and mission pieces.

Advantages:

- extremely cheap;
- easy to fit into ordinary infrastructure;
- frees points for a deliberate premium threat:
  - **Mirage-5:** rear attack.
  - **Polaris Bear:** close pressure.
  - **Kosmosoldat:** heavy infantry assault.
  - **Chernobog:** TAG gun anchor.

Weakness:

- contributes little beyond being Lieutenant and a Regular Order.

---

### Frontovik LT

Use when:

- you want a more credible infantry Lieutenant;
- a conventional Fireteam architecture is already present;
- you do not want to commit to Wallace's Irregular economy;
- you do not want the risk / cost of an aggressive Kosmosoldat LT.

This is the middle-ground command option.

---

### Wallace LT — build around Inspiring Leadership

Wallace should be understood first as an **order-economy architecture**, not as a CC character who happens to be Lieutenant.

The Tactical Awareness Wallace sample list includes Varangians and Denma for **smoke, cheap assault and close-range defence**, but those profiles **already produce Regular Orders**. **Irmandinhos** (and other genuinely Irregular troopers) are what benefit from Wallace's regularization. Separate **combat utility** from **Order-conversion value**.

The correct listbuilding question is not:

> “Do I already have enough Irregulars to justify Wallace?”

It is:

> **“If I select Wallace, which unusually efficient Irregular troops can I deliberately add because he upgrades their order contribution?”**

The more good Irregular profiles you intentionally buy, the more Wallace changes the army's economy.

#### Wallace command economy — no pinned Scots Guard NCO

The uploaded source proposes Wallace LT + a Scots Guard FTO NCO. **That interaction is unsupported by the pinned Kosmoflot N5.3 roster:** current Scots Guard FTO rows list no NCO skill. Wallace's confirmed economy benefit is **Inspiring Leadership applied to eligible truly Irregular troopers**. Spend the LT Order legally on Wallace or find an explicitly legal mechanism; **do not budget a nonexistent NCO conversion**.

---

### Kosmosoldat LT(+1 Order)

Use when:

- the list wants an aggressive, durable Lieutenant;
- you have a realistic plan to use the extra Lieutenant Order **on the Lieutenant itself** (no pinned Scots Guard NCO);
- the Kosmosoldat is already one of the main ranged pieces.

**Correction:** the current roster has **no NCO Scots Guard FTO**, so the previously suggested LT→Scots Guard conversion **cannot be counted**.

A Kosmosoldat LT(+1 Order) is a deliberate **aggressive Lieutenant** option: its extra command resource is valuable only when the Kosmosoldat itself can productively use it, with the usual Fireteam Integrity consequences if it spends a personal LT Order while linked. Otherwise a cheaper Rokot LT or a different command architecture can be safer and more efficient.

---

## Wallace economy lists

A Wallace list should look meaningfully different from a non-Wallace list.

Good candidates to exploit include:

- Irmandinhos;
- Varangians;
- other useful Irregular troops that already perform real battlefield jobs.

A reasonable Wallace shell might begin with:

- Wallace LT;
- 2+ Irregular utility / warband pieces;
- one serious conventional ranged threat;
- Mirage-5 or another asymmetric attack vector;
- cheap Regular / specialist infrastructure.

The mistake is paying for Wallace and then fielding almost no Irregular troops.

If Wallace is not changing the order economy, much of his strategic value is being wasted.

## Fireteam philosophy

The video evidence does **not** support one mandatory Kosmoflot Fireteam.

The three Tactical Awareness sample lists differ too much for that conclusion.

What does repeat is the idea that the Fireteam should usually be **economical infrastructure around one meaningful gun**, rather than a deathstar full of premium profiles.

Useful broad architectures include:

### Rokot mixed infrastructure

Use cheap Rokots for:

- bodies;
- specialists;
- Lieutenant duty;
- Fireteam bonuses.

Then add one or two premium models that solve actual problems.

This leaves points for independent asymmetric pieces.

---

### Kosmosoldat + Patchers

One of the most coherent current compact packages.

Roles:

- **Kosmosoldat** — ranged hammer;
- **Patchers** — specialists, durability, close utility, Fireteam support.

This avoids paying for multiple expensive models that all want to be Team Leader.

---

### Frontovik-based mixed team

Frontoviks are best selected by job rather than by unit name.

Profiles worth considering include:

- HRL for economical long-range / template threat;
- MSV AP Sniper for visual-modifier / lane control;
- Engineer for specialist / anti-armor utility;
- Spitfire-type profiles when the team specifically needs a conventional active gun.

The main principle is:

> **specialized Frontovik > generic premium body added only because the Fireteam allows it.**

---

### Scots Guard FTO — re-evaluate after the October 2025 changes

Early N5 videos often preferred Frontoviks and were lukewarm on Scots Guard because of cost and role overlap.

That advice is now partly stale.

Later updates gave current Scots Guard FTO profiles important utility including:

- Forward Observer on the specific listed option;
- MediKit / Paramedic on the specific listed option;
- **no NCO shown on current FTO entries**;
- Mimetism(-3);
- useful differentiated weapon packages.

This makes Scots Guard much more interesting as:

- specialist on an eligible profile;
- economical Fireteam body;
- **NOT an NCO Order converter** in the pinned current roster;
- secondary gun.

Do not evaluate current Scots Guard solely through early-2025 reviews.

---

## Frontal hammer + asymmetric threat

This is probably the most important recurring Kosmoflot combination.

The Tactical Awareness lists broadly look like:

- strong visible threat from the DZ;
- Mirage-5 or another attack vector that does not approach from the DZ;
- cheap Smoke / close-range control.

Examples of the frontal half include:

- Kosmosoldat;
- Chernobog;
- Frontovik-based shooting package;
- another serious linked gun.

Examples of the asymmetric half include:

- Mirage-5;
- Uxia;
- SAS;
- Bear / warband pressure;
- Camo / midfield attack pieces.

The strategic principle is:

> **Different attack vectors are usually more valuable than another premium gun doing the same job.**

A list with Kosmosoldat + Chernobog + Unknown Ranger may contain excellent profiles, but it can be easier to defend against than:

> **Kosmosoldat + Mirage-5 + Uxia**

because the latter attacks from three different spatial / informational directions.

---

## Uxia and SAS

Do not let the premium units distract from midfield presence.

Uxia / SAS-type pieces solve several problems:

- they begin near the mission rather than walking from the DZ;
- they force the opponent to respect Camo and short-range attack angles;
- they can score without consuming many movement Orders;
- they create a third axis alongside frontal pressure and Mirage-5.

Current Uxia is substantially more attractive than some older N5 commentary suggests because later updates improved her role.

A useful combined-arms structure is:

> **frontal hammer + Mirage-5 + Uxia/SAS**

This is much harder to defend efficiently than simply buying another heavy shooter.

---

## Polaris Bear

Bear is common and good, but the video lists prove it is **not mandatory**.

Two of the three Tactical Awareness lists include one; the Wallace / double-Chernobog list does not.

Take the Bear when the army needs:

- fast Smoke;
- close-range violence;
- a threatening Impetuous / self-propelled attack piece;
- a way to punish opponents who spend too many resources defending long firelanes.

Skip it when the list already has enough overlapping short-range pressure from:

- Wallace;
- Varangians;
- Denma;
- Irmandinhos;
- Duroc;
- other aggressive pieces.

The correct question is:

> **Does the Bear add a new attack vector, or merely duplicate violence I already bought?**

---

## Kibervolk — defensive goalkeeper

Kibervolk deserves more attention than its glamour level suggests.

One Tactical Awareness list takes **two Kibervolk Patrol packages**—each includes a Kibervolk **and** one Dozer KVP in the same Combat Group.

Kibervolk's best role is often not as a primary attacker but as a **goalkeeper / rear-area control piece**.

Use Kibervolk to protect:

- Lieutenant shell;
- Engineers / Doctors;
- retreat routes;
- table edges;
- rear objectives;
- the space vacated when Bear / Mirage / heavy attackers push forward.

Kosmoflot lists frequently contain many pieces that want to leave the DZ.

Someone still has to make the opponent's counterattack expensive.

---

## Dozer KVP / deployable defence

The same Tactical Awareness list's **two bundled Kibervolk Patrols already include two Dozer KVPs**; the Dozers are not separately optional pickups outside those packages.

That suggests a deliberate defensive architecture rather than random utility duplication.

The KVP provides:

- Engineer function;
- deployable / turret area control;
- D-Charges / objective utility;
- local defensive presence.

Combined with Kibervolk, this can create substantial passive friction while the main attack pieces move away.

Use this type of package when the rest of the list is highly aggressive and would otherwise leave the DZ undefended.

---

## Chernobog

Chernobog is a viable primary-engine archetype rather than an automatic inclusion.

Use it when you want:

- durable frontal force;
- a Tactical Awareness attack platform;
- a model that can solve conventional gunfights without requiring a large Fireteam investment.

The main listbuilding warning is **role duplication**.

Chernobog + Kosmosoldat can work, but both are expensive frontal pieces.

Before adding the second one, ask whether the points would produce more value as:

- Mirage-5;
- Uxia / SAS;
- Bear;
- Smoke / warbands;
- specialists;
- defensive area control.

If Chernobog is the primary hammer, the rest of the list should usually become **more asymmetric**, not more frontal.

---

## Unknown Ranger

Unknown Ranger remains a strong self-contained piece, but the video material does not support treating him as a mandatory Kosmoflot staple.

Notably, none of the three Tactical Awareness sample lists use him.

Take him when you specifically need:

- independent gunfighting;
- Tactical Awareness;
- specialist capability;
- a mobile self-funded threat.

Do not take him solely because older N4 Kosmoflot advice treated him as an automatic inclusion.

Current competition for those points is much stronger.

---

## Volkolak — older criticism is stale

Early N5 reviews were often lukewarm on Volkolak.

The October 2025 balance update materially changed that evaluation with improvements to movement, Dodge / CC utility, immunity / profile options, weapon quality and costs.

Therefore:

> **treat pre-October-2025 Volkolak criticism as historical context, not current profile advice.**

Re-evaluate the current profile on its actual present-day role and cost.

## Common traps

### Buying too many frontal gunfighters

Kosmoflot has several attractive expensive shooters.

That does not mean they belong in the same list.

Ask:

> **Does this new model create a different problem for the opponent, or simply consume the same Orders as my existing primary gun?**

---

### Treating Wallace as a generic good Lieutenant

Wallace should materially alter your cheap-unit selection.

If the list contains almost no Irregular troops, consider whether another Lieutenant would be more efficient.

---

### Treating Bear as mandatory

Bear is excellent when it adds fast Smoke / assault pressure.

It is redundant when the list is already overloaded with Duroc, Varangians, Irmandinhos, Denma and similar short-range pieces.

---

### Building a premium Fireteam deathstar

Kosmoflot usually benefits more from:

> **one meaningful gun + cheap specialists / support bodies**

than from putting several premium models into one footprint.

---

### Ignoring DZ defence

Mirage, Bear, Uxia/SAS and aggressive Fireteams naturally pull the army forward.

If nothing remains behind, a counter-deployment / Parachutist / warband can destroy the support shell.

Kibervolk, KVP deployables, cheap warbands and careful Rokot positioning can prevent this.

## Practical archetypes

### Wallace economy

Core idea:

- Wallace LT;
- deliberately high Irregular count;
- one serious conventional gun;
- Mirage-5 / midfield threat;
- economical support.

Use when you want to maximize Kosmoflot's excellent cheap Irregular profiles.

---

### Kosmosoldat combined arms

Core idea:

- Kosmosoldat LT(+1) or primary gun;
- Scots Guard **FTO specialist/secondary gun**, if useful; **no current NCO interaction**;
- 1–2 Patchers;
- Rokot infrastructure;
- Mirage-5 or Uxia/SAS;
- cheap Smoke / defence.

This is one of the clearest current post-buff architectures.

---

### Chernobog pressure

Core idea:

- Chernobog as primary frontal engine;
- cheap orders / specialists;
- Mirage-5;
- midfield Camo;
- Smoke and defensive utility.

Avoid automatically adding another expensive frontal gun.

---

### Infantry toolbox

Core idea:

- Rokot / Frontovik / Patcher / Scots Guard mixed infrastructure;
- no giant centerpiece;
- several smaller independent threats;
- good specialist redundancy;
- Smoke / Camo / edge pressure.

Less spectacular but often very mission-resilient.

## Current listbuilding checklist

Before finalizing a Kosmoflot list, ask:

1. **What is my primary frontal hammer?**
2. **What attacks from a different vector?**
   - **Mirage-5:** rear entry.
   - **Uxía:** infiltrating scoring/attack.
   - **S.A.S.:** camouflaged midfield.
   - **Polaris Bear:** fast close pressure.
3. **Do I have enough Smoke, preferably from more than one source?**
4. **If Wallace is LT, am I deliberately exploiting Irregular troops?**
5. **If Kosmosoldat LT(+1) is present, how will it legally spend its additional personal Lieutenant Order, and what are the Fireteam consequences?**
6. **Does my Fireteam contain useful support bodies rather than four models competing to be Team Leader?**
7. **Who handles the mission while the premium attackers fight?**
8. **Who protects the DZ / support shell after the aggressive pieces leave?**
9. **Am I paying for a second premium gun that duplicates the first?**
10. **Have I checked current Army legality rather than copying an early-2025 Fireteam verbatim?**

The general principle is:

> **Kosmoflot wins by forcing the opponent to solve several different problems at once, not by concentrating every point into the strongest direct gunfight.**
