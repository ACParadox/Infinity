# Peacemaker Armbot — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: A-tier / strong, especially the Auxbot-3B Minelayer; not a must-have.** The **24-point / 0.5 SWC Heavy Shotgun + Auxbot-3B** is the standout **turn-zero midfield speed bump**: one purchase provides a forward-deployed shotgun/Repeater chassis, a synchronized Heavy Flamethrower Auxbot, and a Shock Mine already placed during Deployment. The **27-point / 0 SWC Boarding Shotgun (+1B) + Auxbot-3** is the stronger *deliberate active-turn* choice. Do not buy a Peacemaker expecting a long-range gunfighter or a mission specialist.

**Core distinction:** Compared with the Bulleteer, Peacemaker pays for **starting position, a synchronized direct-template threat, and—on the 3B profile—a pre-placed mine**, not for superior conventional Face-to-Face shooting. It is one of Kestrel's better ways to force an attacker to solve several related midfield problems before reaching the army's TAG, gunfighter, support pieces, or objectives.

## Pinned Kestrel N5.3 profiles

The [pinned Kestrel roster](../../../../sectorials/panoceania/kestrel-colonial-force.md#peacemaker-armbots) lists **AVA 2**, **Regular**, **REM**, **MOV 15–10 cm (6–4")**, **BS 12**, **PH 10**, **WIP 13**, **ARM 1**, **BTS 3**, **STR 1**, **S4**. Shared skills/equipment: **Forward Deployment (+8")**, **Mimetism (-3)**, **Repeater**, **Remote Presence**, **Courage**, **Pulzar**, and **PARA CCW (-3)**. The listed profiles with Auxbots use a **Peripheral (Synchronized)**.

| Profile | Pts/SWC | Included Auxbot | Role / assessment |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Heavy Shotgun + Auxbot-3** | **23/0** | Heavy Flamethrower | Good zero-SWC midfield roadblock and flexible attack |
| **Heavy Shotgun + Auxbot-3B** | **24/0.5** | Heavy Flamethrower, **Shock Mine, Minelayer** | **Default for passive area denial**; best prepaid three-layer threat |
| **Boarding Shotgun, BS Attack (+1B), no Auxbot** | **23/0** | **None** | Niche independent active attacker; usually overshadowed |
| **Boarding Shotgun, BS Attack (+1B) + Auxbot-3** | **27/0** | Heavy Flamethrower | **Best active assault package**; B3 AP gun plus synchronized template |

**Profile precision:** The 23-point Boarding Shotgun (+1B) option has **no Auxbot**, whereas the 27-point version does. The Auxbot-3B's mine is **Shock**, not AP; the **Minelayer skill belongs to the Auxbot**, not the Peacemaker controller. An ordinary Heavy Shotgun is **B2**, whereas the Boarding Shotgun Peacemaker with **BS Attack (+1B)** gets **B3 in its active turn only**. The +1B does **not** increase reactive Burst.

## Best overall use: 24/0.5 Auxbot-3B as a forward speed bump

The 3B package threatens a section of the midfield from Deployment, even if you never spend an active Order on it. Its three elements can either **overlap at a chokepoint** or **stretch laterally into a wide ~32\" potential threat footprint**:

1. **Peacemaker controller:** S4, Mimetism (-3), BS12 Heavy Shotgun and Pulzar; requires an opponent to respect short firing lanes. The Repeater projects hacking coverage if Kestrel has a suitable Hacker.
2. **Auxbot-3B:** A separately positioned, synchronized **Heavy Flamethrower** ready to punish movement through tight corridors and corners. A direct template is particularly relevant against targets that rely on impressive BS and Mimetism.
3. **Shock Mine:** A **pre-positioned** CAMO(-3) deployable threat established using Minelayer during Deployment, without an Order spent to lay it.

This is the useful meaning of **“three threats in Forward Deployment (+8")”**: three overlapping *problems*, not three Regular Orders, and not three independently activatable troopers. The mine is a separate deployable; controller and Auxbot share activations and are constrained by synchronization.

A fourth, **conditional layer** is the advanced **Repeater**: Carbonite/Oblivion/Spotlight pressure requires an appropriate friendly Hacker and relevant program. The Peacemaker's Repeater by itself does not immobilize anything.

**Placement matters more than raw coverage.** Place controller, bot, and mine to pressure *different parts* of a short approach instead of lining all three up for the same enemy template or allowing the enemy to bypass the lot. Give the opponent unattractive choices: discover/remove the mine, trade into the Auxbot's flamethrower, or expose themselves to the shotgun/repeater.

### Wide lateral coverage: Peacemaker → Auxbot → Mine

**An important alternative to stacking the three threats on one corner is to string them out *sideways*, along the forward-deployment line.** Because the **Auxbot-3B itself is the Minelayer**, the distance between the controller and the mine can be much greater than a single 8" Zone of Control:

```text
← Peacemaker's close shotgun threat                     Mine's trigger reach →
    ~8"         up to 8"              up to 8"                ~8"
←───────── [Peacemaker] ───────── [Auxbot-3B] ───────── [Shock Mine] ─────────→
```

- **Controller → Auxbot:** Keep the Auxbot **within 8" of the Peacemaker**, measured by Coherency/Zone of Control; a separation of almost 8" is legal when positioning allows.
- **Auxbot → Mine:** When deploying with Minelayer, place the Shock Mine **within 8" of the Auxbot**, not necessarily within 8" of the Peacemaker. Another almost-8" lateral displacement is possible.
- **Outer threats:** The Peacemaker's Heavy Shotgun has its **+6 close-range band within 8"** on the left; the mine can threaten movement to the right using its **Small Teardrop Template** (roughly another 8" of outward reach in favourable geometry). The Auxbot's Heavy Flamethrower adds a broad template threat from its intermediate position.

**Result: roughly 32" of *potential lateral influence from one 24-point / 0.5-SWC purchase*** (8" close-shotgun reach + 8" controller-to-Auxbot + 8" Auxbot-to-mine + approximately 8" mine template). The three physical positions themselves span **up to roughly 16"**. This is an illustrative footprint, **not 32" of uninterrupted, automatic ARO or mine-trigger coverage**: facing, LoF, terrain, templates, rangebands, and enemy approach routes determine the actual danger. The shotgun can still shoot beyond its preferred range, and the mine's trigger area is its template, **not an 8"-radius ZoC**.

**Deployment rules and caveats:**

- Peacemaker's **Forward Deployment (+8")** describes how far forward of the normal Deployment Zone it can start; it does **not** add another 8" to each horizontal gap. All three placements must remain in their **individually legal deployment areas**, including the Minelayer rule that the mine must remain within the area where its Minelayer trooper could deploy.
- **Controller–Auxbot Coherency** is mandatory: do not accidentally exceed the controller's ZoC. **Minelayer's mine placement** is measured from the Auxbot; a placed mine is not a Peripheral and does not need to maintain later Coherency.
- The mine cannot be placed during Minelayer deployment with an enemy trooper or Camouflage Marker in its trigger area. Once deployed, it only triggers if its Small Teardrop Template can hit a valid enemy without catching a friendly model, and Total Cover can block its trigger area.
- Spreading the pieces increases breadth but can reduce **overlapping defense**: the enemy may isolate and remove the Peacemaker or Auxbot separately. If the controller enters Null or Isolated, the Auxbot disconnects; the already deployed mine remains.
- Best on **dense midfield tables with two or three lateral lanes**. Concentrate the pieces instead when a single vital chokepoint deserves overlapping shotgun, flamethrower, and mine coverage.

This **wide-fence setup** is a major reason to favor the 3B profile for passive defense: few other Kestrel purchases establish several separated midfield threats and a Repeater without spending a single active-turn Order.

**Important mine restriction:** A mine does **not** detonate if its template would hit a friendly model, even an Unconscious one. Do not tuck the mine so tightly beside the Peacemaker/Auxbot that your own models prevent it from triggering. Minelayer placement must also obey its allowed deployment area, zone-of-control, and enemy-in-trigger-area restrictions. The mine is already a deterrent before either player spends Orders; it is *not* an extra ARO from the Auxbot.

### What the enemy has to spend

- **Movement or positioning Orders** to approach from outside the shotgun/flamethrower arcs.
- **Discover, Deactivator, or sacrificial moves** to handle the Shock Mine.
- **Attacks against the controller or Auxbot** to remove direct threats.
- **Hacking/counter-hacking decisions** if you have an actual forward-network plan.

The objective is not necessarily to kill an elite attacker. For **24 points / 0.5 SWC**, making it spend multiple Orders to clear a corridor can already be a success—especially when that preserves the TAG or prevents immediate access to mission objectives.

## 27/0 Boarding Shotgun (+1B) + Auxbot: strongest offensive profile

Choose the **27-point / 0 SWC** version when the intent is to **spend Orders actively attacking**, rather than deploy a mine as a passive obstacle.

- **Boarding Shotgun B3**, BS12, Mimetism (-3), AP ammunition; better active close-range gunfighting than the Heavy Shotgun profile on volume of fire.
- **Forward Deployment (+8")** shortens the approach and may allow a turn-one close-range attack without a long setup.
- **Synchronized Heavy Flamethrower** can burn a different target, threaten a template trade, or help clear models protected by high negative shooting MODs.
- **Pulzar** on the controller provides its own direct-template option when conventional shooting is unattractive.
- Costs **three points more** than Minelayer, but **saves 0.5 SWC**. This is a different tactical job, not a strict upgrade.

In an active Order the controller and synchronized Auxbot declare the same Skills, but may attack **different valid targets**. They do not have to both see or shoot the same model; if one cannot fulfill the chosen Skill it Idles. Enemy reactive models select which activated model to target. Coherency is mandatory.

**When it shines:** A dense-table assault where a cheap, relatively expendable attack package can clear corner guards, cheap defenders, or clustered troops. It is not a premium attack platform: **ARM1/STR1, S4 and Mimetism (-3)** mean it can still die quickly to a good defender, direct template, mine, or Hacker.

## The two 23-point profiles

**23/0 Heavy Shotgun + Auxbot-3 — good budget defense/assault:** The baseline Peacemaker with Heavy Flamethrower support. Prefer this to the 24-point 3B when you cannot afford **0.5 SWC**, or if the position of an extra mine has little value on this table. It still offers two bodies, two close-range attack origins, Forward Deployment and Repeater.

**23/0 Boarding Shotgun (+1B), without Auxbot — niche/usually skip:** A forward-deployed B3 AP shotgun with Mimetism (-3) is serviceable, but removing the Auxbot loses the unit's most distinctive force multiplier. For **+4 points**, the 27/0 profile adds the Heavy Flamethrower Auxbot while keeping the B3 gun; for **less money**, Kestrel's **Bulleteer HSG (+1B) 19/0** has Mimetism (-6) and B3 close-range fire, though it lacks Forward Deployment and a Peripheral. Only take the 23-point solo BSG if the exact price/slot and Forward Deployment fit a specific plan.

## When to take it / when to avoid

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Want the opponent to spend Orders clearing a midfield approach **starting on turn one** | **24/0.5 Minelayer** |
| Dense table, valuable 0–8" corridors, objectives or side routes | **High priority**, particularly Minelayer |
| Need a fast, aggressive turn-one attack with synchronized template pressure | **27/0 Boarding Shotgun + Auxbot** |
| SWC-starved but still want a flamer + controller speed bump | **23/0 Heavy Shotgun + Auxbot** |
| Want an AP B3 shotgun gunfighter rather than forward deployment | **19/0 Bulleteer HSG (+1B)** may be better |
| Want active attacking from 8–24" or farther | **Not the Peacemaker's role**; use main gun / appropriate REM |
| Need to press consoles, Sensor-sweep, or gain a personal extra Order | **Pathfinder Tactical Awareness 17/0** instead |
| Already have extensive overlapping midfield mines/templates | **Lower priority**; avoid paying for redundant coverage |
| Opponent likely to exploit advanced Repeater / cheap enemy hacking | **Deploy conservatively**; avoid giving an easy hackable victim |
| Wide, exposed table without safe forward positions | **Lower priority**; elite long-range guns punish S4/ARM1 |
| No real turn-one role and group is short of Orders for any assault | **Usually skip** rather than pay 23–27 points for an idle attacker |

### TAG/elite gunfighter synergy

For **Tikbalang/Squalos, Griffin HMG or Feuerbach, Crux Knight MMR/Spitfire, or Hetkari MULTI Red Fury**, the Minelayer Peacemaker offers an **independent short-range obstacle** while the premium gun spends Orders doing the hard shooting. It protects routes into the TAG's vicinity, slows skirmisher advances, and forces clearing actions. Unlike an additional premium attacker, its value can be realized **without consuming active-turn Orders**.

The offensive **27/0** profile is more appealing when the primary gun opens a route and the Peacemaker can then exploit a flank/cluster of defenders. You do **not** need both to attack every turn; a secondary attacker earns its place by solving a different engagement or by forcing defensive choices.

**Unlike a Bulleteer HSG, the Peacemaker cannot tag along in an ARMORED Duo.** It has **no Kestrel Fireteam row**, and a controller with an active synchronized Peripheral cannot join a Fireteam or Coordinated Order under the general rules. Its Forward Deployment partially compensates: it does not have to be carried to the midfield by a TAG.

### Combat Group and repair

- **Group 2 Minelayer is reasonable** when that group primarily provides Orders, support, and reactive board control. Its mine, flamer and Repeater can matter without actively spending many Orders.
- **Group 1 is better** for the **27/0 assault profile** if you actually intend to drive the controller/Auxbot deep into enemy lines.
- The Auxbot **does not generate its own Regular Order**; it is part of the controller's single army-list slot/group package.
- Controller has **Remote Presence**: two levels of Unconscious before full destruction, potentially repairable by Engineer/Palbot. A deployed Shock Mine does not vanish just because the controller is lost; the **Auxbot becomes Disconnected** when its controller enters Null or Isolated State, according to Peripheral rules. Repairs only matter if a realistic recovery route exists.

## Comparison to other Kestrel midfield tools

| Alternative | Pts/SWC | Better at | Peacemaker edge |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Bulleteer HSG (+1B)** | **19/0** | B3 AP close-range shots, Mimetism (-6) Face-to-Face defense | **Forward Deployment; Auxbot; Minelayer variant; turn-zero disruption** |
| **Infiltrating Jackal E/M GL Minelayer** | **11/0.5** | Cheap E/M speculative fire and starting Shock Mine; expendable Irregular threat | **Regular Order, Mimetism (-3), Repeater, Heavy Shotgun + synchronized HFT** |
| **Scarecrow BSG** | **26/0** | Hidden/Infiltrating attack, Camouflage, Mimetism (-6), surprises | **Visible area denial, synchronized template, optional prelaid Shock Mine** |
| **Pathfinder TacAware** | **17/0** | Objective actions, Sensor, FO, Tactical Awareness, efficient movement | **Immediate midfield combat pressure and stronger anti-rush defense** |
| **Fennec Thunderbolt Shock Minelayer** | **14/0** | Cheap DZ defense and native FENNECS participation | **Farther forward, distributed bot/flamer threat, mobile Repeater** |

The critical comparison is **Peacemaker Minelayer versus Bulleteer HSG**. Both are affordable, fast, repairable attack REMs, but they pay for opposite strengths:

- **Bulleteer:** better **active close-range gunfight** (B3 AP, Mimetism -6); must travel from its starting position.
- **Peacemaker 3B:** better **no-Order midfield denial** (Forward Deployment, mine, flame, Repeater), but ordinary **B2 Heavy Shotgun** and only Mimetism -3.

Do not consider one categorically superior; buy the role the list is missing.

## Final selection rule

1. **Default speed bump:** **Peacemaker Heavy Shotgun + Auxbot-3B, 24/0.5 — A-tier**. Especially strong into dense-table midfield routes; place threats together for layered defense or string controller → Auxbot → mine across lanes for ~32\" of potential lateral influence.
2. **For a planned active assault:** **Boarding Shotgun (+1B) + Auxbot-3, 27/0 — A-/B+ depending on attack geometry**. Better attack Burst, two shooting origins, no SWC.
3. **SWC-saving support option:** **Heavy Shotgun + Auxbot-3, 23/0 — B+**. Fine if 0.5 SWC is genuinely needed elsewhere.
4. **Usually skip:** **Boarding Shotgun (+1B) without Auxbot, 23/0 — C+/niche**. Loses the reason most lists select a Peacemaker.

**Bottom line:** If a list already has a strong premium active gunfighter, **24/0.5 Minelayer Peacemaker** is a better complementary investment than adding another average gun. It can shape a route and tax enemy Orders from the first turn without diverting your own attack budget. It is **not** automatic when existing Jackals, mines, Fireflies, and templates already cover those same approaches.
