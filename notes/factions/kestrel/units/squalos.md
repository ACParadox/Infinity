# Squalos Mk-II — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: MULTI HMG NCO (66/1.5) is the preferred primary Squalos; MULTI Marksman Rifle (55/1) is a conditional budget TAG; HMG + Grenade Launcher (62/1.5) is a niche indirect-fire choice.** Squalos is a BS15, ARM6, three-Wound gunfighter with Tactical Awareness, BS Attack (SR-1), Combat Instinct and ECM: Guided (-6). The major trade-off against infantry attackers is **S6, no Prone, no Cautious Movement and TAG-only Total Control vulnerability**. These must be evaluated alongside raw armor and Wounds.

**Default Kestrel choices:** Prefer **MULTI HMG NCO66 + Pathfinder Tactical Awareness17 = 83/1.5 ARMORED Duo FT1** when a mobile TAG/specialist team suits the mission. Prefer the **Tikbalang69 + Pathfinder17 = 86/1.5** when Climbing Plus, Mimetism (-3), Heavy Flamethrower and Shock Mines matter more than ranged AP. For a low-price 8–24" attacker, assess **Hetkari MULTI Red Fury48** and **Crux FTO MULTI Marksman Rifle46** before spending 55 on the larger Squalos.

> **Scope and evidence:** Current [Kestrel pinned roster and Fireteam chart](../../../sectorials/panoceania/kestrel-colonial-force.md), [Kestrel Fireteams](../kestrel-fireteams.md), [Tikbalang analysis](Tikbalang.md) and N5.3 rules linked below. Costs/profile names are taken from the main-branch roster. Rankings and Duo proposals below are **theorycraft**, not claims that the exact formations were played in recorded battle reports.

## Base profile and options

**All three:** AVA2, TAG, Regular; MOV15-10 (6-4"), CC18, BS15, PH15, WIP13, ARM6, BTS6, W3, **S6**; **ECM: Guided (-6), BS Attack (SR-1), Combat Instinct, Courage, Dodge (+1"), Dodge (PH=11), GizmoKit (PH=13), Remote Presence, Tactical Awareness**. Each carries MULTI Pistol (+1B) and CCW. Unlike the Tikbalang, Squalos has **no Mimetism, no Climbing Plus, no Heavy Flamethrower and no Shock Mines**.

| Option | Pts / SWC | Loadout and distinct skill | Decision |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **1 — MULTI Marksman Rifle** | **55 / 1** | MULTI Marksman Rifle; cheaper TAG with AP/Shock, DA anti-materiel mode | **B- / conditional**: durable independent 8–24" attacker, but competes with smaller, cheaper HI |
| **2 — HMG + Grenade Launcher** | **62 / 1.5** | Normal HMG, normal Grenade Launcher | **Niche**: direct long gun plus indirect circular templates; lacks sustained AP |
| **3 — MULTI HMG + NCO** | **66 / 1.5** | MULTI HMG; **NCO** | **A / preferred primary Squalos**: ranged AP plus restricted Lieutenant-Order use |

**Important correction:** The current Squalos has **native BS Attack (SR-1), NOT native BS Attack (+1SD)**. SR-1 penalizes the target's Saving Roll; it does not improve hit probability. Two matching Squalos in an ARMORED Duo can gain **FT2 +1SD on eligible BS Attacks** through the Fireteam bonus. See [BS Attack](https://infinitythewiki.com/BS_Attack) and [Fireteam Bonuses](https://infinitythewiki.com/Fireteam_Bonuses).

## 66-point MULTI HMG NCO — preferred main TAG

**Choose it if the Squalos is your main active ranged gun.**

- **MULTI HMG AP mode, B4**: the standout difference from ordinary HMG platforms. AP halves ARM and makes it a more self-contained answer to high-ARM HI/TAGs.
- **Shock mode, B4**: efficient into ordinary infantry when AP is unnecessary.
- **EXP anti-materiel mode, B1**: a threatening single-shot option, especially in ARO; not B4 EXP.
- **NCO**: converts available Special Lieutenant Orders into Tactical Orders usable by this Squalos. If it is **Fireteam Leader**, the converted Order can activate the intact Duo. Its **own Tactical Awareness** Order can also lead that Duo.
- **Combat Instinct**: resistance to Surprise Attack/Stealth tricks; relevant for defending a large exposed target.
- **Trade-offs**: still vulnerable to Total Control, lacks short-range template/mine defense, and is much easier to draw LoF to than S2 infantry.

**Lieutenant interaction:** A **19pt Magistrate Strategos L1 Lieutenant** may convert its Special Lieutenant Order to a Regular Order at Order Count. **That conversion and NCO consumption of the same Order are alternatives, not additive Orders.** NCO is most valuable when the Lieutenant Order would otherwise remain special, and it specifically allows a Squalos-led Fireteam activation. Do not double count the converted Order. [NCO rules](https://infinitythewiki.com/NCO) · [Strategos](https://infinitythewiki.com/Strategos).

**Why pay 4 more than HMG/GL?** The gain is sustained ranged AP fire and NCO rather than an indirect grenade. Unless your list has a planned speculative-fire role, the MULTI HMG is the more generally useful weapon.

## 62-point HMG + Grenade Launcher — when is Speculative Attack useful?

**Real niche:** attacking visible or hidden grouped infantry behind terrain, threatening units whose LoF can be avoided, and projecting circular templates without taking the enemy's preferred direct gunfight. Speculative Attack ignores negative Mimetism, Partial Cover and visibility modifiers, subject to its other requirements, but it is a **Long Skill**, B1 and takes **-6** to the attack Attribute. A legal trajectory and impact point are still required. The normal Grenade Launcher is **not E/M**: it is not the same anti-tech threat as Emily's or an infiltrating Jackal's E/M launcher.

**At 0–24"**, with no other modifiers, the Squalos normally rolls **BS15 - 6 = 9**: **45% unopposed chance** to place the template correctly per entire Speculative Attack Order, *before* any target's Dodge and Saving Rolls. At 24–32" the launcher takes -3 range (BS6, 30% unopposed); at 32–40" it takes -6 range (BS3, 15%). The normal Grenade Launcher has standard **PS7** before Squalos's separate SR-1 modifier; do not invent a PS6 profile override.

**Critical N5.3 restriction: A two-Squalos FT2 does NOT raise speculative-fire hit probability to about 70%.** The FT2 BS Attack (+1SD) bonus **cannot be applied to Long Skills**; Speculative Attack is a Long Skill. The 45% unopposed baseline remains 45% in the Duo. The FT2 bonus *does* help eligible ordinary BS Attacks made by a Squalos Leader.

What the launcher actually buys:

- **Useful:** an enemy sheltering behind a wall within a legal arc, especially several defenders under one template; an exposed Mimetism(-6) specialist who otherwise forces ugly gunfights.
- **Less useful:** single troops that can safely Dodge, targets with high ARM, time-critical situations where repeatedly spending a TAG Order on a B1 speculative roll is prohibitive.
- **Opportunity cost:** the 62pt Squalos has only a **normal HMG**, like the Tikbalang, but lacks the Tik's Mimetism, Climbing Plus, HFT and mines. The 66pt Squalos gives up the launcher and gains AP MULTI HMG plus NCO.

**Verdict:** pick HMG/GL for a *specific terrain/indirect-fire plan*, not because speculative fire is generically efficient. Against enemy TAGs, Kestrel's **11pt infiltrating Jackal E/M Grenade Launcher** is often the more focused indirect disabling tool; **Emily's E/M GL** is another option, but has its own opportunity costs.

Sources: [Speculative Attack](https://infinitythewiki.com/Speculative_Attack) · [Weapon Chart](https://infinitythewiki.com/Weapons_Chart) · [Fireteam Bonuses](https://infinitythewiki.com/Fireteam_Bonuses).

## 55-point MULTI Marksman Rifle — budget TAG or oversized HI?

It is tempting at **55 points**: BS15, ARM6/BTS6, 3W, Tactical Awareness, ECM, Combat Instinct and SR-1. It has B3 AP or Shock at its 8–24" positive band and a **B1 DA** anti-materiel option. But **it is not a long-range HMG replacement**; at 24–32" it is in a negative range band. Its most important weakness relative to infantry competitors is not just cost: **the TAG's S6 silhouette and movement restrictions**.

### Comparison with Hetkari and Crux Knight

| Characteristic | Squalos MMR | Hetkari MULTI Red Fury | Crux Knight FTO MMR |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Points / SWC** | **55 / 1** | **48 / 1.5** | **46 / 0** |
| BS / ammo | BS15; B3 AP/Shock, B1 DA | BS14; B4 AP/Shock, B1 DA | BS14; B3 AP/Shock, B1 DA |
| Main offensive modifier | **SR-1** (enemy save) | **Marksmanship** (no cover BS penalty) | **Native +1SD** (one extra die, discard one) |
| ARM / BTS / W | 6 / 6 / 3 | 5 / 6 / 2 | 5 / 6 / 2 |
| **Silhouette** | **S6** | **S2** | **S2** |
| **May go Prone** | **No — TAG restriction** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| **May declare Cautious Movement** | **No — TAG restriction** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| **Vulnerable to Total Control** | **Yes — can be Possessed** | **No — HI is not a valid Total Control target** | **No — HI is not a valid Total Control target** |
| Other | Tactical Awareness, Remote Presence, ECM | Combat Instinct, E/Marat | Albedo, Specialist Operative, Drop Bears, Martial Arts L2, stronger CC |

**Do not underestimate S2.** The Squalos has a 40mm-wide, 55mm-high silhouette and cannot go Prone; the S2 HI have a 25mm-wide, 40mm-high standing silhouette and can drop to SX height while Prone. They can hide behind low walls/parapets, avoid LoF much more readily between turns, move through S2-only narrow access points and use Cautious Movement when legal. **Not being a valid target at all is often better than relying on ARM6 and a third Wound.** Even an S6 TAG that can see over an obstacle has to accept that it is easier to see in return.

**Examples of actual BS range/cover math at 8–24":**
- **Hetkari** with Marksmanship shooting an opponent in Partial Cover: BS14 +3 range = **17** (ignores target cover's -3 BS modifier); its MULTI Red Fury is **B4**.
- **Squalos MMR** shooting an opponent in Partial Cover: BS15 +3 range -3 cover = **15**, **B3**, with SR-1 affecting the resulting Saving Roll.
- **Crux MMR** shooting an opponent in Partial Cover: BS14 +3 range -3 cover = **14**, **B3 with native +1SD**, plus Albedo against relevant MSV opposition.

These values assume no other MODs and do **not** alone prove the winner of every Face-to-Face or saving-roll outcome. Also, **Crux's documented PS=5 profile override applies to its DA CC Weapon, NOT its MULTI Marksman Rifle**; the standard MMR chart is PS7.

### Practical verdict

- **Hetkari48**: generally a superior *conventional midrange shooter* into covered targets—B4 and Marksmanship, smaller S2 footprint, 7 points saved. Needs a separate answer to some hacking/mission roles and lacks Tactical Awareness.
- **Crux FTO46**: often superior *versatile attacking specialist*—S2, native +1SD, Albedo, Specialist Operative, Drop Bears, close-combat competence and no SWC. The Squalos's extra Wound and Tactical Awareness remain meaningful.
- **Squalos MMR55**: take for a **durable, independent Tactical Awareness TAG** or an economical ARMORED Duo anchor, **not** for better midrange shooting, easier concealment or mission interaction than these HI.

**Rating: B- / conditional** relative to Kestrel alternatives. It is a real budget TAG, but not a universally superior 55-point package. The S2 vs S6 difference is particularly large on dense boards.

## TAG-only disadvantages beyond silhouette

**Squalos and Tikbalang share these limitations**, while Hetkari and Crux do not:

1. **Cannot enter Prone State.** They cannot lower themselves to SX behind a parapet; this applies even when Unconscious (TAGs are exempt from the ordinary automatic Prone result).
2. **Cannot declare Cautious Movement.** Infantry sometimes can cross a line of sight without the usual ARO exposure; TAGs cannot use that movement option.
3. **Access and positioning:** S6 models need larger legal clearances, cannot use narrow S2-only accesses, are harder to hide entirely and more awkward to keep in cover.
4. **Can be targeted by Total Control.** Unlike HI, a TAG can enter **Possessed State** on a failed BTS Saving Roll from that program; the opponent can activate it with their Orders, and possession automatically removes it from a Fireteam. The owner can cancel possession during the relevant Tactical Phase by spending a Command Token or use a successful friendly Total Control attack. **ECM: Guided (-6) does not by itself make the TAG immune to Total Control**.

**Hacking distinction:** HI and TAGs are both generally Hackable by Carbonite/Oblivion/Spotlight, so do not present those as unique TAG weaknesses. **Total Control is the important TAG-specific extra risk**. Avoid ending an expensive TAG in easily accessed enemy Hacking Areas. Use the Engineer/Palbot recovery plan for recoverable technical states, but do not treat ordinary repairs as an automatic answer to Possessed State.

Sources: [Restrictions Chart](https://infinitythewiki.com/Restrictions_Chart) · [Total Control](https://infinitythewiki.com/Total_Control) · [Possessed State](https://infinitythewiki.com/Possessed_State).

## Squalos versus Tikbalang69 — different primary TAG priorities

Both are BS15, ARM6/BTS6, W3, S6, 6-4", Tactical Awareness, Remote Presence and SR-1 with ECM: Guided (-6). Neither can Prone or Cautious Move, and both are Total Control targets.

| Situation | Better fit | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Remove normal infantry / many direct gunfighting AROs | **Tikbalang69** | Mimetism (-3) makes return fire harder; Climbing Plus opens flank angles |
| Attack enemy high ARM with a long gun | **Squalos MULTI HMG66** | AP B4 compared with Tik's normal HMG |
| Vertical board movement | **Tikbalang69** | Climbing Plus and Terrain (Total) |
| Emergency close-range defense / protecting retreats | **Tikbalang69** | Heavy Flamethrower + Shock Mines |
| NCO use in a TAG + Pathfinder Duo | **Squalos MULTI HMG66** | Can use an unconverted Lieutenant Order to lead the team |
| Resist Surprise Attack and Stealth tricks | **Squalos** | Combat Instinct |
| Cheapest TAG anchor | **Squalos MMR55** | But gives up HMG range and many Tik advantages |

**Primary decision:** Prefer the **Tik** when another unit can provide AP/K1/E/M anti-armor and the map rewards Mimetism/Climbing Plus. Prefer the **66pt Squalos** when the TAG itself must supply AP HMG pressure, or its NCO is especially productive. The **62pt ordinary HMG Squalos** is not a straight budget Tik replacement: it loses much of the Tik's board-control kit and must earn its slot through the Grenade Launcher.

## Fireteams — best pairs and what FT level means

**ARMORED is Duo-only** and needs a native **Squalos or Tikbalang** as an anchor. A Squalos linked to a **different-unit Pathfinder/Firefly/etc.** is **FT1**, not FT2, and does **not** gain +1SD. Two Squalos are the same Unit and do receive **FT2** while the Duo remains valid.

| Duo | Total pts / SWC | FT level | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Squalos MULTI HMG NCO66 + Pathfinder TA17** | **83 / 1.5** | FT1 | **Preferred mobile TAG package:** AP, NCO, FO/Sensor/Repeater, two Tactical Awareness sources |
| **Squalos MMR55 + Pathfinder TA17** | **72 / 1** | FT1 | Best budget scoring/utility Duo if a TAG is specifically desired |
| Squalos HMG/GL62 + Pathfinder TA17 | **79 / 1.5** | FT1 | Niche indirect gun plus mission access |
| Squalos MULTI HMG NCO66 + Firefly Engineer23 | **89 / 1.5** | FT1 | Attached repair/NCO option, but MOV10-10 partner slows a MOV15-10 TAG |
| Squalos MMR55 + Firefly Engineer23 | **78 / 1** | FT1 | Cheaper repair duo with similar movement risks |

**Pathfinder Tactical Awareness details:** Its restricted Tactical Order can activate an intact Duo **with Pathfinder leading**, while the Squalos's Tactical Order can activate the Duo **with Squalos leading**. Pathfinder cannot donate its Tactical Order to a Squalos-led attack. The Pathfinder matches the 15-10 MOV, supplies FO/Sensor/Repeater and can detach to score if the TAG takes a different route. The Squalos66's **NCO Order can also activate its intact Duo with Squalos leading**, subject to normal NCO restrictions.

**Repair:** An independent **Machinist15 + Palbot3 = 18pts** is often easier to keep alive than escorting a TAG with a Firefly Engineer23; place the Palbot to cover a plausible recovery position. With a genuine linked Engineer already in range, avoid redundant support without a reason. See [Machinist](machinist.md) and [Palbot](palbot.md).

### Is a two-Squalos Duo ever worth it?

**Verdict: niche heavy-armor skew, not a routine list or merely a cosmetic vanity team.** It has meaningful advantages—**FT2 +1SD on eligible normal attacks, two Tactical Awareness Orders, two independent attack platforms that move as a Duo**—but locks a very large share of 300 points into two exposed S6, Hackable, potentially Possessable models. FT2 does **not** increase Burst, does **not** make two TAGs fire simultaneously, and **does not help speculative grenade attacks** (Long Skill).

| Pair | Points / SWC | Role | Decision |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 2 × MMR55 | **110 / 2** | Cheapest double TAG | Too much redundant midrange exposure |
| MMR55 + HMG/GL62 | **117 / 2.5** | Different range bands, indirect-fire option | Niche; **no speculative +1SD** |
| **MMR55 + MULTI HMG NCO66** | **121 / 2.5** | **Best balanced two-Squalos pair** | AP long gun + midrange partner; NCO and two TacAware Orders |
| HMG/GL62 + MULTI HMG NCO66 | **128 / 3** | Most weapon variety | Very expensive for optional speculative attack |
| 2 × MULTI HMG NCO66 | **132 / 3** | Maximum redundant AP long guns | Excessive concentration; usually skip |

**121 points = 40.3% of a 300pt list**, before specialist support or a Machinist/Palbot. A coherent Fireteam also means the models expose one another to enemy firing lanes, template positioning, hacking-area overlap and awkward S6 routes. Each TAG's TacAware is valuable, but its associated trooper leads when spending that Tactical Order.

**When it can make sense:** a deliberate double-TAG assault list with suitable terrain, sufficient alternate specialists, a credible repair plan and a match-up where two AP-capable durable threats strain enemy defenses. **When to skip:** dense/narrow terrain, high Total Control risk, dispersed objective missions, or when a cheaper gunfighter + specialist/defender produces better order and board coverage.

**Existing guide caveat:** [Kestrel Fireteams](../kestrel-fireteams.md) currently calls double Squalos “vanity/thematic only.” The conclusion here is more nuanced: **niche but mechanically defensible**, never automatic. This note does not silently alter that existing assessment.

## Practical selection checklist

- Need **AP at HMG range** and/or **NCO for an ARMORED Duo**? **Squalos MULTI HMG66**.
- Already have reliable anti-ARM, need **Mimetism, climbing, mines and a HFT**? **Tikbalang69** instead.
- Need a cheap **8–24" attack specialist**? Compare **Crux FTO MMR46** first; it is S2, Prone-capable and does objectives.
- Need a cheap **midrange covered-target gunfighter**? Compare **Hetkari MULTI Red Fury48** first; S2, B4 and Marksmanship.
- Need an inexpensive **durable TAG** specifically to hold/contest a space, supply Tactical Awareness and anchor an ARMORED Duo? **Squalos MMR55**, conditional.
- Have a *real* recurring use for **indirect normal grenades** against hidden/clustered enemies? **Squalos HMG/GL62**, niche, but do **not** count on FT2 +1SD for Speculative Attack.
- Want two Squalos to get **FT2 +1SD on direct shooting** and carry complementary AP/midrange weapons? **121/2.5 MMR + MULTI HMG** is the best balanced skew, with high opportunity cost.
- Facing serious **Total Control**, narrow lanes, or tables where **Prone S2** dominates hiding? Weight both HI options much more highly.

**Bottom line:** **MULTI HMG NCO is the go-to Squalos**, but **Tikbalang is often the superior general-purpose mobile TAG**. **MMR Squalos is B-/conditional against smaller Hetkari/Crux rivals**, and **HMG/GL is a conscious niche** rather than a default speculative-fire engine.
