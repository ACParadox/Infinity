# Yamabushi — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: A-tier utility warband; A+ for the 16pt Camouflage + Shock Mine option.** The mine carrier is the default, particularly in Group 2. It generates a Regular Order even while Camouflaged, can advance 6" and place a mine in a single activation, and is a credible melee assassin, defensive roadblock and offensive mine-fork piece. Unlike infiltrating Jackals and solo Beasthunters, its Order can be spent on another trooper in the same group. Start with one; two are attractive in distributed lists, not mandatory.

## Why the Shock Mine profile wins

The mine is a genuinely distinct tactical tool rather than another gun competing with CC and the Chain Rifle. Three Shock Mines (Disposable 3) allow persistent defense, offensive forks and protection of friendly attackers. They need no BS Roll to place normally or to hit when triggered. A mine is a concealed CAMO(-3) marker, PS7, Shock, Small Teardrop Direct Template; Dodging it imposes PH−3.

- **Group 2 utility:** deploy forward in a protected position and bank a Regular Order from turn one. On a turn without better demands, spend one pooled Order on Move 6" + Place Deployable. The mine can be placed in silhouette contact with any part of the route, allowing the Yamabushi to finish behind Total Cover while leaving a mine on the approach.
- **No Minelayer:** the first mine costs an activation; its placement is an Attack and reveals Camouflage. Camouflage (1 Use) cannot be renewed after that initial marker state is lost. Preserve the marker until the position or attack is worth revealing for.
- **Defense:** cover an objective, corner, TAG approach or vulnerable support piece; force enemies to clear or Dodge the mine before committing. The Yamabushi's Chain Rifle and CC threaten another angle.
- **Reactive use:** Place Deployable is a legal ARO with LoF to the active trooper, subject to the normal placement rules. A newly placed mine cannot trigger until a subsequent Order.
- **Placement constraints:** the mine's location must be legal and supported. An enemy Camouflage marker in the trigger area normally prevents placement unless the stated exception applies (valid non-camouflaged enemy or Intuitive Attack). Avoid exposing the carrier simply to spend an Order.

## Offensive mine forks (similar principle to Tikbalang)

The Yamabushi can use mines proactively rather than merely defend a lane:

1. Move + place a mine on a safe angle threatening an enemy's position or likely route.
2. On a subsequent Order, move the Yamabushi (or another attacker) into a useful firing/attack angle.
3. If the enemy declares or executes an applicable Skill/ARO within the mine's trigger area, the mine must trigger, provided its template can legally hit without affecting allies.
4. An enemy shooting back rather than Dodging may take the mine hit regardless of the outcome of its Face-to-Face Roll against the attacker. A Dodge can potentially avoid both attacks, subject to the applicable rolls and modifiers.

Useful combinations:
- **Mine + Chain Rifle:** two independent direct-template threats; shooting back risks both hits. Excellent against clustered or fragile enemies.
- **Mine + Heavy Pistol:** a rolled B2 attack plus a mine hit. Useful against light infantry when the Yamabushi has a favorable angle.
- **Mine + friendly primary gunfighter:** set the fork for a Griffin, Tikbalang or another attacker, respecting combat group Order pools.
- **Threat of CC:** a mine and a nearby CC23/MA3 Yamabushi create a positional dilemma. A literal simultaneous mine + CC fork is geometry-dependent: a mine never triggers if its template would hit a friendly model, including the Yamabushi in contact with the target. Usually use the mine to force movement or a Dodge first, then exploit the melee opening.

Unlike the Tikbalang, the Yamabushi is not a durable high-Burst gunfighter. The analogy is the independent mine threat, not equivalent shooting power. Use it to manufacture favorable exchanges rather than force ordinary gun duels.

## Why not prioritize the Panzerfaust or Bow?

### Panzerfaust

B1, PS6, AP+EXP, three saves per hit, Disposable (2), +3 range at 16–32". BS11 gives:

| Target in optimal range | Success Value (before opponent's roll) |
|---|---:|
| No other MODs | 14 |
| Partial Cover | 11 |
| Cover + Mimetism (-3) | 8 |
| Cover + Mimetism (-6) | 5 |

The Yamabushi has **no Surprise Attack**, even on the Camouflage options. Camouflage is valuable for selecting an engagement, but does not automatically impose a Surprise Attack MOD. The Panzerfaust can be devastating on an opportunistic Normal Roll or against an expensive target that cannot answer effectively. It is a poor reason to deliberately spend scarce Regular Orders seeking fair B1 gunfights. Take it if the list needs an additional long-range anti-armor tripwire; otherwise prioritize mines.

### Tactical Bow (+1B)

Tactical Bow: PS8, DA, B1 (B2 on option 5), Silent (-6), Anti-materiel, +3 at 0–8", 0 at 8–16", −6 at 16–24". The B2 variant offers a legitimate attack against multi-Wound targets outside melee reach or a qualifying Silent attack. However, it is still BS11 and overlaps the unit's other close-range weapons.

| Weapon | Distinct use |
|---|---|
| Chain Rifle | PS7, B1 Large Teardrop Direct Template; reliable trading without a BS Roll |
| Heavy Pistol | PS6, B2 Shock; +3 at 0–8"; better raw PS against vulnerable infantry |
| Tactical Bow | PS8, DA; niche silent attacks and two saves per hit |
| DA CC Weapon | Dedicated melee, Anti-materiel, CC Attack (Shock) and MA3 |
| Shock Mine | Persistent control and independent attack forks |

If close enough to use the Bow, often the better play is to threaten CC, trade with a Chain Rifle, shoot the Heavy Pistol or prepare a mine fork. The mine carrier already has the B1 Bow if a qualifying opportunity arises. Do not pay more just for the B2 Bow unless that specific use is intended.

## Melee, Dodge and Dogged

Martial Arts L3 grants +3 CC, −3 to the opponent in Face-to-Face Rolls and +1 SD in both turns: CC26 before opposing modifiers, roll two dice and discard one, **not B2**. The DA CCW forces two saves per hit, with CC Attack (Shock) adding Shock. Excellent against ordinary infantry, REMs and many non-specialist TAGs. Against dedicated CC fighters, account for their own skills and MODs.

Dodge (+5) gives PH18 before situational penalties. Stealth, Camouflage (1 Use) and 6" first MOV help approach; there is no native Smoke/Eclipse, so exposed long-range lanes remain a real problem. Do not assume Stealth prevents all AROs.

Dogged allows it to remain operational for the rest of the current Player Turn after entering Unconscious, if activated. Shock that sends it directly to Dead or sufficient additional wounds bypass/end Dogged. It dies at the end of that Player Turn and cannot be healed while Dogged. In the Active Turn this can buy the last attack, legal mine placement or (Paramedic) objective interaction; in the Reactive Turn it can keep a roadblock active, but it will not generate a Regular Order next turn.

## Frenzy and Group 2 Order economy

- **Regular Order while in Camouflage:** unlike Hidden Deployment, a deployed Camouflaged Yamabushi contributes to its group's pool normally.
- **Revealing does not activate Frenzy.** It must directly inflict a Wound or cause an enemy to enter Dead State. Damage from its deployed mines explicitly does not count.
- When triggered, Frenzy grants Impetuous and Limited Cover in the States Phase of that Player Turn, for the remainder of the game. It cancels marker state and prohibits re-entering it.
- Impetuous provides an optional free personal activation in later Impetuous Phases, not an additional pooled Regular Order. Movement and skill combinations are restricted; Move + Move, Move + Dodge, Move + BS Attack and Move + CC Attack can be useful, but **Move + Place Deployable is not an allowed Impetuous combination**.
- Limited Cover removes the enemy's −3 BS MOD from Partial Cover in **both Active and Reactive Turns**; saving-roll cover benefits remain when otherwise applicable. Mimetism remains. Prefer Total Cover.

Practical progression: T1 bank its Regular Order or invest one in advancing and deploying a mine; T2 defend the position or commit to an opportunistic attack; T3 exploit a surviving Frenzy-triggered extra activation, if safe, and remaining pooled Orders for attacking or zone control. It is entirely acceptable never to trigger Frenzy if the unit's Order, mines and deterrence are doing the job.

## Paramedic: mission-dependent alternative

The 16pt FTO Paramedic is a forward-deployed Regular WIP13 Specialist with Dogged, excellent Dodge and melee deterrence. Worth considering for objective-heavy missions when a cheap combat-capable specialist is wanted. It lacks both mines and Camouflage, and cannot join a Kestrel Fireteam. A Pathfinder is generally preferable for dedicated speedy objective work, Sensor and FO utility; the Yamabushi is better at defending itself in close quarters. Do not take the Paramedic merely for healing.

## Competing utility pieces

| Trooper | Cost | Order | Key differentiator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yamabushi Shock Mine | 16/0 | Regular | CC23/MA3, Dogged, Dodge +5, FWD4", one-use Camo, Chain Rifle, mines |
| Camo Beasthunter | 15–17/0 | Irregular | FWD8", reusable Camo, Super-Jump, AP Mines, HFT (B2 on premium profile) |
| Infiltrating Jackal | 10–11/0–0.5 | Irregular | Deeper deployment, E/M disruption, Shock Mines on options 3/5, Minelayer on option 5 |

Jackals are cheaper and better for turn-zero control or E/M, while Beasthunters are better for highly destructive template trades and unexpected angles. Yamabushi have superior dedicated melee, and their pooled Regular Order is especially attractive as other Irregular choices accumulate. A Yamabushi is normally worth considering before adding another standalone Irregular utility piece.

## How many?

- **0:** reasonable when a heavy TAG/Fireteam list already has sufficient defensive utility and needs specialist slots or points elsewhere.
- **1:** default starting point; prefer the 16pt mine version in Group 2.
- **2:** strong option for distributed lists; two mine carriers cost 32pt/0SWC, generate two Regular Orders and bring up to six mines and two melee threats. No need to diversify into a Panzerfaust version just for variety.

Illustrative Group 2 package:

| Trooper | Pts | SWC |
|---|---:|---:|
| Yamabushi Shock Mine ×2 | 32 | 0 |
| Infiltrating Jackal E/M GL Minelayer | 11 | 0.5 |
| Infiltrating Jackal E/M Carbine | 10 | 0 |
| Total | 53 | 0.5 |

Two pooled Regular Orders, two personal Irregular Orders, four mine carriers (one prepaid mine), melee and E/M threats. The Regular Orders can fund either Yamabushi or reinforce a Jackal attack; the Jackals' personal Orders often finance their own local actions. Further group members may be necessary if expecting sustained offensive runs.

**Bottom line:** choose the mine option for its flexible Order economy, reliable placement, mine forks and credible close combat. The Panzerfaust and stronger Bow are interesting situational weapons, not the defining reason to bring Yamabushi.
