# Minescorp Jackals — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: A+/S-value cheap utility, not a mandatory fixed quota.** Jackals have two very different jobs: **6pt FTOs** become economical pooled Regular Orders and deployment-zone defenders when linked to a BCA Magistrate; **10–11pt Infiltrators** are autonomous, Irregular midfield roadblocks with E/M, Boarding Pistols and (on two profiles) Shock Mines. Start by considering **one infiltrating Jackal** in balanced lists, and consider **two** when you have the trooper slots/AVA and intend to exploit independent midfield Orders. The **11pt/0.5SWC E/M Grenade Launcher + Minelayer** is the strongest turn-zero control package; the **10pt/0SWC E/M Carbine + Boarding Shotgun + Shock Mine** is the best SWC-free infiltrator. Neither infiltrator can join a Magistrate Fireteam.

**Why they work:** Their cheap personal Irregular Orders can be *self-funded midfield actions*, rather than stranded Order tokens: an E/M GL Jackal can make a legal Speculative Attack from cover or plant another mine, while an E/M Carbine Jackal can plant a mine or shoot in a favorable short lane. **Only the E/M GL profile can Speculative Attack**; do not transfer this capability to the 10pt E/M Carbine. Orders are not guaranteed to be productive every turn—targets, trajectory, mine stock, geometry and survival still matter.

## Chassis, availability and exact five options

**Shared stats:** ID 1813; AVA **4**; Irregular LI; MOV **10–10 cm** (4–4"), CC14, **BS11**, PH10, WIP12, **ARM0, BTS0, W1, S2**. Fragile, exposed one-Wound bodies with no general-purpose Camo, Mimetism or specialist skill. All five have a **Boarding Pistol** (its **Small Teardrop Direct Template** Blast mode is a useful emergency trade tool) and CCW. **Booty applies to the two FTO profiles only**, as recorded in the pinned roster.

All five consume **one trooper slot each** and share the **AVA 4** cap. The 6pt FTOs are **not Infiltrators**; the 10–11pt Infiltrators are **not FTO/ColCorp candidates** and receive no Magistrate FT Master benefit.

## Infiltrators: the self-funded Order argument

An infiltrating Jackal can deploy safely **anywhere in your half of the table** without an Infiltration Roll, including a protected position near the center line. It thus skips the Orders that a rear-deployed cheap trooper would spend just *reaching* the relevant corridor. Its **Irregular Order is personal**: it cannot be freely spent on a TAG, Griffin or other attacker, but it can finance a useful Jackal activation without draining Group 1.

- **Option 5, E/M GL Minelayer (11/0.5):** On deployment, place a **Shock Mine** legally within ZoC using Minelayer, **for zero Orders**. From a protected midfield position, use a later personal Irregular Order for a **legal Speculative E/M GL** shot against a target out of LoF, or **Move + Place Deployable** to add another mine/advance the trap. This is the superior *passive plus self-funding* area-control variant. It spends one of the mine's disposable uses at deployment.
- **Option 3, E/M Carbine + BSG + Shock Mine (10/0):** No free preplaced mine and **no Speculative Attack**. Its Irregular Order can **Move + Place a Shock Mine**, re-angle a midfield defense, or fire a **reusable B2 E/M Carbine** from a short favorable position. The **Boarding Shotgun** is an excellent AP close-range backup. This is the **best infiltrating choice with no SWC left**, and has a more credible *direct* close-range attack than the GL.
- **Option 4, Combi + Blitzen (10/0):** Can reposition or shoot a long E/M ARO lane, but has **no mine and no speculative weapon**. It is the weakest *routine self-order utility* profile and is mainly a deliberate 16–32" defensive counter to heavy hardware.

**Not literally "never wasted":** An E/M GL requires a legal target, firing trajectory, range and an opportunity worth a low-probability shot; it is not a targetless bombardment. Mines have limited uses and legal placement constraints. If all meaningful actions expose a Jackal to an easy kill, preserving it for a future ARO can be better than spending its Irregular Order. These limitations do not negate how often **starting in midfield** makes its personal Order useful.

### The E/M GL's real accuracy

The E/M Grenade Launcher is **B1**, with range MOD **0 through 24"**. **Speculative Attack applies −6**, so a base **BS11** Jackal fires **on 5 or less (25% chance)** within 24", before any opposed Dodge. At 24–32", the GL's −3 range band worsens that to **BS2 (10%)**. A Speculative Attack is a **Long Skill**, not Move + Shoot; use the personal Irregular Order for the whole attack. It may target models outside LoF only when the attack has a valid target and a permitted trajectory.

This is **opportunistic disruption**, not a reliable source of kills. On a hit, **E/M forces two saves against halved BTS** and can inflict Isolated (and IMM-B on HI/TAG/REM/VH), so a low-probability success can swing the turn. Dodge and target immunities/repair options matter. The *prepaid mine* is the dependable part of the 11pt profile; the E/M GL makes the Irregular Order more frequently relevant.

### E/M Carbine versus Blitzen when SWC = 0

| Factor | **Option 3: E/M Carbine** | **Option 4: Blitzen** |
|---|---|---|
| Price | **10/0** | **10/0** |
| E/M weapon | **B2** active, repeatable | **B1**, Disposable (2) |
| Best E/M range | **0–16" (+3)** | **16–32" (+3)** |
| Secondary | **Boarding Shotgun**: B2 AP, +6 within 8" | **Combi Rifle**: more ordinary anti-infantry reach |
| Mine? | **Yes**, carried Shock Mine | **No** |
| Speculative Attack? | **No** | **No** |
| Best use | Ambush, repeat E/M, place mine, close direct trade | Hold a longer lane for a single high-impact E/M ARO |

**Default option 3:** BS11 becomes **BS14** with the E/M Carbine in its +3 band (before opponent MODs), or **BS17** with the Boarding Shotgun in its +6 band, with normal active Burst 2. A good angle matters more than trying to take a face-to-face gun duel with an elite attacker. The Boarding Shotgun **does not have a Direct Template in N5**; the **Boarding Pistol** does. The Shock Mine is **Shock, not E/M**.

**Pick option 4** when you genuinely require a long-range infiltrating E/M tripwire (e.g., an exposed TAG route at 16–32") and are content to trade away mine placement and short-range repeatable electronic pressure.

### Infiltration placement and survival

- Deploy **on your own half near the relevant midfield objective or approach**, behind Total Cover with a mine/launcher route in mind. Infiltration alone does **not** provide Camouflage, Hidden Deployment or the ability to remain invisible.
- An attempt into the **enemy half** requires **PH−3 = target 7 on PH10**, only **35% success**. A failed roll sends the Jackal back to its DZ/table edge; a Minelayer loses the initial deployed item under the rule. Usually avoid the roll for these 1W utility bodies.
- Put the **mine and Jackal on overlapping but distinct angles** so the attacker cannot resolve both with one trivial exposure. Minelayer placement must remain in legal deployment territory and obey trigger-area restrictions.
- A mine carrier can spend a personal Order **Move + Place Deployable**; a non-Minelayer *must* pay an Order to put its first Shock Mine down. **Shock Mine Disposable (3)** means the Minelayer normally starts with **two remaining** after its prepaid mine, while the option-3 carrier starts with **three**.
- A 10–11pt model is allowed to die making a favorable trade; leaving it standing in open LoF just because it was cheap is still poor deployment.

## The 6-point FTO: deployment-zone defense with a Magistrate

When a **Magistrate** is in a legal **COLCORP Duo/Haris** with 6pt Jackal FTOs, the Magistrate's **FT Master (Jackals)** makes the linked Jackals **Regular at Order Count**. Each yields **one pooled Regular Order rather than a personal Irregular Order**; this does **not** give an extra Order. If the FT Master/team is lost, future Order Counts lose the conversion. A COLCORP Duo **preserves Kestrel's sole Haris slot**; a three-model COLCORP Haris consumes it.

The two 6pt choices are both viable, but attack different lanes:

- **Option 1, SMG + Panzerfaust (6/0):** Preferred **hard-target/open-lane** defense. Panzerfaust is **AP+EXP**, Disposable (2), **+3 at 16–32"**. Against a TAG/HI, the cost-to-threat ratio is excellent, but a BS11, W1 ARO remains unreliable if challenged on favorable enemy terms. SMG remains for close defense and later turns. The Boarding Pistol also allows a direct-template close trade.
- **Option 2, Light Rocket Launcher (6/0):** Preferred **infantry/airborne-entry/cross-lane** defense. The LRL is **reusable**, projects Continuous Damage and has a **Circular Impact Template** mode; good at **8–24"**. Its Blast attack is an **impact template**, **not** an automatic Direct Template. The Boarding Pistol provides the separate close Direct Template.

**FT2** (Magistrate + one Jackal counting as ColCorp) confers **+1 Special Die** to applicable BS attacks: roll one extra die and discard one, **not +1 Burst**. It can improve a launcher ARO but **not** a direct-template Boarding Pistol attack. **FT3** adds Discover/Dodge benefits, not the FT4 +1 BS bonus.

**Defensive setup:** Put the Magistrate/CoC or LT behind Total Cover, keeping it alive at Order Count; use the Jackal as the disposable guard. Duo Coherency means the two models cannot defend opposite DZ corners independently. Do not reveal the Magistrate to take marginal gunfights merely because it is linked.

Between the FTO weapons, select **Panzerfaust** if a long anti-heavy ARO is missing, and **LRL** if close/medium-range infantry and AD entry coverage matters more. Neither is universally superior; both retain the Boarding Pistol's cheap close trade.

## How many infiltrators, and which Combat Group?

**Zero is legal and sometimes correct; one is a very strong baseline; two are justified when the list actually wants distributed area denial.** They are cheap but not free: each takes one of fifteen trooper slots in ordinary 300pt listbuilding and also uses **shared Jackal AVA 4**.

| Infiltrating Jackals | Evaluation | When to choose |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Totally reasonable | Heavy slot pressure; other midfield pieces already cover lanes; FTO AVA needed; mission needs more specialists/Regular Orders |
| **1** | **Preferred balanced starting point** | One self-funded midfield E/M/mine pressure piece without consuming too many slots |
| **2** | **Strong defensive/skirmish plan** | Two distinct flanks, favorable midfield geometry, Group 2 autonomy, sufficient slots/AVA |
| **3–4** | Usually excessive | Specialized skew: sacrifices FTO availability, specialists and other unit types |

**Useful two-Jackal mixes:**
- **GL Minelayer 11/.5 + E/M Carbine 10/0 = 21pts/0.5SWC:** Probably the best **SWC-constrained dual package**. Starts **one Shock Mine** and retains two different E/M attack methods; both can spend personal Orders placing further mines.
- **Two GL Minelayers = 22pts/1SWC:** **Two starting mines** and up to **two self-funded Speculative Attack attempts each turn**, provided both survive and legal targets/trajectories exist. Very strong Turn-0 board coverage, but spending 1 SWC on two low-BS shots is a real cost.
- **Two E/M Carbine/BSG = 20pts/0SWC:** No predeployed mine, but **two reusable B2 E/M threats**, two carried mine stocks, and good close-angle trading. Both require Orders for their first mines.
- **Carbine + Blitzen = 20pts/0SWC:** Only if you intentionally need a close E/M attacker and a longer-range E/M ARO; usually weaker area control than two mine carriers.

### Group 2: independent usefulness, not fake shared Orders

- **One to two infiltrating Jackals in Group 2** can work very well: personal Irregular Orders activate *the Jackals themselves* and leave the Group 1 main attacker budget alone.
- **Two Jackals do not create a shared two-Order pool.** Their two Irregular Orders cannot be handed to another Group 2 troop without spending a **Command Token for conversion** under the appropriate rules, and that conversion remains in their own Combat Group.
- If you need follow-through—e.g. a Jackal disables a TAG and a scorer must capitalize—pair with **some genuine Regular Orders** in Group 2 or plan the handoff across turns. A pure-Irregular Group 2 is less flexible than its nominal Order count suggests.
- Don't deploy two Jackals on the same obvious shooting lane merely to fill Group 2. **Independent one-Order jobs** and *separate* approaches are what justify the second body.
- A Minelayer that begins with its mine already placed is useful **even if it never spends an Order**; the first minelayer's value is primarily its *turn-zero prepaid obstacle*, not a guarantee of three speculative attacks.

### The Magistrate AVA tradeoff

| Magistrate setup | FTO Jackals used | Remaining Jackal AVA for infiltrators |
|---|---:|---:|
| **One Magistrate + one Jackal Duo** | 1 | **3** |
| **One Magistrate + two Jackals Haris** | 2 | **2** |
| **Two Magistrate Duos, one Jackal each** | 2 | **2** |
| **Magistrate LT + two Jackals Haris, plus CoC Magistrate + one Jackal Duo** | 3 | **1** |

The last is a **legal constructed command/order package**, but the Haris slot and **three of AVA 4 Jackals** are committed. Having only one infiltrator left is acceptable; it is not a reason to abandon a better LT/CoC/Order architecture. With only a single Magistrate Duo, AVA is rarely the initial restriction—trooper slots and SWC usually bite sooner.

## Jackals versus alternative Kestrel investments

- **Scarecrow:** More expensive, more survivable and a meaningful midfield **specialist** with a stealth/marker game. Choose when objectives and late scoring matter more than an expendable E/M/mine speed bump.
- **Peacemaker + Auxbot (and optional mine):** Better **multi-body footprint per trooper slot**, native Repeater and close templates; costs materially more and a larger S4/REM footprint. Its area denial is different from a single S2 infiltrating Jackal.
- **Fennec FO/Sensor/Disco:** Gives a **native pooled Regular Order, specialist, Sensor and Eclipse utility**. A better choice when the list is starved for specialists and generic Order liquidity.
- **Fennec Minelayer:** Provides a Shock Mine at deployment and a **native Regular Order**, but its mine starts from the normal deployment area rather than a Jackal's forward infiltration position.
- **Magistrate + FTO Jackal:** Better **pooled Regular Order economy + command insurance/LT support** in a coherent backfield pocket, not the same role as a solo infiltrator.

**Slot-pressure test:** If adding a second infiltrating Jackal displaces the last reliable scorer, Sensor/Repeater node, vital Engineer/Doctor, or useful multi-model Peacemaker package, take **one**. If the scoring/support shell is complete and the list needs cheap midfield denial, **two** are highly attractive.

## Practical deployment and order checklist

1. **Before selection:** Count planned **FTO Jackals** and AVA remaining; decide whether you need **zero, one or two infiltrators**.
2. **Before deployment:** Identify an approach, objective or engineer/TAG path that the first Jackal can **influence from your half of midfield**.
3. **For the 11pt GL Minelayer:** Preplace a **legal Shock Mine** in a separate but supporting angle; don't confuse it with an E/M mine.
4. **For the 10pt Carbine:** Know where a **single Move + Place Deployable Order** can create a useful mine; keep close E/M/BSG lanes protected.
5. **For the 6pt FTO:** Choose Panzerfaust for long anti-armor lanes or LRL for reusable blast/infantry lanes; keep the **Magistrate safe**, satisfy Duo/Haris coherence.
6. **On your Active Turn:** Look for an immediately profitable **personal Irregular action** (spec shot on the *GL only*, direct E/M/shotgun attack, movement or mine placement). Do not manufacture a bad engagement just to spend the Order.
7. **Across the game:** Preserve independent Regular Orders for the primary attacker; use Group 2's limited shared Orders only where the self-funded Jackals created a valuable follow-up.
8. **On opponent's turn:** Prefer **costly trades and multiple defensive layers** to a fair BS11 face-to-face firefight against an elite shooter.

## Final selection hierarchy

1. **Infiltrating E/M GL + Shock Minelayer — 11pts/0.5SWC (opt 5):** Best one-model midfield denial and repeat optional self-funded speculative pressure; **not automatic if SWC tight**.
2. **Infiltrating BSG + E/M Carbine + Shock Mine — 10pts/0SWC (opt 3):** **Best zero-SWC infiltrator** and a great second partner; mine placement and repeatable short-range E/M can justify the Irregular Order every turn.
3. **FTO Panzerfaust/SMG — 6pts/0SWC (opt 1):** Excellent anti-heavy **Magistrate Duo** guard and Order conversion.
4. **FTO Light Rocket Launcher — 6pts/0SWC (opt 2):** Equally compelling **Magistrate Duo** guard for clustered/infantry entry coverage; choose by threatened approach, not by universal rank.
5. **Infiltrating Combi/Blitzen — 10pts/0SWC (opt 4):** Situational 16–32" E/M defense; normally loses to the Carbine's carried mine and repeatable direct attack.

**Bottom line:** **One** autonomous infiltrator is a strong balanced-list starting point; **two** are a defensible, sometimes excellent Group 2 area-denial package, particularly a **11/.5 GL Minelayer + 10/0 E/M Carbine** pair. In parallel, **one 6pt FTO Jackal linked to a Magistrate** remains among Kestrel's most efficient backfield command/Order packages. Do not call either the infiltrators or the Magistrate link mandatory, and never confuse the Infiltrating profiles with the FTO profiles.
