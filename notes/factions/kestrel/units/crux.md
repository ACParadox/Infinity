# Team Crux Father-Knights — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: S-tier, default solo 46/0 MULTI MMR.** A premium midfield assault specialist and area-denial piece, not just a gunfighter. Native +1SD, exceptional PS5 MULTI Marksman Rifle, Albedo (-6), durable HI chassis, CC, mines and mission tools make it unusually self-sufficient. An offensive FENNECS Haris is also excellent, but do not buy escorts solely for one extra Special Die if the intended end state is independent Suppressive Fire. Hidden Deployment profiles are niche/experimental, not regular picks.

## Why the MULTI MMR is the default

Lower PS means more dangerous hits. Crux has HMG-level stopping power per AP hit, three ammunition modes and native +1SD, without spending SWC.

| Weapon | Active B | PS | Ammo | 0–8in | 8–16 | 16–24 | 24–32 | 32–40 | 40–48 |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Crux MULTI MMR | 3 | **5** | AP / Shock; DA B1 | -3 | +3 | +3 | -3 | -3 | -6 |
| Crux Spitfire | 4 | 6 | Normal | 0 | +3 | +3 | -3 | -6 | -6 |
| Maximus AP Thunderbolt (+2B) | 4 | 6 | AP | -3 | 0 | +3 | +3 | 0 | 0 |
| Hetkari MULTI Red Fury | 4 | 7 | AP / Shock; DA B1 | 0 | +3 | +3 | -3 | -3 | -6 |
| AP SMG | 3 | 7 | AP | +3 | 0 | -3 | -6 | — | — |

Native +1SD adds a die to roll, not an additional potential hit. Solo MMR rolls four dice keeping three; FT2 rolls five keeping three. DA anti-materiel mode has B1 (plus applicable SD), with two saves per hit. MULTI Pistol covers 0–8in with AP/Shock/DA options. Spitfire's one additional potential hit and better very-close range rarely justify paying 3 points and 1.5 SWC to lose PS, AP, Shock, DA and 32–40in performance. If the role is sustained B4 conventional midrange shooting, consider Hetkari instead.

**Albedo (-6):** enemies with MSV or Marksmanship suffer -6 when declaring applicable LoF BS Attacks or Discover against Crux. It is not Mimetism: ordinary shooters are unaffected, and it does not negate their Mimetism. Example in mutual +3 range and cover: Crux rolls on BS14; an ordinary BS13 visor enemy rolls on 7. A Marksmanship enemy would ignore the cover BS penalty, so the numerical example changes. Neither Albedo nor SD prevents an unopposed direct-template hit.

## Solo MMR — preferred

Attack into the midfield, score objectives if useful, establish mines and finish in Suppressive Fire when the position merits another order. Crux can use its 6–2in movement, narrow routes, Berserk and Prone positions without dragging fragile 4–4in Fennecs along. Native +1SD means it does not require a team to be a credible primary or secondary attacker.

**Suppression is a major reason to favor solo.** Suppressive Fire is a separate Long Skill; it automatically removes Crux from any Fireteam. The MMR retains PS5 and the selected AP or Shock mode, uses B3 in ARO, and native +1SD still applies (roll four, keep three). Range is 0 at 0–16in, -3 at 16–24in, unavailable beyond 24in; the normal +3 band no longer applies. Do not use DA mode for suppression.

| Suppression platform | Crux MMR | Hetkari RF |
|---|---|---|
| Pts / SWC | **46/0** | 48/1.5 |
| Reactive dice | **B3 +1SD** | B3 |
| PS | **5** | 7 |
| Ammo | AP / Shock | AP / Shock |
| Enemy MOD in cover (before range/other effects) | -6 | **-9** |
| Against MSV (same assumptions) | **-12** | -9 |
| Shooting tools | Albedo (-6) | Marksmanship, BS Attack (-3) |
| CC | **CC22, MA2, NBW** | CC17 |
| Area tools | Drop Bears | E/Marat |
| Specialist | **Yes** | No |

The suppression penalty applies in qualifying FtF rolls within range; Hetkari's BS Attack (-3) is subject to exceptions such as Warhorse, and Marksmanship ignores cover BS penalties. Crux is the more complete objective/midfield occupation piece, while Hetkari is the better universal conventional shooting roadblock. Crux's Religious Troop automatically passes Guts Rolls unless electing the permitted WIP roll to withdraw, helping it hold the position; this can also force it to remain exposed.

Drop Bears have three shared uses, can be placed as deployables or thrown using PH, and become mines. A thrown mine does not detonate in the order it is placed. Mine approaches before entering suppression; do not mistake deployables for a personal direct-template weapon. Crux has no personal direct-template gun.

Avoid overexposure to hacking/E/M, unopposed templates, flank attacks, attacks beyond 24in, and opponents able to force cancellation of Suppressive Fire. Independent friendly specialist/hacking and mine/template screens are valuable. Suppressive Fire cannot be maintained while moving or taking a different ARO; a failed Guts Roll, qualifying states, Loss of Lieutenant or joining a Fireteam also cancel it. Use suppression selectively rather than as an automatic last order every turn.

**Preferred pairing:** a linked Griffin HMG for long-range lanes and MSV1, with solo Crux for midfield scoring, enemy MSV, CC and area defense. No SWC competition between Crux MMR and Griffin. A Black A.I.R. defensive Core can likewise coexist with solo Crux.

## CC, Berserk and mission play

CC22, MA2 and the strong DA CCW (PS5) make Crux a credible heavy-target assassin. NBW ignores opposing negative CC MODs only when Crux is targeted by a CC Attack and declares CC Attack in the CC FtF roll; it does not automatically negate all penalties against a shooting or dodging opponent. D-Charges enable demolition/classified tasks and a powerful alternative melee attack when legal.

**N5.3 Berserk is not the older Normal Roll trade mechanic.** It is a Long Skill allowing Move up to both MOV values combined (8in rather than 6in for ordinary Move + CC Attack), then CC Attack. Target must be in LoF at declaration; declare route and target before ARO. If unable to reach Silhouette contact, the entire action becomes Idle. Opposing applicable BS/CC Attacks and Dodges still produce FtF rolls. No guaranteed trade or inherent CC bonus on Crux's unmodified Berserk. When the Fireteam Leader declares Berserk, team members without the skill Idle, which can create coherency problems. Solo is more flexible for this role.

## Fireteams — excellent alternative, not mandatory

Only FTO options may link. Crux is a Wildcard max 1, **not** a Fennec/Kestrel Counts-as. Each family still needs its native anchor; one Core, one Haris, unlimited Duos. Two matching supports yield FT2 (+1SD); native +1SD stacks with this. FENNECS admits Core/Haris, not Duo. Keep the existing recommendation filter against ordinary Crux Duos.

| Offensive FENNECS Core/Haris | Pts / SWC | FT | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| MMR + Fennec FO/Disco13 + Paramedic12 | **71/0** | 2 | Cheapest preferred team: Eclipse, Sensor, healing |
| Strategic MMR + FO/Disco + Paramedic | **75/0** | 2 | Aggressive forward team |
| MMR + FO/Disco + Minelayer14 | 73/0 | 2 | Mine and backup gun instead of healing |
| MMR + FO/Disco + Bipandra Doctor23 | 82/0 | 2 | Doctor, reliable recovery; less economical |
| MMR + FO/Disco + Bipandra TA25 | 84/0 | 2 | Doctor and extra restricted team activation |
| MMR + Griffin HMG37 + FO/Disco13 | 96/1.5 | 2 | Complementary long and mid guns |
| Strategic MMR + Griffin HMG + FO/Disco | 100/1.5 | 2 | Forward two-gun package |

Crux receives +2SD total in these FT2 teams. Griffin and the Fennec match in the last compositions, not Crux. Griffin handles range and Mimetism/MSV1, Crux handles armor, anti-MSV and CC, Fennec provides Eclipse/Sensor. Griffin also has Pulzar for close template defense. Bipandra's Tactical Order is a separate restricted activation with her leading, not a free Crux gun attack. Paramedic is a cheaper medical option with Crux PH14; weigh the risk of a failed MediKit recovery versus a Doctor. 

Take a team when the supports share the attack route and are valuable in their own right, when Crux is the primary active attacker, or when Strategic Deployment is central to the plan. Fennec support costs 25+ points, slows the initial Move and consumes Core/Haris capacity. If the intended outcome is Crux breaking off into Suppressive Fire, default to solo and independently place cheap support instead. Separating after a productive linked advance is legitimate if the other models still have jobs.

### Is Strategic Deployment worth 4 points?

**Good conditional upgrade, not automatic.** The 50/0 FTO grants the whole Fireteam FD (+4in) at deployment, provided Crux is the first deployed and Team Leader. In a scenario granting an eligible member FD (+4in), the rule can instead grant +8in to the team. This does not override mission deployment restrictions.

Four inches is approximately one Short Move saved on the slower escorts, potentially turning a first Move + Move into Move + Shoot, or allowing better initial routes and angles. It is **not guaranteed to save a full Order**. Multiple bodies benefit in positioning; do not multiply movement saved by membership and call that many Orders saved. Use safe advanced deployment, not maximum distance blindly.

Compare the 4pt premium with Fennec basic-to-Minelayer (+4) or FO/Disco (+3), or saving toward another Regular Order. Pay it when the offensive team genuinely exploits safe forward positions. Skip it in a defensive team, on bad terrain, or on solo Crux.

### Is individual FD (+8in) worth 8 points?

The 54/0 MMR swaps Drop Bears for CrazyKoala, cannot link, and can start about 8in farther forward. That can save roughly one Move + Move Order, enable an immediate attack or put Crux near an objective. Unlike Hidden Deployment it retains its pooled Order if deployed normally.

Normally too expensive: 8 points can fund another cheap Regular Order (e.g. Fugazi if slot/AVA permits), useful throughout the game. Prefer 46pt solo unless the mission or terrain offers a particularly valuable forward starting position. Unlike Strategic Deployment, only Crux benefits.

## Hidden Deployment — niche/experimental

47/0 CrazyKoala or 48/0.5 Armed Turret, both FD (+20) / approximately +8in, AP SMG PS7 and Surprise Attack (-3). Prefer CrazyKoala for close perimeter defense and SWC efficiency; turret is an alternative persistent firing angle, not an additional Koala. No Fireteam access.

**Main advantage is deployment asymmetry, not keeping 16% of the army inactive for multiple turns.** Opponent cannot directly counter-deploy against the concealed position or target Crux while hidden, though they can anticipate likely routes and screen them. Going second can protect Crux from the enemy opening turn. Unlike a hidden sniper, AP SMG has limited reactive reach.

**Order accounting matters:**
- While hidden at Order Count, Crux's Regular Order is not added to the pool. It is reserved for its own revealing activation.
- With ten otherwise eligible Regulars including Crux, nine pooled Orders plus Crux's reserved Order are available. Reveal and Move + Attack, Move + Move or legal Berserk using that reserved Order; revealing does not cost a separate Order.
- Once revealed in the active turn, Crux can use the remaining pooled Orders. If planned to activate immediately anyway, there is no net activation loss that turn, only reduced flexibility.
- If kept hidden while the other troops act, the missing pooled Order is real. Leaving 47–48 points idle for one or two rounds is also a large opportunity cost.
- Revealing via ARO in the enemy turn before friendly Order Count permits normal pooled contribution on the upcoming turn if eligible, but loses the unspent active-turn Surprise Attack opportunity. Hidden Deployment is not recoverable once canceled. It does not confer Surprise Attack on reactive rolls.

Prefer an immediate first-round surprise assault or protected counterattack, rather than deliberately waiting until Turn 3. Consider open FD deployment when needing the pooled Order and visible forward specialist from the start, but that gives up hidden deployment's asymmetry. Hidden profiles replace the exceptional PS5 MMR with an AP SMG and lose Fireteam access: this is a substantial cost even if the first-turn reserved Order is used efficiently. A late scoring mission or very specific ambush can justify them. Do not make them default.

## Crux versus Optimate Agent Maximus

| Attribute | Crux FTO MMR | Agent Maximus FTO |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | **46/0** | 45/1; LT **45/+1** |
| Active weapon | **PS5 B3 AP/Shock**, DA B1 | PS6 B4 AP Thunderbolt |
| Native shooting | BS14 +1SD | BS13 |
| FT2 | B3 +2SD | B4 +1SD |
| Preferred range | 8–24in | **16–32in**, good to 48in |
| Albedo | **-6** | -3 |
| ARM / BTS / health | 5/6/2 real W | 3/3/1W+NWI, Immunity ARM |
| Hacking | Hackable HI | **Non-Hackable MI** (Minimus is Hackable) |
| Extra orders | — | **Tactical Awareness, LT option** |
| CC | MA2, NBW, Berserk, DA PS5 | NBW, CC Attack (-6), AP+DA CCW |
| Utility | D-Charges, Drop Bears, suppression | E/M Carbine, TinBot Repeater or Minimus |
| Fireteams | **FTO Wildcard** | FTO FENNECS only |

**Favor Crux over non-LT Maximus in most balanced lists**, especially with another long gun, a Magistrate Strategos LT, scarce SWC, objectives, or midfield defense requirements. Both are dangerous solo, but Crux already has +1SD and can consolidate with suppression.

**Favor Maximus LT** when the list needs a primary 16–48in AP gun, non-Hackable routes, extra Tactical Awareness, or the SWC-positive Lieutenant. LT has a two-SWC advantage over the identical non-LT Agent profile. Firefly/Squalos NCO elsewhere or linked Sforza can spend the LT Order without breaking Maximus's link; Sforza must lead his own NCO activation. Maximus's Tactical Order can activate his intact team with himself leading. For solo Maximus, 48pt Minimus option adds positioning flexibility and a second model, not an extra pooled order; cannot link because of the synchronized Peripheral. ARM Immunity may make Maximus tougher against specialized AP/DA/EXP ARM-based hits despite lower ARM, while Crux has better raw ARM/BTS and two genuine Wounds. Neither is universally tougher.

At 24–48in Maximus has a major range advantage; Crux excels inside 8–24in and against MSV/Marksmanship. Do not buy both expensive specialists by default; their shared role can consume too many attack points. If roles genuinely differ, Maximus can clear long lanes while Crux holds midfield.

## Crux versus Hetkari

Hetkari RF48/1.5 is the superior sustained conventional midrange attacker thanks to B4, Marksmanship and BS Attack (-3), plus aggressive LT option and E/Marat. Crux46/0 has stronger per-hit stopping power, superior anti-visor penalty, more CC/mission tools and cheaper Fennec support. Both prefer independent operation when the intended end position is suppression. Hetkari is more universal for direct shooting; Crux is the better mission-oriented combined gunfighter/specialist and usually easier to fit into a list already containing a dedicated primary gun.

## Final selection

- **Default: 46/0 solo FTO MMR** for independent midfield assault, scoring, mines and Suppressive Fire.
- **Alternative: 71/0 offensive FENNECS team**, or 75/0 with Strategic Deployment when forward team positioning is genuinely useful.
- **Conditional:** 96/1.5 Crux + Griffin HMG + Disco if both guns share the route and need linked coordination; otherwise split their roles.
- **Rare:** 54/0 FD MMR for a meaningful forward alpha-strike/scoring opportunity; 47/0 or 48/0.5 hidden SMG for an early surprise attack or specific mission, not prolonged inactivity.
- **Normally skip:** Spitfire. The zero-SWC PS5 MMR is the defining advantage of Crux.
