# Sāchā Xenotech Hunters — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: Strong 6-point utility filler, especially when an extra specialist can use her own Irregular Order; not a mandatory inclusion.** Sāchā is a **WIP13 Specialist Operative** with **Forward Deployment (+10 cm / +4")**, **Climbing Plus**, **E/M Grenades (+1B)**, **Flash Pulse**, **Stun Pistol (+1B)** and **Booty** for only **6 points / 0 SWC**. Unlike a Warcor, she has a plausible independent objective-running plan. Unlike a Regular cheerleader, however, her Order is not freely usable by the main attack piece. **Booty is useful in roughly 10 of 20 results (50%) by the valuation below**, but she does not require a good roll to justify herself.

**Practical selection:** Prefer Sāchā over Warcor when specialist redundancy or independent midfield work is the missing capability; prefer the **3pt Warcor 360º** for the cheapest dedicated rear/flank Flash Pulse ARO; take **both for 9pt/0SWC** if two trooper slots are available. A Tech-Bee or other cheap Regular Order can be more valuable than either when your attacking group lacks pooled Orders.

## Profile and deployment correction

|---|---|

| MOV | **10–10 cm / 4–4"** |
| CC / BS / PH / WIP | **17 / 11 / 12 / 13** |
| ARM / BTS / W / S | **0 / 3 / 1 / 2** |
| Skills | **Forward Deployment (+10 cm / +4")**, Booty, Terrain (Total), Climbing Plus, Specialist Operative |
| Weapons | **Flash Pulse, E/M Grenades (+1B), Stun Pistol (+1B), CCW** |
| Fireteam | **None** |

**Important correction:** The pinned roster abbreviates Forward Deployment as `(+10)` without units. This means **+10 cm / +4 inches**, **not +10 inches**. She starts a little beyond her own Deployment Zone, not in the midfield like an Infiltrator. She may deploy closer to a mission objective, but cannot start in contact with it. Climbing Plus helps reach elevated routes once activated; it **does not allow deployment on a vertical wall**.

She is **not** a Forward Observer: Specialist Operative qualifies for appropriate mission objectives but provides no FO attack, Targeted state, Sensor, or bonus to mission rolls on its own. Her **WIP13** is respectable for ordinary objective interactions; individual missions may specify different requirements or specialist bonuses.

## The Irregular Order: what actually pays for itself

An Irregular Order can activate **only Sāchā**. It is not a pooled Regular Order for a Griffin, TAG, Fireteam, or another specialist. She may also be given Regular Orders from her own Combat Group if an objective requires more than one activation. A Command Token could convert her personal Irregular Order to a Regular Order for that turn, but doing this as a routine source of pooled Orders is poor token economy.

**Best uses of her personal Order, in approximate priority:**

1. **Move toward a reachable objective while staying safe.** Forward Deployment (+4") plus an independent Move–Move Order can reduce the Regular Orders needed to reach a console. Her starting position is still noticeably farther back than an Infiltrating Jackal's.
2. **Activate an objective once in position.** WIP13 and Specialist Operative turn that personal Order into actual mission progress instead of an idle Order.
3. **Reposition through vertical terrain using Climbing Plus.** Reach a different approach, bypass an obstructed street or get to an elevated objective route without the normal climbing inefficiency. Movement along vertical surfaces can expose her to AROs; she gets no Partial Cover while on the wall.
4. **Speculative E/M Grenade attack** against a worthwhile enemy behind cover, subject to a valid target and trajectory. This can be done using her Irregular Order, but is a low-probability disruption option, not guaranteed value.
5. **Discover, reposition to a Flash Pulse lane, Dodge or set up a late objective run.** Useful when no button or good E/M target is available, but sometimes preserving her hidden position is better than forcing an activation.

**Do not claim the order is always useful.** She may have no safe movement route, reachable mission task or sensible grenade target. On a turn where only your main gunfighter matters, her Irregular Order is not a substitute for another pooled Regular Order.

**Combat Group:** Group 2 is a natural home if Group 1 already has the principal attacker and a full Regular pool. Sāchā can conduct a low-cost independent task while other Group 2 Irregular troops run their own jobs. Group assignment does not make her Irregular Order shareable. A model still uses **one of the army's 15 trooper slots**.

## Weapons: pressure without pretending she is a gunfighter

### E/M Grenades (+1B): strong effect, awkward delivery

E/M Grenades are **PH-based**, range-limited, circular impact-template weapons with **Speculative Attack**. Sāchā is **PH12**. At successful contact, E/M causes **two Saving Rolls against halved BTS**; a failed roll causes **Isolated**, plus **IMM-B for HI, TAGs, REMs and VHs**, subject to immunities and other rules. E/M does **not** deal Wounds. This is a cheap, potentially high-impact deterrent against expensive hardware.

| Attack | Base target number before additional applicable MODs | Burst |
|---|---:|---:|
| Direct grenade attack, **0–8"** | **15** (PH12 +3) | **2**, from +1B |
| Direct grenade attack, **8–16"** | **9** (PH12 −3) | **2**, from +1B |
| **Speculative**, 0–8" | **9** (PH12 +3 range −6 Speculative) | **1** |
| **Speculative**, 8–16" | **3** (PH12 −3 range −6 Speculative) | **1** |

The **+1B is lost for Speculative Attack**, which is explicitly capped at Burst 1. At PH9, the raw probability of a successful unopposed roll is **45%** before a defender's Dodge or other relevant contest; PH3 is only **15%**. Speculative Attacks avoid the usual Mimetism/Cover negative MODs but do not skip the mandatory −6 and range MODs. They require a legal target and trajectory; they are not arbitrary targetless shots.

**Usage:** Make an opportunistic speculative throw with her own Order against a valuable clustered enemy or HI/TAG/REM that can be caught by the template, preferably within 8". Do not burn precious pooled Orders fishing for low-probability hits, and do not expose an objective specialist just to throw a PH9 grenade without a worthwhile payoff.

### Flash Pulse and Stun Pistol (+1B)

- **Flash Pulse:** WIP-based nonlethal ARO, **B1**. WIP13 becomes **16 in the +3 range band (8–24")**, before other MODs. A successful Stun result can interrupt an enemy's following attacks, but Flash Pulse alone is not durable ARO control.
- **Stun Pistol (+1B):** **B3 active** in a favorable close-range engagement (standard B2 +1B), threatening Stunned rather than Wounds. It is a close-in emergency option, not a reason to pursue gunfights against an opponent with serious Burst or templates.
- **CC17 / ordinary CCW:** An emergency option, not a good reason to seek CC. Booty melee weapons give trade potential, but do not turn her into a melee specialist.

**Deployment discipline:** Her ARM0/W1 chassis has no baseline Mimetism, Camouflage, template weapon or other survival mechanic. Usually deploy near a safe lane to an objective, **not** on an exposed roof as a disposable Flash Pulse sentry. The 360º Warcor is better suited to that dedicated rear ARO role.

## Booty: 10/20 (50%) good or useful results

**Booty is rolled after her model has been placed**, and the new item is added to her normal kit. You cannot plan the final placement around knowing the roll. The table below judges how much each result helps **a 6-point independent specialist**, not how powerful the item is on a premium gunfighter.

|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2** | +1 ARM | Marginal | Welcome durability, but ARM1/W1 remains fragile |
| **3–4** | **Light Flamethrower** | **Excellent** | Direct-template close-approach deterrent; can trade with a much dearer attacker |
| **5–6** | Grenades | Situational | Lethal grenade option, but overlaps her E/M grenade delivery and PH12 |
| **7–8** | DA CC Weapon | Low | CC17 without supporting combat skills; rarely want to commit her to melee |
| **9** | **MSV1** | **Good** | Reduces Mimetism problems on Flash Pulse and close-range attacks |
| **10** | EXP CC Weapon | Low | Strong damage if CC somehow works; poor plan for the specialist |
| **11** | **Adhesive Launcher Rifle** | **Good** | Reusable ranged immobilization deterrent; useful even for a mediocre BS11 shooter |
| **12** | **+2 ARM** | **Good** | Moves her to ARM2 and improves survival on a specialist |
| **13** | **Panzerfaust** | **Good** | Threatens expensive targets from a prepared ARO angle, though BS11/B1 and limited ammunition matter |
| **14** | Monofilament CC Weapon | Niche | Huge trade-up possibility, but no reliable path to winning close combat |
| **15** | **MOV 8–4"** | **Excellent** | Faster first Move makes her personal activation far better for objective access |

| **17** | MULTI Sniper Rifle | Situational | Strong long-range equipment on a poor BS11 long-range platform; may create incidental ARO utility |
| **18** | **+4 ARM** | **Excellent** | ARM4 on a 6-point WIP13 specialist is a meaningful defensive spike |
| **19** | **Mimetism (-6)** | **Excellent** | Excellent protection for reaching/holding objectives and taking opportunistic AROs |
| **20** | HMG | Situational | A major gun on BS11/ARM0/W1; possible emergency threat, not a new primary attack piece |

**Chance breakdown, treating the 20 natural rolls as equally likely:**

- **5/20 (25%) excellent:** 3–4 Light Flamethrower, 15 MOV, 18 +4 ARM, 19 Mimetism (-6).
- **4/20 (20%) good:** 9 MSV1, 11 Adhesive Launcher Rifle, 12 +2 ARM, 13 Panzerfaust.
- **1/20 (5%) useful:** 16 MULTI Rifle.
- **10/20 (50%) marginal, low, niche or situational:** 1–2, 5–8, 10, 14, 17, 20. These are **not automatically wasted**: a rifle/sniper/HMG or Monofilament CCW can still matter when the opponent presents the right opening.

**Total rated useful or better: 10/20 = 50%.** This is a tactical judgment, not a mathematical chance of gaining game value; table, opponent and objective geometry can move borderline results up or down.

## Sāchā versus Warcor, Tech-Bee, Fennec and Pathfinder

| Troop | Pts / SWC | Orders | Specialist? | Best reason to take |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Sāchā** | **6 / 0** | **Irregular** | **Yes, WIP13** | Independent cheap objective runner; FD +4", Climbing Plus, E/M, Booty |
| **Warcor 360º** | **3 / 0** | **Irregular** | **No** | Cheapest 360º rear/flank Flash Pulse ARO; Journalist |
| **Tech-Bee** | **9 / 0** | **Regular** | **Yes, WIP12** | Pooled Order, GizmoKit/Deactivator, deployable repeater, adhesive and Flash Pulse |
| **Fennec FO / Disco** | **13 / 0** | **Regular** | **Yes, WIP12** | Disco Baller, Sensor, FO and Fireteam membership |
| **Pathfinder FO** | **14 / 0** | **Regular** | **Yes, WIP13** | Faster MOV, Sensor, Repeater, FO, and a shareable Order |

**Versus the 3-point Warcor:**

- **Sāchā** is worth the **extra 3 points** when the mission asks for another specialist and you can use her Irregular Order to approach/operate an objective. Booty, E/M Grenades and Climbing Plus are valuable extras. She is an *objective utility* piece rather than a dedicated rear guard.
- **Warcor 360º** is better when the plan is purely to protect a backline against Parachutists/Combat Jump and force opponents to address a cheap Flash Pulse. It also has **Journalist** and costs half as much. Its Irregular Order is usually less independently productive; a 360º visor does not make it a specialist.
- **Both** cost **9 points total, 0 SWC, two personal Irregular Orders and two trooper slots**. This is a legitimate Group 2 package: Warcor holds a covered back/rear angle; Sāchā starts safely 4" forward on an independent button-pushing route. They duplicate a Flash Pulse but not their strategic job. Do not expose Sāchā merely to reproduce Warcor's sentry job.

**Versus a 9-point Tech-Bee:** Tech-Bee's **Regular Order** has more general value when it feeds an established attacking piece; her technical support can also matter in a TAG/REM list. Sāchā's cheaper personal Order is more likely to translate into *her own* objective progress, provided her positioning and mission support that play. A Tech-Bee is not automatically inferior because it starts in the DZ.

**Versus a 13-point Fennec FO/Disco:** Fennec costs more than double but is a Regular, linkable source of **Disco Baller and Sensor**. If those capabilities and a shareable Order are central to the list, prioritize the Fennec. Sāchā supplies a cheap **additional** scorer rather than replacing all of that utility.

**Versus a 14-point Pathfinder:** Pathfinder is a better fast, repeatable objective-support platform and shareable Regular Order, with Sensor/Repeater and FO. Sāchā is the low-cost fallback when a full Pathfinder is unnecessary or unaffordable.

## When to include her

**Favor Sāchā when:**

- Six points remain after the main Kestrel offensive/defensive packages, and there is a free trooper slot.
- The scenario rewards several specialists or late objective access; WIP13 and +4" Forward Deployment have an identifiable task.
- Group 2 has room for an independently operating piece, and Group 1 already contains sufficient Regular Orders.
- A disposable, speculative E/M threat to HI/TAG/REM/VH has tactical value.
- You want a unit whose personal Irregular Order can do meaningful work **without** committing your main gunfighter's Orders.

**Skip or deprioritize her when:**

- The 15-trooper cap is tight or another Regular Order is required for the main attacker.
- Specialist coverage is already ample and mission scoring will be handled by linkable Fennecs, Pathfinders or durable mobile specialists.
- You really need deployment-zone defensive ARO only: **Warcor 360º for 3 points** does that job more efficiently.
- The table exposes her approach route: no Camo, no baseline Mimetism, ARM0/W1 and only +4" FD means she is not a true infiltrating midfield specialist.
- You expect her to function as a primary attacker because of Booty or E/M. That is not what 6 points buys.

## Deployment and turn sequence

1. **Choose a reachable objective or protected lane before placing her.** Start within the legal **+4" Forward Deployment** extension, typically in Total Cover. You cannot deploy touching the objective.
2. **Roll Booty once she is placed.** If you receive a flamethrower, Panzerfaust, adhesive rifle or Mimetism, adjust her *later tactical use* rather than claiming that the deployment position was chosen with knowledge of the item.
3. **Use her own Irregular Order first when the route/task is relevant:** move safely closer, interact if already in position, or exploit a good speculative E/M target. Keep her alive when forcing an activation would create an easy kill.
4. **Only add pooled Orders when the objective warrants it.** A single 6-point specialist can make a difference to the mission without becoming an inefficient full-turn investment.
5. **Preserve the Warcor's separate backfield duty** if both are selected. Their ARO geometry should not require Sāchā to stand in the open.

## Final recommendation

**Strong 6-point filler; take often but not automatically.** Sāchā's defining advantage is **a personal Irregular Order attached to a real WIP13 specialist** with a modest deployment head start, vertical mobility and potentially disabling E/M grenades. The **50% useful-or-better Booty assessment** is a meaningful bonus, but the reliable specialist and self-funded movement are the core reason to roster her.

**Sāchā + 360º Warcor (9pt/0SWC)** is an attractive Group 2 mini-package *when two trooper slots are free*. If forced to choose, pick **Sāchā for mission objectives**, **Warcor for dedicated rear ARO or a strict 3pt remainder**, and **a Regular specialist when what the list really needs is pooled Orders**.
