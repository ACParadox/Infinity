# Scarecrows Active Recon Group — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: S / strongly recommended (KHD).** The **27pt/0SWC Killer Hacker** is the overwhelming default: a concealed infiltrating specialist, independent short-range assassin, counter-Hacker and late-game scorer. The FO saves too little (2pts) to justify losing Trinity and Cybermask in most lists. **One KHD is the normal starting point; two KHDs are a strong midfield-focused option, not mandatory.** Default to **Hidden Deployment** when a productive first-turn activation is plausible, rather than advertising the only native infiltrating Camo (-6) unit in Kestrel.

## KHD — 27/0: three jobs in one

The KHD is not just a Hacker with a backup weapon. It is the same BS13 Mimetism (-6) combat chassis as the FO, with Trinity and Cybermask for only two extra points and no SWC. Even when the enemy brings no Hackers, it remains a capable physical assassin and mission Specialist with two possible Marker States.

### Counter-hacking and Hacker assassination

- [Trinity](../../../../rules/3_Combat/Trinity.md): active B3, WIP13 +3 = **16**, PS6 vs BTS. Starting the active Order in Camouflage, IMP-2 or Hidden Deployment allows **Surprise Attack (-3)** against the target's opposing ARO, subject to counters.
- Prefer an isolated enemy Hacker approached directly within Hacking Area, or use friendly Repeaters. Hacking does not require LoF. Mimetism does not modify hacking Rolls; BTS3 and W1 make losing a cyber duel dangerous.
- Enemy Repeaters grant access to enemy Hackers but impose Firewall (-3) and +3 to their Saving Rolls. Avoid exposing the Scarecrow to several hostile Hacker AROs simultaneously; Surprise Attack applies to the target, not every unrelated ARO.
- KHD is especially valuable with Tikbalang, Squalos, Maximus TAG, Griffin, Hetkari or other hackable attackers. Remove the Hacker preventing the main attack, then exploit the opening with the primary gunfighter.
- A **Fennec HD 16/0.5 + Scarecrow KHD 27/0 = 43/0.5** gives affordable general hacking through FastPanda/friendly Repeaters plus an independent forward anti-Hacker threat. A KHD does not supply Spotlight, Oblivion, Carbonite or Total Control.
- Against high-BTS, multi-Wound or heavily protected Hackers, consider physically shooting them from a flank rather than accepting a bad cyber duel. Cybermask also helps reach favourable angles.
- A KHD can ARO with Trinity against eligible enemy Hackers, but a non-Hacker TAG/HI/REM is not a Trinity target. A hidden KHD is not a general electronic roadblock.

Source: [weapon chart](../../../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/Weapon_Chart.md), [Silent trait](../../../../rules/8_Game%20States%20and%20Glossary/Traits.md), [Surprise Attack](../../../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Surprise_Attack.md).

**Frontal short-range attack:** SMG BS16 B3 before enemy MODs; Boarding Shotgun BS19 B2 with AP. A Scarecrow in Partial Cover, starting in a Marker State or Hidden Deployment, may impose Mimetism -6, cover -3 and Surprise -3 on the targeted opponent's shooting ARO (combined -12 before range and other modifiers). MSV and other counters change this. Surprise is active-only and must be reset by returning to Marker form.

**Rear attack — Silenced Pistol:** under Silent (-6), when attacking from within the target's ZoC and outside its LoF, the target suffers -6 to its opposing Dodge; normal no-LoF Dodge -3 and Surprise -3 can stack to -12 when all requirements apply. The pistol attacks BTS/2 with Shock, making it attractive versus low-BTS Hackers and one-Wound support troops. Silent does not forbid AROs or affect unrelated enemies. Do not mistake the pistol for a universally better gun: lower Burst and weaker PS matter against resilient targets. Choose the best weapon for the actual engagement.

The Nanopulser provides a template against clustered troops, high-Mimetism targets, or enemies unable to risk a trade. At 27pts/W1, sacrificing a Scarecrow to kill cheap fodder is usually bad value; let the cheaper Jackal or Beasthunter trade when possible. Avoid entrenched MSV2 gunfighters and direct-template defenders unless positioning gives a favourable alternative. CC15 is not a dedicated melee assassin.

**Do not buy the non-Specialist shotgun profiles merely for assassination.** The KHD already has B3 shooting, a Silent pistol, direct template and Surprise Attack.

### Specialist and scoring

Infiltration places the Scarecrow near an objective without spending approach Orders. It can clear a defender, interact with a console, then return to a Marker State to survive. Keep it available for late scoring rather than spending the entire premium on a disposable Turn-1 trade.

The KHD and HD qualify as Hackers for appropriate classifieds; D-Charges are on every option, not a unique reason to select a Hacker. The FO adds FO-specific paths (including eligible Flash Pulse/Forward Observer utility), while the HD adds Spotlight-related options not available to a plain KHD. Check mission requirements instead of assuming any Specialist can do every objective. Prefer the KHD over the FO in nearly all ordinary missions.

## Cybermask versus Camouflage (-6)

[Cybermask](../../../../rules/3_Combat/Cybermask.md) is a **Long Skill, No Roll, NFB**, requiring the user to be outside all enemy Trooper/Marker LoF. It grants **IMP-2**, not IMP-1. [Impersonation](../../../../rules/8_Game%20States%20and%20Glossary/Impersonation_State.md) and [Camouflage](../../../../rules/8_Game%20States%20and%20Glossary/Camouflaged_State.md) have different protection profiles.

| Property | Camo (-6) | Cybermask IMP-2 |
|---|---|---|
| Native Discover penalty | -6 from Mimetism | None inherent |
| Ordinary attack / hacking targeting | Protected as Marker | Protected as Marker |
| Intuitive Attack | Possible against Camo | Prohibited against IMP |
| Allied status to enemy | No | Yes: bypass ordinary mine triggers while IMP-2 persists |
| Incidental enemy templates | Can affect CAMO in legal circumstances | Template affecting IMP marker is cancelled under the Impersonation rules |
| Enemy Sensor | Can Discover | Does not Discover IMP |
| MSV3 direct attack without Discover | Can target CAMO | No equivalent IMP bypass |
| Surprise Attack (-3) on reveal | Yes | Yes, subject to counters such as Biometric Visor |
| Re-enter from safe position | One Long Skill | One Long Skill, no roll |

**NFB matters:** Mimetism cannot be used while Cybermask persists. It becomes available again when IMP-2 is cancelled, subject to applicable rules/MODs. The two states do not stack. Cybermask does not pass through occupied silhouettes, permit illegal movement, or guarantee safe attacks in a minefield; cancellation applies to the whole Order, so revealing within a mine's trigger area or along the attack path can expose the Scarecrow.

**Default to Camo (-6) for passive survival.** Against WIP13 and no other Discover MODs/visors, a Camo (-6) marker is discovered on 7 (35%) versus IMP-2 on 13 (65%). Cybermask earns its Order when crossing ordinary mines, avoiding Sensor, bypassing the MSV3 anti-Camo shortcut or approaching template troops otherwise capable of Intuitive Attack. A carefully chosen IMP-2 approach can be better than re-camouflaging even against an opponent with no Hacker.

The KHD has exceptionally flexible marker-state choices for a 2pt premium over the FO. Cybermask is always an available useful *option* when its requirements are met, not an automatically superior state or mandatory end-of-turn action.

## Hidden Deployment — preferred default

[Hidden Deployment](../../../../rules/8_Game%20States%20and%20Glossary/Hidden_Deployment_State.md) and [Infiltration](../../../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Infiltration.md) are both optional. Ordinary Infiltration safely reaches the own half; crossing into the opponent's half requires PH12−3 = 9 (45%). Failure sends the Scarecrow to the own DZ/table edge in Model form, losing the initial hidden/marker deployment and any deployed Minelayer mine. Usually avoid the gamble.

**The only native infiltrating Camo (-6) troop in Kestrel is the Scarecrow.** Blink Team also has Camo (-6) but lacks Infiltration, so an ordinary Camo (-6) marker in midfield largely gives away its unit family. Hidden Deployment conceals exact placement and prevents the opponent from deliberately counter-deploying an MSV attacker, template screen, Sensor or hacking coverage against its known flank. Opponents may still infer the unit from missing points/Orders and cover likely locations.

### Personal reveal Order is productive, not an extra tax

A hidden Scarecrow does not add its Regular Order to the pool during Order Count, but retains it for its own active-turn reveal and activation. With ten otherwise eligible Regular troopers including one Scarecrow:

| First-turn Orders | Ordinary Camo | Hidden |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pooled | 10 | 9 |
| Scarecrow-only reveal Order | 0 | 1 |
| Total usable on a Scarecrow attack | 10 | 10 |
| Usable on a different attacker | 10 | 9 |
| Next Order Count pooled if all alive and deployed | 10 | 10 |

**There is zero additional activation cost if the plan already requires a first-turn Scarecrow Order.** The cost is loss of flexibility (one Order cannot be used by someone else), not an automatic wasted action.

A hidden Scarecrow can use its own Order to:
- **Move + Move** (up to 8" on level terrain), revealing directly as a Camo (-6) Marker and retaining that state throughout; no separate re-camouflage Order is needed.
- **Move + Trinity** or **Move + BS Attack** to launch an attack with Surprise Attack when appropriate.
- **Move + legal mission interaction** to progress scoring, revealing as required.
- Reveal in an eligible ARO on the enemy turn; it contributes normally at the next Order Count if alive and otherwise eligible.

After attacking or scoring, spend a Long Skill outside enemy LoF to re-enter Camouflage or Cybermask if worth the Order. Hidden Deployment itself cannot be regained. It is not invulnerable: Sensor can discover a hidden troop and an area might be covered even without knowledge of the exact position.

| Planned role | Preferred deployment |
|---|---|
| Turn-1 assassin / offensive KHD / early objective | **Hidden**; use personal Order proactively |
| Going second / unexpected reactive ambush | **Hidden**; preserve choice of whether to reveal |
| Late-game specialist | Hidden if concealment payoff justifies missing pooled Orders; otherwise reveal early to Camo |
| Primarily a pooled Order provider for another attacker | Camo |

With two Scarecrows, both may start hidden, then activate both on Turn 1 if useful; or activate one and keep the other hidden to threaten a later ambush. Holding the second hidden through several Order Counts is a real cost. Do not confuse two personal reveal Orders with two unrestricted pooled Orders.

## HD — 29/0.5

Best alternative to the KHD when a forward, initially invisible anti-heavy Hacking Area is the actual job. A hidden HD can reveal with Oblivion, Carbonite, Spotlight or Total Control when a valid target activates within Hacking Area, including without LoF when relevant. No Surprise Attack in reactive turn. It can be a particularly useful counter to a committed heavy attacker or in a Clipper Guided plan.

The HD loses Trinity and Cybermask, and pays +2pts/+0.5SWC versus the KHD. It remains an excellent gunfighter and Specialist. Fennec HD (16/0.5, FastPanda) is preferred for inexpensive general network activation; Scarecrow HD is justified by its unique concealed forward threat and independent offensive access, not merely WIP13. **KHD + HD 56/0.5** is an interesting complementary midfield cyber package, but not the baseline preference over double KHD.

## FO — 25/0: usually skip

The FO retains SMG, Nanopulser, D-Charges, Silenced Pistol, Camo (-6), Hidden Deployment and Surprise Attack. It has Flash Pulse, FO-specific mission/classified utility, and is not Hackable merely by being a Hacker. A viable alternative when two points solve a concrete list constraint, an FO requirement is essential, or the matchup strongly favours avoiding the Hacker vulnerability. Otherwise the 2pt saving is insufficient: the KHD provides far more optional capability even on tables without enemy Hackers.

For pure cheap objective work, a Fennec FO (13/0), Pathfinder FO (14/0) or Drummer FO (21/0) will often be more efficient than buying a 25pt Scarecrow FO without intending to exploit its assassination and survival abilities.

## Non-Specialist options

- **Minelayer 27/0.5:** the AP Mine can be pre-deployed legally within ZoC while the Scarecrow itself remains hidden. The mine can betray a rough area, but not exact placement. Useful for a deliberate concealed anti-armour trap, not a default defender. A Jackal E/M GL Minelayer (11/0.5) or Peacemaker Minelayer (24/0.5) generally delivers cheaper passive control.
- **Repeater 26/0:** a concealed carrier can approach a vulnerable position and deploy a Repeater, enabling remote hacking from more resilient support Hackers. This option is not Minelayer and requires an activation to place the Repeater. It is only one point cheaper than the KHD and lacks general Specialist status; often prefer the Peacemaker's immediate forward Repeater or Fennec's FastPanda. Consider only for a specific network projection plan.

## Suppressive Fire or Marker State?

All three SMG profiles can enter Suppressive Fire. Mimetism (-6), Partial Cover (-3) and the opposing Suppressive Fire shooting MOD (-3) can combine to -12; B3 at BS13 within valid range makes a Scarecrow a serious conventional obstacle. Suppressive Fire takes a Long Skill and leaves the model revealed. Avoid wasting it against approaching templates, MSV2 or opponents able to attack outside the supported range. Re-camouflage is usually better for preserving a late-game Specialist; Cybermask is better for specific anti-Camo threats; Suppressive Fire is better for actively denying a corridor when the Scarecrow can survive the likely counterattack.

## Quantity and group placement

| Package | Points / SWC | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| One KHD | **27 / 0** | Default independent midfield assassin / Specialist |
| Two KHDs | **54 / 0** | Strong asymmetric attacks, two scoring paths, redundant counter-hacking |
| KHD + HD | 56 / 0.5 | Specific heavy-target hacking and Spotlight plan |
| KHD + Banshee KHD | 50 / 0 | Different attack vectors: concealed midfield and airborne flank |
| KHD + Jackal E/M GL Minelayer | 38 / 0.5 | Specialist/assassin + cheap autonomous passive/indirect defence |
| KHD + Jackal Minelayer + Jackal E/M Carbine | 48 / 0.5 | Three infiltrators; distinct roles at lower cost than double Scarecrow |

**0:** valid in a TAG/Fireteam-heavy list that already has objective access, alternative angles and sufficient counter-hacking; not mandatory.

**1:** recommended default. Place in Group 1 for a planned active assassination run so follow-up Orders are available. The reveal Order is useful but not enough for a multi-Order attack by itself.

**2:** very viable if the mission rewards midfield specialists or multiple independent attack vectors. Prefer **two KHDs**, not automatically KHD + FO. Split flanks and responsibilities, and consider putting one in Group 2 only if sufficient Regular Orders exist to fund it. A Group 2 full of Irregular Jackals cannot freely fund a lengthy Scarecrow raid.

**3:** expensive skew (81pts for three KHDs); limited range, one Wound each and duplication make this experimental. Kestrel has too many excellent alternative threats to default to three.

Buy a second KHD when the list needs another self-contained specialist/assassin. Use cheaper Jackals for sacrificial defence and a Fennec HD for general hacking rather than expecting the Scarecrow to do every job.
