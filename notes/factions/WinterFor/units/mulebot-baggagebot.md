- Basic 8/0: cheap Regular order and Baggage reloads eligible Disposable ammunition, particularly Vargar/Quinn/Bøyg Disco Ballers. Deactivator/Repeater are bonuses.

##### Mulebot (Baggagebot / EVO / TR) — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: basic 8/0 A-tier cheap-Order value; EVO 14/0.5 B+ overall and A-tier in a planned support list; TR Combi 17/0 skip / niche.** Take **one basic Mulebot** as an efficient default when you need a Regular Order or Baggage support. Upgrade it to **EVO** for just **+6 points / +0.5 SWC** when at least two *credible* support applications exist, or when one high-impact application alone justifies it. **Do not default to two S4 Mulebots**: deployment-zone congestion, trooper slots and idle Orders matter.

**Role:** Cheap backfield Regular Order, Baggage reload station (especially for the 13-point Disco Baller Fennec), defensive utility through Deactivator/Repeater, or an EVO Supportware hub. Mulebot is *not* a midfield attacker or an efficient primary ARO piece. The EVO itself is not an offensive Hacking Device.

###### Pinned N5.3 profiles

The [Kestrel pinned roster](https://github.com/ACParadox/Infinity/blob/main/sectorials/panoceania/kestrel-colonial-force.md#mulebots) lists **AVA 2 total across profiles**, **Regular**, REM, **MOV 10–10 cm (4–4")**, **BS 11**, **PH 10**, **WIP 13**, **ARM 2**, **BTS 3**, **STR 1**, **S4**, **Baggage, Remote Presence, Courage**, and PARA CCW (-3). No Mulebot can join a Kestrel Fireteam.

| Profile | Pts | SWC | Additional equipment / skills | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| **Basic Baggagebot** | **8** | **0** | **Deactivator, Repeater** | **Default A-tier cheap Order + reload / Disco utility** |
| **EVO Hacker** | **14** | **0.5** | **EVO Hacking Device**; **no native Repeater or Deactivator** | **B+ to A**, depending on support plan |
| **Total Reaction Combi** | **17** | **0** | **Repeater, Combi Rifle, Total Reaction**; no Deactivator | **Skip / niche** |

All three generate **one native Regular Order**, retain **Baggage**, and can be repaired by an Engineer thanks to being REMs with Remote Presence. **Only the basic and TR profiles have a printed Repeater; only the basic has Deactivator.** The EVO does not get those for free by virtue of being a Hacker.

###### Basic 8-point Mulebot: what actually makes it worthwhile

At **8 points / 0 SWC**, a protected Order is already a credible purchase. The bonuses are:

1. **Baggage / Reload:** friendly non-Null troopers inside the Mulebot's **8" Zone of Control** can replenish their expended **Disposable** ammunition/equipment through the **Reload Short Skill / ARO**; in the **States Phase**, *each* eligible allied trooper in ZoC is replenished **automatically, without an Order**. The Mulebot must remain functional. Equipment already deployed remains deployed, and **Non-Reloadable** items are excluded.
2. **Deactivator:** WIP-based way to address revealed mines and other eligible deployables without sacrificing a valuable fighter. Crucially, it is also used to **Activate Disco Ball**.
3. **Repeater:** passive 8" extension of friendly Hackers' hacking coverage **across Combat Groups**, but its utility depends on having a useful Hacker/program; it can also present enemy counter-hacking risks if advanced unwisely.
4. **ARM 2 / S4:** somewhat sturdier than a Fugazi, but a substantially bigger, slower model with neither Mimetism nor Flash Pulse.
5. **Mission value:** Baggage can matter in missions whose scoring rules credit it in Zone of Operations control; **check the actual mission**, rather than assuming bonus points apply universally.

**Do not spend active Orders moving this REM forward solely to make its Repeater relevant**, unless an objective, a clearly valuable hack, or a reload/deactivation target makes the route efficient. It is generally a rear/near-midfield utility unit, not an attack platform.

###### Major Kestrel synergy: 13-point Fennec FO + Disco Baller

The [Fennec FO/Sensor/Disco Baller](https://github.com/ACParadox/Infinity/blob/main/sectorials/panoceania/kestrel-colonial-force.md#fennec-fusiliers) costs **13/0**, can belong to a FENNECS team, and has a **Disposable (2) Disco Baller**. Disco Balls create **Eclipse** cover; the deployed balls remain on the table after their active Eclipse template ends at the beginning of the States Phase.

- **Baggage:** if a Fennec with spent Disco Baller uses is inside the Mulebot's 8" ZoC during the States Phase, it recovers those expended uses automatically. That permits repeated Eclipse projection over successive turns as long as the Fennec and supply bot remain functional and in range.
- **Basic Mulebot's Deactivator:** can use **Activate Disco Ball** as a **Short Skill** to reactivate **an existing allied Disco Ball** in its LoF or ZoC with a **WIP +3 Normal Roll**, also applying Deactivator range MODs. This reactivates the *existing ball's* Eclipse template; it **does not consume a fresh Disco Baller shot** or redeploy a new ball.
- **EVO does have Baggage, but lacks Deactivator** and therefore cannot itself perform Activate Disco Ball. This is a concrete trade-off, not a free upgrade across all support roles.
- The Fennec still has to reach appropriate table geometry: **S4 Mulebot 4–4 MOV does not conveniently follow an advancing Fennec team**. A protected rear position that covers the Fennecs for a turn or two is more realistic than permanent replenishment everywhere.

**Why this changes the valuation:** Kestrel's inexpensive Disco Fennec is a credible, repeatable reason to select a Baggagebot **even when there is no TAG, no REM attack plan and no other valuable Disposable weapon nearby**.

###### Other valuable reload targets

| Friendly unit | Reloadable opportunities | Practical constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **Fennec FO with Disco Baller (13/0)** | Disco Baller shots; notably repeatable Eclipse cover | Must be within 8" for Baggage |
| **Firefly with Panzerfaust / mines / Deployable Repeater** | Expended listed Disposable uses | Strongest when a defensive Firefly remains nearby; chasing an advancing Firefly is inefficient |
| **Fennec Thunderbolt Minelayer (14/0)** | Shock Mines after use | The *deployed* mine is not picked up or refreshed |
| **Jackals** | Panzerfausts, Mines, etc., when the specific profile has them | Infiltrating Jackals usually start outside convenient rear ZoC |
| **Tikbalang** | Shock Mine uses | TAG normally advances away; reload range is the constraint |
| **Banshee with WildParrot / D-Charges** | Eligible expended Disposable uses | Airborne arrival is unlikely to be near the Mulebot at the right time |

**N5.3 rule precision:** Baggage replenishes **uses remaining on the trooper**, not deployables already sitting on the battlefield. Also, a synchronized Auxbot's mine is not simply a reloadable weapon **on the Peacemaker controller**; do not assume Baggage gives the controller extra Auxbot mines.

###### Take 0, 1 or 2 basic Mulebots

| Count | Cost | Preferred circumstances |
|---|---:|---|
| **0** | 0 | You already have enough functional Orders, need S2 Fireteam bodies or specialists more, lack safe S4 space, or your EVO already supplies sufficient Baggage |
| **1** | 8 | **Default** when needing a cheap Regular Order, especially with Disco Fennecs, defensive Disposable weapons or a nearby hacking plan |
| **2** | 16 | **Situational**: two useful cheap Orders, separate reload pockets, mission zone control, and a board that can **actually conceal two S4 models** |

**Two basic Mulebots should not be an automatic TAG-list package.** They are **both S4 (55 mm footprint)**. They compete for covered DZ positions with TAGs, Redeyes/VHs, Peacemakers, Bulleteers, Drumbots and sometimes Palbots. A theoretically efficient cheerleader is bad if it must stand exposed, blocks a TAG's exit lane, or creates a cluster vulnerable to templates and airborne attackers.

Compare a **7-point Fugazi** before buying a second Mulebot: Fugazi is **S3, faster (6–6"), Mimetism (-3), Flash Pulse, Repeater**, and the Kestrel AVA is 1. It often provides a better *first* support REM and a complementary role; unlike a Mulebot it has no Baggage or Deactivator. **Fugazi + one Mulebot = 15/0, two Regular Orders, complementary tools.** A second Mulebot costs 8 points and another S4 hiding spot.

**Two-Mulebot exception:** if a quadrant/sector mission specifically rewards Baggage and both bots can be deployed/advanced safely into different scoring areas, the duplication has genuine mission value. Otherwise prioritize a useful specialist, Fennec team member, different ARO angle, or a better-equipped order source.

###### EVO Mulebot (14 / 0.5): when +6 points is worthwhile

The EVO retains the **Order, Baggage and S4 chassis**, but replaces the basic's Deactivator/Repeater with an **EVO Hacking Device**. It gains **Assisted Fire, Enhanced Reaction, Fairy Dust and Controlled Jump**, **not** Carbonite, Oblivion, Spotlight, Trinity or other conventional attack programs. It is a **Hacker Specialist**, but a poor forward button-pusher.

**Working rule:** If **0.5 SWC is available** and you can name **two genuinely useful EVO applications** (not hypothetical applications unlikely ever to arise), **usually pay the extra six points**. One critical application can also justify it, notably Fairy Dust for an exposed TAG or Assisted Fire for a premium offensive REM. It is *not* worth buying just because you happen to have six spare points.

###### Assisted Fire: best for active gunfighting REMs

- **Long Skill**, costs an Order; target must be a **friendly REM within the EVO's Hacking Area when activated**. Once running, the buff has **table-wide range**.
- Gives the **target REM Marksmanship**, ignoring enemy **Partial Cover and Nanoscreen negative BS MODs** for its BS Attacks; it does **not** improve BS against everything or add Burst.
- Marksmanship makes the recipient subject to **White Noise, Reflective and Albedo** interactions: a meaningful drawback against relevant defenders.
- Buff one impactful attacker rather than using an Order reflexively on a passive REM.

| Candidate | Assessment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Drumbot-2 HRMC; Drumbot-1 MULTI HMG** | **Highest potential** | Premium attack guns; every important covered-target shooting exchange can profit. Drummer *controller is LI*; the **Drumbot is a REM Peripheral**, so target the bot itself, not its controller. **Confirm Peripheral targeting with event arbiter if disputed**: no Drumbot-specific ruling is pinned here. |
| **Bulleteer Spitfire (22/1)** | **Very strong** | High-volume active gun with Mimetism (-6) and improved shooting into Partial Cover |
| **Bulleteer Heavy Shotgun (+1B), 19/0** | **Very strong on dense tables** | B3 close-range gunfighter, Mimetism (-6), better covered-target hits |
| **Sierra HMG 23/1 or Thunderbolt (+1B) 18/0** | **Useful but conditional** | Can be actively used; Marksmanship also improves ARO shooting *once buffed*. Spending an Order solely to pre-buff a backline ARO is less reliable |
| **Peacemaker shotgun** | **Situational** | More relevant if actively pushing short ranges; program affects the targeted REM, not automatically the Auxbot or its separate attacks |

**First/second turn issue for Sierra:** if Kestrel goes **second**, the opponent gets a full first active turn **before** you could spend an Order on Assisted Fire. If Kestrel goes **first**, you can pre-buff Sierra, but that is still an Order spent on reactive defense rather than mission progress. **Don't buy EVO solely to buff Sierra**; if EVO is justified by other roles, this is a bonus.

###### Fairy Dust: one vulnerable premium TAG can be enough

**Long Skill / one Order**; choose **one troop type**: **HI, REM, TAG or VH**. All friendly troopers of **that type** receive **Firewall (-3)** across the table: hostile Comms Attacks are harder to succeed with and the victims receive the Firewall Saving Roll benefit. **Not** one program covering HI + REM + TAG + VH simultaneously. The value depends on *enemy hacking exposure*, not a simple count of owned units.

| Protected models of the chosen type | When spending one Order on Fairy Dust makes sense |
|---|---|
| **One 69-point Tikbalang or Squalos** | **Often yes** if it must cross / hold a hostile Repeater or Hacker area; less useful if the route is clearly outside hacking reach |
| **One exposed Redeye VH** | **Often yes** against real hacking threat; mobility may allow a safer non-hacking route |
| **One essential Bulleteer / Peacemaker / other active REM** | Sometimes: valuable if it must enter hostile hacking coverage |
| **Two important REMs** | Usually strong if enemy hacking can target both |
| **One normal Griffin or other HI** | Often skip unless it is your sole vital attacker exposed to multiple hacking attempts |
| **Two premium exposed HI** | Often worthwhile |
| **Three or more meaningful HI/REMs** | Strong incentive **when** exposed to hacking |
| **Several safe cheerleader REMs in the DZ** | **Not** a reason alone: count *threatened, mission-relevant* units |

**One TAG:** a single expensive TAG can justify both the **six-point EVO upgrade** and **one Order to activate Fairy Dust**, but those are **separate decisions**. Having the EVO does not mean Fairy Dust should be activated every turn if the opponent lacks meaningful hacking. Firewall MODs do not stack into a higher effect when a model already has a stronger Firewall source.

###### Controlled Jump: offensive Banshees **and** defensive ARO

- **Short Skill / ARO**, **No Roll**, table-wide: while active, allied Combat Jump **+3 PH**, enemy Combat Jump **-3 PH**.
- Kestrel **Banshees have PH 12**: a plain PH roll goes from **12/20 = 60% to 15/20 = 75%** before other modifiers. More compelling if committing multiple Banshees to Combat Jump, but do not count on that tactic automatically if safer Parachutist deployment exists.
- **Can declare Controlled Jump as an ARO to any enemy Combat Jump on the table**; the -3 applies **immediately to that jump**. This is valuable **even in a list with zero friendly Combat Jump troops**.
- If the opponent also has Controlled Jump active, **both programs' effects cancel**.
- Because the EVO sustains only one Supportware at a time, **activating Controlled Jump cancels its own previous Fairy Dust / Assisted Fire / Enhanced Reaction**. An opponent can force an unpleasant choice: keep a protective buff, or penalize an incoming jumper. You can switch back on a later active turn by paying the appropriate Order.
- Controlled Jump applies to **Combat Jump**, not every form of Airborne Deployment or Speedballs.

**Controlled Jump as defensive insurance** is a second real application when enemy Combat Jump is a credible matchup concern, but not when the local meta barely fields it.

###### Enhanced Reaction: lower priority

**Long Skill / one Order** gives a targeted REM **Burst 2 in ARO**. It can help a Bulleteer or Peacemaker on a deliberate defensive post. **It does not improve a Sierra**, whose native **Total Reaction** already supplies full weapon Burst (Thunderbolt +1B B3 / HMG B4) in ARO. Enhanced Reaction is usually a *bonus* program rather than a primary reason to buy EVO.

###### Critical Supportware limits and order economy

1. A Hacker can sustain **only one active Supportware at a time**; a trooper benefits from only one Supportware. Changing the EVO's program expends the relevant **Order or ARO** and cancels its previous program; programs targeting a trooper already affected by another Supportware also have cancellation interactions.
2. **Assisted Fire, Fairy Dust and Enhanced Reaction each cost an active Order to start**. The fact that EVO has two useful options does not mean both operate at once.
3. Put EVO in **Group 2** when that group has spare Orders to spend on Supportware. A Group 2 EVO can buff a **Group 1 REM**, and Fairy Dust/Controlled Jump apply across groups. It **cannot send its Regular Order to a Group 1 gunfighter**.
4. If EVO is already present for a premium attack REM, **Fairy Dust / Controlled Jump are compelling flexibility** even if used rarely; if the list has no valuable buff recipient or credible hacking / jumping matchup, prefer basic.

###### Example packages

| List has... | EVO verdict |
|---|---|
| **HRMC Drumbot + hack-exposed TAG** | **Strong yes:** Assisted Fire or Fairy Dust, chosen situationally |
| **Bulleteer assault + exposed TAG** | **Strong yes** |
| **Premium Drumbot + credible enemy Combat Jump** | **Strong yes** |
| **Sierra + credible enemy Combat Jump** | **Usually yes** if timing and alternative Orders justify |
| **Single hack-exposed TAG only** | **Can still be worth it** for Fairy Dust |
| **One passive Sierra, no other worthwhile plan** | **Usually no** |
| **Disco Fennecs but no important hacking/supportware uses** | **Basic may be preferable**: same Baggage *plus* Deactivator to reactivate existing Disco Balls |

**Deployment warning:** swapping one basic Mulebot for EVO does **not** add another S4 silhouette. Taking **EVO + basic** means **two S4s**, and AVA 2 means no third Mulebot. Evaluate physical DZ hiding space before adding the second.

###### TR Combi Mulebot (17 / 0): skip / niche

The profile is **BS 11, Total Reaction, Burst 3 Combi Rifle in ARO, ARM 2, Baggage, Repeater, S4**. It is **not** a ranged dedicated ARO comparable to a long-lane Sierra.

| Comparison | TR Mulebot | Sierra Thunderbolt |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost | **17/0** | **18/0** |
| ARO Burst | 3 | 3 |
| BS | 11 | 11 |
| Weapon | Combi Rifle | Thunderbolt (+1B) |
| Native 360º Visor | No | **Yes** |
| ARM | **2** | 0 |
| Baggage / Repeater | **Yes / Yes** | No / No |

For **+1 point**, Sierra has much more convincing medium/long-range denial, **360º Visor**, and stronger gun pressure. The TR Mulebot only has a case when a **short interior corridor** specifically rewards the Combi's better close range, while **Baggage + a Repeater** also need to be present there. Even then you risk all that utility as soon as you expose the S4 body.

**Do not buy TR because you have free SWC:** it costs **nine points more than a basic Mulebot** for mediocre close-range ARO. If the intention is long-range defense, buy Sierra; if the intention is Baggage or a cheap Order, buy basic. **Rating: C-/D, generally skip.**

###### Combat Groups, deployment and final decision

- **Group 1 basic:** excellent if the main TAG, Griffin or Fireteam can spend its inexpensive Regular Order. **Group 2 basic:** appropriate if Group 2 has meaningful Order consumers; otherwise it risks becoming inert extra capacity.
- **Group 2 EVO:** particularly attractive when its own spare Order can run Supportware for important targets in Group 1. However, group membership does not bypass the **one sustained Supportware per EVO** constraint.
- **Hide the S4 models first.** Avoid exposing two Mulebots to the same blast, enemy AD lane or TAG-clearing attack. Do not block your own TAG / Redeye / attack REM's departure routes. A safe **Fugazi + one Mulebot** is often more useful than an exposed two-Mulebot pair.
- **Don't overcount Repeater value.** Kestrel has useful hackers but isn't automatically a dominant offensive hacking network; a Repeater without an actionable hacker/target often adds little.
- **Don't overcount Baggage value.** It's strongest when a **specific nearby Fennec Disco Baller or other Disposable carrier will actually replenish**; the effective 8" reload footprint is more relevant than a long theoretical list of reloadable items.

###### Final recommendations

| Choice | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **No Mulebot** | Fine if S4 positioning, slot pressure, or better unit upgrades dominate |
| **One basic 8/0** | **Default A-tier pick**, especially with Fennec Disco Baller |
| **Two basics 16/0** | **Niche/situational**, especially zone missions; beware S4 congestion |
| **One EVO 14/0.5** | **Take when two credible uses exist** and SWC permits; one premium exposed TAG or important Drumbot can sometimes be sufficient |
| **EVO + basic 22/0.5** | Worth considering in support-heavy lists with the hiding space and Order consumers |
| **TR Combi 17/0** | **Skip** except very specific short-lane Baggage + Repeater defense |

**Short version:** **one basic Mulebot is easy to justify, EVO is an excellent +6 / +0.5 SWC upgrade when its support toolbox has real jobs, and two S4 bots should not be forced into every Kestrel list.** The standout list-specific details are **Baggage restoring Disco Baller ammunition**, **Deactivator reactivating existing allied Disco Balls**, **Assisted Fire on premium Drumbot/Bulleteer guns**, **Fairy Dust being worthwhile even on one threatened TAG**, and **Controlled Jump being an immediate enemy-Combat-Jump ARO at the cost of the EVO's previous Supportware**.
