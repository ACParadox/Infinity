##### Disco Baller FO — 26/0

The relative opportunity cost is much better than in Kestrel: no 13-point Fennec FO/Disco exists in NCA. Quinn combines mobile Eclipse projection, WIP13 FO, Flash Pulse, D-Charges, MSV1 and close-range Boarding Pistol protection. He provides the support without needing to make the expensive Aquila FTO the source of Eclipse. Aquila itself remains an alternative when the list also wants its premium MSV3 shooting.

###### Eclipse as insurance for premium attackers

A TAG with excellent long-range firepower or a Camo/Mimetism (-6) Swiss Guard HMG can often solve engagements independently, so Quinn may use his Disco Baller less frequently than in a close/mid-range offensive Fireteam. Nevertheless, a list built around expensive attackers can encounter a counter, an unfavorable range band, a dominant ARO position or a lane that is simply too costly to contest. **Eclipse offers an alternative to winning another gunfight.** Use it to bypass the counter, reposition the attacker, reach an objective or withdraw a valuable piece. Quinn can supply this utility to an independent attacker without being in its Fireteam (the Swiss Guard cannot link), or accompany a TAG in an Ultima Ratio Duo. He is particularly useful for getting Shona, Hannibal, Blade-Ops or other close-range pieces into effective range.

Eclipse blocks MSV as well as ordinary LoF; it is not a smoke-shooting combo with Quinn's own MSV1. The screen appears at the Conclusion step, so it cannot negate AROs already declared during that deployment Order. The Disco Baller has two uses; Baggage can replenish eligible Disposable uses, and a Deactivator can reactivate an existing allied Disco Ball under the normal rules.

###### Not a 26-point Eclipse tax

Even if the Disco Baller is never fired, Quinn still has multiple useful jobs:

- **Composition:** unrestricted Wildcard counting as Fusilier and Bolt; contributes to matching FT levels (FT2 +1 SD on eligible BS Attacks, FT3 Discover/Dodge, FT4 BS and FT5 Sixth Sense). Native anchors still apply.
- **Close/mid-range attack:** BS13, MSV1 and MULTI Rifle (optimal 0–16", AP/Shock/DA modes); MSV1 counters Mimetism (-3) and reduces Mimetism (-6) to (-3).
- **Close defense:** Boarding Pistol (+1B) offers B3 hit mode or B2 direct-template mode in the Active Turn (not B2 templates in ARO), plus the MULTI Rifle and Flash Pulse.
- **Reliability:** ARM2, BTS3, Immunity (ARM) against qualifying ARM-targeting ammunition, and Warhorse (including immunity to BS Attack negative MODs and Isolation). Still W1, no Mimetism or second Wound, and no additional personal Order.
- **Mission utility:** WIP13 FO, D-Charges and a Regular Order at zero SWC.

CSU and Blocker are cheaper utility bodies; Blade-Ops and Hannibal offer Tactical Awareness; Black Friar is a stronger dedicated anti-visual-tech gunfighter. None supplies Eclipse. Quinn is not necessarily the most efficient choice for any one of those other jobs in isolation, but the combined package makes paying 26 points reasonable.

**List priority:** Very High in mobile Bolt/Fusilier teams, high even with independent TAG/Swiss Guard attackers as insurance, lower in a predominantly static list or where Aquila FTO already provides sufficient Eclipse. Skip only when the opportunity cost is material or there is no practical Eclipse delivery plan.

##### Chain of Command — 24/0 (additional NCA option)

A cheap, flexible alternative to Bolt CoC 26/0. SMG and Boarding Pistol are short-range protection; E/Mitter gives ranged anti-tech utility. WIP13, MSV1, Warhorse and Immunity (ARM) add utility, but he is still a one-Wound body. This profile has no Disco Baller or FO.

- **Aggressive Aquila LT:** Quinn provides succession insurance without requiring another premium gun.
- **Agent Maximus LT 47/+1 or Maximus TAG LT 84/0:** Protect aggressive Inspiring Leadership command plans; succession does not itself guarantee preservation of the original LT's special benefits.
- **Fusilier LT 10/0:** Generally avoid spending 24 points purely to insure a safely concealed cheap LT.

Deploy the CoC carrier separately from the aggressive LT and avoid the same blast lane. Wildcard access is useful but not a reason to expose the succession piece. Bolt CoC can be preferable for BTS6, Drop Bears, Shock Immunity, Marksmanship and native Neoterra membership. Quinn saves two points and offers more flexible Fireteam access, WIP13, MSV1 and E/Mitter.

##### MMR FO — 26/0

Retains the original 8–24" MSV1 secondary gun/specialist role. Generally skip in favor of Disco unless Eclipse is covered elsewhere, the team specifically needs the range band and zero SWC matters. In NCA, Black Friar Thunderbolt 24/0 is a strong competing anti-visual-tech gun (MSV2, Albedo), but is not an FO and does not count as Fusilier/Bolt. Quinn is useful for composition and objectives rather than as the army's primary gunfighter.

##### Richard Quinn — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: Good / situational, with a particularly strong Griffin HMG Duo application.** Both Quinn profiles are **26 points / 0 SWC**, but the **MULTI Rifle + Disco Baller** version is the more distinctive Kestrel choice. ###### Profile decision: MULTI MMR versus MULTI Rifle + Disco

The two MULTI guns offer the **same AP, Shock and DA ammunition modes**, at different ranges:

| Weapon | Positive +3 range | Outside optimal band | Active Burst / ammo | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MULTI Marksman Rifle** | **8–24"** | **−3 at 0–8" and 24–40"** | **B3 AP or Shock**, **B1 DA** | Better 16–24" support and Suppressive Fire |
| **MULTI Rifle** | **0–16"** | **−3 at 16–32"** | **B3 AP or Shock**, **B1 DA** | Better within 8"; comes with Disco Baller |
| **Boarding Pistol (+1B)** | **0–8"** | 0 at 8–16", −6 at 16–24" | **B3 normal Hit mode, B2 small direct-template Blast mode** in Active Turn | Present on **both** profiles |

All modes use the weapon-chart ammunition and PS, not a Quinn-specific damage bonus. The MMR has a favorable 16–24" band but a worse 0–8" band than the MULTI Rifle; **the Boarding Pistol mitigates the MMR's immediate close-range weakness**.

###### Choose MMR Quinn when

- An existing **Fennec FO/Disco** or another linked team already supplies the Eclipse you actually need.
- A Fireteam wants an **MSV1, BS13, B3 AP/Shock** gun for 8–24" fights, but you do not want to spend more SWC.
- You need a secondary active attacker or an alternative firing lane while preserving a Griffin/Hetkari/Crux for stronger engagements.
- You want WIP13 objective access coupled with genuine midrange shooting rather than a defenseless button-pusher.

**Main objection:** the MMR is not a premium primary gun. BS13, one Wound and no Mimetism make extended head-on exchanges unattractive. In a team already carrying a Griffin HMG, Crux MMR or other dedicated shooter, Quinn often adds **redundant gun reach**, while the Disco profile adds a capability the main shooter lacks. Do not pay 26 points for a merely decent backup MMR without a defined second job.

###### Choose Disco Quinn when

- The linked attacker must **cross defended lanes, get into better range bands, approach a target or reach objectives** without fighting every ARO.
- The team needs a **WIP13 FO travelling with it**, plus close-range protection from MULTI Rifle and Boarding Pistol.
- You specifically want **Eclipse in a KESTREL Duo/Haris**, where a normal Fennec cannot join.
- Your FENNECS Core is already doing another job, or your Haris slot is tied up by Magistrate + Jackals / Hetkari and you still want a linked Disco escort.

**Main objection:** if all you need is one Disco Baller in a FENNECS Core/Haris, a **13-point Fennec** performs that task at half the price and adds Sensor.

###### Boarding Pistol (+1B): meaningful close defense

The pistol is **not just incidental equipment**. Quinn's Boarding Pistol has:

- **Hit mode:** B2 base, **B3 Active Turn** with (+1B), **B1 ARO**, normal ammunition, +3 at 0–8".
- **Blast mode:** Small Teardrop **Direct Template**, B1 base, **B2 Active Turn** from (+1B), **B1 ARO**. Two active templates can threaten a target twice (and possibly multiple models), subject to normal template placement and Dodge interactions.
- **Intuitive Attack** on the Blast mode, offering a rules-dependent answer to hidden/marker or Zero Visibility situations when the required conditions are met.

**The +1B does not carry into the Reactive Turn**, and the FT2 +1SD bonus does **not** enhance the template itself. During an active Fireteam activation, the **Team Leader** is the relevant model shooting; two linked members do not automatically make separate simultaneous attacks.

**Why it matters in Kestrel:** multiple attractive linked pieces (especially Fireflies, Crux FTO and ordinary Fennecs) lack a built-in direct-template gun. Quinn adds a practical, short-range, trade-capable weapon without costing a separate trooper slot beyond himself. Paired with a **Griffin's Pulzar**, it creates **complementary ARO threats**: Griffin's Large Teardrop attacks BTS, while Quinn's Small Teardrop attacks ARM. They can watch different approaches rather than stacking in one spot. This makes it less attractive for an enemy to simply push a close-range model into the link.

**Not unique:** Bipandra's 23pt Doctor has **Nanopulser (+1B)**, another active-turn B2 direct-template option, and Black A.I.R./Magistrate/Sforza have their own single templates. Quinn's value is the **combined** package of Eclipse or MMR, specialist, MSV1, Warhorse, Immunity (ARM), and Boarding Pistol. Don't purchase him solely for template coverage.

###### Durability and tactical reliability

**ARM2 + Immunity (ARM)** is unusually useful on a W1 light infantry model: attacks whose ammunition requires an **ARM Saving Roll** are treated as Normal ammo, ignoring AP modifiers, multiple-roll DA/EXP effects and qualifying special traits. A regular hit generally asks for **one normal ARM save**; a Critical can still require an additional save. **This does not help against BTS-targeting ammunition**, and a failed normal save can still incapacitate a one-Wound Quinn.

**Warhorse** preserves Regular status during Loss of Lieutenant and Retreat!, prevents the **Isolated** state, and ignores enemy **BS Attack (−X)** effects. **MSV1** ignores Mimetism (−3) and reduces Mimetism (−6) to −3 in relevant shooting; it does **not** see through Eclipse or substitute for Sensor against markers.

Compared with an ordinary Fennec (ARM0/W1, no Immunity), Quinn is materially tougher and more independent, **not a reliable point man into unopposed shots**. Use the HMG/Red Fury to clear tough targets and Quinn to take objectives, lay Eclipse, protect the close approach and clean up low-risk opponents.

###### Disco Baller mechanics: avoid the common mistakes

The Disco Baller is **BS-based, Targetless, Disposable (2), Double Shot, capable of Speculative Attack**. Its direct range bands are **0 at 0–8", +3 at 8–16", −3 at 16–24", −6 at 24–32"**. Quinn's BS13 therefore shoots a standard direct deployment on **16 or less at 8–16"**, before other applicable modifiers, compared with the Fennec's BS12 **15 or less**. This is a small accuracy gain, not the reason to pay double.

1. Choose the destination and make the **Normal BS Roll**; it is **not Face-to-Face** with an enemy shot. Double Shot can consume both uses to place two balls in one attack.
2. The Disco Ball and **Eclipse circular template appear only at the Conclusion step** of that Order. **The Eclipse cannot cancel an ARO already declared against that Order.** It is for subsequent movement/attacks.
3. The Eclipse template disappears at the **start of the States Phase**, but the physical Disco Ball remains unless destroyed. A friendly **Deactivator** can later reactivate a deployed allied ball with the applicable **WIP+3 Normal Roll** and range restrictions.
4. **Baggage** can replenish spent Disco Baller uses when the carrying trooper is eligible and in range. An 8pt basic Mulebot is an efficient rear-area support unit, but do not assume a slow S4 bot can keep up with a forward Griffin/Quinn attack pod.
5. Because Eclipse blocks even MSV LoF, **Quinn's own MSV1 does not let him shoot through his Eclipse screen**. Position the screen to cut the opponent's lanes, then emerge where the Griffin has a favorable shot.

A speculative Disco attack is a **Long Skill** with its own −6 modifier; **FT2 +1SD does not apply to Long Skills**. The best use is usually planned lane control, not throwing two balls at low percentages.

###### Why the Griffin HMG + Quinn Duo is the standout

- **No scarce slot conflict:** The Duo leaves the **FENNECS Core** and the **Magistrate+Jackals or Hetkari Haris** available.
- **No wasted Escort Order:** Both members already produce Regular Orders; the pod moves as a single Fireteam when appropriately activated, instead of spending separate Orders to reposition the FO.
- **Better attack tempo:** FT2 gives the HMG's eligible BS Attacks **+1SD**; Quinn brings Eclipse that can prevent bad engagements and help reach objectives.
- **Useful reactive layering:** **Griffin Pulzar + Quinn Boarding Pistol Blast** create independent short-angle template risks. Quinn's ARM2/Immunity (ARM)/Warhorse make the escort less flimsy than a normal Fennec.
- **Range coverage:** HMG handles the long game, Quinn's MULTI Rifle and pistol handle 0–16", and his Flash Pulse/FO add nonlethal utility.
- **Secondary Fireteams:** If a FENNECS Core already contains a **13pt Disco Fennec**, this is a practical way to field a **second *linked* Disco Baller in a separate KESTREL Duo**, unlike a second Fennec which cannot form a FENNECS Duo.

**Opportunity cost:** 63 points is not trivial; Quinn is **26 points of escort**, not a free second gun. The Griffin may work better solo in terrain with awkward Fireteam coherency or when a specialist elsewhere can score at less risk. And a Duo with only two bodies loses its FT2 level as soon as one member goes down.

###### Recommendation and skip conditions
