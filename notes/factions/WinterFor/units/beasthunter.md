### Beasthunters Free Guild — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: A-tier specialist in trading up (not a rules Specialist).** The 15pt Camouflaged Panzerfaust is the economical default; the **17pt Heavy Flamethrower (+1B) + Panzerfaust** is the preferred aggressive version. Heavy Flamethrower is the defining weapon: a cheap model can force expensive enemy infantry, multi-Wound pieces or clustered Fireteams into very unfavorable trades. Camouflage, Forward Deployment (+20 cm / +8"), Super-Jump and AP Mines give the solo profiles both offensive and defensive value. **Not mandatory:** the accumulating Irregular Order and trooper-slot cost is a serious consideration in Kestrel lists already using infiltrating Jackals, Sāchā and Warcor.

#### Heavy Flamethrower: the main reason to take the unit

The Heavy Flamethrower delivers a **Large Teardrop Direct Template, PS6, one ARM save per hit, Continuous Damage**. It needs no BS Roll and ignores Mimetism and Partial Cover MODs applicable to rolled BS attacks. This is an excellent fit for BS11. The Beasthunter is cheap enough to deliberately trade its own Wound for a valuable enemy, rather than trying to win a fair face-to-face gunfight.

- **Trade up:** if an expensive attacker shoots back instead of Dodging, it can kill the Beasthunter but still suffer the automatically hitting template, including potential consecutive failed saves from Continuous Damage. A 15–17pt model killing a 30–50pt attacker is a favorable exchange; clipping multiple enemies or removing a key specialist/LT can be even more valuable.
- **Force a choice:** an enemy that Dodges can avoid the template on a successful Normal Roll, but gives up shooting that activation. This can preserve the Beasthunter and create another opportunity.
- **Fireteam punishment:** cover more than one model with the large template when positioning and LoF permit. Opponents must respect the threat even when the Beasthunter has not yet revealed.
- **B2 for 2pt:** option 4 may put both templates on one target or split them in the Active Turn. It forces two initial saves on a target hit by both, with each failed save potentially generating further Continuous Damage saves. It is not two separate Dodge Rolls: one successful Dodge can avoid both. The ordinary ARO remains B1. Do not add Surprise Attack penalties to a Normal Dodge against the direct templates.

Illustrative probability of at least one initial failed save against a target that shoots back and is hit (no special immunity/save modifiers; before modeling subsequent Continuous Damage):

| Target ARM | B1 HFT | B2 HFT |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 70% | 91% |
| 2 | 60% | 84% |
| 4 | 50% | 75% |
| 6 | 40% | 64% |
| 8 | 30% | 51% |

The second template is especially valuable against multi-Wound targets or grouped enemies; it can turn the Beasthunter into a genuine short-range attack threat rather than only an ARO speedbump. **Pay the extra 2pt when you intend to spend Orders attacking; save them for a primarily defensive piece.** These are conditional damage probabilities, not the probability of winning the entire engagement or killing a multi-Wound target.

#### Other weapons and tactical roles

##### Panzerfaust ambush

Panzerfaust: B1, PS6, AP+EXP, three saves per hit, Disposable (2), +3 at 16–32". The Camouflage marker lets you reserve the shot for a valuable target or force the opponent to Discover first. Active-turn Surprise Attack (-3), Mimetism (-3) and Partial Cover (-3) can combine against an eligible opposing BS Attack when all conditions apply. Do not mistake this for high-Burst gunfighting or assume Surprise Attack applies in ARO. The Panzerfaust adds range coverage and a real anti-TAG/HI threat to a model otherwise armed for close combat.

##### Tactical Bow

B1, PS8, DA ammunition, Silent (-6), +3 at 0–8", reusable. Can be useful for a qualifying attack from behind (including the Silent modifier where its conditions apply), but overlaps the existing flamethrower and DA CC Weapon. At the same cost the Panzerfaust provides a distinct long-range AP+EXP threat. **Normally skip the Bow.**

##### Super-Jump and close combat

Super-Jump lets the model declare Jump as its first Basic Short Skill and combine it with BS Attack, which can produce unexpected flamethrower angles over obstacles or onto elevated positions. It does not grant Jet Propulsion: use a legal trajectory and landing point, and do not assume a curved out-and-back jump. No Partial Cover MODs apply anywhere along the jump trajectory; avoid exposing the model to gratuitous AROs.

CC21, Surprise Attack (-3) on eligible active attacks, Stealth and DA CCW make the Beasthunter a credible assassin against ordinary infantry, REMs and non-CC-specialist TAGs; DA CCW is also Anti-materiel for appropriate objectives. It lacks Martial Arts and is not a dedicated answer to elite CC units. PH13 helps ordinary Dodges but ARM0/BTS0/W1 make any failed defense risky. Religious Troop automatically passes Guts unless the optional WIP Roll to withdraw succeeds; do not assume free retreat into cover.

##### AP Mine roadblock

All four options carry AP Mines (Disposable 3), not Shock Mines. AP is useful against armor; unlike Minelayer Jackals, no mine is prepaid at deployment. Use a personal Irregular Order to Move + Place Deployable, establish a separate threat angle, or reposition. Placing a mine reveals the Camouflage marker; returning to Camouflaged State requires a further legal activation and satisfaction of its requirements.

#### FTO: good weapons, poor Magistrate order synergy

The 9pt FTO is a very cheap standalone DZ defense piece: Mimetism, long Panzerfaust, large template, AP Mines and CC21 make it especially useful against airborne troops or melee intruders. It has **no Camouflage or Forward Deployment**.

Only this profile can join COLCORP (max 1). Unlike Jackal FTOs, it neither counts as ColCorp nor qualifies for **FT Master (Jackals)**. Its Order remains Irregular, even in the Fireteam.

Prefer the Jackal for a passive Magistrate Fireteam; consider the 9pt Beasthunter solo when a dedicated DZ guard is essential and points are too tight for the Camo profile.

#### Competing options

| Unit | Cost | Order | Main strengths |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infiltrating Jackal | 10–11 | Irregular | Deeper deployment, E/M, Shock Mines on selected profiles; cheap roadblock |
| Beasthunter Camo | 15–17 | Irregular | Reusable Camo, FWD8", Large HFT, AP Mines, CC21, Super-Jump |
| Yamabushi | 15–17 | Regular | FWD4", CC23/MA3, Dogged, Chain Rifle, Dodge (+5), Frenzy, some Camo (1 Use) profiles |
| Scarecrow | 25–29 | Regular when generating Orders | Infiltration, Hidden Deployment, Mimetism (-6), specialist options, higher BS |

**Jackals** better suit pure inexpensive area denial and E/M disruption; Beasthunters better suit aggressive destructive trades and ambushes. **Yamabushi** are the strongest direct competitor at similar points: better dedicated melee and Regular Order economy, but shallower deployment, no reusable Camouflage and no B2 Heavy Flamethrower. Chain Rifle is a large direct template but lacks the HFT's Continuous Damage. Frenzy is conditional, not a free Impetuous Order from turn one. **Scarecrows** fill a different role and specialist profiles should not be displaced just to add another attacker. Hidden Deployment does not contribute a pooled Order until eligible under its rules.

#### The cumulative Irregular Order issue — Group 2 is not a solution by itself

A forward Beasthunter can use its own Irregular Order to approach, place a mine, launch a Panzerfaust shot, trade with a HFT, or re-camouflage when legal. Starting 8" forward is helpful. However, it is less consistently self-sufficient than an infiltrating mine carrier or Sāchā in reach of an objective: approaching, revealing, attacking and repositioning can require several activations. It also cannot score typical specialist objectives.

**The real list-building problem is cumulative:** Kestrel has several tempting cheap Irregular pieces (infiltrating Jackals, Sāchā, Warcor, Beasthunter). Each has a reason to exist individually, yet loading all of them into a 15-trooper list can leave too few flexible pooled Orders for the army's main attackers. A linked Jackal converted by a Magistrate is not an additional Irregular Order for this calculation.

| Solo utility package | Points | SWC | Irregular Orders |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| E/M Carbine Jackal + Sāchā + Warcor | 19 | 0 | 3 |
| Above + 17pt Beasthunter | 36 | 0 | 4 |
| E/M GL Minelayer Jackal + Carbine Jackal + 17pt Beasthunter | 38 | 0.5 | 3 |
| Both Jackals + Sāchā + Warcor + 17pt Beasthunter | 47 | 0.5 | 5 |

In a full 15-trooper roster, five unconverted Irregular troopers can leave at most ten native/converted pooled Regular Orders from the other ten troopers, before any special order sources, deployment-state restrictions or casualties. Group 2 personal Orders are not a shared pool; a Command Token can convert one when legal, but this is not sustainable as routine support.

**Practical approach:** 
- Group 2 is the normal home for a solo defensive/ambush Beasthunter if it has a credible one-Order job and some pooled Orders for a follow-up attack. If the Beasthunter is intended for a major attack run, ensure the assigned group can actually fund it.
- One or two infiltrating Jackals and a Beasthunter can complement each other, but **do not automatically add Sāchā and Warcor on top**.
- If forced to choose, keep Sāchā when specialist redundancy matters, Warcor for the cheapest rear Flash Pulse coverage, Jackals for E/M/mines and Beasthunter for killing/trading up.
- If another Irregular slot would undermine the attack group's Order economy, replace a utility piece with a Regular Fennec, Tech-Bee or Yamabushi as appropriate, or skip the Beasthunter entirely. Count pooled Orders after the planned Fireteam conversions rather than assuming a fixed Irregular quota.

#### When to take / skip

**Take:** the list lacks a cheap large-template trade threat; opponent has expensive infantry or crowded Fireteams; vertical terrain permits unexpected attack angles; a Camouflage marker and AP anti-armor threats complement existing E/M; sufficient group Orders remain; anti-materiel CC objective is relevant.

**Skip:** existing Irregulars already strain pooled Orders; lack of specialists or 15-trooper slots is the actual constraint; other template carriers or Yamabushi adequately cover assault; the table offers few safe approach routes; Group 2 cannot fund the intended attacks.

**Bottom line:** 17pt double HFT + Panzerfaust is the preferred aggressive profile and an especially efficient way to trade up; 15pt single HFT + Panzerfaust is the economical defensive/ambush pick. Take neither automatically. The 9pt FTO is a niche DZ guard, and the 15pt Bow is normally a skip. The decision is less about finding 15–17 points than about whether this particular Irregular slot adds enough unique value to justify taking it away from a Regular troop or another utility piece.
