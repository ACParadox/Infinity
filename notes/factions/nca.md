### The 30-second listbuilding rule

**Start with a problem-solving chain, not an impressive Core:** (1) one premium gun to suppress the opponent; (2) a *different* approach if it fails (hidden attack, flank, Sensor, hacking or Redeye); (3) 1–2 Auxilia, cheap Fugazi, and a real objective plan; (4) recovery within reach; (5) optional small Fireteams if they improve the chain. NCA has inexpensive Regulars but its premium guns eat SWC and points. *Reason:* without an alternative opening, NCA can be shooting-locked when the first one or two engagements go wrong (Shepherd). Trent instead almost always starts with **a TAG + Auxilia + support**, a stronger emphasis on TAG-led construction.

### vidIQ transcript findings — concrete NCA listbuilding takeaways

*Transcript source A:* [Robert Shepherd — N5 NCA Faction Focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axa19k7qE1E) (2026-01-18, English). The following are **his recommendations and reasoning**, not an official consensus list:

1. **Plan for PanO lockout, not just your first gunfight.** NCA often buys only **two or three genuinely suitable premium guns** after SWC and points commitments. Since its best pieces are specialized, losing an Uhlan/Swiss and a second long-range answer can leave a Redeye or close-range tool forced into a disastrous long-range engagement. Before deployment, designate **primary ARO solver, alternative solver, third-turn objective piece, and a realistic Engineer/Doctor recovery route**. Keep expensive shooters out of unnecessary reactive engagements; preserve one independent threat to open a lane after bad dice.
2. **Treat cheap troops as real capabilities, not filler.** Shepherd particularly values **Fugazi AVA 3**, the **9-point Tech-Bee**, and **one or two Auxilia**. An illustrative 60-point infrastructure shell is **3 × 7-point Fugazi + 9-point Tech-Bee + 2 × 15-point basic Auxilia = 6 Regular Orders**; the two disposable Auxbots also screen approaches, and the package supplies repeater/flash/support capabilities. This is a *possible* starting module, not a six-unit mandatory tax. The transcript explicitly contrasts NCA's unusually broad cheap support with sectorials forced to choose between elite units and order count.
3. **Auxilia are a defining NCA tool.** Their Auxbots offer 360° defensive coverage, Mimetism (-3), and Heavy Flamethrower corner denial while the *Regular Order* remains on the controller if the Peripheral dies. The **15-point SMG Forward Observer Auxilia + Auxbot_2** is an especially efficient mission/network pick: both profiles are Forward Observers, and both carry Deployable Repeaters (the Auxilia controller carries one; Auxbot_2 carries another). Because synchronized Peripherals activate with their controller, there are opportunities to move/plant repeaters or attempt suitable objectives with both bodies in one Order, subject to coherency, AROs and mission wording.
4. **Conditional two-check objective probability:** if a scenario allows the Auxilia FO and Auxbot_2 FO to *each* perform an independent unmodified WIP roll on the same objective, the nominal success probability is **1 − (8/20 × 9/20) = 82%**, from **WIP 12 and WIP 11**. This is *not* a universal claim that all missions permit two activations of a console, nor that the models can ignore simultaneous-peripheral restrictions. Check the scenario's requirements. More broadly, dual access is a substantive reason to buy an Auxilia specialist instead of a plain cheerleader.
5. **Cheap passive Lieutenant shell is his baseline.** Shepherd argues that many lists do not need an expensive active LT or Chain of Command insurance: one **Fusilier LT plus a separate basic Fusilier decoy**, or an **Auxilia LT** when already purchasing Auxilia, often protects Orders and frees points. He is distinctly skeptical of routine purchases of the **Aquila Disco Baller LT** and **Richard Quinn CoC**, because their cost and role can be weak relative to simpler support. This is an important **difference in evaluation** from the playable Aquila Strategos/Quinn build-around suggestions elsewhere in this note. The best option depends on whether the premium LT meaningfully uses its extra capabilities and whether CoC is separated from the risky LT attack group.
6. **Avoid the automatic five-Fusilier Core.** His opening profile discussion describes generic Fusilier combat loadouts as mostly crowded out by better specialists or guns. Fusilier LT/decoy, and occasionally an affordable Hacker/Paramedic, are the main budget roles. Spend Fireteam slots on a **small purposeful team** if it solves a matchup, not solely to field five bodies.
7. **Limited smoke/visibility control is a real design cost.** He identifies expensive Aquila and Richard Quinn Disco Baller options as relatively awkward ways to obtain a safety valve. The best countermeasures are *not* to claim the army has plentiful smoke: select good attack angles, use camouflage/Hidden Deployment and Surprise Attack, preserve recovery, and plan for target-suppression failure.

*Transcript source B:* [Gravitypool Orbital Operations — Neoterra Is Back!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B4G8-VVj8yo) (2025-10-03, Spanish-language transcript). **These were launch/rework observations; validate profiles against the September 2026 Army snapshot:**

- **Flexible small Fireteams are a genuine advantage:** the presenter specifically explores **Fusilier + 2 Bulleteers** and **Fusilier/Blade Engineer/Bulleteer** constructions; the engineer brings Tactical Awareness and repair potential but **10-10 MOV can constrain a 15-10 REM/TAG's movement**. Determine whether the extra team utility and order are worth slowing the attack vector.
- **Ultima Ratio double Squalos:** a Squalos NCO and lower-cost Squalos create an optional two-TAG attack module with multiple self-funded activations. This is **not the default**: it concentrates points and still needs mission specialists and repair capacity. Spending the Lieutenant Order through NCO should be rules-checked for the intended Fireteam activation rather than assumed to move the whole Duo.
- **Hidden Hexa Counterintelligence:** Hexa's **Counterintelligence + Hidden Deployment** imposes an information dilemma. If you use its Counterintelligence protection when an opponent tries to strip Orders, you may reveal the *existence* of a Hexa while not disclosing its position/loadout. Its KHD/E/Marat provides a separate hidden threat against a hacking-enabled advance.
- **Do not copy the presenter's old Blocker loadout verbatim:** the transcript discusses a **Pitcher on the Firewall Hacker**. That is **inconsistent with current N5.3**: the **15-point Specialist Operative** has the Pitcher; the **19-point Hacker** has Firewall (-3) and **no Pitcher**. The underlying **network projector + defended hacker** plan remains good, but now needs the correct profiles.
- **Aquila FTO assessment differs by plan:** Gravitypool sees the linked Aquila FTO Disco Baller as a credible advancing spearhead; Shepherd questions its cost efficiency as a standard LT/visibility-control pick. **Keep both positions visible** instead of calling it a mandatory or universally poor inclusion.

**Synthesized construction choice (analysis, not a quote):** choose between (A) **support-dense 13–15 troop combined arms**, using inexpensive Regular Orders, 2–3 distinct guns, specialists and optional small Fireteams; and (B) **Tactical Awareness TAG compression**, accepting fewer Regular Orders but investing in Squalos/Uhlan/Pathfinder self-funded activity. Both need explicit objective access and an emergency lane-opening plan. Avoid treating TAG compression as proof that ordinary NCA should always aim for low model count.

## Core identity — specialist pieces and effective activations

The strongest repeated modern NCA theme is:

> **Use cheap structural/control pieces to enable a small number of extremely specialized premium threats, then exploit Tactical Awareness, Hidden Deployment, Repeaters, Sensor and flexible small Fireteams to get more useful activations than the raw Regular-Order count suggests.**

Current NCA has:

- **1 Core**;
- **1 Haris**;
- **unlimited Duos**;
- a very broad Wildcard pool;
- many Tactical Awareness profiles;
- several premium Hidden/Camouflage attackers;
- unusually dense Repeater/Sensor infrastructure for PanO.

A modern NCA list often looks more like:

> **cheap LT / support shell + 2–3 premium threats with different attack vectors + network/control layer + recovery + mission pieces**

than:

> **five-model Fusilier Core + one Swiss Guard + generic REMs.**

The old five-Fusilier structure remains legal and usable, but it is no longer the automatic center of the army.

## Major current list-building principle — threat diversity over redundant weapon roles

The current 2026 community discussion repeatedly recommends a premium package such as:

> **Uhlan + Redeye + Swiss Guard**

This works because those pieces fail differently:

- **Uhlan:** Camouflage TAG, long-range anti-armour hunter, durable chassis, Tactical Awareness.
- **Swiss Guard:** Hidden Deployment, Mimetism (-6), premium BS15 asymmetric attacker/ARO.
- **Redeye:** extreme speed, Mimetism (-6), BS14 exploitation piece.

The useful principle is:

> **Do not merely buy three expensive guns. Buy expensive pieces that attack from different information states, movement vectors and engagement bands.**

If the opponent solves one layer, the others should still function.

# Architecture A — premium combined-arms NCA

A broad, reliable structure is:

- one premium long-range piece, usually **Uhlan / Swiss / Squalos / Aquila**;
- one asymmetric or exploitation attacker, often **Swiss / Locust / Redeye / Garuda / Bulleteer**;
- Blocker / Pathfinder / Auxilia / Fugazi network infrastructure;
- an Engineer or Doctor appropriate to the premium pieces;
- cheap command shell.

A common strong starting idea is:

> **Uhlan + Swiss + Blocker Specialist + Pathfinder + Auxilia/Fugazi + cheap LT**

This gives conventional dominance, Hidden Deployment, Sensor/Pitcher utility, mission scoring, and enough cheap bodies to support the premium pieces.

## Uhlan — current premium darling

The current 70-point Feuerbach Uhlan is one of the most repeatedly praised current NCA profiles.

It brings:

- BS15;
- Camouflage;
- Mimetism (-3);
- Surprise Attack;
- Tactical Awareness;
- Feuerbach;
- Panzerfaust;
- Blitzen;
- BS Attack (+1B).

The community role is best described as:

> **Camouflage hunter-killer TAG, not static gun-platform TAG.**

It can remain a marker until the right engagement, avoid being forced into reactive duty, and use Surprise Attack on the critical attack.

## Swiss Guard — still one of the defining NCA pieces

Current useful profiles include:

- HMG;
- Missile Launcher (+1SD);
- Hacker with MULTI Rifle/X-Visor;
- cheap Forward Deployment AP-SMG profile;
- Forward Deployment KHD.

The important value is not only BS15 shooting.

That means Swiss can function as:

- active-turn assassin;
- hidden reactive threat;
- protected hacker/KHD;
- late-game mission piece;
- cheap-ish forward premium body on the AP-SMG profile.

A Swiss does not need to kill its points to justify itself if Hidden Deployment forces the opponent to respect angles and network threats that may not actually exist.

# Architecture B — Tactical Awareness / TAG compression

The 2026 Satellite-winning list provides strong evidence for a distinct NCA archetype:

> **Low raw Order count, high effective activation count.**

Its key pieces were:

- 2× Uhlan — each Tactical Awareness;
- Squalos Mk-II NCO — Tactical Awareness + NCO;
- Pathfinder Tactical Awareness;
- cheap Fusilier LT;
- Blade Engineer + Palbot support.

The list had only **9 Regular Orders**, but **5 Tactical Awareness Orders** before considering NCO interaction with the Lieutenant Order.

The important lesson is not “three TAGs are good.” It is:

> **NCA can compress a large amount of active-turn work into expensive pieces that partially fund themselves.**

This allows low-headcount lists to remain functional without paying for a conventional 14–15 Regular Order pool.

## Fusilier LT → Squalos NCO

This is one of NCA's cleanest command/economy packages.

Current Squalos Mk-II NCO profile brings:

- MULTI HMG;
- Tactical Awareness;
- NCO;
- BS15 TAG chassis.

A 10-point Fusilier LT therefore contributes a Lieutenant Order that can become useful attack fuel instead of remaining stranded in the DZ.

This package is particularly attractive in TAG-heavy lists where the Fusilier LT is otherwise just cheap command infrastructure.

That means it often self-funds the movement needed to:

# NCA hacking — excellent geometry, not necessarily elite individual hackers

Current community discussion contains a useful disagreement:

- some players call NCA's hackers themselves merely average;
- others call NCA one of PanO's best hacking factions because the network is so dense.

The clean reconciliation is:

> **NCA's hacking quality is decent; its hacking geometry is excellent.**

Network/projection pieces include:

- Blocker Specialist Pitcher;
- Fugazi Repeaters;
- Pathfinder Repeaters;
- Bulleteer Repeaters;
- Peacemaker Repeaters;
- Auxilia Deployable Repeater profile;
- Tech-Bee Deployable Repeater;
- Agent Maximus TinBot Repeater;

Actual hackers include:

- Blocker Hacker;
- Bolt HD/KHD;
- Deva HD/KHD;
- Locust HD/KHD;
- Hexa HD/KHD;
- Swiss HD/KHD;
- Fusilier Hacker.

The goal is usually not to out-hack Nomads directly.

The goal is:

> **Make enemy HI/TAG movement through the midfield expensive and uncertain while your BS14–15 pieces dominate ordinary gunfights.**

## Visible network + hidden hacker

A particularly strong architecture is:

> **Blocker/Fugazi/Pathfinder/Auxilia network + hidden Hexa KHD or Swiss/Locust hacker.**

The opponent can see the Repeaters but may not know where the actual counter-hacker is.

# Final list-building heuristic

A strong NCA list should normally answer these questions before filling the remaining points:

1. **What opens the table?** — Uhlan, Swiss, Squalos, Aquila, Bolt package.
2. **What attacks from a different axis?** — Hidden Swiss/Locust, Redeye, Garuda, Bulleteer.
3. **How do I detect/pressure marker states?** — Blocker, Pathfinder, Hannibal, Aquila, Deva.
4. **How do I pressure hackable targets?** — Repeaters/Pitchers plus one or more appropriate hackers.
5. **Who scores after the expensive pieces fight?** — Pathfinder, Auxilia, Blocker, Blade, hidden specialist.
6. **Can I recover my expensive pieces?** — Blade/Machinist/Trauma-Doc + Palbot as appropriate.
7. **Does my command plan generate usable Orders?** — cheap LT, Squalos NCO, TacAware density, or aggressive Aquila/Maximus LT.
8. **Am I paying for a Fireteam because it solves a problem, or merely because I can fill it?**

The main modern NCA insight is:

> **Do not judge the list only by raw Regular Orders or raw BS. Judge how many useful activations the army can produce, how many different attack vectors the opponent must respect, and how cheaply the support shell can create favorable engagements for the premium pieces.**
