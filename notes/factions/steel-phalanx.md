# Steel Phalanx — listbuilding and profile selection

**Current rules/roster authority:** [Steel Phalanx N5.3 roster, unit profiles and canonical Fireteam chart](../../sectorials/aleph/steel-phalanx.md) (Army **7.26246.159**); [local N5.3 rules](../../rules/README.md). This is the uploaded tactical synthesis, not a separate legality export. The source's already-recorded video/podcast transcript notes are retained with their attribution; **this merge did not re-fetch those transcripts**.

**Fireteam corrections against current N5.3 chart:** Myrmidon and Thorakitai **Core teams max 4 models**. **Scylla FTO belongs in a Steel Phalanx Duo/Haris requiring a Hoplite or Makhai**, not a Thorakitai Core. A Thorakitai Core requires a Thorakites and may add specified Dactyl/Acmon/K2 FTO or Wildcards, but **not Scylla**. **Ajax requires the 39-point FTO profile** to join a Myrmidon Core; his forward-deployed non-FTO option cannot. **K2 Auxiliars need FTO profiles for eligible non-Myrmidon teams**; they do not join Myrmidon Core.

## Core identity

Steel Phalanx is an elite combined-arms sectorial built around unusually efficient heroic infantry, Myrmidon mobility and visual control, compact Fireteams, and a cheap support shell that lets those premium pieces actually receive enough Orders.

The faction is **not** best understood as “take two expensive Myrmidon teams and run them forward.” Current N5/N5.2 examples repeatedly point toward a more disciplined structure:

> **cheap Regular Order infrastructure + one compact purpose-built Fireteam + one major independent or secondary attack piece + specialists/support**

Steel Phalanx has many attractive 25–40 point characters. The primary listbuilding trap is buying too many models that all want to be the active attacker.

A good default sequence is:

1. establish the Order economy;
2. choose the Lieutenant / Chain of Command / NCO architecture;
3. choose one primary attack engine;
4. add one qualitatively different secondary threat;
5. add Eclipse / Smoke access appropriate to the plan;
6. add Engineer, Hacker and specialist support as needed;
7. only then finish Fireteam slots.

---

## Strong recurring building blocks

Across current N5 discussion, YouTube sample lists and tournament material, the most repeated building blocks are:

- **Netrods and Lamedh Rebots** as cheap Regular Orders;
- **Myrmidon Officer**, especially because current Steel Phalanx relies on the Officer for Eclipse and can use the Chain of Command profile to insure aggressive Lieutenants;
- **Ajax FTO** as a brutal close/mid-range assault piece;
- **Phoenix** as an efficient long/mid-range gun and reactive threat;
- **Machaon** as WIP 15 Doctor / specialist / possible Lieutenant;
- **Thorakitai** as inexpensive ARM 3 orders, specialists, E/M and template utility;
- **Dactyl or Acmon** for Engineer support depending on architecture;
- **Scylla** when the list wants a real hacking game;
- **Agamemnon** as a highly coherent primary attack-engine archetype;
- **Atalanta or Agêma** when the list wants a premium asymmetric long-range threat;
- **K2 Auxiliars** as extremely cheap utility / area-control bodies;
- **Alkê** when NCO and a Thorakitai-oriented Fireteam are useful.

The important pattern is that the **support shell often repeats more reliably than the premium centerpiece**.

# Order economy

## 2× Netrod + 2× Lamedh is a real recurring shell

In the Yellow Snake / RXron Steel Phalanx review, all three posted example rosters used:

> **2 Netrods + 2 Lamedh Rebots**

The CNN / Sven sample list also used two Lamedh.

That does not make four cheap REM/order pieces mandatory, but it is strong evidence for the underlying listbuilding principle:

> **Do not spend 250 points on Greeks and then discover that the Greeks do not have enough Orders to function.**

Use the cheap shell when:

- the list contains several 30–65 point premium models;
- the main attack pieces are very Order-hungry;
- you need to reach a healthy model count without degrading the quality of the main threats;
- cheap repeaters / Flash Pulses also contribute to defence.

Reduce the shell when:

- roster slots are more valuable than raw Orders;
- the mission rewards more specialists / midfield bodies;
- Tactical Awareness, NCO and other special Orders already make the list highly efficient;
- a cheap Thorakites, K2 or other body adds materially more table presence than another passive Order.

The principle is **cheap infrastructure**, not a sacred four-model tax.

---

# Myrmidon Officer — more central in current Steel Phalanx

The Myrmidon Officer is much more than a generic command profile.

Current Steel Phalanx relies on the Officer for **Eclipse**, which changes how older N4 advice should be read. Machaon now brings normal Smoke rather than filling the old all-purpose Eclipse role, and Eudoros should no longer be assumed to solve Eclipse access by himself.

That makes the Officer strategically important for:

- crossing MSV-covered lanes;
- moving expensive attack pieces through hostile visual-control networks;
- protecting an advance that normal Smoke cannot safely solve;
- providing Chain of Command for an aggressive Lieutenant;
- serving as a cheap Lieutenant when the list does not want a premium command architecture.

## Chain of Command principle

A recurring architecture is:

> **aggressive Lieutenant + Myrmidon Officer CoC**

This allows the Lieutenant to fight without making Loss of Lieutenant catastrophic.

However:

> **Do not automatically put the Lieutenant and the Chain of Command backup in the same Fireteam or same attack footprint.**

That exposes both command models to the same:

- template;
- mine / deployable;
- hacking area;
- concentrated attack run;
- Guided attack;
- DZ strike;
- bad Fireteam engagement.

CoC is most valuable when succession survives the thing that killed the Lieutenant.

# Major Fireteam architectures

Steel Phalanx should not be built around the assumption that every legal four-model team should be filled with four expensive named characters.

A good Fireteam usually contains **one primary gun / assault piece, one movement or command enabler, one specialist/support model, and one economical body**.

## Myrmidon assault team

Typical roles:

- **Phoenix** — ranged gun / ARO / secondary attacker;
- **Myrmidon Officer** — Eclipse / command utility;
- **Machaon, Ajax FTO or Eudoros** — specialist or assault capability;
- **cheap fourth body / utility Wildcard** — preserve efficiency.

Use this when the team itself is intended to move through the table and solve several engagement bands.

Avoid stacking four expensive heroes that all compete for active-turn Orders.

---

## Phoenix + Officer + Machaon + filler

A strong general-purpose structure.

It combines:

- Phoenix for serious ranged work;
- Officer for Eclipse;
- Machaon for Doctor / WIP 15 objective work / smoke;
- an economical fourth body to avoid turning the team into a giant points sink.

The key is complementarity: each expensive model should solve a different problem.

---

## Phoenix + Officer + Ajax + filler

A much more violent team.

The roles are clear:

- Phoenix opens longer lanes;
- Officer gets the team through hostile visual defence;
- Ajax dominates close and medium engagement ranges and punishes anything that survives the approach;
- the fourth body should usually be cheap or highly functional.

This is expensive but coherent because Phoenix and Ajax do not want exactly the same engagements.

---

## Ajax + Officer + Engineer/support + cheap body

Useful when Ajax is the main midfield hammer.

Officer provides Eclipse movement, while a Dactyl or other legal support option gives the package objective / repair / utility value.

Do not treat Ajax as the long-range opener. His job is usually to exploit a lane after something else has made the initial approach safe.

A useful sequencing concept is:

> **ranged opener → Smoke/Eclipse movement → Ajax clears the midfield**

---

## Thorakitai / utility and Steel Phalanx Duo/Haris packages

The CNN / Sven N5 source illustrates a **combined-arms list**, not necessarily a single legal Fireteam containing all the models it names.

- **Thorakitai Core (up to four):** at least one Thorakites, with eligible **Acmon, Dactyl, Makhai or K2 FTO** (subject to each chart cap), plus legal Wildcards such as **Alkê**. Its cheap ARM3 bodies, specialists and Pulzars provide the support engine.
- **Steel Phalanx Duo/Haris:** **Hoplite or Makhai** must supply the required-source foundation; a **Scylla FTO** can join this team, bringing Pitcher, Hacking Device/Trinity and E/M, alongside eligible support.
- **Separate assault module:** **Machaon and Ajax** can be purchased as independent threats or as members of a valid Myrmidon Core only with the correct FTO profile and required Myrmidon-source member.

The tactical conclusion still holds: **Hoplite/Alkê** shooting, **Scylla** network projection, **Acmon** engineering, cheap **Thorakitai** objectives and **Machaon/Ajax** close assault can cover complementary jobs. **Do not budget these six named roles as one Fireteam**, and do not put Scylla in a Thorakitai Core. The source example's exact team structure is historical unless an actual legal N5.3 split is specified.

---

## Minimal Myrmidon team

Do not assume four models are always required.

A 2–3-model functional package can be enough when the rest of the army includes:

- Agamemnon;
- Atalanta / Agêma;
- Scylla;
- Thyreos;
- Arjuna;
- Makhai;
- independent specialists and cheap support.

This reduces the risk of putting half the army into one attack footprint.

---

# Ajax

Ajax appears repeatedly enough in current sample and tournament lists to treat him as a near-default **consideration**, even though he is not mandatory.

His best role is not simply “another Myrmidon character.”

He is the piece that converts a successful approach into decisive close/mid-range removal.

Use Ajax when:

- another piece can open the long lane;
- Smoke / Eclipse can get him into his preferred engagement band;
- the opponent has durable pieces that need overwhelming short-range violence;
- you want an assault piece that remains dangerous after taking damage.

Do not use Ajax as an excuse to omit a real long-range plan.

A common good division of labour is:

> **Phoenix / Hoplite / Agamemnon opens → Officer or Smoke enables movement → Ajax finishes**

---

# Phoenix

Phoenix is one of Steel Phalanx's strongest general-purpose ranged pieces, but he is **not mandatory**.

His value comes from combining:

- strong active shooting;
- a useful reactive profile;
- Myrmidon durability / Mimetism;
- a weapon that does not depend on MSV;
- Fireteam integration.

## Phoenix as a Turn 1 defender

A tournament Agamemnon example demonstrates an important role:

- Agamemnon does the early killing;
- the Myrmidon team initially stays relatively safe;
- Phoenix provides ARO / lane pressure;
- the team advances later after the opponent has been damaged.

This is often better than spending Turn 1 Orders pushing both Agamemnon and a Phoenix team forward.

Think of Phoenix as potentially:

- **Turn 1:** roadblock / ARO / insurance;
- **Turn 2:** secondary attacker;
- **Turn 3:** mobile objective-clearing gun.

That is a more efficient use of the army than forcing every premium model to attack immediately.

---

# Agamemnon archetype

Agamemnon is one of Steel Phalanx's clearest primary-engine builds.

A recurring coherent shell is:

> **Agamemnon + Engineer + compact Myrmidon package + Officer/CoC + cheap Orders**

The TAG handles the initial brute-force attack while the Greek infantry remain available for the later game.

## Typical division of labour

**Agamemnon**
- opens long lanes;
- attacks hard targets;
- exploits Tactical Awareness;
- can make a very aggressive Lieutenant choice when backed by CoC.

**Dactyl Engineer**
- repairs the expensive TAG;
- provides D-Charges / mission utility;
- may add Deployable Cover, Armed Turret or other useful table control depending on profile.

**Myrmidon team**
- starts safer;
- provides Eclipse / Smoke / short-range violence;
- takes over objective work after the TAG has damaged the opponent.

**cheap Netrod/Lamedh/K2/Thorakitai shell**
- keeps enough Orders in the list to support both phases.

The key principle is:

> **Let the TAG break the table, then let the Greeks inherit a damaged board.**

Do not spend the first turn trying to activate every premium piece.

---

# Engineer choice — Dactyl vs Acmon

Steel Phalanx should usually have a deliberate Engineer plan, but **Dactyl is not an automatic tax**.

## Dactyl

Prefer when:

- Agamemnon or another expensive repairable piece is central to the list;
- the list contains several important REMs;
- you want additional deployable / area-control utility;
- Fireteam integration with the chosen attack group is valuable.

Dactyl becomes particularly compelling when repairing one successful Engineer roll can restore a 60+ point primary attack engine.

## Acmon

Prefer when:

- the list is primarily infantry / Thorakitai-based;
- you want Engineer utility integrated into a conventional fighting Fireteam;
- Panzerfaust, AP gun and D-Charge utility matter;
- there is no single expensive repairable centerpiece demanding a dedicated mechanic.

The rule is:

> **Have the right Engineer for the architecture; do not take Dactyl merely because Dactyl is popular.**

---

# Scylla

Scylla is the natural starting point when Steel Phalanx wants a genuine hacking game rather than a token Hacker for objectives.

Her value is the combination of:

- Pitcher;
- strong offensive hacking / Trinity capability;
- E/M;
- FTO integration on the appropriate profile;
- Charybdis / synchronized repeater and template pressure on the non-FTO architecture.

## Scylla FTO

The source example shows why Scylla FTO can be useful in a **Hoplite/Makhai-founded Steel Phalanx Duo or Haris**, **not** in a Thorakitai Core. Her hacking adds a different attack vector to a legal Steel Phalanx team:

- gunfighter handles normal FtF attacks;
- Scylla threatens Hackable targets;
- Pitcher projects the hacking area;
- E/M creates non-lethal control options;
- specialists can continue toward objectives.

This makes the Fireteam harder to counter with a single defensive tool.

## Charybdis version

Prefer when:

- the synchronized body can safely project a Repeater / Heavy Flamethrower angle;
- you want hacking presence outside a Fireteam;
- multi-body movement and disposable threat projection are more useful than FTO integration.

Do not take Scylla solely because “every list needs a Hacker.” Take her when the list will actually exploit repeaters, Pitchers, Hackable targets or E/M control.

---

# Machaon

Machaon remains excellent, but current advice must distinguish him from older editions.

His main attractions are:

- WIP 15;
- Doctor;
- durable Myrmidon body;
- Smoke;
- strong objective capability;
- viable Lieutenant option.

He is no longer the all-in-one answer to **Doctor + Lieutenant + Eclipse**.

That makes the Myrmidon Officer more important beside him when Eclipse is required.

Use Machaon when:

- the Fireteam wants a high-quality Doctor that can survive near the fight;
- WIP 15 specialist work matters;
- a useful, durable Lieutenant is attractive;
- normal Smoke already solves enough of the movement problem or an Officer supplies Eclipse separately.

---

# Eudoros

Eudoros is best understood as a mobile midrange assault / objective / NCO piece rather than as the old automatic Eclipse carrier.

His useful roles include:

- NCO order conversion;
- mobile midrange gunfighting;
- specialist work;
- Number 2 / Fireteam resilience;
- durable Myrmidon attack presence.

Pair him with the Officer when the list needs Eclipse.

He is particularly attractive in a Lieutenant architecture that creates Special LT Orders which would otherwise be wasted.

---

# Alkê

Alkê becomes much more interesting when viewed as part of the **Lieutenant-order economy** rather than only as a Thorakitai character.

A good architecture is:

> **Machaon LT + Alkê NCO**

Machaon contributes:

- WIP 15 Lieutenant;
- Doctor;
- specialist capability;
- Smoke;
- respectable fighting ability.

Alkê converts the Lieutenant Order through NCO while contributing:

- conventional shooting;
- Thorakitai Fireteam integration;
- Disco Baller / utility depending on profile.

She is therefore often better in lists that deliberately intend to use NCO than in lists where NCO is merely incidental.

---

# Lieutenant architectures

Steel Phalanx has several genuinely different command plans.

## Myrmidon Officer LT

Use when:

- you want a relatively economical command model;
- Eclipse is already wanted;
- you do not need Chain of Command for another aggressive Lieutenant;
- simplicity and survivability matter more than generating extra Orders.

Tradeoff: making the Officer LT means you are not using her strong CoC role.

---

## Machaon LT

Use when:

- Machaon is already in the list;
- WIP 15 and Doctor utility are useful;
- you want a Lieutenant that can participate in the game rather than hide completely;
- an NCO such as Alkê or Eudoros can monetize the Lieutenant Order when appropriate.

If Machaon will fight aggressively, consider a separately protected Officer CoC.

---

## Hector LT

Hector is an architecture, not merely a premium fighter.

Take him because you want:

- LT +1 Order;
- Strategos;
- strong fighting capability;
- TinBot / Fireteam utility;
- NCO conversion elsewhere in the list.

Hector makes the most sense when the list deliberately converts his command resources into extra useful activations.

Do not pay for Hector if the rest of the list has no good way to exploit what makes his LT profile unique.

---

## Agamemnon LT

A strong aggressive-Lieutenant architecture when Agamemnon was already going to be the primary attack engine.

The logic is:

- you already paid for the TAG;
- the TAG is going to spend Orders and expose itself anyway;
- LT value can be extracted from the same centerpiece;
- Myrmidon Officer CoC can protect the army from catastrophic succession failure.

Keep the CoC model physically separate from the TAG's likely attack route.

---

## Phoenix / other fighting LT options

These can work when:

- the profile is already a central part of the list;
- CoC is available;
- the mission or matchup rewards an aggressive command model.

But do not attach Loss-of-Lieutenant risk to a primary ARO / attack piece without a reason.

---

# NCO packages

NCO is particularly valuable in Steel Phalanx because several attractive Lieutenants generate or possess Special LT Orders that would otherwise constrain sequencing.

Useful NCO concepts include:

- **Machaon LT + Alkê NCO**;
- **Machaon / Hector LT + Eudoros NCO**;
- **Hector LT + suitable NCO outlet**, allowing Hector's command economy to fuel another active piece rather than forcing every Special Order through Hector himself.

The key question is not “does the list contain NCO?”

It is:

> **Is there a Lieutenant Order that the NCO can convert into an activation the list genuinely wanted anyway?**

If not, NCO is just a line on the profile.

---

# Atalanta and Agêma — keep their N5.3 loadouts distinct

**Atalanta (AVA 1)**: the **38-point MULTI Sniper** has Hidden Deployment and Surprise Attack, so the opponent cannot pre-position against her easily; the **41-point Total Reaction** sniper instead pays for overt, sustained reactive pressure. Both have MSV2. Pick the role, not just the name.

**Agêma (AVA 2)**: the **27-point MULTI Sniper** still has **Hidden Deployment** and Surprise Attack; the **27-point Missile Launcher** has **Camouflage (1 Use) + Decoy (1)** instead, **not Hidden Deployment**. The missile profile conceals identity / creates deployment uncertainty but is not the same surprise-from-off-table tool. Both have MSV2; choose the cheaper *capability* relative to Atalanta's higher BS, Marksmanship and equipment.

**Why this matters**: N5.2/N5.3 video discussion sometimes shortens the missile-profile change to “Agêma lost Hidden Deployment.” That is true **for the ML loadout**, not for the still-legal sniper. Neither is compulsory when Phoenix, Agamemnon, or Hoplite already supplies sufficient long-range coverage. This distinction is reconciled against the N5.3 Army reference (version `7.26246.159`, verified 2026-10-15).

---

# Smoke + MSV is strong but should not be the only ranged plan

Steel Phalanx can exploit Smoke + MSV interactions, but do not build the entire attack plan around that one trick.

White Noise and other counters can switch off the MSV shooter.

This is one reason Phoenix remains valuable beside Atalanta / Agêma:

- MSV piece solves one family of targets;
- Phoenix provides a high-quality ranged threat that does not depend on MSV.

The list should retain at least one credible attack route when the opponent invalidates the vision-control plan.

---

# Thorakitai

Thorakitai are easy to overlook beside named heroes, but they are central to keeping Steel Phalanx efficient.

They provide:

- relatively cheap Regular Orders;
- ARM 3 bodies;
- specialists;
- E/M threat;
- Pulzar / close-range deterrence;
- Fireteam filler that does not consume 30+ points per slot.

Use them to make premium pieces better rather than asking every Fireteam slot to be individually spectacular.

A Thorakites that spends most of the game providing an Order, guarding a route and pushing one objective may be doing exactly what the list needs.

---

# K2 Auxiliars

K2s are an excellent example of a low-glamour Steel Phalanx piece that can solve expensive problems cheaply.

Useful roles include:

- **FTO-only** filler in eligible Dactyl / Steel Phalanx / Thorakitai teams (**not** Myrmidon Core);
- corner guard;
- DZ defence;
- E/Marat or disposable area control;
- protecting support models from AD / warband / close assault;
- late-game specialist utility on the appropriate profile.

A tournament example reportedly used a cheap K2 primarily as a **corner guard**.

That is a good valuation lesson:

> **An 11–12 point model does not need to kill 30 points to be efficient.**

If it prevents an enemy parachutist or warband from walking into Phoenix, the LT shell, Dactyl or Netrods, it has already protected far more than its cost.

---

# Thyreos

Thyreos is useful when Steel Phalanx needs an **autonomous midfield body** rather than another model starting in the deployment zone.

Its package can provide:

- forward position;
- one-use Camo / Surprise Attack;
- mines;
- D-Charges;
- specialist or Hacker options;
- Monofilament close-combat threat;
- independent mission pressure.

Take Thyreos when the main Fireteam cannot afford to spend early Orders simply walking toward every midfield objective.

It is especially valuable in more distributed lists that deliberately avoid concentrating all capability in one Greek team.

---

# Makhai

Makhai are worth considering as self-funding independent pieces because Tactical Awareness reduces the opportunity cost of activating them.

Useful roles include:

- medium-range independent attacker;
- K1 / anti-armor pressure;
- specialist work;
- MSV1 utility;
- Missile Launcher / ranged threat on the appropriate profile.

They are not cheap enough to add casually. Take one when its particular weapon / MSV / specialist combination solves a gap that the Myrmidon team does not.

---

# Arjuna

Arjuna became more relevant to Steel Phalanx with the 2026 Army update and should be evaluated as a mobile specialist / self-funding utility piece rather than as a traditional Greek hero.

The FTO profile is particularly interesting because Tactical Awareness can add efficiency to a Fireteam without requiring another 30–40 point character.

Use Arjuna when:

- the list wants a mobile specialist that contributes its own Order efficiency;
- a Fireteam needs a useful mid-cost Wildcard rather than another premium hero;
- the army needs more distributed capability outside the primary Myrmidon package.

---

# Lesser-used / situational pieces — general principle

Steel Phalanx's named heroes are attractive enough that quieter profiles can be under-selected.

When evaluating a lesser-used piece, ask whether it provides something the hero stack lacks:

- cheap Regular Order;
- midfield deployment;
- Sensor / discovery support;
- hacking / repeater projection;
- Engineer access;
- E/M / state warfare;
- disposable area control;
- an independent specialist;
- a defensive body that does not require active-turn Orders.

A 20-point utility model that does a job no hero can efficiently cover may improve the list more than the fourth 35-point attack character.

---

# What is *not* mandatory

## Phoenix

Excellent, but not mandatory. Agamemnon, Hoplite, Atalanta/Agêma and other structures can cover long-range needs.

## Machaon

Still excellent, but no longer the old automatic Doctor + LT + Eclipse package.

## Eudoros

Very useful when NCO, mobility and midrange fighting are wanted, but not a compulsory Myrmidon character.

## Atalanta

Powerful, especially from Hidden Deployment, but expensive. Agêma or a non-MSV ranged plan may fit better.

## Two Fireteams

Definitely not mandatory. A compact Fireteam plus independent threats is often cleaner than two expensive teams competing for Orders.

## Maximum model count at any cost

Steel Phalanx can gain substantial action efficiency from:

- Tactical Awareness;
- NCO;
- LT +1 Order;
- Fireteam movement;
- Impetuous movement where relevant.

The goal is enough **usable Orders**, not hitting a model-count target by filling the army with bodies that do nothing.

# Common listbuilding traps

## 1. The Greek Avengers trap

A Fireteam containing several famous heroes looks powerful but can be strategically inefficient if every member is primarily an active attacker.

Ask of every expensive model:

> **What does this model do that the other expensive models cannot already do?**

If the answer is only “also kills things at 8–24 inches,” cut one.

---

## 2. Double expensive Fireteam temptation

Steel Phalanx can construct multiple dangerous teams, but points spent on the second premium team are points not spent on:

- Orders;
- midfield presence;
- Engineers / Hackers;
- Hidden Deployment;
- deployables;
- mission specialists;
- independent secondary threats.

Build the second team only when it has a distinct job.

---

## 3. No long-range opener

Ajax and Myrmidons are terrifying once they reach the right range.

They still need a way to get there.

Include a credible plan such as:

- Phoenix;
- Agamemnon;
- Hoplite;
- Atalanta / Agêma;
- another suitable ranged attacker.

Eclipse helps movement, but repeatedly throwing an elite Fireteam across open table space is not a substitute for ranged pressure.

---

## 4. Treating Eclipse as generic Smoke

Normal Smoke and Eclipse solve different problems.

If the opponent's MSV / visual-control network is the thing preventing movement, ensure the list actually includes the current Eclipse source rather than relying on an older memory of Machaon / Eudoros profiles.

---

## 5. CoC sitting beside the aggressive LT

Do not insure an aggressive LT with CoC and then expose both models to the same attack route.

Succession needs spatial separation.

---

## 6. Support pieces with no supported system

Do not take:

- Scylla without a plan to exploit hacking / repeaters;
- Dactyl without repairable or deployable value;
- NCO without LT Orders worth converting;
- expensive MSV without a role that the rest of the ranged package does not already fill.

Every support profile should plug into an actual architecture.

# Practical list archetypes

## 1. Myrmidon assault

Typical structure:

- Phoenix / Ajax / Eudoros as attack piece;
- Myrmidon Officer for Eclipse and possibly CoC;
- Machaon or other specialist/support;
- cheap fourth body;
- Netrod/Lamedh/Thorakitai support shell;
- independent ranged or midfield threat.

Use when the Fireteam itself is the main delivery system.

---

## 2. Agamemnon spearhead

Typical structure:

- Agamemnon primary attacker;
- Dactyl Engineer;
- Officer CoC if using an aggressive LT architecture;
- compact Ajax/Phoenix/Myrmidon package;
- cheap Orders;
- optional Hidden Deployment ranged piece.

Use when you want the TAG to do the Turn 1 heavy lifting and preserve the Greeks for later turns.

---

## 3. Hector command economy

Typical structure:

- Hector LT;
- one or more meaningful NCO outlets;
- compact Fireteam rather than maximum hero saturation;
- cheap infrastructure to exploit the extra Orders;
- secondary attack piece that benefits from Hector's command economy.

Use when the army is deliberately built around Special LT Order conversion and Strategos.

---

## 4. Thorakitai combined arms

Typical structure:

- Hoplite or Alkê shooting piece;
- **a legal Steel Phalanx Duo/Haris with Hoplite or Makhai + Scylla FTO**, if using a linked network;
- a **separate Thorakitai Core** with Thorakites and permitted Acmon/Dactyl/Alkê support, when budget allows;
- independent Ajax/Machaon or a legal Myrmidon assault Core as another element;
- Atalanta/Agêma/Zayin or another independent ranged/reactive piece.

Use when you want a broader combined-arms game rather than concentrating points into a Myrmidon brick.

---

## 5. Distributed Steel Phalanx

Typical structure:

- small Fireteam;
- Thyreos / Arjuna / Makhai / Scylla;
- one premium hero;
- cheap Orders;
- multiple independent specialists and attack angles.

Use when the mission rewards board coverage and redundancy more than one overwhelming assault team.

---

# Turn sequencing concepts

## Primary attacker first, Greeks later

A recurring strong pattern is not to activate every expensive model immediately.

Example:

1. Agamemnon / Hoplite / long-range piece opens the table;
2. opponent spends resources dealing with that threat;
3. Myrmidon team stays relatively safe and preserves bodies;
4. later turns use Eclipse / Smoke to move the team through the damaged board;
5. Ajax / Phoenix / specialist characters clean up and score.

This preserves flexibility and reduces overextension.

---

## Do not spend Orders proving every model is good

Steel Phalanx has so many efficient profiles that it is easy to give each premium model “a few Orders.”

That is usually worse than concentrating Orders into the model with the best current matchup.

A model that contributes only:

- one ARO;
- one Eclipse toss;
- one Doctor activation;
- one objective interaction;
- passive command insurance;

may still have done its job.

---

# Fast listbuilding checklist

Before finalizing a Steel Phalanx list, answer:

1. **What is my Turn 1 primary attacker?**
2. **What is my second attack vector if that piece dies or faces a bad matchup?**
3. **How many cheap Regular Orders support those pieces?**
4. **Do I need Eclipse, and if so where is the Myrmidon Officer?**

## What changed the evaluation

- **The defensive package is hard to trade into, not invincible.** Robert Shepherd's “closed-loop defence” analysis explains why a Myrmidon group resists *several* attack methods at once: Mimetism (-6) frustrates guns, strong Dodge/CC frustrates short-range trades, and Smoke/Eclipse can change the angle. His 2026 faction-tier discussion independently emphasizes this resilience. **Why it matters:** force the opponent into multiple expensive Orders instead of volunteering a fair long-range firefight. **Counterpoint:** templates, mines, positioning, MSV, and targeted anti-hero attacks still work; Ajax in particular is hackable. ##### “Never take” audit

- **No source-backed absolute veto against any whole currently legal Steel Phalanx unit.** This army has mutually exclusive good archetypes; calling Phoenix, Eudoros, Machaon or Agamemnon “must” makes the other credible builds impossible.
- **Avoid buying two costly, redundant Greek hero Fireteams** with no Orders, independent attack vector, or objective pieces left. Shepherd's older focus and the N5 CNN sample illustrate different viable architectures. It is a **list-construction trap**, not an intrinsically weak named unit.
- **Avoid Scylla FTO25 solely because the label looks linkable** if the chosen team does not qualify. It sacrifices the synchronized Charybdis compared with **non-FTO Scylla32**. Valid FTO teams and the **Scylla FTO + independent Charybdis FTO37** are legitimate separate choices; current canonical [Steel Phalanx Fireteam chart](../../sectorials/aleph/steel-phalanx.md#canonical-fireteam-chart) takes precedence.
- **Avoid old assumptions** that Achilles is in current Steel Phalanx, that Machaon personally gives the old Eclipse package, that a five-person Core is legal, or that non-FTO Ajax can join a Myrmidon Core. These are **legality/edition errors**, not current 'bad units'.

1. **The least debatable generic ALEPH value is Posthumans where available, cheap Regular Order infrastructure, and a repair/network plan.** Steel Phalanx substitutes its compact, complementary Myrmidon/Thorakitai architecture for the OSS/Vanilla Posthuman engine.
2. **Most credible explicit “auto-include” claims in retrieved *modern* media:** Pilot-X in the May 2026 [Tactical Awareness OSS episode](https://open.spotify.com/episode/3E6Og0ydRErBokXHYOG6PG); **15pt Sharvara** is emphatically favored around 75m; the June [Shepherd/Jordan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1dW3i9dpI34) review calls Posthumans extraordinary but *does not establish literal 100% tournament usage*.
