### Fugazi Dronbot (Flashbot) — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: S+ as a cheap Order generator; strong default pick, not an unconditional must-have.** At **7 points / 0 SWC**, Fugazi is an extremely efficient native-Regular cheerleader that also covers an approach with **WIP13 Flash Pulse + Mimetism (-3)** and supplies an **always-on mobile Repeater**. It is worth taking even if it never spends an Order. Advance it only when the resulting position or hacking opportunity is worth the movement Orders and exposure.

#### Pinned N5.3 profile

**Kestrel Fugazi Dronbot:** **AVA 1**, REM, Regular, **MOV 15-15 cm (6-6")**, CC 13, BS 8, PH 11, **WIP 13**, ARM 0, BTS 3, **W 1 (pinned roster)**, **S3**. **Repeater, Remote Presence, Courage, Mimetism (-3)**.

| Profile | Pts / SWC | Weapons | Fireteam |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Fugazi Dronbot** | **7 / 0** | **Flash Pulse**, PARA CCW (-3) | **None** |

The **Kestrel AVA is 1**, not the higher availability that might exist in other PanO rosters. The Fugazi is neither a Specialist nor a significant direct-damage attacker; its primary contribution is a protected, transferable Regular Order.

#### Why it is a near-default cheerleader

- **7 points buys one pooled Regular Order** in its own Combat Group, so the value does not depend on spending that Order on Fugazi.
- **Flash Pulse ARO** is an additional nonlethal threat at useful distances, especially against an attacker that would otherwise face only one dangerous ARO.
- **Mimetism (-3)** makes a conventional BS Attack against it less reliable unless the attacker has appropriate counters. This helps it survive, but ARM0 and a single Wound make it fragile.
- **Repeater** contributes to the entire army's hacking network even while Fugazi is parked defensively.
- **6-6" mobility** lets it change Repeater coverage, reposition after a flank opens, or make a last-turn sacrifice without buying a dedicated mobile hacking piece.

**Do not confuse cost-efficiency with compulsory inclusion.** It still consumes a Combat Group slot and needs a reasonably safe S3 deployment space. Taking its Order only matters when the relevant group can use another Order productively.

#### Flash Pulse: legitimate secondary deployment-zone defence

Flash Pulse uses **WIP13** rather than Fugazi's **BS8**. Its **B1** is not impressive in an active-turn duel, but it makes a cheap reactive check against attackers.

| Range | Flash Pulse MOD | Fugazi target number before other MODs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0–8" | 0 | 13 |
| 8–16" | +3 | 16 |
| 16–24" | +3 | 16 |
| 24–32" | -3 | 10 |

On a successful Flash Pulse hit followed by a failed **BTS** Saving Roll, the target enters **Stunned State**. Stunned prevents declarations of Attacks and imposes **-3 on other Rolls** (except Saving Rolls), potentially disrupting an expensive attack run. **It does not retroactively cancel the Order that caused it.** The state also **automatically cancels at the start of the States Phase of the Player Turn in which it was caused**; Doctor/Engineer can remove it earlier under the appropriate rules. Do **not** describe it as a permanent disable.

**Best deployment:** protect the Order first, then choose a partially concealed lane covering a side approach, DZ entrance, or path to a valuable ARO/command piece. A Fugazi watching a different angle from a Sierra, linked sniper or other meaningful ARO is more useful than exposing it to the same long HMG lane. It can force a hostile attacker to consider a BTS roll in addition to the primary firefight.

**Weaknesses:** one B1 Flash Pulse ARO generally loses many direct fights against effective multi-Burst active guns; it does not protect blind corners without LoF and is no substitute for a template guard, mine, or serious long-range ARO. Avoid handing the opponent a free 7-point kill and shrinking your future Order pool merely for a speculative long-distance Flash Pulse.

#### Repeater: good passive network, conditional active delivery

**The Repeater extends the Hacking Area of every friendly Hacker in the same Army List, across Combat Groups.** It is automatic, active without an Order, and cannot be voluntarily switched off; it stops functioning while the carrier is **Isolated** or in a **Null State**. Its 8" Zone of Control can threaten Hackable HI/TAG/REM/VH targets through appropriate friendly hacking programs, or support **Spotlight** against other eligible targets when the hacker has the program.

##### Three practical uses

| Use | Assessment | Practical plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Passive DZ or approach screen** | **Strong** | Leave Fugazi protected while its 8" Repeater zone makes a nearby route less attractive to enemy HI/TAGs and Hackers. Requires friendly hackers/programs capable of exploiting the situation. |
| **Opportunistic forward network** | **Good, conditional** | Move it through a covered route when 1–2 Orders can place a valuable target inside the Repeater's ZoC, then attack with the relevant Hacker. |
| **Dedicated suicide Repeater run** | **Usually poor** | Spending 2–3 movement Orders plus follow-up hacking Orders just to enable a middling hacker is often inferior to a better attack, objective action, or native forward network source. |

**6-6" is not Forward Deployment.** Fugazi starts in the DZ, so an enemy midfield or backline target may be far away. The REM is S3 and Hackable; navigating exposed spaces or enemy hacking coverage is a real cost. Use its movement as an *option*, not the reason the 7-point unit was bought.

**Enemy counter-use:** Enemy Hackers within the ZoC of **your Repeater** can use it to target **your Hackers**, with **Firewall (-3)** applying to those attacks and the defender gaining the associated **+3 Saving Roll** benefit. The Fugazi itself is Hackable as a REM. Forward placement is therefore not automatically beneficial against an opponent with superior hacking; do not donate a network entry point without assessing both sides.

**Kestrel context:** The sectorial has useful hacking tools but should not assume a Nomads-like premium offensive hacking network. Fugazi's strongest hacking contribution is frequently **deterrence or exploiting a situation that already exists**, rather than building a game plan around reaching the enemy DZ.

#### Combat Group choice: Group 1 versus Group 2

**Default: Group 1 when it has space and another Order helps your main attackers.** Fugazi provides a cheap transferable Order to that group, allowing the Griffin, TAG, attack Fireteam or scoring specialist to spend it. It need not activate itself.

**Group 2 is also an excellent home when its Orders have a clear use.** The important cross-group interaction is:

1. **Group 2 Fugazi** spends **Group 2 Orders** to move into a protected Repeater position (including the one Regular Order it contributes itself).
2. A **Group 1 Hacker** uses **Group 1 Orders** to hack an enemy in the Fugazi's Repeater zone.
3. The network works across groups because the Repeater extends the Hacking Area of friendly Hackers on the *army list*, not just those sharing its group.

**This does not transfer Orders between groups.** Fugazi's Regular Order stays in Group 2, and a Group 2 Order cannot be spent directly activating a Group 1 Hacker. If Group 2 has only one or two Orders, a long forward delivery still may not be feasible; consider it a positioning or backfield-guard group rather than a dedicated push.

| List situation | Preferred placement |
|---|---|
| Group 1 has nine troops and a central attacker needs fuel | **Group 1** |
| Group 1 is full at ten; Group 2 has a useful task or support units | **Group 2** |
| Group 2 has 3–5 usable Orders but few worthwhile activations | **Group 2**; Fugazi gives a low-cost mobility/network task |
| Group 2 consists only of inert Order filler | **Reassess the group**, not just Fugazi's placement |
| Main hacker is in the other group | **Still legal** to use Fugazi's Repeater cross-group |
| No friendly hacker | Repeater is not a reason to activate; take primarily for Order + Flash ARO |

#### When to leave Fugazi out

The strongest arguments against taking it are **opportunity cost and list structure**, not a problem with the 7-point profile:

1. **A legal Fireteam needs another member.** Fugazi **cannot join Kestrel Fireteams**. A 10-point Fennec may be the correct way to meet a formation requirement or improve its level.
2. **The next Order has little marginal value.** If Fugazi would be merely an idle extra body in a group with no meaningful Order consumer, spending 7 points toward a specialist, weapon or defence upgrade may win more games.
3. **A TAG list needs a Tech-Bee first.** The **9-point Tech-Bee** offers a pooled Regular Order, Specialist Operative, Flash Pulse, Deployable Repeater and useful **TAGCom** when an eligible TAG is in the *same Combat Group*. Prioritize that specific synergy if points are tight; ideally take both.
4. **Baggage or reloads are already part of the plan.** A **Mulebot Baggage + Repeater (8 points)** may deliver more list-specific utility for one extra point. It is slower, larger and has no Flash Pulse.
5. **A crowded TAG/REM deployment zone cannot protect another S3 base.** Unlike a small S2 Fusilier, Fugazi is less convenient behind certain cover/gaps. A cheap Order that gets killed immediately may be false economy.
6. **The list requires stronger direct DZ defence.** A Fennec Minelayer/Thunderbolt, template trooper or dedicated ARO piece covers threats that Flash Pulse alone cannot.

##### Comparison with neighboring choices

| Kestrel unit | Pts / SWC | Why choose it instead of Fugazi? | Why still take Fugazi? |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Fugazi** | **7 / 0** | — | Cheapest AVA1 Regular Order with Flash Pulse, Mimetism and always-on Repeater |
| **Mulebot Baggage** | 8 / 0 | Baggage/reload value and added durability | Faster, Flash Pulse, Mimetism; different support role |
| **Tech-Bee TAGCom** | 9 / 0 | Specialist, TAGCom, GizmoKit, Deployable Repeater | 2 points cheaper, naturally active Repeater, much faster |
| **Basic Fennec** | 10 / 0 | S2 Fireteam slot and rifle, LT lookalike potential | 3 points cheaper and provides network/Flash |
| **Pathfinder Tactical Awareness** | 17 / 0 | Specialist FO, Sensor, Combi, Repeater plus personal Tactical activation; can be Wildcard | Ten points cheaper; much better pure passive Order return |
| **Peacemaker + Auxbot** | 23 / 0 | Forward Deployment, immediate Repeater footprint, defensive/attack templates | Sixteen points cheaper; less commitment |

The **17-point Tactical Awareness Pathfinder** is the superior *active* repeater/specialist package when the extra Order will be used; it is not a direct substitute for the 7-point Order filler. The **Tech-Bee** is usually a higher-priority marginal purchase alongside a same-group TAG, but Fugazi is still a strong secondary cheap contributor.

#### Overall assessment

| Role | Rating | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Pure cheerleader / Order efficiency** | **S+** | 7 points for one Regular Order, plus passive utility |
| **Deployment-zone Flash Pulse defence** | **A-** | Useful WIP16 bands with Mimetism, but B1 and ARM0 |
| **Defensive Repeater coverage** | **A** | Useful with an actual hacker and a relevant approach lane |
| **Offensive Repeater delivery** | **B / situational** | Good opportunistically; no forward deployment and fragile |
| **Group 2 utility** | **A when Orders are usable** | Can reposition with Group 2 while Group 1 Hacker benefits |
| **Dedicated combat unit** | **Poor** | BS8, no lethal ranged weapon |

**Practical recommendation:** Include the **AVA1 Fugazi in most conventional Kestrel lists**, especially if the primary group can use another Regular Order. Deploy it safely, give it a realistic Flash Pulse lane and let the Repeater passively influence approaches. **Do not routinely burn multiple Orders on an offensive Repeater push.** The standout case for **Group 2** is that its own Orders can move the Fugazi while an appropriate **Group 1 Hacker** exploits the coverage using Group 1 Orders.
