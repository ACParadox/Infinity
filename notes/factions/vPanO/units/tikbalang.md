### Tikbalang — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: Strong primary TAG / go-to mobile gunfighter.** The Tikbalang combines **BS15, Mimetism (-3), Climbing Plus, Tactical Awareness, Remote Presence, three Wounds, a Heavy Flamethrower and Shock Mines** at a competitive cost. It is exceptionally good at finding favorable firing angles and surviving counterattacks. Its central limitation is the **ordinary HMG: no AP, MULTI or other sustained anti-ARM ammunition**. Bring another answer to opposing TAGs and exceptionally tough heavy infantry.

**Preferred build for a Tikbalang + Pathfinder team:** **69-point standard Tikbalang + 17-point Tactical Awareness Pathfinder**, with a **19-point Magistrate Strategos L1 Lieutenant** elsewhere. The **73-point Tikbalang LT (+1 Order)** is a viable *solo* attack choice, especially with a protected **21-point Magistrate CoC**, but its personal LT Orders cannot activate an intact Duo.

#### Profile and selection

| Profile | Pts / SWC | Difference |
|---|---:|---|
| **Tikbalang** | **69 / 1.5** | Default; one Regular Order and its own Tactical Awareness Order |
| **Tikbalang Lieutenant (+1 Order)** | **73 / 1.5** | Same weapons/attributes; **two LT Special Orders** in addition to Tactical Awareness |

**Both profiles:** MOV 15-10 cm (6-4"), CC17, BS15, PH15, WIP13, ARM6, BTS6, W3, S6. HMG, Heavy Flamethrower, Shock Mine, AP CCW, Crabbot; BS Attack (SR-1), ECM: Guided (-6), Remote Presence, Courage, Tactical Awareness, Terrain (Total), Climbing Plus, GizmoKit (PH=13), Dodge (PH=11), Mimetism (-3).

##### What it excels at

- **Soft-to-medium target removal:** BS15 HMG and Mimetism (-3) make it extremely efficient against ordinary infantry and mid-ARM fighters, especially after gaining a flank.
- **Finding better lines:** Climbing Plus and 6-4" MOV open angles inaccessible to most TAGs.
- **Order economy:** Tactical Awareness supplies a meaningful personal activation every eligible active turn.
- **Independent defense:** Remote Presence, ARM6/BTS6/three Wounds, HFT and Shock Mines discourage attempts to trade into it or reach its backfield.
- **Mine forks and protection:** A previously laid mine threatens an enemy while the Tikbalang fires its HMG; the mine also covers approaches against skirmishers and close assault.

##### Biggest weakness: HMG without AP

The plain HMG does **not halve ARM**. BS15 + Mimetism (-3) is excellent for winning Face-to-Face Rolls, but winning does not guarantee that damage gets through ARM6–9, particularly in cover. Against very heavy targets, its efficiency drops sharply.

The **66-point Squalos Mk-II MULTI HMG NCO** is better for sustained ranged anti-armor shooting thanks to AP mode. The Tikbalang instead offers Mimetism, Climbing Plus, Heavy Flamethrower and Shock Mines. The **62-point Squalos HMG + GL** is a cheaper ordinary-HMG alternative with speculative fire; the **55-point Squalos MULTI MMR** is a budget midrange TAG, not a direct long-range replacement.

**Build an anti-ARM complement:** Black A.I.R. K1 FO, infiltrating Jackal E/M GL, appropriate Panzerfausts or another reliable AP/E/M piece. Do not spend the Tikbalang's premium attack Orders repeatedly shooting at ARM-heavy pieces in cover when a specialized answer is available. The Tikbalang's **AP CCW** is not a reliable substitute for a high-quality AP ranged gun because its CC performance is not specialist-grade.

#### LT upgrade versus Magistrate Strategos

The genuine question is **not whether the army will spend Orders on its TAG**. It is whether **one additional *restricted* personal LT Order** offsets **six extra points across the Lieutenant + Magistrate package, loss of Strategos' reserve-deployment advantage, and reduced compatibility with an intact Pathfinder Duo**.

| | Standard Tik + Magistrate Strategos LT | Tik LT + Magistrate CoC |
|---|---|---|
| Combined points | **69 + 19 = 88** | **73 + 21 = 94** |
| LT Orders generated | One | Two |
| Flexible pool Order | Strategos can convert its LT Order into a Regular Order in the Magistrate's Combat Group | Neither LT Order converts to an ordinary Regular Order through Strategos |
| Personal LT spending | The TAG cannot use a personal LT Order; can receive converted Regular Orders | Tik can spend both on itself or eligible NCO use |
| Reserve deployment | **Two reserve troopers** through Strategos | Normal reserve deployment |
| LT safety | Magistrate can remain safe | TAG LT must accept attacker exposure |
| Command continuity | LT is in backfield | **CoC Magistrate** provides a successor |
| MAG FT Master (Jackals) | Available | Available |
| Tik + Pathfinder Duo | **Strong synergy** | Personal LT Orders break Fireteam cohesion |

The **net six-point premium** does buy another potential TAG activation per turn and a CoC backup, not just four points of LT upgrade. However, **Strategos flexibility and reserve deployment** are real benefits that the LT-Tik list loses.

**Preferred choice:**
- **Standard 69 + Magistrate Strategos 19** when using a **Pathfinder Duo**, multiple attack pieces, or seeking flexible and safe command.
- **Tikbalang LT 73 + Magistrate CoC 21** when using the Tik largely **independently**, exploiting its vertical routes, and valuing an additional self-funded TAG activation more than deployment flexibility. This is a real alternate build, not automatically superior.

##### Crucial Duo Order rule

With a legal **Tikbalang + 17-point Tactical Awareness Pathfinder ARMORED Duo**:
- **Tik Tactical Awareness Order:** the **Tikbalang leads**, and both team members can activate together.
- **Pathfinder Tactical Awareness Order:** the **Pathfinder leads**; both can reposition, but the Tikbalang does *not* lead an HMG attack using Pathfinder's Tactical Order.
- **Regular Order:** either member can lead, as declared.
- **Tik LT Special Order:** **the Tikbalang leaves the Fireteam** to spend it; it is **not** a normal Duo activation. NCO and Tactical Awareness are distinct exceptions using Tactical Orders.

A Tikbalang LT is therefore more self-sufficient **after breaking the team**, but the standard profile is easier to use repeatedly in a **preserved** Duo. The TAG can also elect to separate from Pathfinder once Climbing Plus makes independent movement more attractive.

#### Why Climbing Plus is a major strength

The Tikbalang can **Move on vertical surfaces and attack while climbing**, rather than treating a wall exclusively as a climb obstacle. This grants it *new approaches*, not just the ability to reach a rooftop.

1. **Flank around buildings:** Attack units from side/back angles that avoid their preferred firing lanes.
2. **Bypass street chokepoints:** Route around mines, warbands and crowded ARO corridors via a vertical path, provided a legal route exists.
3. **Pressure rooftop AROs:** Reach higher or lateral angles against snipers/missiles without repeating the same unfavorable frontal engagement.
4. **Escape melee specialists:** The TAG is not a dedicated CC fighter; climbing away may be considerably safer than fighting a martial-arts specialist.
5. **Create new HMG firing lanes:** Rather than solve an ARM-heavy target by frontal volume of fire, reposition to remove its cover or isolate a softer target.

**Mimetism (-3) + Climbing Plus + Tactical Awareness** is the key combination: reach a better line, make enemy return fire less effective, and pay for some repositioning with the restricted personal activation.

**Restrictions:** The Tikbalang does not normally obtain Partial Cover simply for hanging on an exposed vertical wall, and its **S6 base** still needs legal placement/supported movement surfaces. Roofs with cover are often safer endpoints than an open wall. These routes can also separate the TAG from slower support models.

#### Shock Mines: why they matter even with a Heavy Flamethrower

| | Shock Mine | Heavy Flamethrower |
|---|---|---|
| Role | **Persistent area denial and fork** | **Immediate direct-template attack** |
| Timing | Deploy now, potentially trigger in a *later* Order | Resolve in the current attack Order |
| Threat | Shock small template | Continuous Damage large template |
| Can remain after Tik leaves | **Yes** | No |
| Can threaten while Tik attacks with HMG | **Yes, if already placed and triggered** | No; the Tik is choosing its attack weapon |

##### Mine + HMG fork

1. Place a Shock Mine threatening a corridor, corner or target's likely route.
2. On a **later** Order, move the Tikbalang into a firing angle on that target.
3. The enemy may be forced to decide between shooting back against the HMG and Dodging both the HMG and an independently triggering mine.
4. The mine's hit is **not cancelled merely because the enemy wins a Face-to-Face Roll against the Tikbalang's HMG**. Dodging an activated mine suffers the relevant mine Dodge modifier.

**Important:** A mine **cannot activate in the same Order it is deployed**; lay it before trying to create the attack fork.

##### Defensive uses

- Cover the corner behind the TAG before ending an attack run.
- Block the shortest approach to a rooftop, flank or likely TAG hiding spot.
- Drop a mine in ARO to complicate a warband/skirmisher's *next* activation.
- Place it to protect a retreat route or to deter an enemy from moving close enough for melee.

The mine differs from the flamethrower because **the mine remains dangerous when the TAG is elsewhere**, and because a previously placed mine can overlap with **the TAG's HMG attack**. The flamethrower is better for the immediate close-range template threat but cannot independently create that same gun-and-mine fork.

#### Duo partner choices — trimmed shortlist

All listed **Tikbalang + different-unit** ARMORED Duos are **FT1**: they do **not** gain FT2 +1SD. The advantage is coordinated activation and partners' own tools, not an upgraded HMG.

| Duo | Combined pts / SWC | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| **Tik 69 + Pathfinder TacAware 17** | **86 / 1.5** | **Preferred mobile utility Duo**, as long as their routes coincide |
| **Tik LT 73 + Pathfinder TacAware 17** | **90 / 1.5** | Good unit pair, but personal LT Orders dissolve the Duo |
| **Tik 69 + Firefly Engineer 23** | **92 / 1.5** | Conditional ground-route repair/NCO Duo; movement mismatch |
| **Tik 69 + Bulleteer Heavy Shotgun (+1B) 19** | **88 / 1.5** | Niche close-range AP complement; overlaps the Tik's HFT/mine defense |

**Excluded as low-value constructions:** Tik + Firefly **CoC** Duo; Tik + Bulleteer **Spitfire** Duo; double Tikbalang Duo (huge cost, duplicated plain HMGs). Do not recommend them as default partnerships.

##### Pathfinder Tactical Awareness (17) — best conditional utility

Pathfinder provides **FO, Sensor, Repeater, Flash Pulse and its own Tactical Awareness Order** at matching 6-4" MOV. It can travel with the TAG, help detect camo, score objectives and support a friendly hacking network. Its extra Tactical Order can advance the Duo **with Pathfinder leading**, even if the Tik does not shoot with that activation.

**Do not force it to follow the TAG up walls.** Once the Tikbalang starts exploiting vertical movement, keeping the Duo together may cost more than it saves. Pathfinder is fully useful **independently**: its own Tactical Awareness and specialist abilities do not require the TAG.

##### Firefly Engineer (23) — can it keep up?

Usually **not at the Tikbalang's full pace**:

| | Tikbalang | Firefly Engineer |
|---|---|---|
| MOV | **6-4"** | **4-4"** |
| Climbing Plus | **Yes** | **No** |
| Battlefield job | Shoot, flank, climb | Repair/status recovery, NCO/Panzerfaust, technical support |

On mostly **ground-level movement**, an Engineer Duo may work. Firefly can help recover the TAG and **NCO** can use available LT Orders while leading a Duo as Tactical Orders. But on a truly vertical attack route, Firefly may have to take a detour, fall out of Coherency, or be left behind.

**Preferred repair alternative when exploiting Climbing Plus:** **Machinist 15 + Palbot 3 = 18 points**, with the Palbot positioned to access the expected engagement zone. A Palbot still needs a physically legal approach; it cannot teleport to a wall-hanging TAG.

##### Bulleteer Heavy Shotgun (19) — does it add enough?

Unlike the HFT, the Bulleteer's **Heavy Shotgun (+1B)** can make a **Face-to-Face** close-range shooting attack, with **AP** ammunition and B3 in the active turn. Mimetism (-6) and 6-4" MOV make it a serious close attacker, and its Repeater may be useful with a friendly Hacker.

However, the Tikbalang **already has Heavy Flamethrower and Shock Mines** to deter close assault. The Bulleteer brings **neither Engineer nor Tactical Awareness**, so the synergy is modest. Take it **as a worthwhile independent AP shotgun attacker that can sometimes accompany the TAG**, not as mandatory TAG protection.

#### Support, hacking, E/M and recovery

##### Tech-Bee (9/0) — high-priority support

Tech-Bee's **TAGCom [Dodge (PH+3), GizmoKit (PH+1)]** assists TAGs **in her own Combat Group while her skill remains active**. For a Tikbalang, that improves its Dodge target from **11 to 14** and its printed GizmoKit roll from **13 to 14**. The Dodge boost is the real draw; the GizmoKit boost is incremental.

She also supplies a Regular Order, Specialist Operative, Deployable Repeater, Flash Pulse/Adhesive Launcher Rifle and her own GizmoKit. **She is not an Engineer** and cannot simply replace one for clearing Isolated/IMM-B.

##### EVO Mulebot (14/0.5) + Fairy Dust — optional

**Fairy Dust** gives selected TAGs Firewall protection against **Comms Attacks**, imposing the Firewall MOD on enemy hackers and improving Saving Rolls. It is attractive when facing significant hacking and especially in multi-TAG lists, but costs **14 points, 0.5 SWC and an activation Order**. It does not protect against ordinary **E/M ammunition**, and it does not automatically operate before an opponent's first turn.

**Priority:** Engineer + sensible anti-Repeater play first; Tech-Bee next; add EVO when the particular list and meta justify it. An actual friendly Hacker is also needed before the Pathfinder Repeater constitutes meaningful cyber defense.

##### How to avoid losing a TAG to E/M or hacking

- Treat **enemy Repeater coverage** as a route-planning constraint. Clear Repeaters or their supporting hackers before advancing through their influence.
- **ECM: Guided (-6) is not general anti-hacking defense**; Mimetism likewise does not protect against Comms Attacks.
- The Tik has **BTS6**, but even one successful Oblivion/Carbonite/Total Control attack can cause major disruption.
- Against ordinary **E/M**, BTS is halved and multiple Saving Rolls can apply; E/M can inflict **Isolated and IMM-B**. Fairy Dust does not stop this.
- Keep an **Engineer/Palbot recovery route** available. The Tikbalang's Remote Presence makes Engineering recovery valuable, but support cannot help if it cannot physically reach the disabled TAG.
- Use Climbing Plus, HFT and Shock Mines to deny the approach lanes of close E/M carriers and melee specialists.

##### If using Tik Lieutenant: CoC choice

**Default: 21-point Magistrate CoC**, who stays relatively safe while Tik attacks. It keeps **FT Master (Jackals), Counterintelligence and Specialist Operative** and is only 2 points more than the Strategos LT version of the Magistrate.

**23-point Firefly CoC** brings NCO, Albedo, Panzerfaust and combat utility but **is not an Engineer**. Pairing both the LT and its successor on the same exposed attack route is a poor way to protect command continuity.

**No CoC** saves considerable points and is possible in a deliberate high-risk attack list, but an exposed TAG LT is a very obvious target for anti-TAG weapons, hackers and E/M. The default should be **CoC** if the Tik is LT.

#### Recommended starting package

**Default for a mobile utility setup:** Tikbalang **69** + Pathfinder TacAware **17** + Magistrate Strategos LT **19** = **105 / 1.5 SWC** before support. Add **Tech-Bee 9** (in the TAG's Combat Group) and preferably a **Machinist 15 + Palbot 3** for independent repair access.

The Duo is a **tool, not a commitment**: use shared tactical movement when their routes coincide; split when the Tikbalang needs to exploit Climbing Plus or Pathfinder needs to score separately.

**Alternative solo LT build:** Tikbalang LT **73** + Magistrate CoC **21** = **94 / 1.5 SWC**, before support. The extra restricted LT Order is attractive when the Tik fights independently; the cost is flexibility and second reserve deployment. Do not justify the LT profile merely by saying a TAG spends most of the list's Orders—**both TAG profiles do that**.

**Final assessment:** The Tikbalang's standouts are **6-4" Climbing Plus + Mimetism (-3) + Tactical Awareness + Remote Presence**, with **Shock Mines** providing unusually good independent anti-assault and fork play for a TAG. Its decisive list-building drawback is **the ordinary, non-AP HMG**. Choose it to attack where the enemy is weakest, and bring a separate answer for very heavy armor.
