### Palbot — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: Strong / Go-to support purchase with an independent Engineer; situational as a second Servant.** A **3-point Palbot** primarily gives a Doctor or Engineer a fast, remote recovery route. It also serves as a disposable mine-clearer, a cheap close-combat nuisance, a **+1 Burst** contributor to allied CC, and a WIP 13 Discover scout. These secondary uses make the first Palbot easy to justify, but usually do **not** justify buying a second solely as an expendable attacker. A Palbot generates **no independent Order**.

#### Core mechanics and restrictions

- **Controller required:** Every Palbot is assigned to a Doctor or Engineer when building the list. It cannot be freely reassigned mid-game or shared between Controllers. A Controller can have at most **two Servants**, but activates **only one at a time**.
- **Order use:** Spending the Controller's Order activates the Controller and its selected Palbot; **both declare the same Short Skills or Long Skill**, though they need not choose the same target. If either cannot perform a declared Skill, that element Idles. The Controller can declare Move without leaving a safe position while the Palbot advances.
- **Remote skill:** A Palbot in Silhouette contact with a valid allied casualty lets its Controller use **Doctor or Engineer** remotely; the **Controller** performs the WIP roll. One Doctor/Engineer target per Order; the Palbot has neither skill independently.
- **Unlimited range:** Servants have **no operating-distance or Coherency limit** from their Controller. A backfield Engineer can operate a Palbot on the other side of the table.
- **Reactive Turn:** Controller and Servant can receive separate AROs, but generally must declare **the same type of ARO** where each has an ARO. They cannot simply choose entirely different reactions.
- **No Fireteams or Coordinated Orders:** A Controller **with a Peripheral** and its Palbots cannot join a Fireteam or participate in a Coordinated Order. This is a major constraint for **Firefly Engineer** or **Bipandra**, whose value often depends on linked play.
- **If Controller is disabled:** The Palbot enters **Disconnected** at the end of the Order if the Controller enters a Null or Isolated State. Losing the Palbot does **not** remove the surviving Controller's Doctor/Engineer skill; the Controller can still act in person, but has lost that remote route.
- **Not an independent specialist or Order:** Do not count it as an extra Specialist Trooper, console operator, or cheerleader. Its listed point cost **does** count separately for zone control and victory-point accounting, subject to mission rules.

**Order-positioning caution:** Skills are shared. Do not assume the Controller may independently Dodge or remain Prone while the Palbot declares a completely different movement Skill. For example, a Palbot's Jump can require its Controller to declare Jump too; a hiding Controller may have to stand up. N5.3 FAQ 0.1 clarifies that a Peripheral may still perform a Skill if the Controller cannot and must Idle.

#### 1. Clearing enemy mines with a 3-point model — strong tactical use

**The Palbot can intentionally trigger an enemy Mine**, removing a template threat from a route needed by a more expensive model. Mines trigger when an enemy Model or Marker declares or executes a Skill in their Trigger Area, assuming the mine's template is legal. Once triggered, the Mine is removed.

**Typical sequence:** Keep the Machinist/Trauma-Doc protected; activate its Palbot; advance the Palbot with Move into the Mine's Trigger Area; resolve the template and any Saving Roll; then route the attacking TAG or infantry through the cleared area on a later Order.

Advantages:
- Trades a potential **3-point casualty** for reducing risk to a **Tikbalang, Squalos, Griffin, Crux Knight** or other valuable model.
- **MOV 15-10** makes the expendable scout much faster than its Doctor/Engineer Controller.
- Can clear a mine while the Controller stays out of sight, rather than risking a full-size combat specialist.

Limitations:
- The template can catch **other friendly models**: avoid crowding the Palbot with the unit being protected.
- A Mine **does not trigger** if its template would hit one of the Mine owner's allies, even an Unconscious one.
- **Dodge movement does not trigger deployables**; use an activation that actually satisfies the trigger conditions.
- A Camouflage Marker might be a real trooper rather than a Mine. Do not assume every Marker can be safely removed by walking at it.
- Most importantly, sacrificing your **only Palbot** also removes your convenient remote recovery route for the rest of the battle. Do this when the cleared lane matters more than future repairs.

**Priority:** High as a situational side benefit; do not run the Palbot into a Mine by default just because the exchange is cheap.

#### 2. Running into CC: tie up or immobilize an enemy

A Palbot can Move into Silhouette contact with an enemy and enter **Engaged State**, potentially forcing that unit to deal with an unexpected melee problem.

- While Engaged, ordinary ranged attacks at targets elsewhere are not available. The enemy can generally **CC Attack**, **Dodge to disengage**, or use another legal in-CC option; **they do not have to kill the Palbot**.
- It carries a **PARA CC Weapon (-3)**. A successful PARA hit forces a **PH-6 Saving Roll**; failure causes **Immobilized-A**, not damage or an outright kill. The weapon's (-3) applies to the relevant opposed CC interaction.
- CC **11** is unimpressive. Against a genuine melee specialist, a 3-point Palbot is likely to die quickly. The opportunity is primarily against an isolated ranged trooper that must spend resources escaping or dealing with it.
- **Mimetism (-3)** helps against BS Attacks targeting the Palbot but does not make CC 11 into a reliable attack stat. Exposing the bot to an enemy ARO while crossing open ground can end the attempt before contact.

**Use case:** A Palbot already near a weak-CC sniper, fire support REM, or other ranged defender can force an awkward decision or waste an Order. This is opportunistic disruption, **not** a dependable assassin plan.

##### Bonus: +1 Burst for a better allied CC attacker

**Yes, the +1B interaction is real, and the official N5.3 Close Combat examples explicitly involve a Doctor and Peripheral (Servant).**

Under **Close Combat with Multiple Troopers**, the active player selects **one** activated model to make the CC Attack. That attacker receives **+1B per eligible allied Trooper or Peripheral** already in Silhouette contact with the target, provided the supporting model is not Null or Immobilized.

For example:
1. A Palbot survives in Silhouette contact with a target.
2. A **separately activated allied melee attacker** gets into contact or is already there.
3. When that ally makes its CC Attack, the Palbot can contribute **+1B** even though the Palbot is not the one rolling the attack.
4. The Palbot's **PARA weapon and its -3 modifier do not transfer** to the ally's weapon. The bonus is to Burst, not the ally's CC value or weapon effects.

The same overall multiple-combatants framework applies in ARO, with its own participation restrictions. This can be meaningful beside a good CC unit, but planning a long Palbot excursion purely to provide +1B is usually inefficient.

#### 3. Discover and scouting — legitimate extra value

At **WIP 13**, the Palbot can make reasonable **Discover** attempts against suspicious enemy Camouflage Markers.

- An inexpensive, fast model can expose itself to investigate a mine-or-trooper Marker while keeping its Controller safe.
- The Controller and the Palbot still follow the **same declared-Skill** requirement. If the Controller cannot legally Discover that target, it Idles while the Palbot may resolve its own attempt.
- A successful Discover can reveal a dangerous model for subsequent attacks and reduce uncertainty before committing a valuable gunfighter.
- A failed Discover or unexpected ARO may cost the Palbot; assess that cost as both **3 points** and **the lost recovery route**.

**Useful, not a dedicated Sensor replacement.** In Kestrel a Pathfinder has more specialist scouting functionality; a Palbot's Discover is a bonus attached to a recovery purchase.

#### 4. ARO bait, screening, CC obstruction and zone points

- **ARO tax / probing:** Move a low-cost Servant to draw reactions or see which enemies contest an approach. It can force an opponent to expend attention but is not a magical free ARO detector; the Controller also activates and can be exposed if carelessly positioned.
- **Physical blocking:** Its model can complicate a narrow approach and create an incidental CC threat. It is a **S1 base**, so this is modest rather than a major wall.
- **Contest a zone:** A Palbot's **own 3 points** count toward applicable zone-control objectives because Peripherals with their own listed cost contribute to zone and victory-point totals. Do not assume the same thing makes it a Specialist Trooper.
- **Protect a TAG against possession (niche):** Keeping a Palbot in Silhouette contact with a friendly TAG can create an immediate Engaged situation if that TAG becomes **Possessed** and therefore enemy-controlled. The opponent may have to resolve the engagement before making an ordinary attack run. This is most practical for an unusually stationary TAG, not a fast Tikbalang advancing across the table. It does **not** prevent possession itself and should be treated as a situational rules interaction, not an anti-hacking plan.

#### 5. One Palbot versus two, and who should receive them?

**Default: one Palbot per independent Doctor/Engineer.** Buy the second when it creates another meaningful **recovery location** or when two distinct Controllers each need one. Do **not** routinely buy two simply to sacrifice one to mines.

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Solo **Tikbalang / Squalos / Maximus TAG** with no accompanying Engineer | **Machinist + 1 Palbot (18 pts)** is the strong default |
| TAG plus important **Sierra / other REMs** in a different part of the table | Consider **Machinist + 2 Palbots (21 pts)** if each covers real repair demand |
| Multiple expensive infantry ARO pieces (e.g. **Black A.I.R.**, Griffin, Hetkari) | **Trauma-Doc + 1 Palbot (17 pts)** for dispersed healing |
| Two widely separated casualty areas and an infantry-heavy list | A second Trauma-Doc Palbot can be useful, but niche |
| **Bipandra Tactical Awareness** in a FENNECS/KESTREL Fireteam | **Do not attach a Palbot** if keeping her linked is important; losing Fireteam membership defeats much of her value |
| **Firefly Engineer** riding in a legal ARMORED/KESTREL team | Prefer Firefly's direct, linked support; buying a Servant would prohibit its Fireteam membership |
| Only an expected *one-way* TAG attack, no practical repair route | The entire Engineer/Palbot insurance package can be skipped |
| Wanting a second bot mainly for mines/CC | Usually **skip** unless three spare points have little better use and the first bot's repair access must be preserved |

**Group 2 is a practical home for Machinist + Palbot.** The Controller supplies its own Regular Order, which can be spent on recovery or an occasional Palbot move without consuming the main attack group's budget. The Palbot itself is **not** a second Group 2 Order.

**Choose deployment locations by future position, not just starting position.** One Servant near the TAG's plausible end-of-turn retreat/repair route is more useful than two Servants left in irrelevant back corners.

#### Important limitations and common misconceptions

1. **Palbots cannot share Controllers or become a new Doctor's/Engineer's Servant if the assigned Controller dies.** The association is established in the army list.
2. **Two Servants do not produce two simultaneous Doctor/Engineer uses.** Activate one chosen Servant at a time; one recovery target per Order.
3. **A Palbot does not gain Doctor/Engineer or count as a specialist** just by carrying remote support capability.
4. **No independent activation:** it does not function like a Fugazi and cannot spend a Regular Order from the pool on itself.
5. **CC ties enemies up but does not force them to kill it.** Dodge/disengagement and legal allied intervention remain options.
6. **The +1B CC bonus is not a second Palbot CC attack.** One selected attacker makes the CC Attack with its modified Burst.
7. **Sacrificing it is not truly free.** Its loss may strand your expensive TAG or REM without access to its hidden Engineer.

#### Final recommendation

**Strong / Go-to with a solo TAG or independent support Doctor.** The **first 3-point Palbot** is excellent value because it makes recovery reachable while keeping the Controller hidden. Mine clearance, CC disruption, potential **+1B CC support**, WIP 13 Discover, possession obstruction and low-cost screening are genuine extra uses, not its main purchasing justification.

**Second Palbot: Niche / Conditional.** Spend the additional three points when it covers a separate high-value asset or another side of the table. If the only argument is “one sacrificial Palbot plus one real Palbot,” that is ordinarily weaker than improving the main list elsewhere.
