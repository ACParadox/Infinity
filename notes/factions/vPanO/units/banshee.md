### Banshees, Airborne Assault Unit — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: Strong / Recommended, not mandatory.** The **23/0 Killer Hacker (KHD)** is the default: a flexible airborne anti-Hacker specialist, close-range attacker and late-game scorer that can also deploy normally for immediate Order generation and network defence. The **19/0 Light Shotgun** is excellent cheap airborne attack value. The **25/0.5 HD** is mission- and matchup-dependent, and the **22/0 WildParrot** is a niche E/M disruption tool. The Banshee complements a primary gunfighter rather than replacing one.

#### Pinned profiles

Source: [Kestrel N5.3 roster](../../../../sectorials/panoceania/kestrel-colonial-force.md#banshees-airborne-assault-unit), Army revision `7.26246.158`; [Human Sphere profile](https://human-sphere.com/Banshee) also confirms Spec. Trained Troops classification.

**AVA 3; Regular MI, Spec. Trained Troops, Cube; no Fireteam access.** MOV 4–4" (10–10 cm), CC15, BS12, PH12, WIP13, ARM2, BTS3, VITA1, S2. Shared MSV1, Parachutist, Combat Jump and Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion).

| Opt | Points / SWC | Loadout | Verdict |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **19 / 0** | Light Shotgun, Pulzar (+1B), D-Charges, Pistol, CCW | **A:** cheap flank attacker / trade piece |
| 2 | **22 / 0** | SMG, Pulzar (+1B), D-Charges, WildParrot, Pistol, CCW | **B:** niche E/M disruption |
| 3 | **25 / 0.5** | SMG, Pulzar (+1B), D-Charges, Pistol, CCW; HD | **A−:** mission/anti-heavy hacking alternative |
| 4 | **23 / 0** | SMG, Pulzar (+1B), D-Charges, Pistol, CCW; KHD | **A+: default** |

All options have the B2 active-turn BTS-targeting large direct template (Pulzar +1B), MSV1 and D-Charges. Only the two Hacker profiles are general mission Specialists; the non-Hackers can still complete eligible Classifieds.

#### Why the chassis works

- **Alternative attack vector:** bypass the front-facing AROs that a Griffin, Hetkari or TAG would otherwise have to fight. Enter near exposed support, a vulnerable Hacker, an objective or the back of a committed attack piece.
- **MSV1:** eliminates ordinary Mimetism (-3) and reduces Mimetism (-6) to -3; useful against a skirmisher once it is a legal target. Does not bypass all marker-state restrictions.
- **Pulzar (+1B):** two large teardrop direct templates in active turn, PS7 versus BTS. Can force two saves on an enemy that chooses to shoot rather than Dodge, or hit clustered troops. One ARO template normally. A successful Dodge can avoid both; the Banshee is still vulnerable to return fire.
- **SMG:** B3, PS7 Normal ammunition, +3 at 0–8", 0 at 8–16", -3 at 16–24"; Suppressive Fire when worthwhile. Do not assume this is an AP SMG. The Banshee is a close-range attacker, not a long-range gunfighter.
- **Jet Propulsion:** improves vertical/terrain access after arrival, opening unusual firing angles and elevated objective routes.
- **D-Charges:** every profile qualifies for Sabotage and has a close-contact anti-materiel option.
- **Limits:** one Wound, no native Mimetism or marker state, no Fireteam bonuses, and mediocre ordinary shooting outside favorable range. ARM2/BTS3 does not make it durable. Choose a good engagement instead of contesting entrenched AROs.

#### KHD — 23/0, preferred

##### Airborne Hacker assassination

[Trinity](../../../../rules/3_Combat/Trinity.md) is B3 in the active turn, WIP13 +3 = **16** before other MODs, PS6 vs BTS, and targets enemy Hackers. Use the Banshee's unusual entry angle to engage the Hacker obstructing the main Kestrel attack. No LoF is required when the target is legally within Hacking Area.

- Prefer a landing outside all hostile LoF and Hacking Areas, then move into a favorable isolated duel; entry itself can provoke hacking AROs, and the arrival Order is a Long Skill, not a simultaneous attack.
- [Enemy Repeaters](../../../../rules/5c_Equipment/Repeater.md) within ZoC can give access to any enemy Hacker, but impose Firewall (-3), reducing the Banshee's ordinary Trinity target number to 13 before other MODs and granting the defender +3 to Saving Rolls. Check all legal enemy hacking AROs before committing: this is not a safe shortcut into a stacked network.
- Against a vulnerable enemy Hacker it may be more efficient to use the SMG or Pulzar from an unexpected physical angle instead of accepting Firewall or multiple hacking AROs.
- Removing a key Hacker can release a TAG, HI or REM attack route. Trinity cannot directly disable non-Hacker TAGs, HI or REMs.

##### Cybermask and scoring

[Cybermask](../../../../rules/3_Combat/Cybermask.md) is a Long Skill, no Roll, requiring the user to be outside the LoF of enemy Troopers and Markers. It generates an IMP-2 Marker until canceled, permitting protected repositioning, approaches to objectives and an improved end-of-turn defensive position. It is **not** native Camouflage, does not confer Surprise Attack, and is not a free action or a guaranteed safe movement route.

Typical sequence: arrive in Total Cover → Cybermask if justified → advance through favorable terrain → reveal to hack, attack or score → return to Total Cover / Cybermask again if enough Orders remain. It is often better to take an immediate good attack or score than to spend the extra Order on Cybermask unnecessarily.

##### Normal deployment is a real option

A KHD deployed normally in the DZ generates its pooled Regular Order from Turn 1, provides Trinity coverage through friendly Repeaters and retains its MSV1/Pulzar as close-range counterattack and DZ defence. This is valuable when protecting an expensive main-group TAG or HI plan against enemy Hackers. It can use Cybermask to advance later if needed.

It does **not** replace a conventional HD if the objective is to deter non-Hacker HI/TAG/REM movement with Carbonite or Oblivion, or to apply Spotlight. A KHD by itself only threatens enemy Hackers electronically.

**Preferred network pairing:** Fennec HD/FastPanda **16/0.5** + Banshee KHD **23/0** = **39/0.5**. The Fennec projects Repeaters and supplies general hacking; the Banshee supplies anti-Hacker pressure and an independent flanking specialist. A normally deployed Banshee can benefit from Peacemaker, Pathfinder, Fugazi or other friendly Repeaters immediately.

#### Other profiles

##### Light Shotgun — 19/0

Excellent budget choice if the list already has enough specialists and hackers, and wants an independent airborne attacker instead.

- B2 direct-fire shotgun, +6 at 0–8" (BS18 before other MODs), 0 at 8–16", -3 at 16–24". MSV1 is particularly useful against exposed Mimetism skirmishers.
- The same B2 Pulzar and D-Charges make it a credible short-range trade piece against support bodies or clustered targets.
- Good second Banshee, or first Banshee when saving four points matters more than the KHD's mission and cyber options.
- No general Specialist status or Cybermask; fewer useful jobs if the attack lane is closed. Do not expect it to operate Specialist-only consoles.

##### WildParrot — 22/0

The [WildParrot](../../../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/WildParrot.md) buys a distinct one-use E/M attack vector without SWC. Place it legally within ZoC (not necessarily silhouette contact); it becomes active at the end of the placement Order and threatens subsequent actions, rather than instantly affecting the Order in which placed. It is static once placed, not a pursuing model.

- Enter a flank and position the WildParrot to threaten a TAG, HI, REM or VH's next movement, retreat or objective approach.
- E/M requires two Saving Rolls against BTS/2; a failed save can Isolate and additionally Immobilize-B eligible troop types. This can trade up significantly.
- Useful when terrain allows airborne E/M placement on a valuable approach that Jackal mines or E/M weapons cannot readily reach.
- Not a Minelayer and not a Specialist. Do not buy it just to put a cheap mine near the own DZ.
- Usually pay the extra **one point** for KHD flexibility unless WildParrot is part of the intended attack or denial plan.

##### Standard HD — 25/0.5

A different role, not simply a worse or higher-cost KHD. Spotlight, Carbonite, Oblivion and Total Control threaten a much broader range of targets. In exchange it loses Trinity and Cybermask, and costs 0.5 SWC.

- Best when the mission requires Spotlight Classifieds or when a surprise Hacking Area against a valuable TAG/HI/REM is more important than killing enemy Hackers.
- Can force Reset against an exposed heavy attacker, creating an opening for another piece's attack. With the target isolated or immobilized, exploit the result instead of unnecessarily trading the Banshee.
- **Documented example:** [battle-report analysis #158](../../../battle-reports/151-160-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md): an ordinary Banshee HD threatened Achilles, which dropped Suppressive Fire to respond; Redeye then killed Achilles over several Orders. A reported example of complementary attack vectors, not a guaranteed combo.
- **Firefly HD 24/0.5:** better for conventional on-table hacking defence, BTS6, NCO, Veteran Classified coverage and Fireteam delivery.
- **Fennec HD 16/0.5:** cheaper network support with FastPanda. Neither alternative replicates the Banshee's airborne placement.
- Choose the Banshee HD on the strength of its unique approach angle or scoring route, not simply to fill a generic Hacker slot.

#### Deployment and Order economy

[Combat Jump](../../../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Combat_Jump.md) and [Parachutist](../../../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Parachutist.md) are optional. Make the normal-deployment versus off-table decision based on mission, terrain, opponent and Order budget.

| Mode | Reliability / consequence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Normal DZ deployment | No entry Roll; contributes pooled Order immediately | Immediate cyber screen, defence, safe alternative on cramped tables |
| Parachutist | No entry Roll; legal table edge **outside enemy DZ** | Side objectives, safe flank entry; preferred when the route is good |
| Combat Jump | PH12 = 60% unsupported; friendly Controlled Jump raises to 75%, enemy Controlled Jump lowers to 45% when applicable individually | Legal horizontal landing deeper in enemy territory or near distant objectives when payoff warrants risk |

A failed Combat Jump goes to the **own DZ touching a table edge**, not scatter. Neither airborne entry grants Partial Cover on the arrival Order or permits deploying Prone, in contact with an enemy/neutral model or objective, or an illegal landing surface. Combat Jump cannot land inside buildings/enclosed scenery. Check enemy shooting and hacking AROs at the landing location before declaring entry.

An off-table Banshee contributes **no pooled Regular Order** during Order Count. Its own Order pays for entry; further actions that turn require Orders from its group. It adds its Regular Order to the pool on subsequent Order Counts if eligible. Holding it until Turn 3 has already sacrificed earlier pooled Orders, so the scoring payoff must justify the delay.

- **Group 1:** appropriate for a planned early raid or important Hacker-removal sequence, provided the remaining initial pool still supports the primary attacker.
- **Group 2:** good for a flexible specialist or secondary raid if enough Reg Orders remain after entry for movement, attacks and scoring. A group full of isolated Irregular Jackals cannot readily fund a multi-Order Banshee run without conversion or reassignment.
- Do not spend all available Orders opening a flank and then have none left to exploit it. Map landing, first attack, follow-up, last scoring action and protective exit.
- EVO is easier to justify if several jumpers or other concrete Supportware uses exist; a single opportunistic Banshee does not automatically justify an EVO merely to raise one jump by 15 percentage points.

Predator is a formal eligibility count, not a realistic plan for CC15. The plain KHD has **no Spotlight**. These numbers are theoretical and do not guarantee access, successful Rolls, required enemy targets or enough Orders.

The Banshee's key advantage is **execution**, not raw card count: it can reach a distant HVT, enemy-half scenery, enemy-DZ Mapping site, or enemy model without walking through the entire midfield. Keep it alive when later mission scoring matters more than immediate damage. It can even be worth using the non-Hacker 19pt option to complete Sabotage or Identity Check while reserving the Hacker to score elsewhere.

| Unit | Cost / SWC | Prefer it when |
|---|---:|---|
| Banshee KHD | 23 / 0 | Airborne flexibility, anti-Hacker raid, mobile specialist, two templates |
| Scarecrow KHD | 27 / 0 | Hidden Deployment, Infiltration, Mimetism (-6) and Surprise Attack for a prepositioned midfield cyber threat |
| Scarecrow FO | 25 / 0 | Hidden forward scoring, marker-state survival and FO rather than hacking |
| Black A.I.R. KHD | 31 / 0 | Guaranteed legal enemy-DZ edge access with Parachutist (Deployment Zone), Elite classification, MSV2 and Mimetism (-3); no Combat Jump or Banshee B2 Pulzar |
| Firefly HD | 24 / 0.5 | Better regular deployed hacking resilience, NCO, linked utility and broader Classified qualification |
| Fennec HD | 16 / 0.5 | Cheap deployed network activation and FastPanda projection |
| Pathfinder FO | 14 / 0 | Very cheap fast deployed scorer, Repeater, Sensor; no airborne insertion |

A Banshee KHD is **not** an automatic replacement for a Scarecrow KHD. A Scarecrow can already be in the right midfield spot, whereas the Banshee can choose a new entry point after the opponent has committed. An on-table Banshee gives reliable immediate pooled Orders; a Hidden Deployment Scarecrow does not while hidden.

#### When to take one, two, or none

**One is the normal recommendation** when there is a genuine job for it. Two can create asymmetric pressure, but should not both begin off-table if the first-turn pool cannot sustain the main attack. Three is an experimental skew rather than a default.

| Package | Total | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 KHD | 23 / 0 | Most versatile default |
| KHD + LSG | 42 / 0 | Two distinct cheap flank attackers; one cyber/scoring, one physical attack |
| HD + LSG | 44 / 0.5 | Anti-heavy hacking / Spotlight scoring plus cheap physical attack |
| KHD + HD | 48 / 0.5 | Deliberate hacking and mission package, preferably one starting on-table |

**Take when:** far-side scoring/classifieds, vulnerable opposing Hackers, flanking opportunities, a need for close-range templates, or a primary attacker that benefits from enemy cyber threats being removed. **Skip when:** specialists and attack vectors are already covered, legal entry geometry is poor, the roster cannot fund follow-up Orders, or an additional conventional Hacker/cheap defensive trooper is more valuable.

#### Community and battle-report evidence

The [Kestrel listbuilding guide](../../kestrel/kestrel-listbuilding.md) records positive assessments from Shepherd and Daboarder, with Daboarder fielding two Banshees, and a more skeptical 2026 podcast comparison with better-protected airborne attackers. The latter is a valid concern: Banshees lack Mimetism, and an isolated 1W trooper can disappear quickly.

The best justification is the **low price, B2 Pulzar, choice of safe entry modes, and role flexibility**. A KHD that spends the game passively hiding behind a DZ wall may not justify the premium over a cheaper support model, but normal deployment is a legitimate fallback or deliberate counter-hacking plan. Prefer one KHD by default; use the 19pt LSG as the cheaper attack-only alternative; pay for HD or WildParrot only for their distinct capabilities.
