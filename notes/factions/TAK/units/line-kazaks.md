# Line Kazaks — Tartary Army Corps (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: B overall — situational, but A-tier as a cheap native Fireteam anchor or Lieutenant shell.** Take **0–3** in ordinary TAK lists. The **10pt Forward Observer** is the default active-team anchor, the **9pt Lieutenant + 9pt Rifle** is a cheap command shell, and the **9pt Rifle** is useful if points or same-family composition matter. Do not buy ordinary Line Kazaks ahead of more useful cheap defensive troops merely to generate Orders. A full five-equivalence Core is a deliberate architecture, not a requirement of TAK.

## Lieutenant and decoy

**Line Kazak LT 9/0 + basic Rifle 9/0 = 18/0**, two Regular Orders plus one Lieutenant Order. Identical public loadouts conceal which model is the LT; this is not the Decoy Special Skill. Keep the two bodies in different safe locations where practical, and avoid revealing the actual LT through unnecessary use of the personal Order. The LT does not have to join a Fireteam.

| Command option | Pts / SWC | Why choose it |
|---|---:|---|
| Line Kazak LT alone | 9 / 0 | Minimal points, but identification risk if there is no matching Rifle profile |
| LT + Rifle lookalike | 18 / 0 | Cheap command concealment, two Regular Orders |
| Voronin LT | 22 / 0 | Counterintelligence, +1 Command Token, Sensor, WIP14 and counts-as Line Kazak; TAK does not inherit Vanilla Strategos |
| TankHunter LT Minelayer | 25 / 0 | Camo, mine package and useful independent weapons |
| Veteran Kazak LT FO | 36 / 0 | Durable WIP14 specialist and active combat piece |
| Veteran Kazak LT AP HMG | 45 / 1 | Main attacker and SWC saving over the non-LT AP HMG; exposure risk |

A cheap LT and lookalike cost four points less than Voronin, but consume two slots rather than one. Compare the saved points with the loss of command utility and the opportunity cost of the extra body. Do not automatically buy the 25pt TankHunter CoC solely to insure a hidden, inexpensive Kazak LT; the CoC is more attractive if its mine and anti-heavy role are independently useful. With an aggressive Veteran LT, insurance has a clearer purpose.

## Forward Observer — default native anchor

One point above the Rifle buys **WIP13 Forward Observer and Flash Pulse** while retaining a native Line Kazak composition contribution. That is an excellent incremental upgrade even though it is not a premium infiltrating specialist.

- Near-side objectives, late-game scoring, mission-dependent Classified coverage and a nonlethal ranged ARO.
- Forward Observer can establish Targeted for eligible Guided attacks; do not build a whole low-efficiency artillery plan solely around this modest WIP13 model.
- Flash Pulse is a useful defensive option, not a replacement for a mine or direct template at a close corner.
- A Scout or Strelok often reaches midfield objectives more efficiently thanks to their starting positions and markers. The Line Kazak can preserve those more valuable pieces by handling safer tasks.
- The upgrade is not necessary if the model spends the entire game concealed as a stationary Order generator. A FO with Flash Pulse is not the identical-loadout lookalike for a Rifle LT.

## Paramedic — compare the Kazak Doktor first

| Medical option | Pts / SWC | Composition | Assessment |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Line Kazak Paramedic | 11 / 0 | Native Line Kazak | Ranged MediKit, useful when maintaining distance matters |
| Kazak Doktor | 12 / 0 | Counts as Line Kazak | WIP13 Doctor plus MediKit; default medical anchor for just one point more |
| Konduktor FTO Paramedic | 10 / 0 | Does not count as Line Kazak | Cheapest Regular medical specialist, but WIP12 and composition trade-off |

A standard PH11 patient has a **55%** MediKit recovery roll after a successful hit or contact application; a WIP13 Doctor has a **65%** contact recovery roll. The Veteran Kazak has **MediKit (PH=15)**, giving a **75%** patient roll when eligible and Unconscious. This is not a way to heal an active NWI Veteran. A failed patient PH roll can kill the casualty. The Kazak Doktor also carries a MediKit, so the Paramedic's usual advantages are the one-point saving and native-unit identity, not exclusive ranged healing.

FT2 grants an extra Special Die to ranged MediKit attack rolls; FT4 adds +1 to the applicable attack Attribute. Neither increases the patient's PH recovery roll. A single successful B1 MediKit hit remains one patient roll, not two.

**For independent Order economy, buy Konduktors/Traktors first when their roles and deployment fit.** For a larger pure Core, Barsuk FTO is usually an excellent one-point upgrade over a redundant 9pt Rifle, except when a native Kazak or matching LT loadout is required. A Konduktor cannot substitute for the native minimum or preserve the same equivalence bonus.

The existing [cheerleaders ranking](../../../list-building/cheerleaders-ranking.md) places the Line Kazak Rifle at **#34**, while the 7pt Konduktor FTO is **#1**. This illustrates the distinction between independent value and sectorial structural value; it does not mean the native Line Kazak anchor is dispensable in its own Fireteam. The [DZ defense ranking](../../../list-building/dz-defenders-ranking.md) also favors Konduktor and Barsuk profiles.

## Heavy weapons — generally skip

- **HMG 20/1:** BS11 and no intrinsic gunfighting MODs. In FT2 it rolls B4 +1SD (five dice, retain four); FT4 improves BS to 12. A full team can make it a competent secondary gun, but the bare profile is not an apex attack piece. Compare the 19/0 Barsuk FTO AP Marksman Rifle for shorter-range armor conversion, the 30/1 Frontovik AP Spitfire for Mimetism and AP, the 33/1.5 TankHunter AP HMG for an independent Camo attack, or the 36/1.5 Spetsnaz HMG for Marksmanship. Different range bands and SWC mean these are alternatives, not identical replacements.
- **Missile Launcher 17/1.5:** A low-cost body with a dangerous EXP/AP threat where appropriate. At FT2, reactive B1 +1SD means one retained result, not two hits. FT4/5 improves the shooting platform, but the model remains ARM1/W1. The 19/1.5 Frontovik HRL buys BS12, Mimetism and Warhorse for two points more (different weapon/ammunition); the 28/0.5 Frontovik MSV1 AP Sniper saves one SWC and is a much more flexible visor-equipped lane defender. Independent Vystrel Neuro AP Sniper 26/0.5 adds a repairable Sapper/Neurocinetics alternative.

Prefer a cheap team supporting a real ARO gun over buying both Kazak heavy weapons. A missile remains a legitimate niche when the list specifically needs its ammunition and long-range threat at minimal points. See [gunfighters](../../../list-building/gunfighters-ranking.md) and [ARO rankings](../../../list-building/aro-units-apex-and-value.md).

## Fireteam composition — the real reason to take Line Kazaks

The Line Kazaks family allows Core and Haris. It requires **at least one actual Line Kazak**, even if every other member counts as one.

- **Counts as Line Kazak:** Kazak Doktor (max 2), Frontovik (max 1), Dozer (max 1), Barsuk FTO (Wildcard max 3), Voronin (Wildcard max 1).
- **Legal but not equivalent:** Veteran Kazak (max 2), Konduktor FTO (max 2), Beasthunter FTO (max 1).
- Native Line Kazaks: 1–5 in the chart, with overall physical team limits and army AVA respected. INTEL is an optional Spec-Ops entry, not ordinary matched-play glue.
- Mixed composition is not automatically bad. FT2 supplies +1SD, FT3 adds Discover/Dodge bonuses, FT4 adds +1 BS and FT5 grants Sixth Sense. A premium Veteran already has Sixth Sense and cannot be counted as a Line Kazak in this family.

Each is an alternative package, not a simultaneous army. Three-body teams can use Core or Haris; four/five require Core. Costs use the referenced roster; the latest Army chart confirms the applicable access.

The 23pt team is cheap, but linking the Konduktors is only worthwhile if coordinated movement or their same-unit bonus matters; independent deployment may cover more approaches. The 48pt Haris and 60pt/69pt Core put bonuses on a substantially better gun. The 67pt Veteran alternative sacrifices composition; a Veterans Haris with its own eligible Frontovik can be better if the primary goal is an active Veteran gun. Consider the alternative Gunnery or Mobility Haris before committing the single Haris slot.

The [#087 report](../../../battle-reports/081-090-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md#L176) documents a five-model Core with four Line Kazaks (one Lieutenant) plus a Frontovik sniper, alongside a separate Veterans Haris. [#127](../../../battle-reports/121-130-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md#L204) documents the three Line Kazaks/Voronin/Frontovik formation in a returning-player tutorial. These support the architecture, not proof that the exact constructed loadouts above won a tournament.

## Quantities and collection

- **0:** Entirely reasonable with another LT and Gunnery, Veterans or Mobility teams, using independent cheap Orders instead.
- **1:** Default FO native anchor or cheap standalone LT.
- **2:** LT + Rifle lookalike, or two native anchors if using separate Line Kazak Core/Haris; alternatively a native anchor and one backup same-family body.
- **3:** LT + lookalike + separate FO, or a larger inexpensive Core. Good practical ceiling for most collections.
- **4–5:** Only for a deliberate native-heavy Core or dual-family architecture; generally inferior to including useful Counts-as troops.

The [owned TAK inventory](../../../../inventory/tak.md) records **three Line Kazaks**. This is sufficient for the recommended architectures; the [TAK buy list](../../_buy-lists/tak-buy-list.md) already advises against duplicate Line Kazak boxes. Prefer rifle figures for the LT/lookalike and FO. No compulsory purchase; heavy-weapon sculpts are low priority unless a specific list repeatedly uses them.

**Bottom line:** a 10pt FO anchor or 9pt LT is worthwhile; the 9pt Rifle is a structural tool rather than a premium cheerleader. Build around the dangerous Frontovik/Veteran or artillery package, not the Line Kazak itself.
