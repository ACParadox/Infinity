## Overview

**What Varuna does exceptionally well:** **spend very little to force expensive enemy Orders in ARO**, then counterattack with linked MSV/Mimetism guns and independent Camo or fast specialists. **Helots, Kamau, Zulu-Cobras and Montesas** make a strong defensive-to-offensive sequence. Its redesigned mixed **Undertow, Cavalry and Varuna** teams are much more flexible than an obligatory oversized Kamau Core.

### Particularly strong or unusual Fireteams

- **Patsy + two Helot FTO Haris:** **Patsy Garnett 34 + two 10-point Helot FTO LRL = 54/0**, with **FT Master (Helots)** converting the two otherwise Irregular Helots at the *next Order Count* while a valid team exists. Useful mission/ARO shell, not cheapest possible Order farming. Non-FTO Camo Helots cannot join.
- **Undertow Core/Haris:** **Kamau or Varuna ORC** required-choice gun, with **foot Montesa FTO** for specialist assault and **Varuna Machinist** counting as Kamau; pressure can continue after the first shootout.
- **Cavalry Duo:** **Squalos Mk-II or motorized Montesa** is required-choice, with legal **Kamau/Bulleteer** support; a mobile alternative to a purely static Kamau shell.
- **Forward Montesa Duo:** exactly **two foot Montesas**; don't replace either with the motorized profile.

### Unusual access, gaps and trade-offs

- **No Chain of Command profile in the 27-unit roster.** **Quinn FO/Disco is not CoC**; protect exposed ORC/Uma/Zulu/Cutter LTs accordingly.
- **Disco/Eclipse:** **Quinn** offers projected anti-MSV crossing without providing one-sided shooting *through* Eclipse.
- **Exceptional cheap Irregular ARO density:** Helot FTO Sensor launchers and independent Camo(1 Use) variants create different traps; AVA3, no general free Regular conversion.
- **Anti-Mimetism and midfield denial:** **Kamau MSV2/MSV1** and **Zulu-Cobra Jammer/Pitcher/FO/KHD** cover different problems with mutually exclusive loadouts.
- **Two TAGs, no Vehicle:** **Cutter** and **Squalos** are both legal, but **Redeye VH is not**, and Cutter does **not** inherit Squalos Tactical Awareness.
- **N5.3 Fireteam redesign:** older Kamau Core assumptions and non-FTO Helot link assertions should not override [the current Varuna chart](../../sectorials/panoceania/varuna-immediate-reaction-division.md#fireteams).

## Core identity

Current N5 Varuna is still a **defensive / counterattack PanO sectorial**, but it should not be built like old N3/N4 Varuna.

The critical redesign is structural:

- the old Kamau-centric Fireteam paradigm is gone;
- Varuna now works through **mixed Varuna, Undertow, Advanced Montesa and revised Cavalry Fireteams**;
- Zulu-Cobras, Montesas and Varuna ORCs are much more central to current listbuilding discussion;
- Helots and Kamau still create excellent reactive friction, but the faction should not spend so much on static AROs that it lacks an active turn.

A good high-level model is:

> **cheap reactive friction + midfield control / information + one mobile counterattack package + enough mission and recovery infrastructure**

The biggest listbuilding mistake is still easy to make:

> **Varuna is good at defence, but maximizing defence is not the same thing as building a good Varuna list.**

You usually want the opponent to spend Orders solving your reactive shell, then have enough mobile pieces left to exploit the damaged board.

## Strong recurring building blocks

Across current N5 discussion, redesign analysis, tournament-list material and community examples, the pieces that repeat most strongly are:

- **Zulu-Cobra**, especially Sensor / Jammer utility;
- **Kamau**, especially MSV / long-range and utility profiles;
- **Montesa**, particularly FTO / specialist / Blitzen builds;
- **Varuna ORCs**, which are much better here than generic ORC reputation suggests;
- **Helots** as extremely cheap disposable reactive friction;
- **Clipper FTO** as a less obvious but efficient ranged option;
- **Quinn** when Forward Observer, MSV1 or Disco Baller utility solves a specific architecture problem (**not** Chain of Command in the pinned roster);
- **Machinist** when the list contains TAGs, REMs or expensive repairable pieces;
- **Cutter or Squalos Mk-II** as optional centerpiece architectures rather than mandatory faction identity.

The recurring N5 foundation is often a combination of:

- **Zulu-Cobra**;
- **Montesa**;
- **ORC**;
- **Kamau**.

It appears more consistently than a **Cutter with an oversized Kamau link**.

## Zulu-Cobra

Zulu-Cobra is probably the closest thing Varuna has to a genuine **auto-consider**.

The important point is not that one Zulu profile is mandatory. It is that almost every profile solves a useful Varuna problem.

### Sensor / Jammer

This is the default board-control Zulu.

Use it when you want:

- Camo / marker pressure;
- Sensor against midfield skirmishers;
- Jammer control over approach routes;
- Forward Deployment utility;
- a model that can punish the opponent for trying to solve Kamau / Helot defence through the midfield instead of directly.

This profile is particularly coherent with Varuna's layered defence:

1. long-range AROs constrain obvious movement;
2. opponent uses Camo, midfield or short-range routes;
3. Zulu forces additional Discover / Reset / Sensor / pathing decisions.

The Zulu does not have to kill models to be valuable. **Denying clean movement is enough.**

---

### Pitcher / Hacker Zulu

Do not take this merely because Varuna can field a Hacker.

Take it when the list actually benefits from projected repeater coverage:

- Hackable enemy pieces are likely mission problems;
- you have enough hacking presence to exploit the Pitcher;
- Spotlight / control states matter to the plan;
- another repeater angle meaningfully changes the table.

It is less attractive if the hacking network is otherwise shallow.

---

### MULTI Marksman / X-Visor Zulu

This is one of the more easily overlooked Zulu profiles.

It gives Varuna a Camo combat piece that can engage from the midfield rather than functioning only as a Jammer / Sensor utility model.

Use it when:

- the list already has enough Sensor / Jammer;
- you need an additional independent attack angle;
- you want a model that can exploit gaps created by the main gunfighter;
- midfield range-band flexibility matters.

A useful rule:

> **Ask why you are not taking a Zulu before asking why you are taking one.**

## Kamau

Kamau remain fundamental to Varuna, but current Varuna should not be understood as “build the army around a Kamau Core.”

Their value now comes from being **excellent components inside mixed teams**.

### MSV2 MULTI Sniper

Still a signature long-range roadblock.

Use it when:

- the table offers a lane worth contesting;
- Smoke / visual-modifier attackers are a concern;
- you want a reactive piece that the opponent must actively solve;
- the rest of the list contains enough active pieces that the sniper does not become your whole game plan.

Do not deploy it merely because Varuna is “supposed” to have one. If the table gives it no useful lane, hide it or choose another profile.

---

### HRL Kamau

A strong alternative when you want:

- lower cost;
- template pressure;
- mid/long-range deterrence;
- a reactive piece that changes how tightly the opponent can move models.

The HRL often fits more naturally than the MSR when points are tight or the rest of the list already has a premium long-range gun.

---

### Paramedic / Tactical Awareness Kamau

This is a very useful **active Fireteam utility** profile rather than a static ARO piece.

It contributes:

- specialist capability;
- recovery utility;
- an additional special Order;
- respectable gunfighting;
- useful movement efficiency inside a small team.

This is one of the profiles that demonstrates why current Varuna should not reduce Kamau to “sniper guy.”

---

### Sensor / Triangulated Fire Kamau

Worth considering when:

- the opponent is marker-heavy;
- Zulu Sensor is absent or overloaded;
- the Fireteam wants its own Camo-solving tool;
- difficult MOD stacks make Triangulated Fire useful.

This can reduce the need to rely on a Pathfinder for every Sensor job.

---

## Montesa

Montesa appears to be one of the biggest winners of the N5 Varuna redesign.

Its importance comes from the combination of:

- heavy-infantry durability;
- specialist options;
- Blitzen / E/M threat;
- BS Attack (+1SD);
- useful Fireteam access;
- strong efficiency for the role it performs.

### FTO Montesa

The FTO Paramedic / Blitzen style profile is one of the most coherent Varuna glue pieces.

Use it when you want:

- a durable specialist moving with the team;
- a model you are more comfortable exposing than an expensive Kamau gunfighter;
- strong reactive E/M deterrence;
- a close/mid-range body that complements a longer-range Kamau.

#### Important +1SD note

Do **not** describe the Montesa as simply having “Burst 3 in ARO.”

Its Shadow Dice improve the reliability of the reactive roll, but they are not ordinary Burst. Treat the profile as a **very reliable low-Burst reactive threat**, especially with Blitzen, rather than as a literal B3 ARO gun.

---

### Motorized Montesa

The bike is a different architecture.

Use it when:

- the army needs an independent fast specialist;
- you want Turn 2/3 objective reach;
- the Fireteam is already doing something else;
- rapid lateral movement matters more than team bonuses.

The Red Fury / aggressive bike profile is better when you specifically want a fast independent attack vector.

A useful distinction is:

> **FTO Montesa = team glue / resilient specialist.**
>
> **Motorized Montesa = independent speed / objective or attack projection.**

---

## Varuna ORCs

Do not import generic ORC prejudice into Varuna.

Current Varuna ORCs are repeatedly praised because the sectorial-specific combination of **Stealth + Warhorse** makes them far more functional as forward heavy infantry.

Their role is different from Kamau:

- **Kamau** = precision, Mimetism / MSV / specialist utility;
- **ORC** = durable active body that can actually lead a team through danger.

Use an ORC when:

- you want a Fireteam that advances rather than remains static;
- the team needs a durable point model;
- you need a conventional active gun rather than another reactive sniper;
- hacking / ZoC movement would otherwise make a heavy team awkward and Stealth materially improves the route.

The key listbuilding lesson is:

> **A mixed ORC / Kamau / Montesa package is often more coherent than stacking several expensive Kamau gunfighters.**

---

## Helots

Helots remain classic Varuna defensive efficiency.

Their job is often not to kill anything.

A Helot succeeds when it:

- forces Discover / attack sequencing;
- constrains a lane;
- makes an opponent spend Orders clearing a cheap model;
- protects the real army by creating uncertainty and pathing cost.

### How many?

#### One Helot

Very easy to justify.

Use it as a cheap defensive speed bump that creates disproportionate reactive friction.

#### Two Helots

A meaningful layered shell.

Good when your premium pieces are deliberately hidden during the opponent's first turn.

#### Maximum Helots

This is where you should check whether the army is becoming too reactive.

Three cheap ARO pieces can be excellent, but every roster slot and SWC point spent on models whose main value is the opponent's turn is one less active-turn solution.

The principle is:

> **Buy enough Helots to make Turn 1 expensive, not enough Helots to make your own Turn 1 weak.**

---

## Clipper FTO

The Clipper FTO is one of the more interesting less-obvious N5 Varuna pieces.

It is attractive because it can provide serious missile pressure without spending Kamau prices.

Use it when:

- the Fireteam wants an inexpensive long-range weapon;
- SWC / points make another premium gun awkward;
- you want a different threat profile from a sniper;
- the team can protect the REM from easy short-range collapse.

Do not reduce the Clipper to Guided Missile duty. Its FTO profile can be selected simply because it is an efficient direct-fire weapon in the correct team.

---

Compare full packages before buying an indirect-fire plan:

- **Squalos Mk-II HMG + GL (62/1.5):** Speculative attacks at hidden support while keeping HMG primary.
- **Clipper Guided (16/1.5):** Spotlight/Repeater/FO payoff, **not** the FTO +1B launcher.
- **Kamau, a TAG or a fast scorer:** may return more value than an Order-intensive Guided engine.

[Global apex/value tiers, when-to-use decisions, and MCP-reviewed opposing views](../list-building/indirect-fire-speculative-guided-apex-value.md).

## Quinn

Quinn is useful because he solves **specific structural problems** rather than because he is mandatory.

### Command resilience — Quinn does not provide CoC

The pinned Varuna roster gives **Quinn FO MULTI Marksman Rifle and Disco Baller/MULTI Rifle options**, **not Chain of Command**. Therefore Quinn cannot provide succession insurance to an aggressive:

- **ORC Lieutenant**;
- **Uma Lieutenant**;
- **Cutter Lieutenant**.

There is **no Varuna Chain of Command profile** in this roster.

If using an active Lieutenant, mitigate the risk through **concealed deployment, carefully timed activation, alternate attacks and survivable command positioning**. Do not count on a nonexistent backup to prevent Loss of Lieutenant. The generic advice to keep command assets in different attack footprints remains sound, but does not grant a CoC option here.

---

### Disco Baller Quinn

This is genuine Varuna-specific utility.

Use it when:

- visual control helps an expensive team cross lanes;
- the list wants additional repeater / area-control utility;
- the Fireteam benefits from a model whose job is enabling movement rather than being another gun.

Do not take Disco Baller merely because it is unique. Take it when the table plan actually uses the visual-control effect.

---

- **Quinn FO + Disco 26/0** is Varuna's precision Eclipse option, especially when Smoke/MSV/strong Mimetism makes Kamau/ORC FtF odds poor. It does **not** provide Chain of Command. Use a protected Wildcard/score route after the cloud, not a decorative Disco throw.
- **Opposing evaluations:** Dante treats Quinn/vision control as a solution to hard AROs; older gun-first evaluations prioritize another Kamau or a TAG. Buy Quinn only when a real scoring/attack route needs him. See [global comparison](../list-building/vision-control-units-apex-and-value.md).

- **Toolbox cost:** Quinn's linked/Wildcard specialist and LoF utility can justify a physical objective route, not a Chain of Command role; on open tables where Disco is unused, extra Kamau/ORC or cheaper specialists may be more efficient.

**Cross-faction verdict and opposing transcripts:** [toolbox apex/value guide](../list-building/jack-of-all-trades-toolbox-units-apex-value.md).

## Fireteam architectures

The strongest current lesson is:

> **Do not start a Varuna list by deciding on a five-model Core. Start with roles and build the smallest team that improves them.**

### Kamau + Montesa Undertow

This is probably the most characteristic N5 Varuna pairing.

The division of labour is clean:

- Kamau provides the precision ranged piece;
- Montesa provides durability, specialist capability and Blitzen threat;
- the team can move rather than existing only to provide bonuses to one sniper.

This is particularly attractive when the rest of the army contains independent midfield and counterattack pieces.

---

### ORC + Kamau / Montesa package

Use when the Fireteam itself is supposed to advance.

Typical roles:

- ORC = durable leader / active gun;
- Kamau = precision / MSV / specialist / secondary gun;
- Montesa = resilient specialist / E/M threat;
- cheaper utility body = keep the team from becoming a giant points sink.

Do not fill every legal slot with an expensive elite model unless each one solves a different problem.

---

### Fusilier + Kamau Duo

A useful low-cost architecture when you want the Kamau gun without buying a full defensive brick.

Use when:

- the Kamau mainly needs mobility / team support;
- the list wants to spend points on Zulu, Montesa, ORC, TAG or midfield pieces instead;
- a larger team would be mostly filler.

---

### Montesa-focused teams

Advanced Montesa Fireteam access makes it reasonable to build around the Montesa as more than a support model.

Use when:

- mission scoring and resilient movement matter;
- the table is dense enough that a durable close/mid-range team is preferable to a static sniper package;
- you want Blitzen / E/M deterrence integrated into the main team.

---

## Lieutenant architectures

Current sources do **not** show a single strong Varuna LT consensus. Treat Lieutenant choice as an architecture decision.

### Fusilier LT

The simplest option.

Use when:

- points are tight;
- the rest of the list already contains the real tactical pieces;
- NCO can convert the LT Order;
- you can hide the LT safely enough.

Weakness:

- can become obvious in a list with only one or two Fusiliers;
- contributes little beyond the Order.

---

### Zulu-Cobra LT

A much more functional LT profile.

Use when:

- the Zulu utility package is already wanted;
- you value a less conventional LT shell;
- the model can remain protected until its board-control role is needed.

Tradeoff:

- once the Zulu starts doing Jammer / Sensor work, the LT itself is exposed.

There is **no Quinn CoC in Varuna**; an active Zulu Lieutenant requires a protection plan and accepts genuine Loss of Lieutenant risk.

---

### Uma LT

Use when:

- Uma is already part of the mobile mission plan;
- you want an LT that actually contributes movement and specialist work;
- the command architecture has enough protection / succession that an active LT is acceptable.

This is a more aggressive architecture than Fusilier LT.

---

### ORC LT

Use when:

- the ORC is already central to the Fireteam;
- a durable LT body is desirable;
- Firewall / team utility from the selected profile matters;
- CoC exists if the ORC is expected to fight aggressively.

Do not make an ORC LT simply because it is durable. Make it LT because the rest of the architecture benefits from it.

---

### Cutter LT

This is a deliberate all-in architecture.

Use when:

- the TAG itself is the plan;
- you accept concentrating command and a huge fraction of the army into one model;
- succession / CoC or mission context makes the risk acceptable.

The NCO Cutter is generally the cleaner option when the list wants a passive LT elsewhere.

## Cutter and Squalos Mk-II

TAG Varuna is viable, but **TAG Varuna is an archetype, not the faction default**.

Current N5 discussions emphasize these non-TAG units more than older Varuna material did:

- **Zulu-Cobra**;
- **Montesa**;
- **ORC**;
- **Kamau**.

### Cutter

Take the Cutter when the TAG itself is intended to be the main problem-solving engine.

Its strengths are:

- extreme gunfighting quality;
- marker / concealment play;
- high resilience;
- NCO architecture with a passive LT;
- ability to solve engagements that normal PanO pieces may find inefficient.

Its weakness is opportunity cost:

- it consumes roughly one-third of the army;
- support and redundancy shrink;
- every failed attack run matters more;
- Varuna no longer needs a Cutter to have a strong identity.

A useful heuristic:

> **Take Cutter because the mission / matchup package wants a Cutter, not because “Varuna equals Cutter.”**

---

### Squalos Mk-II

Squalos is better understood as a **cheaper conventional TAG embedded inside a Varuna army**.

The NCO / Tactical Awareness profile is particularly coherent when Lieutenant Orders would otherwise be wasted.

Use Squalos when:

- you want a TAG but still want points for Zulu / Montesa / ORC / specialists;
- NCO order conversion matters;
- you want a strong conventional active gun without building the entire army around marker-state TAG play.

The grenade-launcher profile is useful as **secondary indirect problem solving**, especially against models protected by geometry, but speculative fire should not become the entire plan.

Current linked tacticas do **not** repeatedly identify Squalos as a universal Varuna staple. Treat it as a strong architecture choice rather than a default inclusion.

---

## Croc Men

Croc Men are less frequently highlighted than Zulus, but they solve a qualitatively different problem.

Use them when you want:

- Hidden Deployment;
- Infiltration;
- Mimetism-heavy midfield scoring;
- mines / hidden area control;
- a specialist that can remain absent from the opponent's information until needed.

They are particularly useful when you want **hidden scoring or a surprise midfield vector**, not merely another visible control piece.

Do not replace every Zulu with a Croc Man. The two profiles do different jobs:

- Zulu = visible / semi-visible control and information pressure;
- Croc Man = hidden presence and delayed reveal.

---

## Echo-Bravo

Echo-Bravo gives Varuna a lateral attack / mission vector that does not begin in the normal deployment shell.

Use it when:

- the opponent is likely to leave flank weaknesses;
- you need access to support pieces protected from frontal attack;
- a late-game specialist arriving from the side can swing scoring;
- the main army needs a qualitatively different threat direction.

Do not force an Echo-Bravo into every list. Its value increases when the rest of the army is visibly frontal and the opponent is likely to overcommit against your main defensive shell.

---

## REMs

Varuna has enough faction-specific toys that it is easy to forget normal PanO REM efficiency.

### Fugazi

Excellent filler when:

- you need a cheap Regular Order;
- another repeater improves the hacking network;
- Flash Pulse / Mimetism contributes to defence;
- a roster slot remains available.

Not mandatory.

---

### Pathfinder

Use when the list wants:

- fast specialist movement;
- Sensor;
- repeater coverage;
- Deactivator;
- objective support.

It becomes less automatic when Zulu and Kamau already provide enough Sensor / mission utility.

---

### Sierra

Take for a specific reactive job, not because Varuna is a defensive sectorial.

Add a **Sierra** only if it fills a gap left by an existing ARO shell involving:

- **Helot**;
- **Kamau**;
- **Clipper**.

The danger is **defensive overinvestment**.

---

### Bulleteer

Still a strong inexpensive active attacker when the list needs a medium-range gun without paying ORC / TAG prices.

Take it because the list needs a cheap asymmetric secondary attack piece, not because PanO traditionally likes Bulleteers.

---

Support profiles should solve a roster-legal problem:

- **Baggage/Repeater bot (8) vs EVO (14/0.5):** choose EVO with repeated Assisted Fire or Controlled Jump utility.
- **Tech-Bee TAGCom (9):** aids only eligible same-group Cutter/Squalos TAGs if actually rostered; not the Redeye Vehicle.
- **TinBot:** only applies as printed, within legal Fireteams.
- **Strategic Deployment:** requires the printed skill and usable deployment geometry; Kestrel Crux/Jackal Fireteam benefits do not transfer.

[Whole-game support apex/value and reviewed opposing opinions](../list-building/force-multipliers-special-abilities-apex-value.md).

## Machinist and recovery infrastructure

Machinist becomes more valuable as the list adds:

- Cutter;
- Squalos;
- Clipper;
- Sierra / Bulleteer / other REMs;
- expensive repairable pieces.

The key question is not “does Varuna have an Engineer?” but:

> **Is there enough repairable value in this list that one Engineer Order can preserve a major attack engine?**

If yes, Engineer support is often efficient.

If the list is mostly infantry and expendable Helots, Engineer tax becomes less compelling.

## Strong current listbuilding skeletons

These are **architectures**, not fixed lists.

### Balanced Varuna

Start with:

- Zulu Sensor / Jammer;
- one Kamau long-range profile;
- FTO Montesa;
- one Helot;
- one mobile active gun such as ORC / Kamau / Bulleteer;
- specialists and support.

Then add:

- second Zulu / Croc / Echo-Bravo if more board presence is needed;
- Clipper if long-range firepower is still light;
- Quinn if **FO / MSV1 / Disco Baller** solves a real problem; no CoC option exists here.

---

### ORC counterattack Varuna

Start with:

- ORC-led mobile Fireteam;
- Kamau or Montesa complementary profiles;
- Zulu midfield control;
- Helot defensive friction;
- cheap LT or CoC architecture.

This is one of the most coherent ways to avoid the “all defence, no active turn” trap.

---

### Cutter Varuna

Start with:

- Cutter NCO;
- protected cheap LT;
- Engineer;
- minimal but efficient reactive shell;
- Zulu utility;
- cheap specialists.

Do **not** then buy another huge premium attacker unless the mission package explicitly demands redundancy at that level. The Cutter already consumes the centerpiece budget.

---

### Squalos Varuna

Start with:

- NCO / Tactical Awareness Squalos;
- LT architecture that gives NCO useful Orders;
- Machinist;
- Zulu;
- Kamau / Montesa defensive-mobile package;
- cheap scoring pieces.

This preserves more roster flexibility than Cutter while retaining a TAG centerpiece.

## Common traps

### 1. Rebuilding old Kamau Core Varuna

Current Varuna Fireteams were deliberately redesigned.

Do not try to recreate an obsolete Kamau castle by buying expensive bodies whose only job is buffing one sniper.

---

### 2. Too many ARO pieces

A full static ARO package can look oppressive on paper:

- **Helot**;
- **Kamau MULTI Sniper**;
- **Kamau HRL**;
- **Clipper**;
- **Sierra**.

It can also leave the active turn with too few Orders and too few mobile attackers.

The goal is **Order taxation**, not maximum reactive gun count.

---

### 3. Too many premium guns competing for Orders

A list can include individually strong units yet demand too many active-turn Orders from the same pool:

- **Cutter**;
- **ORC HMG**;
- **Multiple Kamau shooters**;
- **Montesa**;
- **Bulleteer**.

Choose one primary attack engine and one meaningfully different secondary vector.

---

### 4. Taking a Hacker without a hacking architecture

Zulu Pitcher and other hacking pieces are attractive, but they need:

- useful repeater coverage;
- worthwhile targets;
- enough hacking quality to justify the Orders.

Do not pay for token hacking that never affects table decisions.

---

### 5. Treating TAGs as mandatory faction identity

Historical Varuna advice often centered the Cutter.

TAGs remain optional because Varuna can build credible lists around:

- **Zulu-Cobra**;
- **Montesa**;
- **ORC**;
- **Kamau**.

---

### 6. Assuming a nonexistent CoC safety net

Aggressive Lieutenants can expose Varuna to Loss of Lieutenant:

- **ORC LT**;
- **Uma LT**;
- **Zulu-Cobra LT**;
- **Cutter LT**.

**Quinn is not CoC, and Varuna has no CoC profile in the pinned roster.** Do not rely on an unavailable successor.

---

## Practical pregame checklist

Before deploying Varuna, identify:

1. **Which lane is actually worth defending?** Do not expose Kamau / Clipper just because they are ARO pieces.
2. **What route will the opponent use to bypass the long gun?** Put Zulu / Helot / mines / Jammer pressure there.
3. **What is the primary active-turn attacker?** Make sure it begins safe and has enough Orders.
4. **What is the secondary attack vector?** Prefer something qualitatively different: midfield Camo, Echo-Bravo flank, Montesa speed, ORC durability, TAG brute force.
5. **Which pieces actually score the mission?** Varuna can overbuy guns very easily.
6. **Can the opponent identify the LT?** If yes, decide whether the risk is acceptable or whether CoC / alternative LT placement is required.
7. **Is the list too static?** If removing the Kamau ARO piece makes the rest of the army passive, rebalance the list.

---

## Current source-derived conclusions

The strongest conclusions supported repeatedly by the current material are:

- **Zulu-Cobra is one of the most universally useful Varuna units.**
- **Montesa is much more central in N5 than older Varuna advice implies.**
- **Varuna ORCs are genuinely strong and should not be dismissed as generic ORCs.**
- **Kamau remain excellent but should be treated as mixed-team components, not the automatic army centerpiece.**
- **Helots remain premium defensive efficiency, but taking the maximum is not automatically correct.**
- **Kamau + Montesa Undertow is one of the most characteristic N5 Varuna combinations.**
- **TAG Varuna remains viable but is now one architecture among several.**
- **Current Varuna rewards small purpose-built Fireteams and layered defence more than static castle-building.**
