**Reference archive:** [Primary source recordings, N5.3 tactical cross-references, battle reports and purchasing audit](references/vanilla-pano_refs.md).

The source guide mixed 2025/early N5 Fireteam concepts into present-day advice; they have been reframed below as **unlinked unit synergies or historical examples**, not legal N5.3 vanilla links. This change affects movement/order compression, team bonuses, and the whole-list value of purported "Duo" packages. Use [N5.3 rules](../../rules/README.md) and the current roster, not dated podcasts, for legality.

## Overview

**What Vanilla PanO does exceptionally well:** combine **the best independent PanO shooting, command, marker, TAG and mission tools without a sectorial roster's narrower choices**. Swiss Guard Hidden guns, Cutter/Tikbalang/Squalos TAGs, cheap synchronized specialists and Camo/airborne alternatives give enormous choice. The fundamental cost is equally distinctive: **Vanilla PanO has no standard Fireteam chart**, so there are *no* normal Duo/Haris/Core shared activations or link bonuses.

### Particularly strong or unusual Fireteams

**None.** The [Vanilla PanO reference](../../sectorials/panoceania/panoceania.md#fireteams) explicitly has **no standard Fireteam chart**; FTO labels do not unlock Duo/Haris/Core bonuses.

Its best **unlinked synergies** are:

- **Magistrate Strategos LT + independent gun:** flexible converted LT Order helps power a Swiss or TAG without claiming nonexistent Fireteam movements.
- **Joan LT + eligible non-Religious Irregular troopers:** Inspiring Leadership converts Orders only when their actual rules and profiles permit it; Joan has no Vanilla Haris.
- **Swiss Guard + Pathfinder/Peacemaker:** Hidden first-shot threat and independent Sensor/Repeater/forward denial cover different axes.
- **Tikbalang or Squalos + Tech-Bee + Engineer:** a self-funding TAG backed by support; each separate model still costs its own activations.

### Unusual access, gaps and trade-offs

- **Broad premium roster:** **Cutter, Tikbalang, Squalos, Swiss, Zulu-Cobra, Drummer** can coexist as legal independent choices; **Jotum and Uhlan** remain absent from the Vanilla roster.
- **Aerial VH and AD options:** **Redeye** supplies rare off-axis flight; **Banshee KHD** supplies a cheap landing hacker, with No Cover/hackability or Combat Jump risk respectively.
- **Unusual low-cost tools:** **Fennec Disco/Sensor**, **Drummer double FO**, **Magistrate Strategos/CoC**, **Peacemaker Auxbot** and **Zulu-Cobra Jammer** offer many complementary niche solutions, but not one imaginary combined profile.
- **Real CoC exists:** **Magistrate CoC 21** can insure an attacking LT, but **AVA1** prevents also taking its 19-point Strategos LT profile.
- **Disco/Eclipse is not normal smoke-shooting:** the Eclipse template blocks friendly MSV too; do not import other PanO sectorials' link/smoke abilities.
- **Fireteam absence is absolute:** a supposed Karhu Duo, Joan Haris or Bulleteer + Blade Duo in older source material is **only an unlinked tactic** under the pinned N5.3 chart.

## Core identity

Current N5.3 Vanilla PanO is much broader than the old stereotype of **BS15 + TAG + cheap Orders**.

The faction still wins through elite gunfighting, but current Vanilla gains a lot from:

- strong Lieutenant / NCO / Chain of Command architectures;
- high-efficiency TAG and HI attack pieces;
- cheap REM infrastructure;
- meaningful midfield control;
- better hacking and repeater play than older PanO editions;
- complementary independent units, specialists and repair support rather than unsupported vanilla Duos/Haris.

Vanilla **has no standard Fireteam access** in the pinned N5.3 Army reference, so the best way to think about it is:

> **one premier attack engine + one qualitatively different secondary threat + cheap efficient infrastructure + enough midfield / mission / recovery tools**

The major listbuilding trap is buying too many excellent gunfighters. PanO can easily field several premium shooters that all want the same Regular Orders. The difficult part is ensuring that the army still has:

- usable Order economy;
- midfield presence;
- specialists;
- hacking protection;
- recovery;
- attack vectors that solve different problems.

---

**Vision-control roles are independent, not Fireteam bonuses.** Vanilla can field suitable Disco/Albedo tools where listed but cannot borrow Kestrel/Varuna/NCA Fireteam permissions. Check each exact profile and roster entry before choosing:

- **Fennec FO/Sensor/Disco (13 points in Kestrel):** that price and use are from its Kestrel context; verify Vanilla availability rather than importing its Order plan.
- **Aquila:** premium visor/Disco option when the actual profile supports scoring or movement.
- **Quinn:** alternative Disco specialist package where legal.
- **Vargar:** projected Eclipse/utility option where rostered.
- **Konstantinos:** another premium profile to evaluate for an actual scoring or mobility role.

**Normal Smoke** can support friendly MSV shooting; **Disco/Eclipse** blocks even friendly MSV. Choose the appropriate effect rather than assuming either grants one-sided fire. [Global vision-control tiers](../list-building/vision-control-units-apex-and-value.md).

## Recurring listbuilding archetypes

### Joan economy

Core idea:

- Joan v2 as an aggressive command piece;
- cheap Irregular utility becomes much more valuable through Inspiring Leadership;
- Chain of Command protects the aggressive LT architecture;
- NCO pieces convert Lieutenant Orders when Joan herself should not spend them.

Common supporting pieces:

- Magistrate CoC;
- Helots;
- Warcor;
- Motorized Bounty Hunter;
- Karhu / Cutter / Squalos / other NCO;
- cheap REMs.

This is one of the most uniquely Vanilla PanO architectures.

---

### TAG engine

Core idea:

- one highly efficient TAG performs the main opening work;
- the rest of the army provides Orders, midfield pressure, specialists and recovery;
- avoid spending so much on a second premium gun that the list becomes too thin.

Common TAG choices:

- Tikbalang;
- Cutter;
- Squalos Mk-II.

Support commonly includes:

- Machinist / Engineer;
- Fugazi;
- Pathfinder;
- Fennec utility;
- Zulu / Peacemaker midfield pressure;
- Magistrate or Joan command architecture.

---

### Swiss hidden hammer

The Swiss Guard remains one of Vanilla PanO's cleanest independent attack packages.

The basic concept is:

1. hide the premium attacker;
2. force the opponent to deploy without knowing its angle;
3. reveal only when the important target / lane is identified;
4. spend Orders until the relevant part of the opponent's army stops functioning.

This is particularly attractive because the Swiss does not need Fireteam infrastructure and can remain protected if you go second.

---

### REM pressure

Instead of one giant centerpiece, Vanilla can use several cheap asymmetric attack pieces:

- Peacemaker;
- Bulleteer;
- Pathfinder;
- Drummer packages;
- Fugazi infrastructure.

This architecture can create multiple threats without concentrating too much value in one model.

---

### Compact unlinked support packages (not Fireteams)

The **underlying tactical pairing** is useful even when the two models do not activate as a Fireteam. Select:

- one gunfighter;
- one specialist / support model;
- complementary movement and engagement bands.

Examples of **independent, unlinked** synergies:

- Karhu Feuerbach covered by a Karhu Engineer;
- Bulleteer with a Blade Engineer positioned for repair;
- Bolt sniper plus a utility Bolt, each activating on their own;
- Joan supported by relevant specialists and independent command assets.

These pairs gain **no Duo/Haris bonuses and no shared team activation** in current vanilla. If you need linked movement, you must select a sectorial with the corresponding chart.

---

### Tactical Awareness / NCO economy

Vanilla has unusually strong ways to convert special Orders into useful activations.

Relevant pieces include:

- Joan;
- Tikbalang LT(+1 Order);
- Karhu NCO;
- Squalos NCO;
- Cutter NCO;
- Firefly NCO profiles;
- Tactical Awareness Pathfinder / Bipandra / TAGs where applicable.

The key principle is:

> **Do not just count Orders. Ask where every special Order can actually be spent.**

---

Choose support purchases after defining the independently activating attack plan:

- **Tech-Bee TAGCom (9/0):** printed PH +3 Dodge/GizmoKit +1 for eligible same-group TAGs.
- **Mulebot Baggage (8/0) vs EVO (14/0.5):** reload/disposable utility versus actual eligible REM/Combat Jump supportware.
- **Maximus FTO TinBot (Repeater) (45/1):** no Vanilla Fireteam or cross-group TinBot benefit can be inferred from FTO labeling.
- **Squalos, Redeye and Tikbalang:** may justify saving 6 points/0.5 SWC on the basic Mulebot over EVO.

## Joan v2

Joan is one of the defining Vanilla PanO architecture pieces.

Her value is not only combat power. She changes the economics of the entire list.

She contributes:

- real active-turn shooting;
- Tactical Awareness;
- Inspiring Leadership;
- strong mobility;
- very high-quality close combat;
- several useful LT configurations;
- command economy that makes Irregular troops materially better.

### Joan economy

Joan increases the value of units such as:

- Warcor;
- Helots;
- Motorized Bounty Hunter;
- other inexpensive Irregular utility pieces.

This means cheap models that would normally produce self-contained or awkward Orders become part of a usable army-wide economy.

A strong architecture is:

> **Joan LT + cheap Irregular utility + protected CoC + NCO outlet where useful**

Do not take cheap Irregular models simply because Joan can regularize them. Each one should still contribute a useful table role.

---

### Joan + Chain of Command

The Magistrate CoC is one of the cleanest partners.

The important principle is:

> **Do not put Joan and the CoC model in the same obvious attack footprint.** There is no standard vanilla Fireteam to justify clustering them.

Otherwise the same:

- template;
- mine;
- hacking area;
- Guided attack;
- DZ strike;
- concentrated attack run

can kill or disable both command models.

Chain of Command is most useful when succession survives whatever killed the Lieutenant.

---

## Magistrate

The Magistrate is one of Vanilla PanO's strongest low-profile command/support pieces.

It supports two very different architectures.

### Chain of Command Magistrate

Use when:

- Joan is aggressive;
- Tikbalang LT is central;
- another expensive LT is expected to fight;
- Loss of Lieutenant would seriously hurt the list.

The model remains useful because it is also a specialist and contributes Counterintelligence.

---

### Strategos Magistrate LT

This is one of Vanilla's most easily overlooked Lieutenant options.

Use when:

- you do not need the LT itself to be an attacker;
- you want Strategos utility;
- you want Counterintelligence built into the command model;
- you want a relatively economical specialist LT.

This is often a better default than automatically taking a Fusilier LT.

---

## Karhu

Karhu are among Vanilla PanO's most generally useful independent NCO and gunfighter/support pieces.

Their attraction is the package of:

- NCO;
- MSV1;
- Mimetism;
- Climbing Plus;
- strong mobility;
- good weapon and specialist choices.

### Feuerbach + Engineer support pairing (unlinked)

This is a complementary pair of **separate** vanilla troopers, not a legal Duo. The Engineer can recover the Feuerbach trooper or other valuable nearby assets, but both spend activations independently.

The Feuerbach provides:

- long-range pressure;
- AP / anti-armour capability;
- reactive threat;
- good vertical access through Climbing Plus.

The Engineer provides:

- specialist capability;
- mines / utility;
- recovery for TAGs / REMs / HI where relevant;
- another useful NCO body.

The two models naturally want to operate in similar parts of the board.

Do not assume the Feuerbach is mandatory.

The cheaper MULTI Rifle Karhu profiles remain useful because NCO is unit-wide.

Use them when:

- the list already has enough long-range shooting;
- you need an NCO outlet rather than another premium gun;
- you want MSV1 / Mimetism / Climbing Plus utility;
- AP Mine / E/Marat utility helps the midfield plan.

This is often a better use of points than adding a second or third expensive ranged attacker.

## Swiss Guard

The Swiss Guard remains the classic Vanilla PanO hidden hammer.

Its core value comes from:

- Hidden Deployment;
- Mimetism (-6);
- BS15;
- 2W / ARM5 durability;
- excellent weapon profiles.

### HMG Swiss

The simplest role:

- reveal when the correct lane is identified;
- attack the opponent's most important long-range / midfield piece;
- keep spending Orders while the engagement remains favourable.

It is especially good in Vanilla because it needs no Fireteam support.

---

### Missile Launcher Swiss

Use when you want:

- hidden reactive threat;
- a model the opponent cannot safely account for during deployment;
- a later active-turn AP / explosive problem solver.

Do not expose it unnecessarily simply because it has a strong ARO weapon.

---

### Forward / cheaper Swiss profiles

The cheaper AP-SMG / forward profiles deserve consideration.

Use when:

- you want the durability and Mimetism of a Swiss without paying HMG prices;
- the table is dense;
- midfield / near-midfield pressure matters more than long-range reach;
- the saved points produce another meaningful threat elsewhere.

## Zulu-Cobra

Zulu-Cobra is one of the cleanest Vanilla midfield/control pieces.

### Sensor / Jammer

This is the default utility choice.

Use it to:

- pressure Camo / markers;
- control approach routes;
- punish enemies trying to bypass your long-range guns through the midfield;
- protect the gap between your deployment zone and the main firefight.

The Zulu does not need to kill anything to be efficient. Making movement awkward is already valuable.

---

### KHD / Pitcher

Take when the list has a real hacking plan.

Good when:

- projected repeater coverage matters;
- Hackable targets are likely;
- Spotlight / control states are valuable;
- another repeater angle materially changes enemy movement.

Do not take it merely to satisfy a vague idea that every list needs a Hacker.

---

### Spitfire / combat Zulu

Useful as an independent Camo attack piece.

Take it when:

- the list already covers Sensor / Jammer;
- another medium-range attack vector is more useful than utility;
- you want a self-contained flanker that does not consume a Fireteam.

---

### X-Visor MMR / Minelayer

A lesser-used but strong flexible choice.

It combines:

- midfield gunfighting;
- range-band flexibility;
- Minelayer / area denial;
- marker-state deployment pressure.

This is particularly useful if the list already has a Sensor piece and wants another autonomous threat.

Use when:

## REMs

Vanilla PanO repeatedly benefits from cheap REM infrastructure.

### Fugazi

One of the closest things to a generic staple.

Use when:

- you need a cheap Regular Order;
- another Repeater improves the network;
- Flash Pulse / Mimetism contributes to defence;
- roster space remains available.

Multiple Fugazi appear repeatedly in sample and tournament lists.

Still, do not treat them as a sacred tax if the 15-model cap makes another cheap active piece more valuable.

---

### Pathfinder

Much more than a Sensor bot.

It can provide:

- Specialist / FO;
- Sensor;
- Repeater;
- Deactivator;
- fast movement;
- Tactical Awareness where the current profile provides it.

This makes it particularly valuable in elite lists where every pooled Regular Order is contested.

---

### Pilot-X + RacerBot Mk-III — hybrid REM support

**Pilot-X 21/1** is a **WIP13 Hacking Device (Zero Pain)** troop with RemDriver for one eligible **REM**, **not** for a TAG. A legal Pilot-X Team can add **one Mk-III for 29/1** or **two for 37/1**, each an 8-point Regular REM with **MOV20-10**, Deployable Repeater, PARA Mine Dispenser, Light Riotstopper and 360º Visor. The package supplies **two/three Regular Orders** if all are eligible/deployed, not one synchronized controller Order. Team members stay in the **same Combat Group**.

**Best when:** Vanilla wants a REM enhancement (e.g. Sierra), a defensive Hacker, and one/two independently positionable ground network/denial routes. Repeaters are carried, **not predeployed**; reaching cover and placing them costs Orders. **Why not automatic:** 1 SWC competes with attack guns, the parent has only one Wound, and 8-point Mk-III bots can be removed by hacking or ordinary attacks. A Fugazi, Pathfinder or Peacemaker is often a cheaper single-purpose solution. [Whole-game #4 Best mobile Repeater](../list-building/network-projection-repeaters-pitchers-apex-value.md#3-best-mobile-repeater); [hybrid force-multiplier tradeoff](../list-building/force-multipliers-special-abilities-apex-value.md).

**Profile distinction:** RacerBot **Mk-III** is Pilot-X-dependent; **standalone Mk-I and Mk-II**, where legal, are distinct orders/support profiles and do **not** inherit the Mk-III toolkit or the Pilot-X dependency. The OSS/ALEPH Pilot-X version **23/.5** is not the PanO **21/1** loadout.

### Peacemaker

A strong midfield pressure / repeater / template package.

Use when:

- you want to start projecting threat before spending Orders;
- you need another midfield obstacle;
- the opponent has vulnerable Hackable pieces;
- a disposable template / remote presence can create awkward ARO choices.

Two-Peacemaker architectures have appeared in concrete Vanilla sample lists.

---

### Bulleteer

Still one of PanO's best cheap asymmetric attackers.

Use when:

- the primary attacker is optimized for longer range;
- you need a cheaper secondary attack piece;
- Mimetism (-6) can create favourable FtF odds;
- an Engineer can support it efficiently.

#### Bulleteer + Blade Engineer support (unlinked)

A useful low-cost pairing, **not a current vanilla Duo**:

- mobile Mimetism (-6) attack pressure from the Bulleteer;
- specialist support and repair from the Engineer;
- compatible routes **without Fireteam movement compression**.

Each model uses its own activation; do not budget the Engineer's order as a free team move.

### Tikbalang LT(+1 Order)

A very strong command / order-engine architecture.

Use when:

- the Tik is expected to fight aggressively;
- the list has NCO elsewhere to convert special LT Orders when appropriate;
- CoC is protected separately if losing the Tik would be catastrophic.

The profile's strength is not simply that the Tik generates many Orders. It is that Vanilla can **route those Orders to different NCO pieces**.

---

### Cutter

The apex assassin.

Use when you want:

- Hidden Deployment / marker-state pressure;
- Mimetism (-6);
- ARM8 resilience;
- BS15 direct gunfighting;
- a model that heavily influences enemy deployment before revealing.

The Cutter is still fully viable, but it is an architecture rather than a default faction requirement.

---

### Joan LT + Cutter NCO

A particularly coherent Vanilla interaction.

Joan provides the command economy and the Cutter converts Lieutenant Orders through NCO.

The major warning is hacking concentration.

If Joan and Cutter are both caught in the same repeater / Oblivion network, the list can lose both its command and primary attack economy at once.

Therefore the list must include a way to:

- remove repeaters;
- kill enemy Hackers;
- attack the network from another angle;
- avoid putting both premium Hackable pieces into the same hacking footprint.

---

### Squalos Mk-II

A conventional but extremely efficient TAG family.

#### NCO MULTI HMG

Use when:

- the LT architecture generates useful special Orders;
- you want the TAG itself to convert them;
- you want a conventional long-range gun without making the TAG the Lieutenant.

#### HMG + Grenade Launcher

Use when indirect attack angles solve a real problem:

- prone support models;
- hidden Engineers / Doctors / Hackers;
- models protected by geometry rather than modifiers.

Do not make speculative fire the main plan.

#### Budget MMR Squalos

A very interesting lesser-used architecture.

Use when:

- the army already has a long-range gun;
- you want a durable Tactical Awareness TAG at lower cost;
- the saved points buy another specialist, defender or secondary attacker.

It is not the apex gunfighter profile. It is the **cheap armored anchor**.

---

### TAG selection heuristic

- **Maximum raw assassin:** Cutter
- **Best mobility / efficiency:** Tikbalang
- **Best NCO order engine:** Squalos Mk-II NCO
- **Budget armored centerpiece:** Squalos MMR

## Black A.I.R.

Black A.I.R. is a legitimate self-contained reactive piece.

The Neurocinetics MULTI Sniper gives:

- Mimetism;
- MSV2;
- strong long-range ARO pressure;
- no Fireteam slot requirement.

This matters in Vanilla because it competes with other guns and support slots, **not** with a nonexistent Duo/Haris slot.

Use Black A.I.R. when you want **one roster slot to provide dedicated lane control**.

---

## Bipandra

Bipandra is much more interesting in current N5 than her older-edition reputation suggests.

The important profiles combine:

- improved Doctor reliability;
- specialist value;
- Tactical Awareness on the relevant profile.

Use her when:

- the list contains expensive infantry worth recovering;
- Palbot projection gives the Doctor useful reach;
- Tactical Awareness effectively funds movement / a recovery attempt;
- a generic Trauma-Doc would save points but materially reduce reliability.

She is particularly attractive in elite Joan / Swiss / HI-heavy lists.

---

## Teutonic Specialist

A strong inexpensive active mission piece.

Use it for jobs that your 40–100 point centerpiece should not waste Orders doing:

- objective interaction;
- clearing mines / deployables;
- Panzerfaust pressure;
- close combat against vulnerable targets;
- absorbing one or two enemy Orders;
- late-game movement toward scoring areas.

This kind of cheap 2W utility repeatedly appears in successful lists because it fills the gap between passive cheap Orders and premium attack pieces.

---

## Blocker

Current Blockers are much more than generic WIP13 Hackers.

Relevant utility includes:

- Sensor;
- X-Visor;
- Discover support;
- Hacker / Firewall options;
- Pitcher / specialist profiles.

Use when you want PanO to play an actual information / hacking game rather than simply fielding a token Hacker.

They are especially useful when:

- the list contains Hackable premium models that need defence;
- marker control is important;
- projected repeater coverage helps your midfield plan.

---

## Locust Hacker

The current Locust gives Vanilla a very different hacking vector from a DZ Hacker.

Its strengths include:

- Hidden Deployment;
- Mimetism (-6);
- Forward Deployment;
- Stealth;
- Pitcher / hacking access.

Use when:

- you want the hacking threat to begin closer to midfield;
- surprise / hidden information matters;
- the opponent's repeater / Hackable structure can be attacked from an unexpected angle.

Its higher cost means it should be selected for a deliberate mission / matchup role rather than as generic specialist filler.

---

## Redeye

Redeye is a legitimate underused mobility / geometry piece.

Its appeal is the combination of:

- strong BS;
- Mimetism (-6);
- heavy armour;
- Sensor;
- Super-Jump;
- useful Spitfire / Blitzen / grenade options.

Use when:

- table geometry rewards vertical or lateral movement;
- you need a medium-range attack piece that can reach unusual angles;
- Sensor contributes to the matchup;
- the list can protect it from hacking.

Do not take it merely as another gunfighter. Its value is **mobility + geometry + Sensor**.

---

## Aquila Guard

Aquila is a strong meta call rather than a generic staple.

MSV3 fundamentally changes how it interacts with markers and visual modifiers.

The FTO-labelled / specialist / Disco Baller profiles can give an **independently activated** model more than pure shooting; FTO alone grants no vanilla Fireteam permission.

The Strategos LT profile is particularly interesting because it combines:

- premium MSV gunfighting;
- command utility;
- specialist value;
- Strategos.

Use Aquila when:

- marker-heavy opponents are common;
- MSV3 materially changes the matchup;
- you want the Lieutenant to contribute real combat value;
- the list can tolerate concentrating more points in the command model.

## Unlinked support architectures — formerly called Fireteams in older sources

**N5.3 legality:** no standard vanilla Fireteam chart. All of the following are **independent models**, not Duos/Haris. Their value is complementary tools, not shared Orders, Fireteam bonuses or a dedicated link slot.

### Karhu Feuerbach supported by Karhu Engineer

Take both when the long-range AP/DA threat, specialist recovery and two NCO options are worth the price. Their compatible movement routes help an Engineer stay in repair range, but **they activate separately**.

### Bolt sniper supported by a utility Bolt

A **Bolt sniper + utility Bolt** only makes sense if MSV pressure and the Hacker/CoC role are both needed. Do not buy a second model just to imitate an obsolete Bolt Duo; other ARO options include:

- **Black A.I.R.**;
- **Swiss Guard**;
- **Karhu**;
- **Helot**.

### Bulleteer supported by Blade Engineer

The fast Mimetism (-6) attacker benefits from a nearby Engineer to recover it after a failed run. **No shared movement Orders**: budget the attack and subsequent support separately.

### Joan command ecosystem — not a Haris

Joan can anchor a distributed package of cheap Regularized Irregular troops (subject to Inspiring Leadership's actual restrictions), a protected CoC alternative and a separate NCO Order outlet. Keep successors **away from the attack footprint**. A historical Joan Haris is not validated in the pinned current vanilla roster.

## Lieutenant architectures

Vanilla PanO has several genuinely different LT plans.

### Joan LT

Use when:

- Inspiring Leadership is part of the list economy;
- Joan is intended to participate actively;
- NCO / CoC infrastructure can exploit and protect the architecture.

---

### Magistrate Strategos LT

Use when:

- the list wants a relatively cheap command model;
- you want Strategos and Counterintelligence;
- the LT itself does not need to be a premium attacker.

This is one of the best generic default choices.

---

### Tikbalang LT(+1 Order)

Use when:

- Tik is the primary active engine;
- NCO elsewhere can convert the LT Orders;
- CoC exists if needed;
- the list is comfortable with an aggressive Hackable LT.

---

### Zulu LT

Use when:

- Zulu utility is already wanted;
- the LT can remain conservative until needed;

Tradeoff:

### Aquila Strategos LT

Use when:

- you want the Lieutenant to fight;

---

### Cutter LT

Use only when the list deliberately accepts the concentration of:

- command;
- roughly one-third of the army;
- primary attack capability

in one model.

The model is difficult to kill conventionally, but command failure becomes catastrophic if it is disabled or isolated.

Still useful when:

- the LT simply needs to remain hidden;

Do not default to it purely from old PanO habit. Magistrate, Joan, Zulu and Tik offer much more interesting architectures.

## Common listbuilding traps

### Too many premium guns

Taking all of these premium pieces can starve the army of Orders, specialists and midfield coverage:

- **Swiss Guard**;
- **Cutter**;
- **Karhu Feuerbach**;
- **Black A.I.R.**.

The question for every additional gun should be:

> **What problem does this solve that the existing attacker cannot?**

If the answer is only “it shoots well,” reconsider it.

---

### Too much ARO investment

Vanilla has many attractive defensive pieces:

- Black A.I.R.;
- Bolt sniper;
- Swiss ML;
- Helots;
- Sierra;
- mines / deployables.

Take enough to tax the opponent, then stop.

A list that spends too heavily on reactive pieces may successfully survive Turn 1 and then lack enough proactive tools to win the mission.

---

### TAG / HI stacking without hacking protection

This is one of the most important practical warnings.

If several expensive Hackable pieces share the same repeater footprint, one enemy hacking network can disable the command piece and primary attacker at once.

Build a plan to:

- remove repeaters;
- attack enemy Hackers;
- project your own hacking defence;
- route around the hacking area;
- keep critical pieces physically separated when necessary.

---

### Confusing historical Fireteams with current vanilla support synergies

The current generic PanO N5.3 roster has **no standard Fireteams**. Older videos describe packages that can still offer complementary roles, but **not** shared movement or Fireteam bonuses:

- **Karhu Duo**;
- **Bulleteer + Blade Duo**;
- **Joan Haris**.

Budget **independent Orders** for those units. An actual Fireteam requires the corresponding sectorial chart.

## Practical all-comers construction sequence

A useful generic build order is:

1. choose the **Lieutenant / NCO / CoC architecture**;
2. choose one **primary attack engine**;
3. add one **qualitatively different secondary threat**;
4. add **midfield control**;
5. add **cheap Regular infrastructure**;
6. add the appropriate **Engineer / Doctor / Hacker** support;
7. add one **reactive tax piece** if needed;
8. position independent gunfighter / specialist / Engineer support packages so their separate activations are efficient (**no standard vanilla Fireteams**);
9. spend remaining points on mission coverage rather than another redundant gun.

A common shell can look like:

> **2 cheap REMs + Joan/Magistrate command package + Swiss/TAG + Zulu/Peacemaker + Karhu/support package + one inexpensive defensive piece**

The exact units should vary by mission.

## Battle-report-derived army skeletons — no vanilla Fireteams (2026-11-03)

**Hard rule:** the [current vanilla PanO reference](../../sectorials/panoceania/panoceania.md#fireteams) has **no standard Duo, Haris or Core**. Historical reports sometimes label pairs or Harises in vanilla: document their useful **unit choices**, but **do not put historical link bonuses or shared activation in a current N5.3 list**.

- **Independent Joan command and counterattack shell — [#169 Borderbash champion](../battle-reports/161-170-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md):** the player used **Joan v2 Lieutenant**, **two Helot FTOs** (Red Fury and SMG/LRL), **Karhu Feuerbach**, **Karhu Paramedic**, **Swiss Guard HMG Hidden Deployment**, **Banshee KHD**, **Magistrate CoC**, **two Fugazi**, **Teutonic Specialist**, **Motorized Bounty Hunter** and inexpensive specialist/support pieces. The winner called **Joan + two Helots a Haris** and **two Karhu a Duo** *under the historical rules*. **Neither is a legal current vanilla link**. Translate the strategy into **Inspiring Leadership/CoC command**, **independent Helot AROs**, **Swiss solo first strike** and **Karhu scorer/long gun each paid its own Orders**.
- **Hidden Cutter with ordinary mission infantry — [#143 Corporate Appropriation](../battle-reports/141-150-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md):** the game fielded **Cutter Hidden Deployment**, **Croc Man**, **Fugazi/cheap REMs** and mission support; **no Fireteam was declared**. PanO won **5–4**, but the Cutter struggled against a difficult enemy gun. The practical list rule is **buy enough independent specialists/defenders to finish after the TAG encounters a bad matchup**.
- **Three-TAG extreme skew — [#214 Firefight](../battle-reports/211-220-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md):** the spoken roster used **Cutter + Squalos Mk-II + Tikbalang Lieutenant** together, supported by **Pathfinder TacAware, Machinist, Flash REMs and Baggage**. These are **three separate TAGs, not a TAG Fireteam**. In a current list, audit the **Lt risk, hacking exposure and number of Regular scoring bodies** before imitating this intentionally unusual army. It is a useful **stress test**, not a reliable default.
- **Jotum/forward-specialist example — [#192 The Dig](../battle-reports/191-200-n5-battle-report-tactical-analysis.md):** the roster used **Jotum**, **two Vargs**, **Shona**, **Liang Kai**, **Locust KHD**, **Pathfinder** and support. The video described the Vargs as linked; **that observation is not a current vanilla Fireteam permission**. Keep the lessons about independent midfield assault and late console switching, but spend separate Orders or choose **SWF** to field an actual Varg Fireteam.

**Practical N5.3 list start:** select **one principal TAG or Swiss Guard**, **one independent midfield specialist/denial route**, **one protected LT/CoC and/or NCO economy**, **real Engineer/repair and cheap Regular Orders**. The #214 triple-TAG and #169 old-linked Joan variants are **different experiments**, not members of one combined “winning list.” No list in this section should be entered as a Core/Haris/Duo in a generic PanO roster.

## Source-derived consensus and disagreements

### Strong consensus

Current sample lists and community material repeatedly support:

- cheap REM infrastructure;
- Joan as a major command architecture;
- Karhu as one of Vanilla's best small-team / NCO pieces;
- Swiss as a premier independent attacker;
- Zulu / Peacemaker-style midfield pressure;
- TAGs as optional architectures rather than mandatory faction identity;
- limiting expensive models that compete for the same Orders.

### More player-dependent choices

There is less consensus on:

- Cutter vs Tik vs Squalos;
- Black A.I.R. vs Bolt / Swiss reactive pieces;
- how heavily to invest in hacking;
- whether Joan economy is better than Strategos / TAG-LT architectures;
- how many cheap REMs to take before the 15-model cap makes another utility piece better;
- whether formerly linked packages remain worth buying as **separate, non-Fireteam** pieces.

This is important because Vanilla's strength is **choice breadth**, not one solved shell.

---

## Bottom line

Vanilla PanO is strongest when it cherry-picks the best concepts from several PanO subfactions without importing their restrictions.

Its current identity is best summarized as:

> **premium attack piece + asymmetric secondary threat + excellent cheap REM infrastructure + complementary unlinked support pieces + unusually strong LT/NCO/CoC architecture**

The most useful default considerations are:

> **Joan / Magistrate command package, Swiss or TAG primary attacker, Zulu / Peacemaker midfield control, Karhu support, Fugazi infrastructure, and one inexpensive reactive tax piece.**

Do not build Vanilla by asking “what are the strongest PanO profiles?”

Build it by asking:

> **Which strong PanO profiles solve different problems while sharing the same Order economy efficiently?**
