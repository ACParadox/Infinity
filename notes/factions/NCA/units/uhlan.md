# Clausewitz Uhlans — Neoterran Capitaline Army (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: S-tier Feuerbach (70/1.5), S+ value; A− Spitfire/HRL (80/1.5). Must-own, not must-field. Prefer the Feuerbach almost exclusively unless deliberately building a forward assault TAG.** The Uhlan is an independent Camouflage hunter-killer, not a static gun platform. An apex TAG in its own right: exceptional active anti-armour lethality, S-tier defensive concealment and reactive deterrence, and outstanding value. One of the strongest reasons to play NCA.

**Authority:** [Pinned NCA roster](../../../../sectorials/panoceania/neoterran-capitaline-army.md#clausewitz-uhlans) · [NCA listbuilding](../nca-listbuilding.md) · [NCA transcript research](../nca-references.md) · [Weapon chart](https://infinitythewiki.com/Weapons_Chart). Reviewed 23 November 2026. Ratings are tactical judgments.

## Profiles

AVA2, Regular TAG, MOV 6-4", CC15, BS15, PH15, WIP12, ARM6, BTS6, STR3, S6. Camouflage, Mimetism (-3), Surprise Attack (-3), Remote Presence, Courage, Tactical Awareness, Warhorse, BS Attack (SR-1), ECM: Guided (-6), Dodge (PH=11), GizmoKit (PH=13). No Fireteam, NCO, Lieutenant or Hidden Deployment option. Both profiles have AP CCW and Crabbot.

| Opt | Pts/SWC | Weapons and modifiers | Rating |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 2 | **70/1.5** | Feuerbach, Panzerfaust, Blitzen, MULTI Pistol; BS Attack (+1B) | **S** |
| 1 | 80/1.5 | Spitfire, Heavy Rocket Launcher (+1B); BS Attack (AP) | A− |

**Profile correction:** Both variants have native BS Attack (SR-1), in addition to their option-specific AP or +1B. The abbreviated pinned roster omits SR-1; [Corvus Belli's October 2025 update](https://infinityuniverse.com/en/news/army-update-october-25) explicitly added it alongside Warhorse, and the [current Human Sphere profile](https://human-sphere.com/Uhlan) confirms both and the fixed Dodge/GizmoKit values. SR-1 penalizes enemy Saving Rolls, not their Face-to-Face rolls; effective PS is a convenient shorthand for the saving-roll calculation.

The 70-point profile is only four points more than the Squalos MULTI HMG NCO. It buys a fundamentally different attack mechanism: marker protection, Surprise Attack, persistent Mimetism, long-range anti-armour fire and Isolation immunity.

## Feuerbach — preferred hunter

| Weapon/mode | Active B | ARO B | Ammo | Base PS | Effective PS (SR-1) | Positive range |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| Feuerbach Burst | 3 | 1 | AP+DA | 6 | 5 | 16–32" |
| Feuerbach alternate | 2 | 1 | EXP | 6 | 5 | 16–32" |
| Panzerfaust | 2 | 1 | AP+EXP | 6 | 5 | 16–32" |
| Blitzen | 2 | 1 | E/M | 6 | 5 | 16–32" |
| MULTI Pistol | 3 | 1 | AP or Shock | 7 | 6 | 0–8" |
| MULTI Pistol anti-materiel | 2 | 1 | DA | 7 | 6 | 0–8" |

- The Feuerbach retains a 0 range MOD at 32–48", where an ordinary HMG suffers −3. Its B3 AP+DA mode forces two saves against half ARM per ordinary hit; excellent against expensive HI and TAGs, though lower Burst than an HMG. SR-1 further increases lethality on every required save.
- Panzerfaust offers AP+EXP for a decisive shot; Blitzen disables vulnerable targets through E/M. Native BS Attack (+1B) is mandatory in the Active Turn when applicable: both shots consume both standard Disposable uses. Do not count multiple B2 attacks without reloading or remaining ammunition.
- The pistol provides genuine close-range flexibility but does not replace a direct template or CC deterrent.
- +1B is **active-turn only**; ordinary reactive shooting is B1, unlike +1SD which also applies reactively. The Feuerbach chart's alternate mode does not list an Impact Template trait; do not assume it behaves like a missile blast template.
- No weapon on this profile has Suppressive Fire. End attack runs in Camouflage or a protected position rather than planning suppression.

### Reactive weapon package — S-tier deterrence

**The Feuerbach and disposable weapons are particularly efficient in ARO.** HMGs also drop to B1 in ARO, so the Feuerbach no longer suffers a Burst disadvantage while retaining far more lethal ammunition. The Panzerfaust and Blitzen are best viewed principally as reactive deterrents, with situational active-turn uses; this is a tactical interpretation, not a claim about designer intent.

| ARO choice | Saves per ordinary hit | Preferred use |
|---|---|---|
| Feuerbach EXP | 3 ARM | Light/medium infantry, targets where three saves are preferable |
| Feuerbach AP+DA | 2 half-ARM | Armoured threats, sustained reactive anti-armour fire |
| Panzerfaust AP+EXP | 3 half-ARM | Premium attackers within favorable range; maximum anti-armour deterrence |
| Blitzen E/M | 2 half-BTS | Disable HI/TAG/REM or isolate valuable targets where applicable |
| MULTI Pistol | 1 AP/Shock or 2 DA | Emergency 0–8" ARO |

All benefit from SR-1 on applicable saves. Choose ammunition according to the target's ARM/BTS, immunities, cover and desired result. E/M can impose Immobilized-B on applicable troops and Isolation on nonimmune targets; Warhorse blocks Isolation, not necessarily the other state. The Feuerbach has a 0 range MOD at 32–48", whereas the Panzerfaust/Blitzen and conventional HMG have −3.

In the active turn, the B3 Feuerbach generally offers better sustained efficiency than the B2 disposable guns, each of which would consume its two standard uses in one attack. In ARO, each is B1 and spends only one use: potentially two Panzerfaust and two Blitzen AROs across the game. Do not expend them automatically against cheap targets or unfavorable odds. Use a disposable actively if a decisive E/M or AP+EXP attack is worth the ammunition.

The Uhlan is not a permanent exposed ARO tower. Its ideal defensive posture is a protected Camouflage marker with the option to reveal into a favorable engagement. Surprise Attack is active-turn only, and revealing in ARO gives up the marker protection; choose carefully.

### Example of asymmetric attack

Against a conventional BS14 HMG without MSV at 32–48", both sides in partial cover and no other MODs:

- Uhlan opening from Camouflage: B3 on 12 (BS15, range 0, cover −3).
- Reactive opponent: B1 on 2 (BS14, range −3, cover −3, Mimetism −3, Surprise Attack −3).
- After revealing, opponent instead shoots on 5 in the same conditions; Surprise Attack is not persistent.

These are illustrative target numbers, not universal face-to-face probabilities. Enemy MSV, Combat Instinct, Sixth Sense, different weapon ranges and other MODs can change the result.

### Practical use: attack, retreat, re-camouflage

1. Deploy as a Camouflage marker; unlike Hidden Deployment it can contribute its Regular Order while deployed and eligible. S6 often makes its identity obvious, but the marker protection still matters.
2. Select a favorable lane and exploit Surprise Attack on the opening engagement, ideally using range asymmetry. Avoid exposing multiple enemy AROs simultaneously.
3. Use the Feuerbach for repeated attacks or select Panzerfaust/Blitzen when the target justifies the limited ammunition.
4. Budget movement and an Order to re-enter Camouflaged State when requirements are met (including being outside enemy LoF and relevant Sensor restrictions). Do not assume a TAG can use Cautious Movement.
5. Keep a repair route and layered defensive coverage. Preserve the TAG rather than expose it to the entire opposing active turn.

**The attack–retreat–re-camouflage cycle is the defining strategy, not a minor convenience.** A fresh marker protects the Uhlan during the opponent's turn and restores eligibility for Surprise Attack (-3) on its next active reveal. Tactical Awareness helps fund the cycle. Even when the S6 marker's identity is obvious, opponents normally cannot simply shoot it: they must Discover it first, use a legal Discover + BS Attack sequence, or employ a counter such as MSV3 or Sensor. A failed Discover can waste enemy Orders. Indirect template effects, intuitive attacks where legal, and marker-state counters still exist. Do not confuse Camouflage with Hidden Deployment or absolute immunity.

Once revealed, the enemy still faces Mimetism (-3), ARM6 (ARM9 in partial cover against normal ammunition where applicable), three STR, dangerous reactive ammunition, Warhorse and potential repairs. Four defensive layers: **avoidance (marker), evasion (Mimetism), deterrence (reactive guns), resilience (armour/STR/Warhorse/repair).** Overall survivability is S-tier even though raw physical durability alone is only A-tier. Do not leave it needlessly exposed just because it has a good ARO.

## Spitfire / Heavy Rocket Launcher — forward assault

| Weapon/mode | Active B | Ammo/traits | PS | Positive range |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| Spitfire | 4 | AP, Suppressive Fire | 6 | 8–24" |
| HRL hit | 3 | AP, Continuous Damage | 5 | 16–32" |
| HRL blast | 3 | AP, Continuous Damage, circular impact template | 6 | 16–32" |

Native BS Attack (AP) applies to both guns; shared SR-1 also applies to their relevant Saving Rolls; the HRL weapon-specific +1B makes it B3 in the active turn. Continuous Damage can inflict additional damage after failed saves. The HRL blast mode gives a genuine anti-cluster option, unlike the Feuerbach.

Best when the mission rewards advancing and occupying midfield space, engaging clustered Fireteams and using Spitfire Suppressive Fire. Camouflage and Suppressive Fire are alternative end states, not simultaneous protection.

**Why usually skip:** +10 points, shorter primary range, no disposable E/M or AP+EXP options, and greater exposure to hacking, mines and melee. It lacks a direct template, close-combat protection and native Firewall. The Feuerbach can often avoid those risks by staying farther away. Strong profile, but narrower value proposition.

## Warhorse, durability and vulnerabilities

Warhorse prevents Isolation (including Oblivion and the Isolated component of E/M), keeps the Uhlan Regular in Loss of Lieutenant and Retreat, and ignores enemy BS Attack (-X) special-skill modifiers. It does not ignore Mimetism, range, cover or Surprise Attack simply by being Warhorse.

- Carbonite, Total Control, Spotlight, Immobilization from E/M and ordinary damage remain relevant. Warhorse is not hacking immunity.
- Camouflage and range management are the main protection against enemy repeaters; no native Stealth or Firewall is listed.
- Remote Presence supplies additional Unconscious depth and Engineer reroll options; ARM6/BTS6/STR3 is a resilient but repair-dependent chassis.
- S6 cannot go Prone or declare Cautious Movement; terrain, narrow passages and hiding space are meaningful limitations compared with S2 Swiss Guard.
- **Primary weaknesses: dedicated CC specialists and close-range assault.** CC15, no Martial Arts/CC defensive MOD, no mines or direct template on either profile; the Feuerbach version also lacks Suppressive Fire. At 0–8" its main gun and disposable guns are at −3; at 8–16" they are neutral. The B3 AP/Shock or B2 DA MULTI Pistol at +3 within 8" is a credible emergency weapon, not a reason to advance.
- Direct templates bypass Mimetism; cheap close-range attackers can force unfavorable trades. The Feuerbach wants 16–48" engagements, ideally with screened approach routes. MSV reduces Mimetism; MSV3 and Sensor can counter marker protection. High-Burst attackers and concentrated pressure remain dangerous.
- Unlike the Tikbalang, it lacks Climbing Plus, terrain mobility, flamethrower and mines. Avoid entering cramped midfield spaces without a specific mission or target justification. Use inexpensive Auxilia or Peacemaker screens, mines from other troops, a counter-CC piece and hacking support.
- No native specialist on the TAG chassis; Crabbot can help with appropriate mission tasks but is not a substitute for multiple mobile specialists.

### Cutter — absolute apex, but 27 points more

The Cutter is at the top end of conventional gunfighting: Mimetism (-6), ARM8, B4, SR-1 and reusable Camouflage mean few opponents can comfortably engage it. Hidden Deployment is its other genuinely distinctive advantage: the enemy cannot see its position during deployment, must plan around an unknown threat, and may face a surprise reactive reveal if the Cutter goes second. It can also remain hidden until its own attack run. Hidden Deployment is not the same thing as Surprise Attack in ARO. The Cutter LT alternative is 95/2.5.

The Uhlan shares Camouflage and Surprise Attack but loses 3 points of Mimetism, 2 ARM, Hidden Deployment and one active Burst. In return it gains Warhorse, Tactical Awareness (an extra generated personal Order, rather than NCO reallocating a Lieutenant Order), the smaller S6 silhouette, AP+DA, better long-range bands, versatile disposable weapons and **27 points plus 0.5 SWC of savings** against the NCO Cutter. Cutter retains Suppressive Fire. The Uhlan can fund a Blade-Ops Engineer (23) and still cost only 93 points: two Regular and two Tactical Awareness Orders, versus one Regular and access to an existing LT Order on the Cutter.

Both have excellent reactive guns: the Cutter's B1 EXP MULTI HMG and the Uhlan's B1 EXP or AP+DA Feuerbach. The Uhlan can additionally select AP+EXP Panzerfaust or E/M Blitzen. Its raw physical durability is inferior but its overall defensive package remains outstanding. Prefer Uhlan on value if hypothetically choosing between them at current prices; Cutter is preferable if absolute gunfighting dominance and hidden deployment uncertainty are the central strategy.

### Tikbalang — similarly priced, mobile assault alternative

For just one extra point over the excellent 69-point standard Tikbalang, the Uhlan gains Camouflage, repeatable Surprise Attack, Warhorse, B3 AP+DA instead of normal HMG ammo, better extreme range, and Panzerfaust/Blitzen. These are major gains against premium threats. The Tikbalang instead has Climbing Plus for bypassing defended angles and finding shots from elevation, Terrain (Total), a B4 HMG for ordinary targets, flamethrower/mine defense, Suppressive Fire and ARMORED Duo options. Its +1 WIP and +2 CC are minor secondary differences. The Tikbalang is not strictly inferior: terrain-rich tables and forward assault missions make its toolkit very attractive. Nevertheless, the Uhlan's repeatable marker protection and anti-heavy lethality give it the edge for a general long-range hunter.
