### Warcor — Kestrel Colonial Force (Infinity N5.3)

**Verdict: Recommended cheap defensive filler; not a must-have. Prefer the 3-point 360º Visor profile almost universally.** Warcor is a very inexpensive **WIP13 Flash Pulse ARO** and deployment-zone flank/rear sentry. It is not a true cheerleader: its **Irregular Order stays with the Warcor**, so do not mistake its 3-point cost for a discount Regular Order.

#### Pinned Kestrel N5.3 profile

**Warcors: AVA 1 · Irregular LI · no Fireteam access.** MOV 10-10 cm (4-4"), CC11, BS9, PH11, **WIP13**, ARM0, BTS0, VITA1, S2. **Journalist**.

| Profile | Points / SWC | Weapons | Relevant ability |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **360º Visor — preferred** | **3 / 0** | Flash Pulse, Stun Pistol, PARA CCW (-3) | Genuine 360º LoF arc for normal movement/attack ARO opportunities |
| **Sixth Sense — niche** | **3 / 0** | Same | Responds to *attacks targeting the Warcor* from outside its LoF; negates Stealth against it; other defensive benefits |

The profile abilities are **alternatives**: the Sixth Sense Warcor does *not* also have a 360º Visor. Both are AVA1 options and neither is a Specialist.

#### 360º Visor versus Sixth Sense

**Take the 360º Visor for the intended job: guarding a deployment-zone edge, rear access route or flank.** It provides an actual 360º LoF arc instead of the normal 180º, subject to terrain, visibility, and the need for real line of fire. A Parachutist, Combat Jump attacker or flanker *moving* behind it can therefore provoke a Flash Pulse ARO when visible, rather than only once that enemy attacks.

**Sixth Sense is narrower but does something different:**

- It allows a response to **Attacks directed at the Warcor** by enemies outside its LoF. It does **not** give blanket 360º LoF against ordinary enemy movement behind it.
- Enemy **Stealth is ineffective** against it, which can help against nearby troops attempting to move through its Zone of Control without giving normal AROs.
- When targeted by a BS Attack through a **Zero Visibility Zone**, it ignores the associated -6 Poor Visibility MOD.
- Dodge or Reset declarations ignore negative MODs except the specific Immobilized/Isolated exceptions in the rule.
- **N5 correction:** Sixth Sense does **not** ignore Surprise Attack penalties; **Combat Instinct** does that now.

Sixth Sense may be justified if you expect Stealth close-range attacks, attacks from outside LoF, or visibility-based attacks against the Warcor, and the model's position already covers the approach it needs to cover. This is usually worse than the reliable extra ARO arc provided by 360º Visor.

**Selection rule:** **360º by default; Sixth Sense only to answer a specific expected matchup.**

#### The Flash Pulse: why a 3-point Warcor can matter

Flash Pulse is a **Burst 1, WIP-based, BTS-targeting Stun** attack (PS7), rather than a BS9 gun attack. At WIP13:

| Range | MOD | Target number before opposing MODs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0–8" | 0 | 13 |
| 8–24" | +3 | **16** |
| 24–48" | -3 | 10 |
| 48–96" | -6 | 7 |

If a Flash Pulse connects and the opponent fails the **BTS Saving Roll**, that trooper becomes **Stunned**: it cannot declare Attacks and has -3 on other Rolls except Saving Rolls. This can interrupt an expensive attacker, including a TAG, but it **does not retroactively invalidate the action that inflicted the state**. Stunned is not lasting lockdown: it automatically cancels at the start of the States Phase of the Player Turn in which it was caused (and Doctor/Engineer may clear it sooner, as applicable).

The Warcor does not reliably win fights against a serious multi-Burst attacker. Its value is **threatening a potentially costly nonlethal result, forcing route changes or spending enemy Orders to clear a 3-point model**. Treat the Warcor as an ARO nuisance or speedbump, *not* as the primary defence of a TAG or Lieutenant.

#### Best Kestrel uses

##### 1. Deployment-zone rear and flank coverage — best role

Place the **360º** Warcor behind or beside your main units, preferably partially obscured, with legitimate sightlines to a side/rear arrival route. The visor lets the model watch multiple directions without presenting its back as a blind arc. It can punish attackers moving toward exposed TAG support, an Engineer, a Lieutenant or a backline specialist.

**Do not put it on the most exposed roof simply because it costs only 3 points.** A Warcor that hands the opponent an easy long-range kill may produce zero meaningful delay. Narrow lanes and offsets from your main ARO pieces are more productive.

##### 2. Independent secondary ARO / order tax — strong

Use an **alternate angle** alongside the Sierra TRbot, a Black A.I.R. Neurocinetics sniper, a Blink sniper or a defensive Fireteam. The aim is to make the opponent solve more than one ARO geometry, not to compete with those real damage pieces.

A Warcor that demands even one or two extra enemy Orders can repay its tiny point cost. Conversely, opponents may simply ignore it or win the Face-to-Face roll. Do not assume automatic order-tax value on an open table.

##### 3. One cheap expendable body — situational

The Warcor can spend **its own Irregular Order** to reposition, attempt Discover, or deliberately take a risky path to trigger an enemy mine and clear space for a more important trooper. It can also be used to probe routes and draw out reactions.

This is an opportunistic backup task, not the reason to include it. Do not sacrifice the Warcor when its ongoing rear-coverage ARO is more valuable.

##### 4. Journalist support — minor bonus

Under the current N5.3 wiki rule, **allied troopers inside the Warcor's Zone of Control get +3 to Guts Rolls; enemy troopers inside its ZoC suffer -3 to theirs**. This can slightly help nearby defensive troops hold ground or force enemy troops to fall back. It is usually irrelevant to units with **Courage**, such as a Sierra or Tikbalang, when they choose their automatic Guts result.

Useful if it comes free with the intended positioning; **not** a reason to expose the Warcor or build around it.

#### Combat Group choice

**Group 2 is a natural place for Warcor when Group 1 contains the main attack engine.** Since the Warcor is Irregular, it does not contribute a pooled Regular Order to either group. It can watch the deployment zone and use its personal Order for a small utility move without reducing Group 1's usable pooled Orders.

However, **the Warcor still occupies a Combat Group slot and an army-list trooper slot**. Group 1 is perfectly acceptable if there is a free slot; placing it in Group 2 does not inherently make its ARO better. Do not bump a valuable Regular troop out of the main group to fit it.

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Main attack group is full; auxiliary group has space | **Group 2** |
| Group 1 has an unused slot and you want Warcor's defensive coverage | **Group 1 is fine** |
| Group 2 needs pooled Orders for a specialist or another active unit | Warcor does **not** supply one |
| Both groups or list cap are constrained | **Skip Warcor** rather than compromise the order engine |

#### Warcor versus Kestrel's other cheap picks

| Unit | Cost | Order | What it buys instead |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Warcor (360º)** | **3 / 0** | **Irregular** | Cheapest wide-arc WIP13 Flash Pulse ARO; Journalist |
| **Fugazi** | **7 / 0** | **Regular** | WIP13 Flash Pulse, Mimetism (-3), mobile Repeater and 6-6" movement |
| **Basic Mulebot** | **8 / 0** | **Regular** | Baggage, Repeater, increased durability, pooled Order |
| **Jackal FTO** | **6 / 0** | **Irregular** | Lethal weapon/Booty and COLCORP Fireteam access; not a 360º sentry |
| **Tech-Bee** | **9 / 0** | **Regular** | Specialist Operative, Flash Pulse, deployable Repeater and TAGCom support |

**Warcor versus Fugazi is the key tradeoff.** For four extra points, Fugazi provides a **Regular Order**, a Repeater, fast movement and Mimetism (-3). That is usually a much stronger general-purpose purchase if there is room in the budget and combat group. But Fugazi has no 360º Visor and costs more than twice the Warcor's points: Warcor retains a strong niche as a 3-point rear sentry.

In TAG-heavy Kestrel lists, also prioritize the **Tech-Bee** when its TAGCom and Regular Order are relevant; Warcor is a cheap complementary defence, not an alternative TAG-support specialist.

#### When to take / when to skip

**Take it when:**
- **Exactly 3 points remain**, the list has a free trooper slot, and you have useful rear/flank sightlines.
- Your deployment zone is vulnerable to airborne arrival, rear assaults or flanking.
- Your serious ARO units have the front covered but lack cheap **360º side/rear coverage**.
- You want a disposable minor utility activation without diverting Regular Orders.

**Skip it when:**
- Spending **3–4 more points** gives you a useful **Regular Order** (especially Fugazi), a critical specialist upgrade, a mine or a necessary Fireteam member.
- Your list or Combat Group slots are scarce; an Irregular body does not fuel your best attacker.
- Deployment is too cramped or the board has no useful safe rear line of fire.
- You already have overlapping peripheral/minelayer/template coverage that makes this ARO redundant.
- You need a dependable attack stopper rather than a fragile B1 Flash Pulse nuisance.

#### Overall assessment

| Role | Rating |
|---|---|
| **360º Warcor: points-to-ARO efficiency** | **A** |
| **360º Warcor: DZ rear/flank lookout** | **A** |
| **Sixth Sense alternative** | **C / niche** |
| **Actual active-turn Order contribution** | **Poor — Irregular** |
| **Survivability or lethal stopping power** | **Poor** |
| **Default Kestrel inclusion** | **Recommended filler, not mandatory** |

**Final rule:** **Take the 3-point 360º Warcor when the list has spare points and space, particularly to watch the backline. Skip Sixth Sense unless solving an identified Stealth/visibility problem. Do not trade away a useful Regular Order or critical list function to fit it.**
