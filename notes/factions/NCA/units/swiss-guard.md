# Swiss Guard — Neoterran Capitaline Army (N5.3)

**S-tier, faction-defining; must-own, not must-field. Prioritize the Missile Launcher (+1SD) over the HMG after reviewing the community evidence. The HMG is an outstanding dedicated active gunfighter; the KHD is an elite, resilient midfield mission specialist rather than a cost-efficient dedicated hacker.** Normally take zero or one Swiss; two are a deliberate premium-threat architecture.

**Sources:** [Pinned NCA roster](../../../../sectorials/panoceania/neoterran-capitaline-army.md#swiss-guard), [NCA listbuilding](../nca-listbuilding.md), [NCA references and transcript research](../nca-references.md), [Hidden Deployment](https://infinitythewiki.com/Hidden_Deployment), [Camouflaged State](https://infinitythewiki.com/Camouflaged_State), [Surprise Attack](https://infinitythewiki.com/Surprise_Attack), [Firewall](https://infinitythewiki.com/Firewall), [Cybermask](https://infinitythewiki.com/Cybermask). Ratings are local tactical judgments; community opinions and observed play are distinguished below.

## HMG versus Missile Launcher

| Capability | HMG 56/1.5 | ML 60/1.5 |
|---|---|---|
| Active-turn shooting | Burst 4, repeatable attacks | B1 +1SD, selective high-impact attacks |
| Reactive-turn shooting | Normally B1 | Normally B1 +1SD |
| Preferred range | 16–32 inches | 24–40 inches |
| Anti-armour | Normal HMG ammunition | AP+EXP hit mode |
| Against clustered Fireteams | Single-target fire | Impact template mode |
| Sustained assault | Excellent | Less reliable |
| Reactive pressure | Good but not its main job | Exceptional |
| Uniqueness in NCA | Premium but has substitutes | Faction-defining |

The ML's +1SD adds a die to the roll, then one die is discarded; it is not an extra retained hit or Burst. It applies in both turns. Surprise Attack is an active-turn benefit, not a reactive modifier. Use the appropriate missile ammunition mode; template mode and AP+EXP hit mode are distinct.

The HMG is more reliable at clearing multiple enemy pieces through repeated engagements. The ML compensates for its lower active Burst with devastating consequences when a hit lands, particularly against expensive armoured units. Four extra points and identical SWC buy a major improvement in reactive utility without making the Swiss useless actively.

### HMG — attack, retreat, re-camouflage

- If going first, consider deploying as a Camouflage marker rather than Hidden Deployment. The marker already protects the unit and contributes its Regular Order to the shared pool.
- Choose favourable HMG-range engagements and exploit Surprise Attack on the first reveal; BS15 and Mimetism (-6) remain powerful thereafter.
- Budget Orders to retreat to a protected position and re-enter Camouflaged State when its requirements are met. Repeat the cycle on later turns, rather than exposing a 56-point model for an unnecessary ARO.
- Suppressive Fire is an alternative defensive end state when the geometry is appropriate; it is not simultaneously maintained with Camouflage.
- Use faster secondary attackers (Redeye, Bulleteer, Garuda) to exploit lanes the Swiss opens.

The HMG is a sound choice when the army genuinely needs sustained independent long-range firepower. Its main disadvantages are normal ammunition, slow movement, hacking exposure and no extra Orders.

### ML — hidden ARO and active counterattack

- Going second, Hidden Deployment creates uncertainty about the defended lanes. Reveal only when the opponent has committed to a favourable engagement, vulnerable target or clustered formation; a legal ARO is required.
- Do not automatically reveal against the first premium HMG. The value of an ambush is the ability to choose the engagement rather than simply being a visible gun.
- BS15, Mimetism (-6), +1SD and two wounds provide an unusually threatening combination of reactive accuracy, lethality and resilience.
- On the next active turn, use the ML to remove isolated long-range targets or heavily armoured threats. It is not merely a stationary defensive purchase.
- Going first, Camouflage deployment and selective active missile attacks are entirely valid. Re-camouflage or establish an appropriate reactive position afterwards.
- The Light Shotgun and Pulzar help cover the ML's poor close-range bands.

A Swiss ML is still a 60-point investment that can be bypassed by vision control, hacking or alternative routes. Hidden Deployment withholds its Order from the shared pool while it remains hidden at Order count; its own Order can be used to reveal in the active turn. Do not let the Swiss consume points without a meaningful active or reactive job.

Four video reviews and one podcast discussion all emphasize the ML over the HMG as the distinctive Swiss gunfighter; this is a small, qualitative sample, not a tournament fielding rate. **Revised priority:** the HMG is an apex active gun but the ML is the more unusual and broadly threatening purchase. Do not interpret this as requiring a Swiss in every list.

## KHD — 47/0: durable midfield assault specialist

**The KHD primarily helps the Swiss protect himself from enemy hackers while advancing and holding objectives. Buy the protected specialist body, not a 47-point backline Trinity platform.**

For three points over the 44-point AP SMG profile, the KHD adds specialist eligibility where the mission permits, Trinity and Cybermask. It retains BS15, ARM5, BTS6, two wounds, Camouflage, Hidden Deployment, Mimetism (-6), Stealth, Firewall (-3), FD (+10 cm), CC21 and Martial Arts L1.

| Enemy threat / mission need | Swiss response |
|---|---|
| Conventional gunfire | BS15, Mimetism (-6), ARM5, two wounds, cover when eligible |
| Hacking | BTS6, Firewall (-3), two wounds, Trinity against legal Hacker targets |
| Repeater coverage / movement | Marker states and Stealth where applicable; neither makes him universally immune to hacking |
| Close-range assault | AP SMG, Pulzar, Heavy Pistol |
| Longer range | Disposable Flammenspeer |
| Ordinary melee opponents | CC21, MA1, AP CCW |
| Objective scoring | Hacker specialist; durable model to retain objectives or contest zones |
| Defending after scoring | Re-camouflage or Suppressive Fire, layered support |

The Firewall imposes the applicable negative MOD on enemy Comms Attacks and adds +3 to the Swiss's relevant Saving Rolls (effectively BTS9 before other effects). Do not assume this stacks with every other Firewall. Mimetism does not modify hacking rolls. WIP13 is ordinary for an elite hacker: durability is excellent, offensive hacking efficiency is not.

### Practical use

1. Have a long-range attacker (Swiss ML, Uhlan, Squalos, etc.) clear the approach.
2. Advance the KHD from its slightly forward deployment, using Camouflage, Stealth or Cybermask as appropriate.
3. Remove vulnerable defenders with the AP SMG or Pulzar. Use Trinity to threaten enemy Hackers rather than spending the entire pool duelling an apex enemy hacker.
4. Score the objective, retain a carried objective if the mission permits, or contest a scoring zone.
5. Protect the Swiss with cheap screens, friendly Repeater coverage and appropriate marker or Suppressive Fire positioning.

Especially interesting for Supplies-style missions, zone control, and scenarios where a specialist needs to survive after completing a task. Check the actual ITS mission eligibility and scoring rules rather than assuming all missions reward this durability.

Cybermask provides an alternative marker state, not simultaneous Camouflage and Impersonation benefits. The Swiss cannot use normal HD control programs such as Spotlight, Carbonite or Oblivion with its KHD alone.

### Weaknesses and counters

- **Dedicated CC specialists** are the principal close-range counter: CC21/MA1 is credible against ordinary opponents but not equivalent to Shona, an Oniwaban or other apex melee troops. Screen approaches with Auxbots, mines and other inexpensive threats.
- E/M attacks, Oblivion and Carbonite can disable or isolate the Swiss without removing both wounds. Firewall and BTS do not make him immune.
- Direct templates bypass Mimetism; MSV reduces or negates its gunfighting advantage.
- The AP SMG lacks sustained long-range reach; the Flammenspeer has limited ammunition.
- No Tactical Awareness or Fireteam access and only 4-4 MOV. Avoid using 47 points as a passive objective guard if cheaper troops can do the same work.

### Comparison with cheaper KHDs

| Unit | Pts / SWC | Prefer when |
|---|---:|---|
| Locust KHD | 27/0 | Hidden forward hacker with FD (+20 cm), Pitcher, Firewall (-3), D-Charges and same WIP13 |
| Bolt KHD | 22/0 | BTS6, Fireteam access, E/M Mine, Marksmanship and cheap linked protection |
| Deva KHD | 20/0 | WIP15, NWI, Immunity (Isolated), efficient conventional network hacking |
| Hexa KHD | 27/0 | Hidden DZ counter-hacker with reusable Camouflage, E/Marat and Counterintelligence |
| Swiss KHD | 47/0 | Durable independent shooter, specialist and objective holder in the same body |

The Swiss does not buy better offensive WIP than the Locust. The additional 20–27 points buy substantial physical resilience, BS15, reusable Camouflage, and close-assault capabilities. If the only job is defending the network or removing enemy Hackers, choose a cheaper KHD.

### Community assessment of KHD

- WIP12 E201 (approximately 18–26 minutes): Trent likes the flexible weaponry, Firewall, marker states, defensive options and survivability. In a StarCo game he absorbed repeated hacking attacks from Uhahu but needed roughly six Orders to inflict one wound; an example of resilience without necessarily efficient hacking. He has paired ML + KHD.
- Swiss Miss is strongly positive about the KHD; Gravitypool appreciates its toolkit but prefers the ML. Shepherd calls it interesting while emphasizing ML.
- Infinity the Dante is skeptical of the AP SMG's limitations and high cost. [Loss of Lieutenant E157](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xTO7FjWO0KQ) considers it a luxury versus the Deva KHD, although mission-specific value is acknowledged.

The disagreement is resolved by role: **the Swiss KHD is not a must-have or the default KHD, but is a meaningful premium pick when a resilient forward specialist is worth the price.**

## Other profiles

- **44-point AP SMG:** all the durable assault qualities but no KHD. Normally spend the extra three points on the KHD for specialist and counter-hacking capability; take the non-hacker only when points are genuinely tight and the job is pure assault.
- **56-point MULTI Rifle HD:** actual HD control programs, reusable Camouflage, X Visor and durability are real advantages, but expensive versus the Blocker HD (19/0.5), Bolt HD (24/0.5), Deva HD (25/0.5) and Locust HD (29/0.5). Generally skip unless a mission specifically rewards the complete protected control-hacker package.

The Swiss HMG excels at winning conventional face-to-face rolls, but TAGs provide extra Orders and more durability; Aquila solves a different vision problem. The ML offers a defensive capability none of the cheaper alternatives fully replicates, though multiple cheap ARO pieces may be better value in a given list.

Two Swiss are legal but consume points and shared Orders quickly. Preserve a cheap command shell, Auxilia/Fugazi/Blocker/Pathfinder infrastructure, independent mission runners and a realistic Trauma-Doc recovery plan. Do not mistake the KHD's Firewall for a shared Fireteam benefit; Swiss cannot link.

**Bottom line:** ML first for its exceptional active/reactive dual role, HMG when sustained conventional attack is specifically needed, KHD when an unusually hard-to-remove objective specialist is worth 47 points. Must-own, not must-field.
