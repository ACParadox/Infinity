## Core identity

**Morats are visible, aggressive, surprisingly resilient combined-arms soldiers**, not merely a rush/CC sectorial. Build around **good angles + stacked defensive modifiers + small Fireteams + cheap smoke + real hacking + mission coverage**.

- **Warhorse / Religious:** many Morat troopers resist Isolation, BS Attack (-3), Retreat/Loss-of-Lieutenant disruption and other common defensive plans. **Not all allies, TAGs or REMs share those protections.** Check the precise rule/profile instead of saying the whole roster is immune.
- **Excellent force concentration:** many viable Duos and Haris rather than one slow, overcommitted five-model Core; move useful specialists and guns together.
- **Weakness:** mostly BS12–13; losing the N4 large-Core BS bonus hurts. **Make the opponent take bad rolls:** MSV through Smoke, imposed BS (-3), carefully selected positive rangebands. Merely firing more dice at a better shooter is not enough.
- **No normal Camo/Hidden/Cybermask shell:** threats are usually visible on deployment. Opponents can plan around your guns and objectives. Use placement, Smoke/Eclipse, reserves, Combat Jump/Parachutist and multiple independent attack lanes instead of relying on surprise.
- **N5 incidental infowar:** Dartok Pitcher hackers plus Rodok options, REM repeaters and T-Drone let you contest a hacking midfield without building a Nomads-style all-in network.

**Practical list skeleton:** 1 safe/useful LT engine → 1 reliable ranged gun → 1 alternate angle (Rasyat, Rodok/Krakot, warband, TAG) → Dartok/Pitcher + specialists → Smoke/disco-ball delivery → Ikadron and defensive REMs → recovery/mission scoring. Target a healthy Regular-order pool, not a mandatory 15-model template.

### Distinct viable plans

1. **Control/shooting:** Yaogat Spitfire + Dartok + Rindak, Yaogat sniper/Dartok duo, Sogarat LT, Oznat+Gakis, Ikadron and Daturazi. Establish Smoke, shoot with MSV, leave Pitchers/repeaters behind and preserve specialist scoring.
2. **Armored hammer:** Sogarat LT + Sogarat NCO Duo or Raicho/Bultrak as primary piece; cheap Smoke, Dartok and independent flank pressure. Don't buy **all three** expensive armor packages reflexively.
3. **Attack from unexpected axes:** Rasyat or Dropsuit, Daturazi, compact Rodok/Krakot team and a straightforward long-range firebase. Trades deployment certainty for flanking reach.
4. **Order engine:** Oznat + cheap Hungries can supply economical Regular activation infrastructure while another team does the mission; also creates Smoke angles without dedicating an entire expensive assault team.

## Opposing opinions worth preserving

- **Sogarat vs Kornak:** Robert's played-N5 preference is the Sogarat (especially LT + NCO duo) for outstanding gunfight modifiers, durability and disco balls; older Morat command doctrine preferred Kornak's flexible Strategos command. Which wins depends on points, usable LT Orders, the need for an independent LT and the number of expensive guns.
- **Yaogat vs Suryat:** Robert prefers Yaogat shooters because MSV2 + plentiful Smoke lowers opposing odds more than Suryat's extra wound helps; Suryat advocates still value Tactical Awareness, resilience, TinBots and heavy link play. Use Yaogat to *win the initial FtF*; Suryat to *keep a robust team functioning*.
- **Kaitok vs Rindak:** Kaitok wins close-range brutality per point; Rindak is a more expensive but repeatably helpful specialist/recovery package. Their roles differ; upgrading solely for combat damage is wrong.
- **Daturazi solo vs link:** a pair operating independently spreads Smoke and retains Impetuous utility; linking creates shared movement/close-assault efficiency but concentrates the enabling pieces.
- **Raicho/Bultrak vs Sogarat:** TAGs bring large/mobile weapons platforms and may enable distinct firing lines; the Sogarat currently competes hard by offering exceptional defenses and gun power without demanding a TAG-centric list. TAG preference depends on mission, geometry, Engineer availability and points.
- **Hacking 'weak' vs 'strong':** Tactical Awareness calls the Morat hacking game comparatively limited next to premium Combined Army hackers; Robert argues **Dartok + Rodok + Pitchers + remotes** produce a very efficient upper-middle-tier package. Both can be true: strong relative to its listbuilding cost, not a dominant infowar faction on demand.
- **Oznat/Hungries:** efficient regularized-order team vs dedicated aggressive melee delivery. A passive two-Gaki Smoke support team is often the safer economic use; advancing the Hungries is worthwhile when they can actually reach valuable trades.

## TAGs, vehicles and REMs — Combined Army cross-faction lens

### Raicho Armored Brigade (Morat TAG)

- **Why:** true TAG chassis and long-range gun pressure; unlike several infantry alternatives it can occupy a distinctive heavy/mobile attack role. Use it to dislodge blockers or control lanes while infantry presses objectives.
- **Build around:** reliable approach lanes, an Engineer/recovery plan, screens against close assault, hacking risk and cheap units that score while Raicho consumes Orders.
- **Against taking:** Sogarat's Warhorse and gunfight modifiers compete directly for premium gun points; a naked Raicho without support can collapse the order economy if immobilized, isolated or pinned. A TAG is not automatically the correct primary hammer.
- **Status:** Morat-specific TAG. Do not transplant recommendations for other Combined Army TAGs into MAF unless Army actually permits that profile.

### Bultrak Mobile Regiment (TAG in the pinned N5.3 Morat roster)

- **Why:** another high-investment, heavily armed alternative with distinct weapon/loadout decision making. Compare **actual rangebands, burst, movement, resilience, command economy and points** to Raicho/Sogarat, not just silhouette or model aesthetics.
- **Counterargument:** in his July 2025 N5 faction focus, Robert says the Sogarat pressures both Bultrak and Raicho out of his typical list. Neither is ruled out; establish the specific matchup/mission in which the Bultrak does something the cheaper setup cannot.
- **Rules warning:** the pinned Morat roster explicitly classifies Bultrak as a **TAG**; apply TAG (not VH) mechanics. Future Army revisions should still be rechecked.

### Shared Combined Army REM infrastructure

- **Ikadron:** unusually efficient **Baggage** / Order infrastructure; replenish disposable Pitchers, Smoke/disco-ball-related ammunition where permitted, and protect the model so it can serve multiple turns.
- **Q-Drone:** **Total Reaction** long-lane ARO, forces the enemy to spend orders/make a plan before exposing attack routes. Beware marker-state tricks, AP, smoke, hacking and pieces that impose better modifiers.
- **R-Drone:** inexpensive Flash Pulse/repeater-style support and orders where legal; best when it costs an enemy Orders to clear it rather than trying to serve as the list's primary ARO.
- **E-Drone:** EVO support, selected to enable a specific AD/hacking/REM play instead of assuming all lists need one.
- **T-Drone:** **Guided Missile Launcher** capitalizes on Targeted enemies; useful with Dartok/repeater reach. Its SWC/points are squandered if the list cannot create Targeted states reliably or if it steals too many Orders from objectives.
- **M-Drone / Med-Tech Obsidon Medchanoid ("Doctor Worm"):** mission, repair and doctor infrastructure as allowed by Army; watch differences between Engineers, doctors and remote peripherals. Support roles matter when Raicho/Bultrak or multiple recoverable guns justify them.
- **REM-specific caution:** *do not assume Warhorse, Religious, Morat keywords or Morat Fireteam eligibility transfer to drones*. Loss of Lieutenant / Isolation / hacking vulnerabilities may be materially different. Verify actual current profiles.

**Cross-faction research rule:** Vanilla Combined Army, Shasvastii, and Onyx coverage helps establish Q/T/R/E drone efficiency and opposing counters, but **do not treat shared equipment as shared Fireteam rules or availability**. For Morat-specific TAG evaluation, favor Raicho/Bultrak discussion over broad 'Combined TAGs' rankings.

### Quick vehicle/TAG/REM decision test

1. **Which specific obstacle does it solve?** Long ARO, armored blocker, hacking network, lane denial, mission scoring or asymmetric access.
2. **What is the support tax?** Engineer, repeater protection, Baggage reload, Smoke or orders diverted from specialists.
3. **What does it displace?** A Yaogat + Dartok team, Sogarat LT/NCO engine, specialist, warband or third attack axis.
4. **What happens if it goes down on turn 1?** If scoring and command economy collapse, the list is too concentrated.

## Matchup and deployment principles

- **Versus layered Mimetism or BS (-3):** capitalize on Morat Warhorse and MSV where applicable, but check what still affects you: cover, range, Surprise from other forms and non-BS hazards aren't universally cancelled.
- **Versus Smoke-dependent armies:** Yaogat MSV2 can exploit opposing/your own Smoke and prevent easy smoke-based advances; opposing MSV can reciprocate.
- **Versus high-end hacking:** Warhorse limits isolation but **does not turn HI/TAGs into unhackable troops**. Fight hackers through Pitchers/Trinity, avoid repeaters or trade in a different attack axis.
- **Versus hard ARO castles:** a frontal rush is not mandatory. Throw Smoke, engage with MSV, move by compact link, pressure edges via Rasyat and force hard ARO decisions with cheap warbands.
- **Versus enemy TAGs:** layered options: linked AP/appropriate weapons, Krakot or Daturazi CC, hacking on hackable targets, mission play. Don't send an expensive Morat HI into a poor unmodified FtF merely because it has many wounds.

## Vision control — role/value update (23 October 2026)

- **Core plan:** **Dāturazi Regular Smoke 14/0 + Yaogat MSV2** converts average BS into favorable active FtF; Oznat Smoke GL/Hungries extends the enabling angle. **Sogarat Disco** offers an Eclipse-style route on a much more expensive anchor; **Rasyat Eclipse** arrives from another vector.
- **Disagreement:** Shepherd favors Yaogat/Smoke over simply stacking wounds on Suryat; Suryat proponents value resilience/TacAware and less reliance on setup. If no one needs to shoot, a Disco/Eclipse crossing may score more cheaply than fighting. [Global tiers](../list-building/vision-control-units-apex-and-value.md).
