## Ranking conventions

- **Apex:** maximum competitive impact in a practical 300-point all-comers list, not raw power without regard to investment.
- **Value:** competitive contribution relative to points, SWC, Order economy, and opportunity cost.
- Rank **Apex** and **Value** separately while presenting them together where practical.
- Record exact legal 2–5 member profiles, Fireteam level, total points and SWC, role, and pinned Fireteam-chart/roster source for each ranked composition.
- In every Fireteam comparison table, give **one bullet per physical model** in the **Units / exact profiles** column (including individually repeated copies), plus separate **# units**, **FT level**, and **points / SWC** columns; member count and Fireteam level are not interchangeable.
- Use descriptive Fireteam names rather than arbitrary composition IDs in headings and rankings; preserve links to canonical sectorial references and note if an exact team is theorycrafted rather than battle-report tested.

### Apex investment bands

| Fireteam points | Classification | Rule of thumb |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ≤120 | Preferred Apex | Normal all-comers consideration |
| 121–135 | Conditional Apex | Requires unusually strong capabilities |
| 136–150 | Exceptional Apex | Requires substantial justification |

## Cross-role Fireteam evaluation guidelines

**Core principle:** prefer the smallest, least expensive *legal* Fireteam that performs its intended job exceptionally well. Larger teams only outrank compact ones when their extra members, levels, coverage, resilience or Order advantages justify their total opportunity cost in a complete 300-point list. Evaluate the **whole Fireteam**, not just its headline model.

| Quality | What to evaluate | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Role synergy and counter coverage** | Complementary members that solve different problems; tools that directly compensate for the main attacker's vulnerabilities rather than redundant premium profiles. | Very high |
| **Range-band coverage** | Relevant short-, mid- and long-range options; no easily exploitable gap on the routes and lanes the team must contest. | Very high |
| **Points / SWC efficiency** | Contribution of *every* paid member, Fireteam slot, Combat Group slot and outside support requirement; avoid a fifth-model tax. | Very high |
| **Action / Order efficiency** | Useful repositioning with one Order, productive alternate Leaders, and genuine restricted/pooled Order interactions where present. | Very high |
| **Reliable delivery** | Suitable movement speeds, terrain access, Smoke/Eclipse/visibility control, ability to reach objectives or favorable engagement positions. | High |
| **Useful Fireteam level** | L2 BS Attack (+1 Special Die), L3 Discover/Dodge, L4 +1 BS Attack Attribute and L5 Sixth Sense **when those bonuses advance the role**. | High; role-dependent |
| **Resilience and redundancy** | Remains effective after a casualty, an Isolated/disabled key member, lost level, disrupted approach or loss of the primary gun. | High |

### Range-band coverage

Assess **effective positive range bands and realistic access**, not just the number of different weapons. As a practical checklist, examine close threats (roughly 0–8"), short/mid engagements (roughly 8–24") and longer fire lanes (roughly 24"+); these are tactical zones, **not universal weapon bonus ranges**. Check each selected weapon's actual Range Chart.

- A long-range HMG/sniper/Feuerbach benefits from a shotgun, direct-template weapon, SMG or similar close defense. A melee/smoke assault package may instead need a mid-range gun or a reliable way to bypass distant AROs.
- Prefer **complementary** ranges to buying multiple expensive guns that want exactly the same engagements. A single good gun covering an essential band is often enough.
- Do not penalize a specialized team for lacking a band it should *avoid*, provided it has a credible route, screen or alternate answer. Weigh which bands matter for its primary mission and map position.
- Range coverage is only real if the relevant model can feasibly reach and use that band while the team stays functional.

### Role synergy: counterplay coverage

Score **distinct, practical counters**, rather than simply counting weapons, skills or profile keywords. Examples (all conditional on the actual rules, matchup, positioning and weapons):

| Enemy advantage / problem | Possible complementary answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Mimetism / negative shooting MODs** | Appropriate MSV, Sensor/Discover tools, direct-template attacks or alternative engagement lines. |
| **MSV-based overwatch** | Albedo in the applicable matchup, White Noise, Eclipse or line-of-fire avoidance; ordinary Smoke alone may not counter the visor. |
| **High ARM / tough targets** | AP, K1, high-damage attacks, E/M against susceptible targets, or relevant hacking against Hackable enemies. |
| **Strong long-range ARO** | Smoke/Eclipse or White Noise where applicable, flanking, indirect pressure, movement through blocked LoF, or a better-range gun. |
| **Hackable HI / TAG / REM** | Hacking and Repeater projection, E/M and anti-armor weapons, with suitable protection against return hacking. |
| **Camouflage / hidden approach** | Discover/Sensor, area templates, mines, close screens and protected approaches; do not treat every Sensor or template as a universal answer. |
| **Close assault / clustered threats** | Direct templates, mines, perimeter defenses, strong CC defense and short-range weapons. |
| **Enemy hacking network** | Firewall/TinBot where applicable, Killer Hackers against suitable targets, network clearance, or routes outside Hacking Areas. |

**Coverage must be actionable:** Albedo with no credible way to threaten an MSV ARO, a K1 weapon that cannot obtain an engagement, or a Hacker with no useful Hacking Area adds limited real counterplay. Prioritize a few reliable, complementary answers over superficial "toolbox" breadth. Count *counter coverage* separately from *range-band coverage*: they solve different problems.

### Sizes to evaluate first

**Fireteam size and Fireteam Level are different.** Level depends on qualifying same-Unit or explicit Counts-as identities, not just physical bodies; confirm sectorial chart limits and legal creation conditions. L2 **+1 Special Die is not +1 Burst**. Benefits are cumulative but may fall after casualties; L5 is not automatically preferable to an efficient L2/L3 package.

| Members | Default evaluation | When to prioritize |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| **2 (Duo)** | Compact value and mobility; often sufficient for L2 shooting. | Gunner + support, specialist + escort, attacker + Smoke/utility, cheap Order engines. |
| **3 (Haris or legal Core)** | **General-purpose starting point**: strong synergy without a large movement footprint. | Most attacking, objective, area-control, toolbox and economic packages. |
| **4 (Core)** | Offensive Core compromise when four matching members earn L4's accuracy improvement. | Strong gun + useful support/specialists; when the fourth model has its own job. |
| **5 (Core)** | Full-bonus/large-footprint option, not the default. | Cheap Order contributors, serious layered ARO/area denial, full-role utility and valuable L5 Sixth Sense. |

Suggested **value scouting ranges**, not eligibility cutoffs: Duo **25–65 pts**, three members **40–85 pts**, four **65–110 pts**, five **75–120 pts**. Expensive elite Duos, Tactical Awareness packages and uniquely capable formations can legitimately exceed them. Apply the separate **Apex investment bands** above as written, rather than targeting 120 points.

### Emphasis by Fireteam goal

| Category | Sizes to scrutinize first | Leading criteria and common traps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gunfighting** | 2–3; 4 if L4 genuinely pays | FtF quality, complementary ranges, MSV/Albedo/visibility or armor counters, attack delivery. Avoid duplicate expensive guns with overlapping jobs. |
| **Defensive / ARO** | 2–3 **and** 4–5 | Strong reactive shot(s), staggered lanes, close screens, counterplay coverage, recovery and survival after losing an anchor. A large Core does not grant free group AROs. |
| **Mission Specialists / Objective Play** | 2–3 | Qualified specialist(s), route protection, speed, recovery and ability to score after clearing resistance. Avoid five bodies that escort just one useful button pusher. |
| **Board Control / Area Denial** | 3; 4–5 if coverage is broad | Persistent mines/E/M/adhesive/templates, Repeater + hacker projection, multiple approaches and sustainable denial. Pure lane shooting alone is not full board control. |
| **Order Economy / Force Multipliers** | 2–3, or *cheap* 5 | Net pooled Regular Orders, FT Master conversion, TA/NCO activations, specialist/support compression; distinguish personal Tactical Orders from transferable pool Orders. |
| **Combined-Arms / Toolbox** | 3–4; 5 if unusually efficient | Complementary gun, specialist and technical/control tools; **range and counter coverage are particularly important**. Avoid paying for unrelated skills unlikely to be used together. |
| **Close Assault / Breakthrough** | 2–3; 4 when extra staying power pays | Reliable protected delivery, short-range and CC tools, anti-screen/anti-armor counters, then scoring or holding the breach. Pure CC lethality without a route is insufficient. |

**Ranking application:** keep Apex and Value independent; assess role accomplishment first, then practical **range coverage** and **counter coverage** as separate cross-role criteria. Reward only capabilities that can be employed in realistic turns, including after likely casualties. Do not allow broad coverage to overwhelm the team's core purpose or justify an inefficient extra model.
