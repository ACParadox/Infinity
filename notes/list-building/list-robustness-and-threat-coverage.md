# List-Building Strategy — List Robustness and Threat Coverage

A strong Infinity list should not merely contain powerful pieces. It should have **credible answers to the main problems an opponent or mission can present**, and it should continue functioning after losing one important piece.

The objective is not to fill every checkbox with a dedicated model. The best lists use **role compression, complementary tools, and overlapping coverage**.

> **Robustness = enough different ways to attack, score, defend, and recover that the opponent cannot cripple the whole plan by removing one piece.**

This is a high-level audit. Detailed execution belongs in the dedicated notes on [Lieutenant and command structure](lieutenant-and-command-structure.md), [Hacking and network projection](hacking-and-network-projection.md), [DZ strikes and DZ defense](dz-strikes-and-dz-defense.md), [Mission, scoring, and Specialist strategy](mission-scoring-and-specialist-strategy.md), [Movement, positioning, and vision control](movement-positioning-and-vision-control.md), and [Target priority and attack sequencing](target-priority-and-attack-sequencing.md).

For a detailed audit of weapon ranges, anti-armour ammunition, Direct/Impact Templates and non-lethal alternatives, see [Weapons, ammunition and engagement selection](weapons-ammunition-and-engagement-selection.md).

## Coverage is not the same as redundancy

Distinguish three levels of coverage:

- **Primary answer:** the tool you expect to use against that problem.
- **Secondary answer:** another reasonably efficient solution if the primary answer dies, is badly positioned, or is countered.
- **Emergency answer:** inefficient, risky, or Order-heavy, but prevents the matchup from becoming unwinnable.

For example, against a TAG:

- primary: AP HMG;
- secondary: Oblivion through Repeaters;
- emergency: E/M warband or CC specialist.

That is normally more robust than having three AP guns that all attack from the same range band and lose to the same defensive mechanism.

### Prefer orthogonal redundancy

The best backup usually attacks through a **different axis**.

Instead of:

> gunfighter + slightly worse gunfighter

prefer combinations such as:

- gunfighter + Hacker;
- gunfighter + Camo attacker;
- long-range attacker + short-range assault piece;
- direct attack + E/M control;
- visible attacker + Airborne/Impersonation attack.

If one defensive mechanism counters the primary piece, the second tool should ideally force a different problem.

## 1. Long-range attack

Have a credible way to remove or bypass pieces controlling long firelanes.

Possible solutions include:

- HMG/AP HMG;
- Feuerbach or MULTI weapons;
- strong Marksman/sniper-type active shooter;
- TAG;
- long-range Fireteam gun;
- Guided/Spotlight;
- Smoke/MSV attack;
- White Noise against MSV;
- Pitcher into hacking against Hackable defenders;
- enough mobility or deployment advantage to attack from a different range or angle.

### Audit

Ask:

> **If the opponent puts a strong ARO piece across the central lane, what do I actually do?**

A weak answer is simply that the list's one HMG fights it.

A stronger answer might be that the HMG can fight it, Smoke can bypass it, a Hackable target can be attacked through the network, or a forward attacker can approach from another lane.

A list does not necessarily need a dedicated long-range monster if it has reliable **bypass tools**.

## 2. Mid- and short-range attack

Long-range dominance does not solve dense tables, midfield Camo, rooms, corners, objectives, or defended consoles.

Include something capable of operating effectively once the fight collapses into midfield and close-range engagements.

Useful traits include:

- high mobility;
- Camo or Impersonation;
- Direct Template Weapons;
- high-Burst short-range guns;
- Shotguns;
- Mines or Drop Bears;
- Smoke/Eclipse;
- good Dodge;
- CC;
- NWI, Dogged, or other durability;
- deployment advantage.

This role often becomes more important on Turns 2–3 than the long-range gun.

### Audit

> **If the main long-range attacker clears the first obstacle, who actually advances through the midfield?**

Do not force an HMG platform to solve every problem at 8–16 inches.

## 3. Anti-Camo and marker-state capability

A list needs some way to avoid being crippled by marker-state defence and the Discover tax.

Strong tools include:

- Sensor;
- efficient Discover pieces;
- multiple cheap models able to Discover;
- forward troops that can contest marker space;
- deployables controlling where markers can safely move;
- weapons that punish the model efficiently once revealed;
- mobility allowing attacks from unusual angles;
- your own markers, forcing reciprocal information problems.

The important distinction is:

> **Anti-Camo does not mean merely being able to Discover. It means being able to resolve a marker problem without spending half the turn doing so.**

A single expensive attack piece repeatedly Discovering is poor anti-Camo coverage.

### Camo stress test

Imagine facing:

- five to seven midfield markers;
- several Mines;
- one important hidden attacker;
- a marker sitting beside an objective you must reach.

Ask how many Orders the list spends simply obtaining information and opening safe routes.

## 4. Anti-MSV and vision-control answer

Do not assume Smoke is universally sufficient vision control. Enemy MSV can turn that plan against you.

Possible answers to strong MSV pieces include:

- White Noise;
- Eclipse;
- attacking outside LoF;
- hacking;
- Speculative or Guided attacks;
- templates from favorable positions;
- close combat;
- attacking from an unexpected deployment vector;
- simply using a gunfighter capable of winning the direct engagement.

Likewise, if a list depends heavily on an MSV + Smoke interaction, ask:

> **What happens when the relevant MSV troop dies?**

Vision control should improve the list rather than become a single point of failure.

## 5. Anti-HI and anti-TAG

Every list needs a credible plan against very durable, high-ARM/BTS targets.

### Direct destruction

Possible tools include:

- AP;
- DA/EXP;
- high-Damage weapons;
- high-Burst weapons with suitable ammunition;
- powerful CC.

### Disable or control

Possible tools include:

- Oblivion;
- Carbonite;
- E/M;
- Immobilization;
- Isolation;
- other states that prevent the premium piece from functioning normally.

### Bypass

Sometimes the correct answer is:

> **Do not kill the TAG.**

Instead, remove or pressure:

- its Engineer;
- Orders feeding it;
- Repeaters supporting it;
- Specialists;
- Lieutenant or command structure;

then score around it.

A robust list should usually have at least **one efficient hard-target answer plus another way of controlling, avoiding, or exploiting the target**.

## 6. Anti-Hacker capability

If the list contains important Hackable pieces or relies heavily on its own Repeater network, hostile Hackers are a predictable threat.

Possible protection includes:

- KHD;
- HD/HD+ with strong anti-Hacker programs;
- TinBot/Firewall support;
- Zero Pain;
- direct physical attack on enemy Hackers;
- Camo, Airborne, or Impersonation hunting;
- destroying hostile Repeaters;
- avoiding the network entirely.

A Hacker is not fully protected merely because another Hacker exists.

Ask:

> **Can I contest the enemy Hacker through the network, and can I physically remove or bypass the network if I lose the cyberwar?**

As covered in [Hacking and network projection](hacking-and-network-projection.md), hacking should be evaluated as a package of Hacker quality, network projection, complementary devices, and anti-Hacker defence rather than as isolated profiles.

## 7. Hacking defence

This is broader than anti-Hacker offence.

A Hackable-heavy list should consider:

- Firewall/TinBot access;
- friendly KHD/Trinity;
- Engineer support;
- Reset opportunities;
- alternative movement routes around Repeaters;
- ability to remove Repeaters;
- ability to fight without exposing all premium Hackable models simultaneously.

### Important distinction

**Killing the enemy Hacker is one defence. Making its network irrelevant is another.**

A TAG or HI list that cannot safely cross a midfield Repeater network has a serious structural weakness even if its own Hacker is excellent.

## 8. Deployment-zone defence

Assume the opponent can reach the DZ.

Defend against:

- Combat Jump;
- DZ Parachutists;
- Impersonators;
- deep Infiltrators;
- motorcycles and Vehicles;
- fast Super-Jump or Climbing troops;
- Smoke-assisted assault;
- CC missiles.

Useful layers include:

- Mines;
- templates;
- cheap disposable AROs;
- Hackers and Repeaters;
- overlapping short-range coverage;
- troops watching table edges and landing areas;
- spread-out Order generators;
- protected Lieutenant placement.

The objective is normally not to make the DZ impenetrable. It is to make a DZ strike **cost enough Orders and risk that it cannot casually erase the Order pool**.

See [DZ strikes and DZ defense](dz-strikes-and-dz-defense.md) for the detailed architecture.

## 9. Midfield presence

A list that deploys almost entirely in its own DZ often pays an enormous Order tax later.

Useful Turn-0 midfield presence includes:

- Camo;
- Infiltrators;
- Forward Deployment;
- Mines;
- Repeaters;
- Perimeter weapons;
- cheap skirmishers;
- advanced Specialists.

Midfield presence simultaneously:

- slows enemy movement;
- protects approaches;
- provides scoring access;
- creates attack vectors;
- taxes enemy Orders;
- pushes your own starting position closer to useful work.

You do not necessarily need to **own** the midfield. You should normally have some way to **contest** it.

## 10. Specialists and scoring

Do not evaluate Specialists simply by number. Evaluate **independent scoring routes**.

Three Specialists clustered in one Fireteam may provide less resilience than a combination such as:

- infiltrating Specialist;
- fast Specialist;
- durable rear- or midfield Specialist.

Ask:

- Can I score on Turn 1?
- Can I score deep objectives?
- Who scores on Turn 3?
- Can I still score if my primary Specialist dies?
- Can a Specialist operate without consuming the entire Order pool?

A combat-mediocre profile may be excellent if it gives the list a scoring route it otherwise lacks.

See [Mission, scoring, and Specialist strategy](mission-scoring-and-specialist-strategy.md) for detailed Specialist quantity, redundancy, end-game closers, and mission-specific planning.

## 11. Backup attack piece

Every list should answer:

> **What happens if my primary attacker dies before taking an Active Turn?**

The backup does not need to be equally powerful. It needs to be good enough to:

- remove a critical obstacle;
- exploit a weak flank;
- conduct a limited attack run;
- create space for Specialists;
- finish damaged enemies;
- prevent the opponent from playing completely uncontested.

### Ideal backup

The best secondary attacker often:

- occupies a different range band;
- attacks through a different defence;
- begins in a different area;
- uses a different Order source;
- doubles as Specialist, support, or ARO until needed.

A list with one apex attacker plus several 20–30-point asymmetric threats may be more resilient than a list that spends heavily on two very similar apex attackers.

## 12. Lieutenant plan

Do not merely select an LT profile. Build a **command structure**.

Possible architectures include:

- cheap protected LT + decoy + NCO;
- protected LT + NCO;
- Strategos LT;
- aggressive LT + Chain of Command;
- highly durable/mobile LT with consciously accepted risk;
- list designed to remain somewhat functional through Loss of Lieutenant.

The detailed [Lieutenant and command structure](lieutenant-and-command-structure.md) note emphasizes judging Chain of Command partly by **how badly the army functions during Loss of Lieutenant**, not merely by the chance that the Lieutenant dies.

### LT robustness test

> **If my Lieutenant dies during the enemy Turn 1, can I still accomplish anything useful next turn?**

If the answer is essentially no, command protection becomes a major list requirement.

## 13. Recovery

Recovery is most valuable when it protects large investments.

Consider:

- Engineer;
- Doctor or Paramedic;
- Servant bots;
- Engineer access to TAG/REM/HI concentrations;
- ability to repair important states as well as lost STR;
- Fireteam reconstruction;
- Command Tokens reserved for critical recovery.

But do not automatically buy recovery.

A list built mostly from cheap disposable attackers may prefer **replacement capacity** over Doctors and Engineers.

> **Recovery matters when restoring the piece is cheaper and more reliable than replacing its function elsewhere.**

## 14. Ability to function after losing the primary piece

This is perhaps the best single list-robustness test.

Before finalizing the list:

> **Delete the most important model from the roster and mentally play the mission.**

Can you still:

- attack?
- remove an ARO?
- deal with armour?
- reach objectives?
- contest the midfield?
- defend the DZ?
- spend the main Order pool productively?
- score Turn 3?

If losing one model removes several of those simultaneously, the list is structurally fragile.

Common examples include:

- single apex gunfighter;
- sole Hacker behind a large Repeater network;
- only Engineer in a TAG-centric list;
- only fast Specialist;
- LT who is simultaneously the main attacker;
- one model providing all Smoke;
- one MSV model enabling the entire vision-control plan.

## Role compression

**Cross-faction unit selections:** [Jack-of-all-trades / Toolbox Units — N5.3 apex and points-value rankings](jack-of-all-trades-toolbox-units-apex-value.md) compares actual multi-role solo pieces and legal small-team packages, including when paying for unused capabilities reduces reliability.



You cannot afford a dedicated model for every checklist item.

Instead, look for profiles covering several requirements.

### Sensor Specialist REM

Can provide:

- anti-Camo;
- Specialist capability;
- Repeater;
- mobility;
- secondary attack utility.

### Camo Engineer

Can provide:

- Specialist;
- recovery;
- midfield presence;
- late-game scoring;
- additional anti-materiel or support utility depending on profile.

### KHD infiltrator

Can provide:

- anti-Hacker capability;
- midfield presence;
- Specialist capability;
- marker-state attack;
- backup scoring.

### Warband with Smoke plus E/M or CC

Can provide:

- vision control;
- DZ defence;
- anti-HI/TAG capability;
- close assault;
- cheap trading.

### TAG

Can provide:

- long-range attack;
- mid-range attack;
- mobility;
- durable zone presence;
- sometimes Lieutenant or extra-Order utility.

The more checklist boxes a troop covers **without making all those functions depend on that one troop surviving**, the better.

## Failure-mode audit

Before declaring a list finished, run several imaginary failures.

### Primary gunfighter dies

Can something else open the table?

### Opponent brings a TAG

Can you kill, disable, or ignore it efficiently?

### Opponent brings heavy Camo

Can you operate without paying enormous Discover taxes?

### Opponent has strong MSV

Does your Smoke-based plan collapse?

### Opponent dominates hacking

Can your HI/TAG/REM still move?

### Enemy DZ attacker succeeds

Are your Lieutenant, Orders, and support all clustered together?

### Midfield becomes Mine/Camo/Repeater hell

Can you clear or bypass it?

### You lose the Lieutenant

Does the following turn still have useful actions?

### Your best Specialist dies

Do you still have realistic scoring routes?

### You go second and lose three or four Orders

Does the list still produce a coherent first Active Turn?

These tests reveal weaknesses much faster than asking whether every individual profile is efficient.

## Threat-coverage checklist

| Requirement | Primary answer | Backup / alternative | Audit question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Long-range attack** | Primary gunfighter | bypass / second vector | Can I clear a major long-range ARO? |
| **Mid/short attack** | assault piece | skirmisher / warband | Can I fight through dense midfield? |
| **Anti-Camo** | Sensor / efficient discovery | deployables / multiple cheap Discoverers | Can I handle marker spam without wasting a turn? |
| **Anti-MSV** | White Noise / Eclipse / equivalent | non-LoF attack / alternate angle | Does enemy MSV invalidate my vision plan? |
| **Anti-HI/TAG** | AP / high-damage attack | hacking / E/M / CC / bypass | What do I do against armour plus durability? |
| **Anti-Hacker** | KHD / Trinity | physical assassination | Can I remove hostile cyber pieces? |
| **Hacking defence** | Firewall / KHD / network defence | bypass / remove Repeaters | Can my Hackable pieces still operate? |
| **DZ defence** | layered defence | disposable templates / Mines | Can one infiltrator or AD troop eat my Order pool? |
| **Midfield presence** | infiltrator / Camo / advanced deployer | fast Turn-1 projection | Do I contest the table before spending Orders? |
| **Specialists** | multiple scoring paths | backup closer | Can I still score after casualties? |
| **Backup attack** | secondary attacker | several trade pieces | What if attacker #1 dies immediately? |
| **LT plan** | protected or active command architecture | CoC / LoL resilience | What happens if the LT dies Turn 1? |
| **Recovery** | Doctor / Engineer where justified | functional redundancy | Can I restore expensive infrastructure? |
| **Post-loss function** | distributed capability | role compression | Remove my best model: is this still a list? |

## Green / Yellow / Red audit

Do **not** require every row to be Green. Some armies intentionally accept weaknesses.

Classify each requirement:

- **Green:** strong primary answer plus credible backup.
- **Yellow:** one good answer or several mediocre answers.
- **Red:** no realistic answer.

A competitive list can contain some Yellow categories.

Be suspicious when:

- several important categories are Red;
- several Green categories are all supplied by **the same model**;
- multiple answers all fail to the **same counter**.

The real target is:

> **Few catastrophic matchups, multiple scoring routes, multiple attack vectors, and no single casualty that turns the army off.**

## Fast final check

Before saving a list, answer five questions:

1. **How do I score the mission?**
2. **How do I remove or bypass the opponent's hardest roadblock?**
3. **How do I operate against markers, hacking, and vision-control pieces?**
4. **What protects my Orders, Lieutenant, and Specialists?**
5. **If my best model dies immediately, what is Plan B?**

If question 5 has no convincing answer, the list probably needs more resilience rather than more raw power.
