# List-Building Strategy — Movement, Positioning, and Vision Control

These notes are primarily table-play strategy rather than force-construction rules, but they strongly affect which movement, marker-state, and vision-control tools are worth including in a list.

For **N5.3 Smoke/Eclipse/Disco Baller/Mirrorball/White Noise units, apex versus points-value tiers, specific matchup choices, and MCP-reviewed videos/podcasts**, see the [cross-faction Vision Control guide](vision-control-units-apex-and-value.md).

For a deeper silhouette-by-silhouette treatment of access width, vaulting, rooftops, templates, Super-Jump, Climbing Plus, and table scanning, see [Terrain, silhouettes and table geometry](terrain-silhouettes-and-table-geometry.md).

## Slice the pie

When approaching a defended lane, expose the active trooper to **as few ARO pieces as possible**.

Move only far enough around a corner, doorway, rooftop edge, or other piece of terrain to gain LoF to one enemy while keeping the other defenders blocked. Resolve that engagement, then continue moving to reveal the next threat separately.

This converts one bad engagement against several AROs into a sequence of more favorable one-on-one engagements.

When practical, use the first movement segment to expose the smallest amount of the active trooper needed to gain the desired LoF. After the attack, use later movement to return behind Total Cover or otherwise improve the trooper's final position.

The core principle is:

> **Do not fight an entire ARO network at once when terrain lets you solve it one model at a time.**

## Advanced movement

Movement skills are problem-solving tools, not merely ways to travel toward the enemy.

### Dodge movement

A successful Dodge may provide movement during the Effects step. That movement **does not generate AROs or trigger Deployable Weapons or Equipment**.

This creates valuable positional plays:

- Dodge into or out of Cover;
- Dodge around a corner or into Total Cover;
- Dodge into Silhouette contact and force Engaged State;
- Dodge out of Engaged State when the movement allows it;
- use high-PH or Dodge-bonus troopers to gain position without creating a second wave of AROs from the resulting movement.

The important distinction is that **declaring Dodge can generate or respond to ARO conditions normally, but the movement gained after a successful Dodge does not itself create new ARO opportunities**.

### Cautious Movement

Cautious Movement can cross an otherwise dangerous route without generating enemy AROs when its requirements are met.

In particular, the movement must **begin and end outside the LoF and ZoC of enemy Models and Markers**; Hackable models must also respect enemy Hacking Areas. It is a Long Skill, so the safety comes at an Order-efficiency cost.

Use it when one full Order is cheaper than risking an important specialist, support piece, or attack piece to a dangerous ARO.

Do not forget the profile restrictions: TAGs, REMs, Vehicles, Motorcycle troops, Aerial troops, and Targeted troops cannot use Cautious Movement.

### Climb and Jump

Do not treat the table as two-dimensional.

Before spending several Orders walking around an obstacle, check whether **Climb or Jump** creates a shorter route or a better angle. Vertical movement can:

- bypass a defended doorway or corridor;
- reach an unexpected firing position;
- avoid a mine/repeater/ARO route;
- attack a rooftop or elevated objective;
- shorten an assault path;
- create a new slice-the-pie angle that does not exist at ground level.

This is especially important for troops with movement bonuses, Climbing Plus, Super-Jump, Aerial, or unusually high MOV.

## Forking reactive decisions

A strong active-turn position often gives the opponent several bad choices while allowing the active player to choose the appropriate follow-up **after seeing the ARO declaration**.

### Visible-model fork

For example, move a capable short-range/CC trooper toward an enemy that has several imperfect responses. Depending on the situation, the defender may choose to:

- shoot;
- Dodge;
- Reset or Hack;
- use CC or another available ARO.

After that ARO is declared, choose the second Short Skill that best exploits it: shoot, Dodge, enter CC, Hack, continue moving where legal, or use another relevant option.

This is strongest when the active piece is genuinely competent at several possible follow-ups. A good fork is not merely uncertainty; it is a position where **each reasonable enemy response gives up something else**.

### Camouflage-marker fork

Camouflage creates an even stronger declaration problem.

Against a Camouflaged Marker, normal ARO options are restricted to **Discover, Dodge, Look Out!, or Reset**, and an eligible reactive trooper may instead delay its ARO until the marker declares the second half of its Order.

If the opponent delays and the marker does not reveal, that reactive trooper loses the ARO. If the opponent commits to Discover or Dodge instead, the marker can choose its second skill with that information known.

Use this to create decision pressure:

- Move + Move when the opponent delays, gaining safe position;
- force Discover attempts from models that would rather shoot;
- reveal and attack when the declared response is favorable;
- remain a marker when preserving uncertainty is worth more than attacking.

Do not describe this as a generic Hidden Deployment rule: it is specifically a **Camouflaged-marker ARO interaction**. Hidden Deployment has its own reveal timing and can transition into a marker state on eligible profiles.

## Hidden Deployment roles

Hidden Deployment is strongest when the value of **surprise placement and reveal timing** is worth temporarily withholding the trooper's Order from the normal Order Pool.

### Hidden ARO piece

A common strong use is an ARO trap positioned where the opponent does not know a threat exists.

Good examples include:

- a dangerous template weapon covering an approach;
- a strong MULTI Sniper or similar long-range ARO weapon;
- an apparently empty firelane that becomes dangerous only after the opponent commits;
- a second hidden ARO stacked with a visible ARO so the attacker solves one threat and unexpectedly discovers another.

The goal is not merely to surprise the opponent. It is to make the opponent **commit movement or an attack plan based on incomplete information**, then reveal where that commitment is expensive to reverse.

### Hidden active-turn fighter

Hidden Deployment is less automatically valuable when used only to hide an ordinary active-turn gunfighter. The model is withholding an Order until it reveals, and active-turn attack pieces already have the initiative advantage.

It becomes much more attractive when the profile also brings things such as:

- Camouflage after revealing from Hidden Deployment;
- Mimetism (-6);
- Surprise Attack;
- Infiltration or an excellent starting position;
- a weapon/range combination that can immediately exploit that position.

In other words, Hidden Deployment should add more than simply "the opponent could not shoot this model on Turn 1."

### Hidden specialist / sneaker

Hidden Deployment is excellent on mission pieces that do not need to reveal immediately.

A hidden or subsequently Camouflaged specialist can:

- survive an alpha strike without needing a protected deployment position;
- remain near an objective without advertising its presence;
- reveal only when the objective actually needs to be scored;
- preserve marker-state protection while moving toward the objective when the profile permits it.

For these profiles, patience is often more valuable than an early attack. **Stay hidden or Camouflaged until revealing produces mission value.**

## Smoke shooting

Smoke becomes an offensive gunfighting tool when paired with Multispectral Visors.

- **MSV2 and MSV3** ignore the Visibility Zone MODs when drawing LoF through Smoke, enabling extremely favorable or effectively one-sided shooting situations against enemies that cannot see back through the Smoke.
- **MSV1** can also draw LoF through a Zero Visibility Zone, but applies a -6 MOD, so the same tactic exists in a weaker form.

This makes Smoke + MSV2/3 one of the strongest reasons to deliberately pair a vision-control piece with a ranged attacker.

Do not treat Smoke and MSV as independent list ingredients when the faction can combine them efficiently: the combination is substantially more valuable than either piece alone.

## Vision control and slicing the pie

Vision-control zones can create artificial terrain.

Smoke, Eclipse, White Noise, and similar effects can block one or more enemy AROs while leaving another target accessible. This allows the active player to **manufacture a slice-the-pie situation** even where the physical terrain would otherwise expose the attacker to several defenders.

Typical sequence:

1. identify several overlapping ARO pieces;
2. place the relevant vision-control zone to remove most of them from the engagement;
3. attack the remaining visible threat;
4. change or reposition the vision-control effect as necessary and solve the next threat separately.

The best vision-control lists therefore do more than simply "have Smoke." They can use vision control to reshape which engagements are possible.

## Vision control for bypassing instead of fighting

Do not assume every ARO piece needs to die.

Vision control can instead let a model:

- cross a dangerous lane;
- reach an objective;
- enter a room or protected midfield area;
- get into CC;
- reposition a specialist;
- retreat after an attack run.

If killing a strong ARO piece would take several Orders and substantial risk, spending one or two Orders to **make that piece irrelevant** may be the better play.

This is particularly important in objective missions: the purpose of vision control is not only to improve gunfights, but to let the army choose when gunfights are unnecessary.

### Match the vision-control tool to the threat

- **Smoke:** excellent general LoF denial and especially strong with friendly MSV2/3.
- **Eclipse / Reflective vision control:** particularly useful against MSV pieces that would otherwise see through ordinary Smoke.
- **White Noise:** asymmetric answer to MSV/Marksmanship pieces and especially valuable when a hacking network can project it where needed.

The strategic principle is:

> **Vision control is both an offensive modifier and a movement resource. Use it to isolate fights, bypass fights, and deliver short-range or CC pieces to the engagements they actually want.**
