REMs should be evaluated by the battlefield role their chassis enables rather than treated as one generic category.

A recurring strength of basic REMs is that they can provide **very cheap Regular Orders** while also carrying useful passive abilities. The main limiting factor is often not points but **deployment footprint**: S3 REM bases take meaningful space, cannot use every hiding place available to infantry, and can become difficult to keep protected when a list already contains many REMs, TAGs, VH, motorcycles, or other large-base models.

When comparing cheap Order generators, evaluate both:

- **points per Regular Order**, and
- **how much protected deployment-zone space the model consumes**.

A REM can be dramatically cheaper than a line trooper while still being a worse third or fourth cheap body if the deployment zone is already physically congested.

## Flash Pulse / Repeater REMs

Cheap Flash Pulse bots are often close to **default or must-pick profiles** at their usual prices.

They commonly combine:

- one of the faction's cheapest Regular Orders;
- a Repeater;
- fast movement;
- a WIP-based Flash Pulse ARO;
- enough mobility to reposition the Repeater later if needed.

In some factions they are **much cheaper than the cheapest line infantry**, which makes them exceptionally efficient ways to build the Order pool.

One or two are usually easy to justify. The main reason not to keep adding them is often physical rather than economic: three S3 REMs can consume a surprising amount of safe deployment space, especially alongside Baggage bots, TR bots, TAGs, VH, bikes, Servant REMs, and other large bases.

Therefore:

> **Flashbots are often limited by deployment density before they are limited by efficiency.**

If the list has lots of spare hidden S3 positions and few equally cheap Regular Orders, taking several can be excellent. If the DZ is already crowded with large silhouettes, cheap infantry may be worth paying extra for simply because it is easier to hide and position.

## Cheap Baggage REM

The basic non-EVO Baggage bot should not be evaluated only as a reload platform. It is often one of the **cheapest Regular Orders available in the faction**, sometimes rivaled only by the Flash Pulse bot.

That means the default case for one Baggage bot can already be strong:

- very cheap Regular Order;
- Baggage;
- often a Repeater and/or Deactivator or similar utility;
- a body that can occupy or protect part of the deployment zone.

### One Baggage bot is often easy to justify

Even when the list does not desperately need reloads, one cheap Baggage bot can be efficient simply because the opportunity cost is so low.

Baggage then becomes additional upside for lists containing reloadable Disposable weapons/equipment such as:

- Pitchers;
- Disco Ballers;
- FastPandas;
- Panzerfausts;
- Blitzen;
- Mines or Drop Bears where the carrier can be reloaded;
- other important Disposable equipment.

The strongest Baggage lists tend to have a real **reload ecosystem** rather than one isolated Disposable weapon.

### The second Baggage bot needs more justification

Two Baggage bots can be useful when:

- reloadable pieces operate on **two different parts of the table**;
- there is a strong need for Baggage coverage in two locations;
- the list has many reloadable carriers;
- Baggage bots are substantially cheaper than alternative Regular Orders;
- the list is not already crowded with large-base models.

The main drawback is often **base congestion**. One large REM base is usually easy to hide; two Baggage bots plus Flashbots, combat REMs, TAGs, VH, motorcycles, etc. can make deployment awkward.

If the faction has cheap or cheaper Regular infantry that are also needed for Fireteams, Lieutenant concealment, specialist roles, or deployment flexibility, the second Baggage bot becomes less automatic.

A useful rule is:

> **Take the first Baggage bot for cheap-Order efficiency plus useful logistics; take the second because the list actually benefits from a second Baggage location or because it remains the best available cheap Regular Order after considering deployment footprint.**

### Baggage with Pitchers and Disco Ballers

Pitchers are one of the cleanest Baggage synergies because their limitation is often ammunition rather than relevance. A projector can fire its Pitchers, later return or move within Baggage range, reload, and rebuild the network again.

Disco Ballers also benefit strongly. Existing deployed Disco Balls remain on the table; reloading the carrier allows additional future deployments rather than merely replacing the old ones.

## Total Reaction REMs

A Total Reaction bot is primarily a **reactive Order tax with a secondary active-turn gun role**.

Its job is not necessarily to kill an elite attacker. Its job is to make the opponent spend Orders and appropriate resources before using an important lane.

Good TR placement usually covers one or two strategically relevant approaches rather than exposing the bot to half the table. Useful lanes include:

- approaches to the deployment zone;
- mission-objective routes;
- flanks;
- access to Lieutenants, Engineers, Hackers, or other support pieces;
- corridors the opponent would otherwise use freely.

The bot is successful if the opponent must spend several Orders solving it, even if the bot kills nothing.

### Thunderbolt TR versus HMG TR

Where both variants exist, they fill slightly different roles.

**Thunderbolt / lower-SWC TR:**

- usually cheaper in SWC and often cheaper in points;
- strong enough to punish weak or careless attackers;
- excellent when the bot's main purpose is reactive pressure;
- leaves more SWC for premium active-turn weapons elsewhere.

**HMG TR:**

- higher Burst and stronger conventional active-turn firepower;
- better as a **backup long-range attacker** if the primary gunfighter dies or is disabled;
- more attractive if the list has EVO support that can improve its active-turn shooting;
- less attractive when SWC is already tight or the list has plenty of better active guns.

A useful choice rule is:

- already have strong active attackers and want cheap reactive pressure -> **Thunderbolt TR**;
- need the TR bot to double as a credible Plan-B gunfighter -> **HMG TR**.

### TR bots and Engineers

Remote Presence makes repair support particularly relevant. A TR bot that forces several enemy Orders, falls Unconscious, then gets repaired can force the opponent to solve the same problem again.

This means the bot's value should be measured partly in **enemy Orders consumed**, not only kills.

### TR bots in Group 2

TR bots are often excellent Group-2 pieces because they:

- contribute in ARO without consuming Orders;
- may only need one or two Orders in a normal turn;
- can use Group-2 Orders as emergency attackers;
- are expendable enough that losing them does not cripple Group 1;
- can be transferred to Group 1 later if needed.

### TR bots and EVO

Do not count **Enhanced Reaction** as special synergy with a model that already has Total Reaction; its native reactive Burst already solves that problem.

The more relevant EVO interaction is usually **Assisted Fire** or other active-turn Supportware, especially on an HMG TR bot that is expected to serve as a backup gunfighter.

## Guided Missile REM

Treat a Guided missile REM primarily as a **payoff for an already-good Targeted / Spotlight / network package**, not as the reason to build that package.

It becomes attractive when the list already wants several of the following:

- broad Repeater coverage;
- strong Spotlight Hackers;
- Pitchers, FastPandas, or forward Repeaters;
- Forward Observers;
- Sensor/FO REMs;
- Baggage that already supports the network projectors.

If all of that infrastructure is useful even when the missile REM never fires, adding Guided can be efficient.

If several models are being added only to make the missile bot work, the package becomes fragile and expensive.

### Passive value of Guided

The Guided bot creates pressure even when it never fires. Once Targeted becomes dangerous, the opponent may need to:

- Reset;
- avoid or destroy Repeaters;
- hunt Hackers;
- attack the missile bot;
- alter movement to avoid easy Spotlight opportunities.

That pressure is part of the profile's value.

### Group placement

An occasional Guided payoff piece often fits Group 2 well because it may sit idle until a target appears, then consume only a few Orders.

A list built around repeated Guided attacks needs much more reliable Order access and should not assume a tiny secondary pool will always be enough.

### Keep it hidden

A basic Guided REM is usually a poor conventional Missile Launcher gunfighter: low BS, little defensive MOD stacking, one STR, and a large silhouette.

Do not expose it just to gain a mediocre Missile Launcher ARO. Its advantage is that it can contribute **without LoF**. Hide it and preserve the payoff piece.

## Sensor / Forward Observer REM

The Sensor/FO REM is usually the most generally useful of the standard basic REMs because its roles reinforce one another.

It commonly provides:

- high movement;
- Forward Observer;
- Sensor;
- Repeater;
- Flash Pulse;
- Deactivator;
- sometimes Triangulated Fire;
- sometimes Tactical Awareness.

### Fast specialist

Its most important job is often simply being a **fast specialist**. High movement converts directly into Order efficiency when reaching objectives.

A mobile REM may spend one or two Orders reaching a console that would cost a slower infantry specialist several Orders.

### Sensor

Sensor gives the list a real answer to Camouflage, Hidden Deployment suspicion, mines, and dense skirmisher areas.

One fast Sensor piece is often enough; there is usually less need to duplicate Sensor unless the mission or table strongly rewards it.

### Mobile Repeater

The Repeater is especially valuable because the REM already wants to advance toward objectives. Moving the specialist naturally moves the hacking network at the same time.

That is stronger role compression than spending Orders on a dedicated Repeater carrier whose only task is to extend hacking coverage.

### Forward Observer

FO provides a WIP-based nonlethal attack that can apply Targeted. On a mediocre BS chassis, that can be more valuable than pretending the REM is a conventional gunfighter.

Its value rises when:

- Guided exists;
- other shooters can exploit Targeted;
- the opponent is forced to spend Orders Resetting;
- mission objectives reward Forward Observers.

### Triangulated Fire

Where present, Triangulated Fire gives a weak BS REM a situational problem-solving mode against stacked Mimetism, Cover, or bad range bands.

It does not make the REM an apex attacker, but it gives a cheap specialist a useful low-probability attack option against targets it would otherwise barely be able to engage.

### Tactical Awareness variants

Sensor/FO REMs with Tactical Awareness are particularly attractive because the extra Order can self-fund:

- movement toward an objective;
- a Sensor sweep;
- Repeater repositioning;
- part of a specialist run.

This is especially valuable in Group 2.

### When to skip the Sensor/FO bot

It becomes less necessary when:

- the mission set barely rewards specialists;
- the list already has abundant fast midfield specialists;
- Sensor is already plentiful;
- hacking-network extension is irrelevant;
- terrain makes S3 movement unusually awkward;
- the troop slot matters more than the points.

## Generic ranking of the basic REM types

As a broad starting point:

- **Flash Pulse / Repeater bot:** often a near-auto-include because of extremely efficient Regular Orders and passive utility; watch S3 deployment congestion when taking several.
- **Sensor/FO bot:** usually the most generally useful utility REM; fast specialist, Sensor, Repeater, and FO all have independent value.
- **Thunderbolt TR:** efficient reactive Order tax when the list already has strong active attackers.
- **HMG TR:** better when the list needs backup active-turn long-range firepower.
- **Cheap Baggage:** often strong simply as one of the cheapest Regular Orders; becomes much stronger with a real reload ecosystem. The second copy is more dependent on geography and base congestion.
- **Guided Missile REM:** highly list-dependent; weak as an isolated purchase, excellent as a cheap payoff for infrastructure the list already wanted.

The overall pattern is:

- **Flashbot = extremely cheap Order + passive network/ARO utility.**
- **Baggage = extremely cheap Order + logistics multiplier.**
- **TR = make the opponent spend Orders, with a possible backup-gun role.**
- **Guided = convert Targeted/network infrastructure into lethal pressure.**
- **Sensor/FO = spend your own Orders efficiently while solving missions, Camouflage, and network positioning.**
