# Impetuous and Irregular troop sequencing

**Deployment backsolve — [Loss of Lieutenant E176 Part 2 ~76–82m](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UoYjrRvC5ic&t=4560s), full transcript checked 27 Oct 2026:** Before placing an Impetuous Warband, choose the **safe end position and Smoke target** for its first constrained activation; then work **backwards** to place its base. Starting an inch behind the DZ line can allow a legal 4-inch first movement to finish behind a corner, where the same model starting on the line would be forced into an ARO or skip its free activation. The speakers also highlight **Move + Idle** as a possible N5 alternative to an unsafe second skill: check exact Impetuous/Movement constraints and table terrain. Treat the free movement as **part of a plan**, not an obligation to rush a 10pt smoke enabler to its death. [Deployment clinic](deployment-initiative-and-reserve-strategy.md#impetuous-and-peripherals-are-deployment-problems-not-only-activation-rules-e176-7685m-and-125135m).

## N5.3 Warbands / cheap trade cross-reference (2026-10-23)

[N5.3 Warbands and Cheap Trading Units](warbands-and-cheap-trading-units-apex-value.md) ranks 5–23pt smoke/template/CC/E/M models by target, delivery and Orders. **Impetuous activations are optional but constrained; Irregular personal Orders cannot power another gunfighter.** Religious Troops are excluded from Inspiring Leadership regularization; the current sectorial Fireteam chart governs any FT Master conversion.

## Core principle

Impetuous and Irregular troops should be treated as **semi-autonomous Order packages**, not merely as cheap bodies.

The best versions do useful work with resources that cannot easily be spent elsewhere:

- an Impetuous activation that costs no Order;
- the troop's own Irregular Order;
- sometimes Smoke, Eclipse, Berserk, templates, CC, or other high-impact short-range tools;
- only then, if the opportunity is good enough, pooled Regular Orders.

The central sequencing idea is:

> **Use constrained resources to create the opportunity; spend flexible resources only to extend success.**

For an Impetuous + Irregular troop, a strong turn often looks like:

**Impetuous development -> personal Irregular action -> Regular Orders only if the attack is worth extending.**

## N5.3 rules baseline

Current N5.3 matters here because some older advice assumes that an Impetuous activation must be taken.

That is no longer the practical rule:

- every Impetuous troop receives an Impetuous Token during the Tactical Phase;
- during the Impetuous Phase, the player **may** activate each such troop once without spending an Order;
- an unused Impetuous Token simply disappears at the end of the Impetuous Phase;
- if the troop does activate, its movement and allowed Skill combinations are restricted by the Impetuous rules.

So:

> **A free activation that worsens your position is not free.**

Do not expose a model merely because the activation costs no Order.

The Impetuous Phase happens before the Orders Phase. This is strategically important: you cannot first clear a sniper with a normal attacker and then return to use an Impetuous activation. If the route is bad during the Impetuous Phase, decline it and activate the model normally later if necessary.

**Movement restriction when elected:** Impetuous-phase movement must use the full corresponding MOV, prioritizing entering Silhouette contact with an enemy, otherwise progressing toward the enemy Deployment Zone under the rule's specified route priorities. Move/Jump/Climb cancels Prone, cannot finish Prone, and Impetuous troops cannot enter prohibited Marker States. Consequently, a desirable safe corner is only a legal Impetuous endpoint if those restrictions permit it; you cannot freely choose any safe development move.

Also note that Impetuous itself is not the same thing as losing Cover. Current N5 separates Impetuous from rules such as Limited Cover / No Cover. Do not import old-edition assumptions automatically. In particular, **Frenzy** can separately grant both Impetuous and Limited Cover.

**Fireteam caveat:** choosing an Impetuous-phase activation causes that trooper to leave its Fireteam; if it is the Team Leader, the Fireteam is cancelled. This is particularly important for FT Master packages and other links whose value depends on maintaining the team. See [Impetuous](../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Impetuous.md) and [Fireteam Integrity](../../rules/6_Command%20and%20Fireteams/Fireteam_Integrity.md).

## 1. Decide whether each Impetuous activation actually improves the turn

Before moving an Impetuous troop, ask:

> **Does this activation make the model's next useful action cheaper or stronger?**

Good Impetuous activations often:

- advance behind Total Cover;
- reach a useful corner;
- establish Smoke or Eclipse;
- bring a Chain Rifle, E/M weapon, Adhesive/PARA weapon, or CC threat into one-Order striking distance;
- reach an objective approach;
- occupy a blocking position;
- move a disposable defender toward an important lane;
- create a credible threat that the opponent must respect next turn.

Decline the activation when it would:

- cross a strong ARO lane for little gain;
- walk into a mine, Perimeter weapon, template trap, Repeater net, or other control zone;
- expose the troop to MSV when relying on Smoke;
- pull a DZ defender away from the lane it was protecting;
- move past the corner where the model actually wants to fight;
- abandon a strong defensive position;
- reveal or commit a troop before another part of the plan is ready;
- leave the troop somewhere its own Irregular Order will still not be useful.

### Practical rule

Do not ask **"Can I take the free move?"**

Ask **"Where does this model need to end so its own Order becomes useful?"**

---

## 2. Sequence the Impetuous Phase as a coordinated opening to the turn

Do not resolve Impetuous troops in arbitrary table order.

Their activations can alter the board for later Impetuous activations and for the entire Orders Phase.

A useful default sequence is:

1. **visibility-control pieces** — Smoke / Eclipse first where possible;
2. **safe development moves** — troops that can advance without taking real risk;
3. **troops exploiting the newly created screen**;
4. **immediately profitable attacks or trades**;
5. **marginal or dangerous activations last**, often declining them entirely.

This turns the Impetuous Phase into an opening maneuver rather than a collection of unrelated free moves.

---

## 3. Smoke from an Impetuous activation is turn infrastructure

An Impetuous activation allows combinations including Move + BS Attack, so a troop with Smoke Grenades can use them during that activation.

This is especially valuable when the Smoke does more than protect the thrower.

Example sequence:

1. Warband A advances safely and throws Smoke across a dangerous lane.
2. Warband B uses a later Impetuous activation to cross or approach through that visual screen.
3. Warband C advances on another protected angle.
4. The Orders Phase begins with several pieces already farther forward and the Smoke still affecting the table.
5. A normal attacker, specialist, or CC piece can now exploit that screen.

The important idea is:

> **The first Impetuous troop can build infrastructure for the rest of the turn.**

### Safe Smoke vs emergency Smoke

Distinguish:

**Safe Smoke:** the troop throws Smoke without first exposing itself to a dangerous attack. This is excellent Order economy.

**Emergency Smoke:** the troop's forced movement exposes it and the Smoke roll becomes its defense. This may still be worthwhile, but it is not automatically a good use of the free activation.

Do not march a warband into a high-Burst gun simply because it has Smoke.

---

## 4. The ideal Impetuous activation sets up the Irregular Order

An Irregular troop's Order belongs to that troop rather than entering the normal shared Order Pool.

Therefore, the best deployment and Impetuous movement often aim to make **one personal Order enough to perform the troop's job**.

Typical sequences:

- Impetuous Move + Smoke -> Irregular Order into CC;
- Impetuous Move + Dodge -> Irregular template attack around the corner;
- Impetuous Move + BS Attack -> Irregular Order to finish a weakened target;
- free advance -> Irregular Order to reach or operate near an objective;
- free advance -> Irregular move into a roadblock position;
- free advance -> Irregular attack with E/M, PARA, Adhesive, shotgun, Chain Rifle, etc.;
- free advance -> Irregular Order to Engage an expensive shooter.

A cheap troop that can reach its useful action using **Impetuous + Irregular** has effectively paid much of its own transportation cost.

## 5. Deploy every Irregular piece with a one-Order job in mind

A cheap Irregular model that requires three pooled Regular Orders before it can contribute may not actually be cheap in practice.

During deployment, identify a likely one-Order role for each Irregular troop.

| Role | Deployment goal |
|---|---|
| Smoke warband | One activation from a useful Smoke angle |
| CC warband | Behind Total Cover with a realistic route toward midfield |
| Template defender | Near the approach it must protect |
| Irregular specialist | Close enough that its own Order advances the scoring plan |
| Cheap ARO / observer | One movement away from a useful lane or observation point |
| Disposable flanker | On a flank where one activation creates an immediate threat |
| Perimeter / area-control piece | Able to use its own Order to reinforce the relevant zone |

The test is:

> **If this troop receives no pooled Regular Orders this turn, can its own resources still matter?**

If the answer is consistently no, reconsider either its deployment or why it is in that Combat Group.

---

## 6. Spend constrained Orders before flexible Orders — once their purpose is known

A strong Order-economy principle is:

> **Use the least flexible resource that accomplishes the required job.**

If an Irregular troop was going to activate anyway, prefer its personal Order before spending a shared Regular Order on it.

Otherwise you can end the turn having spent flexible Regular resources while leaving a personal Irregular Order stranded.

A useful hierarchy is often:

**Impetuous activation -> personal Irregular Order -> specialized / restricted resources -> shared Regular Orders.**

But this is not a rigid rule.

Hold the Irregular Order when:

- another attacker must clear a lane first;
- you do not yet know which flank will matter;
- the troop may need a late-turn reposition;
- using it now commits information or exposes the model unnecessarily;
- converting it later may become more valuable;
- the model's current personal action is weak but its later opportunity may be strong.

The principle is **constrained Orders first when their useful purpose is already clear**, not "spend every Irregular Order immediately."

---

## 7. Convert Irregular Orders only when flexibility is actually worth a Command Token

A Command Token can transform an Irregular Order into a Regular Order in that troop's Combat Group for the current Player Turn.

This is extremely useful, but it should usually be treated as **liquidity**, not as a routine tax for fielding Irregular troops.

Good reasons to convert:

- you need exactly one more Order to score;
- a primary attacker needs one more activation to complete a high-value task;
- the Irregular troop no longer has a useful personal action;
- the Irregular Order is otherwise likely to expire unused;
- another model in the same Combat Group has generated a much better opportunity.

Poor reasons to convert:

- converting automatically at the start of every turn;
- converting merely because the Order is Irregular;
- spending Command Tokens to make a badly designed Group 2 function normally every turn.

Command Tokens also compete with:

- Coordinated Orders;
- Fireteam creation / rebuilding;
- Doctor and Engineer rerolls where permitted;
- Guts manipulation;
- Retreat-related effects;
- other mission- or army-specific command uses.

So the conversion needs a concrete payoff.

### Important limitation

An Isolated troop's Irregular Order cannot be converted into a Regular Order.

## 8. Pure-Irregular Group 2s have an Order-liquidity problem

Four Irregular troops do not create a four-Order shared pool.

They create four separate one-model budgets.

That matters when one troop finds an unexpectedly strong attack.

Example:

- Warband A has its personal Order.
- Warband B has its personal Order.
- Warband C has its personal Order.
- Cheap utility troop D has its personal Order.

If Warband A suddenly needs three more activations to exploit a breach, B/C/D's Orders cannot simply be handed to it.

The group has **activity**, but little **liquidity**.

This is why a small secondary group often functions better with at least a small Regular pool.

---

## 9. Build Group 2 around troops that largely self-fund

A strong secondary Combat Group can contain several pieces whose normal role requires little shared funding.

Example concept:

- 2-3 Impetuous + Irregular warbands;
- 1-2 cheap Regular generators;
- possibly a utility or defensive piece.

Each warband may have:

- one Impetuous activation;
- one personal Irregular Order;
- access to the small shared Regular pool only if it finds a particularly valuable opportunity.

This makes the Regular Orders function like an **extension cord** for the best-performing piece rather than the group's primary fuel.

### Good Group 2 archetypes

#### Autonomous assault group

Several self-funded close-range threats plus a small Regular pool.

Purpose:

- attack one flank;
- throw Smoke;
- trade into enemy midfield pieces;
- threaten objectives;
- create disorder without consuming Group 1's main attack budget.

#### Defensive / utility group

Cheap AROs, DZ guards, Irregular observers, disposable blockers, or similar pieces.

Purpose:

- perform jobs that rarely need several consecutive Orders;
- preserve Group 1 for active-turn offense;
- occasionally convert an otherwise unused Irregular Order when needed.

#### Development group

Cheap Impetuous / Irregular models whose own resources move them toward midfield during Turns 1-2.

Purpose:

- improve board position cheaply;
- create future Turn 2-3 threats;
- absorb enemy attention without draining Group 1.

### Bad Group 2 pattern

Several troops are technically cheap, but all of them require pooled Orders before they can do anything meaningful.

That group will look efficient on the army list and feel starved on the table.

---

## 10. Regular Orders in Group 2 are opportunity capital

Do not mentally assign every Regular Order in a mixed secondary group in advance.

Their highest value is often letting whichever self-funded piece succeeds **continue**.

Example:

1. Warband A uses Impetuous movement and dies accomplishing nothing.
2. Warband B uses Impetuous + Irregular to remove a midfield defender.
3. That opens access to an Engineer, Lieutenant, objective, or expensive gunfighter.
4. The group's two Regular Orders now go to Warband B because the board state has changed.

The Regular pool lets the group exploit **emergent success**.

That is much stronger than spreading one extra Regular Order across every cheap piece regardless of opportunity.

## 11. Inspiring Leadership changes Irregular economics completely

Current Inspiring Leadership states that, while the user is the Lieutenant, on the table, and not in a Null State, all troops in the Army List that provide Orders possess the Regular Training Characteristic.

That transforms eligible cheap Irregular bodies from isolated private budgets into shared Order generators.

**Eligibility exception:** troops with the **Religious Troop** Special Skill do not gain the army-wide Regular Training/Courage effects from Inspiring Leadership. The skill also provides other conditional benefits (including a special Lieutenant-Order Coordinated Order), but this section evaluates its Order-pool effect only. See [Inspiring Leadership](../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/Inspiring_Leadership.md).

Without Inspiring Leadership:

**4 Irregular troops = four private Orders.**

With Inspiring Leadership active:

**those troops provide flexible Regular Orders to their respective Combat Groups.**

This makes cheap warbands particularly efficient because the same model can provide:

- a Regular Order;
- an Impetuous activation if it has Impetuous;
- Smoke / Eclipse;
- templates;
- CC;
- roadblocking;
- defensive trades;
- objective pressure.

### Strategic consequence

An Inspiring Leadership Lieutenant may be part of the army's **Order engine**, not merely its command piece.

Losing that Lieutenant or having the skill cease to apply can sharply reduce the army's flexibility on the following Order Count.

Protect the source of regularization accordingly.

## 12. FT Master can create a localized regularization engine

FT Master provides another way to regularize otherwise Irregular troops.

During the Order Count step, if the FT Master is part of a Fireteam, all members of that Fireteam are Regular.

This can make a Fireteam containing cheap normally-Irregular bodies into an efficient Order package.

Strategically, this creates two modes:

### Cohesive mode

Keep the troop inside the Fireteam so it contributes to the Regular Order economy and Fireteam structure.

### Release mode

Break or leave the team when the troop's independent mobility, Impetuous behavior, trade potential, or personal attack is worth more than maintaining the formation.

The important lesson is not to evaluate a normally-Irregular profile in isolation when an FT Master package changes how its Order is generated.

**Timing and team dependency:** FT Master regularizes members **during the Order Count step** if the master is in a valid Fireteam. An Impetuous-phase activation causes its member to leave the Fireteam (or cancels it if that member is Leader); plan for the resulting Fireteam and future Order Count consequences instead of assuming the link survives an independent rush. An Irregular member spending an Irregular Order likewise leaves a Fireteam under Fireteam Integrity rules. The FT Master ability does not prevent Loss of Lieutenant. See [FT Master](../../rules/5b_Special%20Skills/FT_Master.md) and [Fireteam Integrity](../../rules/6_Command%20and%20Fireteams/Fireteam_Integrity.md).

## 13. Impetuous activations are best for low-value movement and high-value setup

The free activation is disproportionately valuable for movement you would otherwise resent paying a Regular Order for:

- leaving the DZ;
- traversing safe ground behind buildings;
- reaching a corner;
- establishing Smoke;
- approaching an objective;
- moving a disposable template piece toward a choke point;
- setting up a future short-range attack.

It is much less attractive for:

- crossing an enemy-controlled firelane;
- entering a layered mine / Repeater / Perimeter defense;
- exposing a model to a Burst-heavy ARO;
- moving into MSV-controlled Smoke;
- abandoning a useful defensive position.

Think of Impetuous as:

> **Free logistics with positional restrictions, not free aggression.**

---

## 14. Cheap warbands are often strongest when they do not attack immediately

The presence of Impetuous, Smoke, templates, and strong CC can tempt players into automatic suicide runs.

But a warband may be more valuable by ending its activation:

- behind a corner threatening a template;
- within one movement of an objective;
- blocking a route;
- threatening CC if an enemy advances;
- screening a more valuable troop;
- covering the army's retreat route;
- forcing the opponent to spend Orders clearing it next turn.

A 5-10 point piece that forces two or three enemy Orders may have accomplished more than one that immediately trades into another cheap model.

This links Irregular sequencing directly to defensive architecture and trading strategy.

## 15. Do not let the Impetuous Phase sabotage the real turn plan

Before activating any Impetuous troop, identify the turn's actual priorities:

- required scoring;
- primary attack;
- objective routes;
- expected retreat / end position;
- important Smoke lanes;
- models that must remain hidden;
- Regular Orders that must stay available.

Then evaluate every Impetuous activation against that plan.

Common mistakes include:

- moving a warband into the path of your own attacker;
- throwing Smoke that also blocks your own non-MSV firelane;
- exposing a troop whose survival mattered more than its free movement;
- abandoning DZ defense before an enemy Parachutist has been dealt with;
- advancing so far that the model cannot later retreat to a useful position;
- chasing a tempting target that contributes nothing to the mission plan.

The Impetuous Phase should **support** the Orders Phase, not dictate it.

## 16. Turn-1, Turn-2, and Turn-3 usage differs

### Turn 1 — development

Prioritize:

- safe free movement;
- Smoke infrastructure;
- creating midfield threats;
- occupying useful corners;
- forcing the opponent to respect short-range threats;
- preserving pieces that may be valuable later.

Do not burn every warband simply because it can reach something.

### Turn 2 — exploitation and attrition

This is often the best turn for:

- pushing through gaps created on Turn 1;
- trading into enemy support pieces;
- attacking Engineers, Hackers, Doctors, specialists, and Order generators;
- consuming enemy Orders with aggressive positioning;
- using Group 2 Regular Orders to extend whichever self-funded piece breaks through.

### Turn 3 — scoring and denial

The value of preservation changes dramatically.

A cheap Irregular or Impetuous troop may now be worth more for:

- reaching a zone;
- touching an objective;
- contesting an area;
- body-blocking a route;
- killing or Engaging an enemy specialist;
- forcing one final enemy Order expenditure.

Points efficiency matters less than **mission-state efficiency**.

## 17. Practical turn algorithm

At the start of the turn:

### Before the Impetuous Phase

1. Identify the minimum Orders required for scoring.
2. Identify the primary attack or development plan.
3. Identify Smoke / Eclipse lanes that would improve that plan.
4. Decide which Irregular troops must survive for later jobs.

### During the Impetuous Phase

5. Examine every Impetuous token before moving anything.
6. Establish useful Smoke / Eclipse first.
7. Advance troops that benefit safely from that screen.
8. Take immediately profitable attacks or trades.
9. Decline activations that create more risk than value.

### During the Orders Phase

10. Spend personal Irregular Orders when their useful action is clear.
11. Observe which self-funded troop has created the best opportunity.
12. Feed that troop shared Regular Orders only if the marginal return remains good.
13. Convert an Irregular Order only when the resulting flexibility has a concrete higher-value use.
14. Stop the attack when continuing would consume Orders required for scoring, retreat, or preservation.

---

## 18. Listbuilding checklist

For every Irregular / Impetuous troop, ask:

| Question | Desired answer |
|---|---|
| Can its Impetuous activation usually improve position safely? | Yes |
| Can its own Irregular Order perform a real job? | Yes |
| Does it need pooled Orders merely to become relevant? | Usually no |
| Does it have a one-Order role from deployment? | Yes |
| Does Group 2 contain at least some liquidity if one piece succeeds? | Usually |
| Am I relying on Command Token conversion every turn? | Preferably no |
| Does Smoke / Eclipse from this troop help other pieces too? | Ideally |
| Can the troop remain useful without immediately attacking? | Yes |
| If regularized by Inspiring Leadership / FT Master, have I accounted for the dependency? | Yes |
| Can the army still function if the regularization engine disappears? | Ideally yes |

---

## Compact doctrine

**Impetuous develops -> Irregular performs -> Regular extends -> Command Token rescues flexibility.**

Build Irregular troops so their **own Order is useful**.

Build Impetuous troops so the free activation places them where that personal Order becomes useful.

Build Group 2 so most pieces **self-fund**, while a small Regular pool lets the best opportunity continue.

And above all:

> **Never spend a free activation merely because it is free. Spend it because it improves the rest of the turn.**
