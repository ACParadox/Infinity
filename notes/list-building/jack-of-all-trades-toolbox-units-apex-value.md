# Jack-of-All-Trades / Toolbox Units — N5.3 Apex and Value

_Research: 2026-10-23. **Qualitative tier judgment, not a measured win-rate ranking.** Prices and legal Fireteams are **profile-specific** in the committed [N5.3 sectorial references](../../sectorials/); media are for tactical reasoning, not rules or prices. A “toolbox” can be a single all-rounder or a **small legal team** whose roles work together, but **never credit one profile with another's equipment**._

## How to rank real versatility (not simply count skills)

1. **Two or more competent, usable jobs.** Strong active gun, real WIP/remote hacking/Engineer, accessible Specialist, threatening melee, deployable denial, safe command or active mobility. A Pistol does not make a hacker a viable long-range gunfighter.
2. **Same route, same Orders.** A model going forward to attack can score a nearby objective or clear its own mines. An Engineer left in the DZ and a short-range gunfighter stranded away from REMs have **theoretical** roles.
3. **A plan if one subsystem is countered.** A competent gun still fights nonhackables after hacking fails; a KHD cannot automatically Oblivion TAGs without a printed program upgrade.
4. **Actual complete price.** Count SWC, legal teammates, extra delivery Orders, engineer targets, defensive support, group/Fireteam cost and **Specialist status of the chosen profile**. A linked module can have more total capability but lose on points efficiency.
5. **Surviving and completing objectives.** A 60pt hero with everything can monopolize Orders and expose a single failure point. Two 25–35pt independent toolboxes may win more missions.

**Tier meaning:** S = exceptional role-compression ceiling/value; A = strong or mission-specific; B = useful secondary jobs but costly to buy only as toolbox; C = inefficient role conflict. **Apex and value are separate judgments**; units are not implied legal across every sectorial of a parent faction.

## Solo versus compact team: which wins?

| Scenario | Buy | Why / don't buy |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown mission, lots of cyber/vision problems, one elite centerpiece | **Asura HD+** | Hard to make completely irrelevant, but add cheap separate scorers to reduce failure risk |
| Vertical rooftop objective guarded by MSV/ARO and CC targets | **Shikami42** | Eclipse+Climbing/Super-Jump+2W+Specialist; ordinary durable attacker may fail to reach it |
| Enemy HI/TAG and Hackers share a central lane | **Nourkias48** or **Mary27** | Nourkias fights/CCs the exposed heavy; Mary is the safer, cheaper projected-cyber answer |
| Shas midfield chaos, LT assassination threat, hard buttons | **Caliban selected FO/CoC/Engineer** | Pick **one profile** based on need; Camo/Protheion remains valuable even after job changes |
| Tohaa mission requires contesting and holding a dense midfield | **Draal Dazer or Viral Mine** | Deny movement while scoring, not just shooting a high-point gun |
| A Gecko/REM attack requires a close repairer with a second gun | **Evader Engineer** or **Gecko+Evader+Stempler** | Full module beats solo at redundancy; the 24pt Engineer often beats a 35pt Spitfire on value |
| PanO cheap Fireteam needs a scanner, scored button and safe approach | **Fennec13** | Cheap points/SWC superstar, but can't win a high-quality FtF gunfight |
| A durable infowar hacker also needs an attack profile in MO | **De Fersen Spitfire46** | MO's premium BS14/WIP14 hybrid; don't spend 46 to hack once then sit behind Total Cover |
| Need stealthy hacking reach from midfield | **Mary27** vs **Moran15** | Mary attacks Hacker/HI, Moran **projects** a safer Repeater and stalls movement; two distinct specializations |
| Need one versatile fast gun and nearby independent score/Doctor | **Mukhtar Duo** | Allows two specialists/jobs at different positions, but cannot claim one model owns both gun and Doctor |
| Need an all-in-one elite killer but not score | **Sheskiin** | More reliable proactive gunfights than many true toolboxes; pair with separate objective units |

**Practical construction test:** at list-writing time identify **two actions the unit will probably take during the same game**, and one plausible third fallback after the enemy counters the first. If you cannot name these, buy the simpler/cheaper profile. On the table, spend active Orders on the **mission**, not on using every weapon simply because it was purchased.

## Opposing opinions and evidence

1. **Broad skills ≠ a good toolbox.** [Infinity the Dante — Toolbox Units Guide N5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q5kyCDufa8c) (YouTube MCP transcript checked) praises the Evader **35pt Engineer + AP Spitfire** and De Fersen because both primary jobs meet a high competence bar, but strongly criticizes Wildcat Engineer role/position conflict and considers Elena Kovac mixed. **Counter:** an underwhelming solo unit can be a legal Fireteam filler, objective specialist or matchup tech. A 2025 evaluation cannot determine updated roster legality.
2. **Asura versatility or cost bloat?** [Shepherd + Ollie — Mazebreaker OSS/Corregidor analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NRRP4Fects8) (full YouTube MCP) calls Asura a premium infowar attacker and an efficient source of useful activations; observes she takes **fair gunfights** without Mimetism. [Loss of Lieutenant E168](https://open.spotify.com/episode/4MxU8qsoJlbZkYCLt1fo6d) (Podcast MCP) debates hacker vs AP gun and whether 60+pt role compression is worth it. Both: choose **HD+63** for toolbox, **AP Spitfire61** for anti-armor gun.
3. **Evader Engineer or simply specialist gun?** [WIP12 E208 — Corregidor after Mazebreaker](https://open.spotify.com/episode/342lz18SRGSZ8QWf1Zg3Z9) (Podcast MCP, esp. ~44–51m) likes **Gecko + Evader + Stempler**, using repair/network/FT + attack in one order economy; **counter:** very expensive small team and Haris slot. The pinned file explicitly separates **35pt Engineer** and **31pt Specialist Operative** AP Spitfires.
4. **Shikami solo versus expensive double Shikami.** [Shepherd — Ōban faction focus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AhicCreNCUQ) praises Shikami's movement + Eclipse + specialist toolkit; [Tactical Awareness S3E16](https://open.spotify.com/episode/5EJe58gg1Lyo9xZxff2g75) discusses two Shikami and objective access. **For two:** redundancy and multiple approaches; **against:** same elite Order demand, fewer other tools. Take two **only** if both have separate work.
5. **Caliban swiss-army knife vs concentrated gun.** [MetaChemistry #139 — 2026 Shasvastii tournament winner](https://open.spotify.com/episode/6FErkreAsQoUKosH4WkdPW) (Podcast MCP, around ~18–49m) discusses triple Caliban, a returning MSV Spitfire, Protheion survivability and protected late scoring. **For:** Camo, multiple specialists, CC and recovery snowball; **against:** 1W and profile role split, need enough direct long-range shooting.
6. **Nourkias as universal problem solver vs Sheskiin simple gun.** [Shepherd — Combined Army N5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qoyylw0p0JM) (YouTube MCP) stresses that Nourkias needs delivery/hackable/close targets, while Sheskiin handles many problems **just by shooting**. **For Nourkias:** more job types, hacker/CC/anti-TAG; **for Sheskiin:** better first fight and fewer constraints, but **not a scoring specialist**.
7. **Faction toolboxes versus durable specialists.** [Shepherd — Haqqislam N5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dmv4pfdTbc0) (YouTube MCP) favors fast Mukhtar and practical specialist Duos; some role-hungry coordinated-order lists prefer **unlinked** Mukhtars for activation flexibility. [Shepherd's Ariadna CanCon review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zTqYE_FddwQ) and [Loss of Lieutenant #164](https://open.spotify.com/episode/0X4QIoP24cKONtltxClHMH) favor several independent cheap flexible specialists rather than a single deluxe hero.
8. **Karakuri durable objective anchor versus Shikami independent scorer.** In Ōban, Karakuri win repeated midline **hold** tests due to STR3 and repair potential; Shikami is better to **arrive safely** behind MSV/height. Paying for both is correct only with sufficient pooled Orders and meaningful distinct roles.
