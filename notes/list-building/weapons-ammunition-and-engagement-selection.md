# Weapons, ammunition and engagement selection

**Current unit-level comparison:** [Active-turn gunfighter apex/value rankings](active-turn-gunfighters-apex-and-value.md) applies these weapon and range-band principles to exact 2026 N5.3 profiles, movement/exit Orders, Fireteams and separate TAG comparisons. This is the **how-to** primer; the linked guide ranks **who to field and when**.

## Core principle

Weapon choice in Infinity is not mainly a question of **which gun is strongest**.

It is an **engagement-selection problem**:

> Given this target, this position, these range bands, these defenses, and these possible consequences, what weapon or mode gives the best result for the Orders being spent?

A useful default hierarchy is:

1. **position / range band**;
2. **Face to Face odds**;
3. **Burst**;
4. **target defense**;
5. **PS / ammunition effect**;
6. **secondary effects and follow-up position**.

In an opposed gunfight, winning the Face to Face Roll usually matters more than having spectacular ammunition.

Once the target cannot oppose effectively, ammunition and PS become much more important.

### Short version

**Burst and MODs get hits through. Ammunition and PS convert hits into consequences.**

> N5 terminology note: lower PS is more dangerous. Do not read a larger PS number as "more damage."

**Rules baseline:** the project's [N5.3 + N5 FAQ v0.1](../../rules/README.md). Exact weapon characteristics come from the current [Weapon Chart](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/Weapon_Chart.md), with the [N5.3 weapon updates](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/N5.3_Weapon_Updates.md) taking precedence over older generated entries. The [Possibility of Survival (PS)](../../rules/3_Combat/Possibility_of_Survival_%28PS%29.md) rule defines Saving Roll Success Value as applicable ARM/BTS + PS + Cover (+3 where relevant) + other MODs.

**Related strategy:** [Probability and Face-to-Face decisions](probability-and-face-to-face-decision-framework.md), [Target priority and attack sequencing](target-priority-and-attack-sequencing.md), [List robustness and threat coverage](list-robustness-and-threat-coverage.md), [Trading and attrition](trading-and-attrition.md), [Terrain and engagement geometry](terrain-silhouettes-and-table-geometry.md), and [ARO units](aro-units-apex-and-value.md).

## 1. Think in engagements, not weapon rankings

Do not rank weapons in isolation.

The same weapon can be excellent or poor depending on:

- starting position;
- distance to target;
- carrier mobility;
- whether the target can return fire;
- Cover;
- Mimetism / MSV;
- target ARM / BTS / PH;
- target Wounds / STR;
- whether the target is Hackable;
- whether nearby models can be caught by templates;
- whether the attacker is expendable;
- whether the objective is to kill, disable, move, or merely tax Orders.

A weapon profile only becomes meaningful when attached to a **carrier and battlefield position**.

For example:

- an HMG on a slow backline trooper is a long-range attack tool;
- a Spitfire on an Infiltrator starts close enough that its shorter range is barely a weakness;
- a Boarding Shotgun on an airborne attacker can become a DZ assassination weapon;
- a Missile Launcher on a cheap ARO body can be valuable even if it almost never fires in the active turn.

# 2. Active-turn gun families

## HMG

The HMG is the default **long-range active-turn gun**.

Its main strategic advantages are:

- high Burst;
- strong PS;
- a broad useful long-range band;
- the ability to attack enemy ARO pieces without first spending several Orders closing distance;
- enough range to exploit terrain and pie-slicing from your own half of the table.

### Use an HMG when

- you need to solve enemy long-range AROs;
- the attacker begins in or near your Deployment Zone;
- the table has substantial open lanes;
- you want one model to project force across multiple areas;
- the target's shorter weapon can be forced into a bad range band.

### Main weakness

Close range.

Do not treat an HMG carrier as automatically good at every distance.

A strong HMG platform becomes much more complete if it also carries something like:

- Heavy Pistol;
- template weapon;
- shotgun;
- strong CC;
- E/M / PARA secondary weapon.

The secondary weapon is often what prevents an expensive HMG platform from becoming vulnerable once something reaches its minimum comfortable range.

---

## Spitfire

The Spitfire is not simply a weaker HMG.

It is an **aggressive medium-range weapon**.

Its ideal carrier is usually able to manufacture the preferred engagement distance through:

- Forward Deployment;
- Infiltration;
- Camouflage;
- motorcycles;
- high MOV;
- Climbing Plus;
- Super-Jump;
- Airborne Deployment;
- Smoke / Eclipse;
- good terrain access.

### Use a Spitfire when

- you expect to fight mainly in the midfield;
- the carrier already begins far forward;
- the piece can quickly reach 8-24 inch fights;
- you want high Burst without paying for extreme range;
- terrain naturally shortens engagement distances.

### Warning sign

If the Spitfire carrier regularly spends two or three Orders merely reaching a useful firing position before attacking, the list may actually need an HMG or another longer-ranged solution.

---

## Red Fury

The Red Fury occupies similar battlefield space to the Spitfire but is generally better understood as an **anti-light / anti-medium infantry sweeper**.

Its strengths include:

- high Burst;
- useful medium-range bands;
- Shock on the generic profile;
- somewhat forgiving reach compared with some shorter weapons;
- frequent availability on mobile attack pieces.

### Use a Red Fury when

- the expected targets are mostly one-Wound infantry;
- Shock matters;
- you want a medium-range sweeper rather than a dedicated heavy-armour killer;
- the carrier supplies strong MODs such as Mimetism or Marksmanship.

An **AP Red Fury** is a different category: high Burst plus AP makes it a much more credible armour solution.

**N5.3 distinction:** the added AP Red Fury has **B4, PS 7, AP (ARM/2)** rather than the standard Red Fury's Shock. It provides armour penetration, not the same Shock interaction as the generic version. See [N5.3 weapon updates](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/N5.3_Weapon_Updates.md).

---

## Marksman Rifle

Marksman Rifles are frequently underestimated because the base weapon looks less spectacular than an HMG or Spitfire.

Their real strength is **efficient medium-range flexibility**.

They become particularly good when the carrier already has:

- high BS;
- Mimetism;
- Marksmanship;
- Surprise Attack;
- forward deployment;
- Camo;
- unusual ammunition such as AP, Breaker, K1, or Viral.

### Use a Marksman Rifle when

- the piece is a secondary attacker rather than the army's only gunfighter;
- the carrier's MOD stack is doing much of the work;
- you want to exploit a range mismatch rather than brute-force a superior HMG;
- you need a specialized ammo type in a flexible medium-range platform.

### Key principle

Do not ask whether a Marksman Rifle is stronger than an HMG.

Ask whether you can force the HMG to fight **outside the HMG's preferred geometry while your Marksman Rifle remains comfortable**.

# 3. Long-range ARO and heavy weapons

## Sniper Rifle

The Sniper Rifle's primary value is **range dominance**, not raw damage.

It is best used when the opponent's weapon can be forced into a worse band.

### Use a sniper when

- controlling very long lanes;
- forcing HMG / Spitfire / rifle users into poor MODs;
- protecting a lane the opponent cannot simply ignore;
- exploiting strong BS, Mimetism, MSV, Fireteam bonuses, or Hidden Deployment.

A sniper that is fighting another long-range weapon on equal MODs is much less impressive than a sniper forcing a short-range attacker to engage from the wrong distance.

### MULTI Sniper

The MULTI version is especially valuable because ammunition choice can be adapted to target type.

Think in terms of:

- AP against armour;
- DA when multiple saves matter;
- Shock against vulnerable one-Wound targets where applicable.

---

## Missile Launcher

The Missile Launcher is a **high-consequence, low-Burst weapon**.

It is usually much more attractive as an ARO piece than as a primary active-turn gun.

Why:

- in ARO, Burst is already limited;
- one successful missile hit can be devastating;
- template mode punishes clustering;
- hit mode can punish armoured targets;
- the threat forces the opponent to respect the lane even if the launcher never scores a hit.

### Blast mode

Prefer Blast when:

- several enemies are clustered;
- a Fireteam is bunched up;
- a Doctor is beside a patient;
- an Engineer is standing beside a TAG / REM;
- a cheap secondary target can be clipped while attacking the main model;
- damaging several models is more valuable than maximizing damage against one.

### Hit mode

Prefer Hit when:

- the main target is the entire problem;
- AP matters;
- fighting TAGs or heavy infantry;
- there are no meaningful secondary targets.

### Common mistake

Do not use a Missile Launcher merely because EXP looks impressive.

A B1 active-turn attack against a superior gunfighter may simply die before the ammunition matters.

---

## Feuerbach

The Feuerbach is one of the clearest examples of mode selection.

### Burst mode

Use the higher-Burst AP+DA mode when:

- active-turn Face to Face odds matter;
- attacking armour;
- you need reliability as well as lethality.

### EXP mode

The EXP mode becomes more attractive when:

- firing in ARO;
- Burst is already reduced;
- one successful hit needs maximum consequence.

### General lesson

**Multiple-save ammunition becomes more valuable as your available Burst decreases.**

That is why a low-Burst EXP weapon can be an excellent ARO despite being an awkward active-turn problem solver.

---

## Thunderbolt

The Thunderbolt is best thought of as a **medium-to-long-range control gun**.

It is useful when:

- you need a secondary long-range weapon;
- a Fireteam wants credible reach without a full sniper / HMG role;
- the carrier expects to fight from midfield toward the enemy Deployment Zone;
- the army already has a primary B4 attacker.

It is less attractive as the army's only active gun if you frequently need to brute-force strong AROs, because lower Burst is less reliable in repeated Face to Face engagements.

**N5.3 distinction:** the standard Thunderbolt is **B2, PS 6, Normal versus ARM**, while the **AP Thunderbolt** added in N5.3 keeps the same B/PS/range bands but forces **ARM/2** saves. Select the AP version for armour pressure where available; neither becomes a B4 active-turn bully merely by changing ammunition. See [N5.3 weapon updates](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/N5.3_Weapon_Updates.md).

---

# 4. Burst versus lethality

The most important weapon-choice distinction is between:

1. **probability of winning the Face to Face Roll**;
2. **consequence after a hit lands**.

High Burst improves the first.

Low PS, AP, DA, EXP, Continuous, and other ammunition effects improve the second.

## Prefer Burst when

- the enemy can shoot back;
- the enemy has high BS;
- the enemy has Mimetism;
- losing the attacker would be expensive;
- you need consistency;
- clearing several normal ARO pieces;
- your ammunition is already sufficient to hurt the target.

## Prefer maximum hit consequence when

- the attack is unopposed;
- the target is Immobilized;
- the target has been forced into a weak Dodge / Reset;
- you are firing in ARO and Burst is already low;
- the target is extremely valuable;
- one successful hit needs to disable the target immediately;
- you already have a large advantage to hit.

### Practical rule

Do not choose EXP over B4 simply because three saves look impressive.

First ask:

> How likely am I to win the roll and make the target roll any saves at all?

---

# 5. AP, DA, EXP and Continuous

## AP: reduce the defense

AP is primarily an **armour / BTS efficiency tool**.

Use it against:

- TAGs;
- heavy infantry;
- armoured REMs;
- high-ARM targets in Cover;
- high-BTS targets when using an ammunition type that applies AP to BTS.

AP is less valuable against models with little relevant defence.

Do not pay a tactical premium to halve ARM 0 or ARM 1 when ordinary ammunition already solves the target.

**Rules nuance:** AP halves the specified **ARM or BTS**, **rounding up**; values above zero cannot be reduced below one. AP does not itself remove a valid Partial Cover bonus. See [AP Ammunition](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/Armor_Piercing_%28AP%29_Ammunition.md).

---

## DA: multiply saves

DA is strongest when:

- each individual save is already difficult;
- the target has multiple Wounds / STR;
- one hit needs to have a serious chance of removing the model;
- firing a lower-Burst weapon where each successful hit must matter.

---

## EXP: maximum save pressure

EXP is the same strategic idea taken further.

It becomes especially valuable on:

- ARO pieces;
- disposable weapons;
- weapons that only expect one successful hit;
- attacks against multi-Wound or multi-STR targets.

But EXP does not repair poor Face to Face odds.

---

## AP+DA / AP+EXP

Combined ammunition is premium anti-armour because it attacks both parts of the defensive problem:

- reduce the quality of each save;
- increase the number of saves.

This is why weapons like Feuerbachs, Panzerfausts and anti-armour missile modes can be disproportionately dangerous to TAGs and HI.

---

## Continuous

Continuous Damage is strongest when the target is already likely to fail its first save.

Once a save is failed, the effect can continue generating further saves until the target finally succeeds or is removed. Apply the current Continuous Damage rules on the relevant weapon or ammunition profile; this is not an additional Burst or an automatic Face-to-Face advantage.

### Best targets

- light infantry;
- poor ARM targets;
- models that cannot afford even one failed save;
- clustered troops caught by flamethrower-style templates.

### Strategic lesson

Continuous does not need a huge headline PS value to be dangerous.

Its value comes from turning **one failed save into a possible chain of further saves**.

---

# 6. E/M

E/M attacks **functionality**, not merely Wounds.

Against the right target, a living but Isolated / Immobilized model can be almost as useless as a dead one.

E/M is especially valuable against:

- TAGs;
- heavy infantry;
- REMs;
- Vehicles;
- Lieutenants;
- Fireteam anchors;
- expensive attack pieces;
- models whose Regular Order is strategically important.

### Why E/M is strong

A successful E/M attack can impose severe states while attacking BTS rather than ARM. **One E/M hit forces two BTS/2 Saving Rolls**; one failed save causes **Isolated**, and **HI, TAG, REM and VH** also enter **Immobilized-B**. Pure E/M is non-lethal control rather than a wound-dealing attack; combined N+E/M has additional effects. See [E/M Ammunition](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/Electromagnetic_%28E_M%29_Ammunition.md) and [Combined Ammunition](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/Combined_Ammunition.md).

Against HI / REM / TAG / Vehicle targets, the combination of **Isolation + IMM-B pressure** can completely stop an attack run.

### E/M versus killing

Against a 70-point TAG, killing it may require several Orders.

If one E/M attack leaves it Isolated and Immobilized, the practical result may already be:

- attack ended;
- Regular Order lost or compromised;
- Engineer Orders required;
- opponent forced to divert resources.

That can be a better Order trade than continuing to shoot for STR loss.

### Best target profile

Particularly attractive against:

**high ARM + weaker BTS + high strategic value.**

### Less attractive against

- cheap disposable infantry;
- targets that are easier to kill conventionally;
- models whose Isolation does not meaningfully affect the opponent's plan;
- targets standing beside an Engineer who can easily restore them.

---

# 7. PARA / Adhesive

PARA attacks PH rather than ARM or BTS.

**Rules nuance:** PARA generally imposes a **PH−6 Saving Roll** and causes **Immobilized-A** on failure; it does not wound the target. A target **without a PH Attribute ignores PARA**, so this is not a universal anti-REM or anti-TAG shortcut. See [PARA Ammunition](../../rules/4_Ammunition%20and%20Weaponry/Paralysis_%28PARA%29_Ammunition.md).

This makes it a **defence-bypass control tool**.

Adhesive Launchers and similar weapons using PARA mechanics are useful because even extremely armoured models cannot simply rely on ARM.

### Use PARA against

- high ARM;
- high BTS;
- expensive gunfighters;
- TAGs / HI when PH is a more vulnerable axis;
- models that only need to be neutralized temporarily;
- targets you plan to finish with another weapon afterward.

### Common sequence

**PARA -> Immobilize -> switch to lethal weapon / CC / objective play.**

The first attack does not need to kill the target if it converts a dangerous Face to Face gunfight into a much easier follow-up.

---

# 8. Stun

Stun is valuable when **removing combat effectiveness is enough**.

The classic example is the Flash Pulse.

**Exact state effect:** Stunned troops **cannot declare Attacks** and take a **−3 MOD to their Rolls except Saving Rolls**. The state is automatically cancelled at the **start of the States Phase of the Player Turn in which it was caused** (with possible Doctor/Engineer removal under the state rules). Its value is denying the remaining attack sequence, not permanent shutdown. See [Stunned State](../../rules/8_Game%20States%20and%20Glossary/Stunned_State.md).

A cheap model does not need to kill an expensive attacker to succeed defensively.

If the attacker:

- spends Orders reaching the lane;
- gets Stunned;
- is forced to reposition;
- loses offensive efficiency for the remainder of the turn;

then the cheap ARO piece may already have generated excellent value.

### Use Stun when

- killing the target is unrealistic;
- delaying the attack is enough;
- the defender is cheap;
- the opponent has limited Orders remaining;
- the target's offensive MODs are what make it dangerous.

---

# 9. Attack ARM, BTS or PH?

Before attacking an important target, identify the vulnerable defensive axis.

Check:

- ARM;
- BTS;
- PH;
- Wounds / STR;
- troop type;
- Hackable status;
- immunities / state interactions;
- nearby Engineer / Doctor support.

## High ARM, low BTS

Prefer options such as:

- Breaker;
- Viral where available;
- E/M;
- hacking;
- BTS-targeting templates;
- other effects that bypass ARM.

## High BTS, moderate ARM

Shoot it conventionally.

Do not use a BTS-targeting weapon merely because it is exotic.

## High ARM and high BTS

Look for ways to bypass or reduce both:

- AP;
- K1;
- PARA;
- AP+DA;
- AP+EXP;
- E/M if the resulting states are strategically useful;
- hacking programs that impose states rather than relying on raw damage.

## K1

K1 becomes especially valuable against extreme ARM because it removes much of the benefit the target paid for.

The higher the target's ARM, the more attractive K1 becomes relative to ordinary ammunition.

---

# 10. Shotguns

Shotguns should be understood as **range-band ambush weapons**.

Their main strategic strength is the huge close-range modifier advantage they can create.

### Use a shotgun when

- inside the best close band;
- the opponent's HMG / sniper / Missile Launcher is suffering poor range MODs;
- entering from Airborne Deployment near the enemy DZ;
- attacking around corners;
- pushing into rooms / corridors;
- the carrier can reach close range cheaply.

### Key idea

A shotgun attack is often powerful not because the weapon has magical ammunition, but because the engagement becomes something like:

**your large positive range MOD versus their negative range MOD.**

That differential can outweigh raw BS differences.

### Common mistake

Do not keep using a shotgun at mediocre distance simply because the profile technically allows it.

The weapon is telling you to **change position first**.

---

# 11. Direct Template Weapons

Direct Templates change the structure of the engagement.

Instead of rolling BS to win a Face to Face gunfight, the template automatically threatens models under it and the opponent must decide how to respond.

**Geometry still matters:** a legal Direct Template must include its Main Target in the Area of Effect, and scenery providing Total Cover can block effect from the template's **Blast Focus**. A template trade is not a way to attack freely through solid cover. See [Direct Template Weapons](../../rules/3_Combat/Direct_Template_Weapons.md) and [Template Weapons and Equipment](../../rules/3_Combat/Template_Weapons_and_Equipment.md).

### The key question

> Do I want to win a gunfight, or do I want to force / accept a trade?

## Use a Direct Template when

- the enemy's BS / Mimetism makes shooting unreliable;
- several enemies can be hit;
- the attacker is disposable;
- the target is much more expensive than the attacker;
- the template carries a particularly strong effect such as Continuous, E/M, or PARA;
- forcing a Dodge is itself useful;
- you want to punish a model that cannot afford to take the hit.

## Do not template automatically

Example:

A 40-point HI reaches a 10-point rifleman at close range.

If the HI templates and the rifleman shoots back, the HI may accept an unnecessary Normal Roll.

If the HI instead uses a shotgun / pistol / superior gun and wins the Face to Face Roll, the expensive attacker can survive and continue the attack run.

### Strategic distinction

**Face to Face weapon = preserve the attacker.**

**Direct Template = force consequences / trades.**

---

# 12. Impact Templates

Impact Templates punish **clustering and support positioning**.

Their value is not merely the attack against the Main Target.

The real question is:

> What else can be caught if this roll succeeds?

Excellent targets include:

- Fireteams standing too close together;
- Doctor beside patient;
- Engineer beside TAG / REM;
- clustered cheerleaders;
- troops grouped around an objective;
- models standing immediately behind a primary ARO piece.

### Deployment implication

When deploying, do not only ask:

> Can the enemy see this model?

Also ask:

> If this model is hit by an Impact Template, what else becomes a Secondary Target?

---

# 13. Blast versus Hit mode

For weapons offering both area and single-target modes, use a simple rule.

## Blast

Choose Blast when **secondary targets make the attack better**.

Examples:

- clustered Fireteam;
- Doctor beside patient;
- Engineer beside TAG;
- multiple Order generators;
- objective area packed with troops.

## Hit

Choose Hit when **the Main Target is the entire problem**.

Examples:

- TAG;
- HI attack piece;
- lone ARO model;
- target where AP matters more than the template.

Do not automatically choose the template mode because it feels more dramatic.

---

# 14. Disposable weapons

Disposable weapons are **limited tactical resources**, not ordinary guns.

Examples include:

- Panzerfaust;
- Blitzen;
- some missile / rocket systems;
- disposable deployment weapons;
- other profiles marked Disposable.

### Spend them when

- a TAG enters the ideal range;
- an expensive attacker exposes itself;
- it is clearly your best ARO;
- disabling the target changes the entire turn;
- the opponent must cross that lane;
- the expected value of the current shot is already high.

### Preserve them when

- the target is a cheap Order generator;
- odds are terrible;
- a normal weapon already solves the target;
- a much more important target is likely later.

### Do not over-hoard

A Panzerfaust still loaded when the carrier dies generated zero value.

Disposable weapons are meant to be used when the tactical window appears.

---

# 15. Range-band layering

A strong list should not merely contain several good weapons.

It should contain **overlapping answers across multiple engagement distances**.

## 0-8 inches

Possible answers:

- Direct Templates;
- shotgun;
- pistol / Heavy Pistol;
- SMG;
- CC;
- PARA;
- E/M;
- mines / Perimeter weapons.

## 8-16 inches

Possible answers:

- rifles / combis;
- SMGs;
- shotgun still functioning;
- Spitfire / Red Fury;
- Marksman Rifle;
- close assault tools.

## 16-24 inches

Premium territory for:

- Spitfire;
- Red Fury;
- Marksman Rifle;
- HMG;
- many specialized weapons.

## 24-32 inches

You want something like:

- HMG;
- sniper family;
- Feuerbach;
- Thunderbolt;
- other dedicated long-range attack weapon.

## 32+ inches

You need a deliberate answer, but it does not have to be a gun.

Possible solutions:

- Sniper Rifle;
- Missile Launcher;
- Feuerbach;
- Thunderbolt;
- HMG accepting a weaker band;
- Smoke + MSV;
- Eclipse;
- White Noise;
- hacking / Pitcher network;
- Camo flanker;
- Airborne Deployment;
- Guided attack;
- simply refusing the gunfight and routing elsewhere.

---

# 16. Avoid false redundancy

Four excellent 8-24 inch attackers are not necessarily a robust weapons package.

They may simply be **four models sharing the same weakness**.

### Bad redundancy

- Spitfire;
- Red Fury;
- Marksman Rifle;
- another Spitfire;

with no credible answer to a 40-inch sniper lane.

### Better redundancy

- HMG for long-range active fights;
- Marksman Rifle for midfield;
- E/M warband for TAG / HI control;
- Smoke / White Noise / flanker for long-range ARO bypass;
- shotgun / template for close defence.

The list now has multiple **solutions**, not merely multiple guns.

---

# 17. Redundancy should be by problem solved

Do not ask:

> Do I have two HMGs?

Ask:

> If my HMG dies, can I still solve the problems it was supposed to solve?

## Long-range ARO problem

Possible answers include:

- HMG;
- sniper;
- Feuerbach;
- Smoke + MSV;
- White Noise;
- Eclipse;
- Guided;
- Camo flank;
- Airborne Deployment;
- Impersonation;
- hacking if the defender is Hackable;
- objective route that avoids the lane.

## TAG problem

Possible answers include:

- AP HMG;
- Feuerbach;
- Panzerfaust;
- Missile Launcher;
- E/M;
- hacking;
- PARA;
- CC specialist;
- K1;
- repeated Guided attacks.

**AP HMG + E/M warband** is often better redundancy than **AP HMG + AP HMG** because the opponent cannot counter both with exactly the same defensive plan.

# 18. Engagement-selection checklist

Before declaring an important attack, run through this sequence.

## Step 1: Compare both range bands

Do not merely ask:

> Am I in +3?

Ask:

> What is the total modifier differential between my weapon and theirs?

Your +3 versus their +3 is ordinary.

Your 0 versus their -6 can be excellent.

Range-band manipulation is often more important than raw BS.

---

## Step 2: Can the target oppose me?

### If yes

Prioritize:

1. Burst;
2. positive MODs;
3. enemy negative MODs;
4. acceptable ammunition.

### If no

Prioritize:

1. ammunition effect;
2. lower PS / better save pressure;
3. target's weakest defence;
4. chance to remove or disable in one Order.

---

## Step 3: Which defence should I attack?

Compare:

- ARM;
- BTS;
- PH.

Then choose the tool that attacks the weakest or most relevant axis.

Examples:

- AP / K1 -> ARM;
- Breaker / E/M / Viral / hacking -> BTS;
- PARA -> PH.

---

## Step 4: What result do I actually need?

Possible desired outcomes:

- dead;
- Unconscious;
- Immobilized;
- Isolated;
- Stunned;
- forced Dodge;
- forced Reset;
- forced movement through Guts;
- simply pushed out of the scoring / firing position.

Do not spend five Orders killing a model if one Order of Stun or E/M already removes it from the mission.

---

## Step 5: Are there secondary targets?

If yes, consider:

- Impact Template;
- Direct Template;
- Blast mode;
- Continuous template;
- E/M template;
- Speculative Attack where applicable.

---

## Step 6: Am I willing to trade?

### Yes

Direct Templates and sacrificial attacks become much stronger.

### No

Prefer a Face to Face weapon and preserve the attacker.

This distinction is especially important for expensive HI, TAGs and primary attack pieces.

---

## Step 7: What happens if the target survives?

A target surviving on one Wound / STR may still be fully functional.

A target surviving while:

- Immobilized;
- Isolated;
- Stunned;
- out of position;

may be strategically neutralized.

Always evaluate **post-hit functionality**, not merely remaining Wounds.

---

# 19. Target -> preferred tool

| Target / situation | Preferred approach |
|---|---|
| Basic infantry in Cover | High-Burst gun in good range |
| One-Wound infantry | High Burst; Shock where relevant |
| Camo / Mimetism at very close range | Template, shotgun, Intuitive Attack where available |
| High-ARM HI | AP, K1, AP+DA, E/M |
| TAG | AP HMG, Feuerbach, Panzerfaust / ML, E/M, hacking, PARA |
| High ARM / lower BTS | Breaker, E/M, hacking, BTS-targeting weapon |
| High BTS / lower ARM | Conventional / AP ballistic weapon |
| Multi-Wound model | DA, EXP, AP+DA, AP+EXP |
| Clumped Fireteam | Impact Template / Blast |
| Engineer beside TAG | Impact Template / Blast if geometry allows |
| Elite gunfighter inside 8 inches | Shotgun, template, CC, PARA, E/M |
| Elite gunfighter at long range | HMG / sniper / Feuerbach; manipulate range |
| Strong shooter that only needs neutralizing | Stun, PARA, E/M |
| Enemy shooting back | Burst + MOD advantage first |
| Unopposed valuable target | maximize ammunition / PS consequences |
| Sacrificial close-range attack | Direct Template |
| Long-range ARO role | Sniper / ML / Feuerbach / Thunderbolt depending purpose |

# 20. List-building audit: Weapons & Engagement Coverage

Before finalizing a list, check the following.

| Question | Desired answer |
|---|---|
| Do I have a credible 0-8 inch answer? | Yes |
| Do I have a strong 8-24 inch attacker? | Yes |
| Can I fight effectively around 24-32 inches? | Yes |
| Do I have a deliberate 32+ inch solution or bypass? | Yes |
| Can I attack high ARM? | Yes |
| Can I attack BTS or impose states instead of shooting ARM? | Yes |
| Do I have at least one template threat? | Usually |
| Do I have a high-Burst Face to Face piece? | Usually |
| Do I have an ARO where one successful hit is genuinely scary? | Preferably |
| Can I punish clustering? | Preferably |
| Can I neutralize a TAG without relying on one model? | Yes |
| If the primary SWC gun dies Turn 1, can I still attack? | Yes |
| Are several attack pieces all dependent on the same range band? | No |

# 21. Common list-building mistakes

## One-range-band army

Example:

- several excellent Spitfires / Red Furies / Marksman Rifles;
- no long-range solution;
- no Smoke / White Noise / flanker.

Result:

A single strong sniper lane can dictate the entire first turn.

---

## Ammunition without delivery

A Panzerfaust, Missile Launcher or EXP weapon may look terrifying on paper.

If the carrier cannot get a favourable roll, the ammunition rarely matters.

Always evaluate:

**delivery probability x consequence of hit.**

---

## High Burst without target answer

A generic B4 weapon can still struggle if it is repeatedly attacking the wrong defensive axis.

Example:

- normal ammo into extreme ARM;
- ARM weapon into low-BTS target where E/M / Breaker is available;
- shooting a model that could simply be Immobilized.

---

## Too many active guns, no ARO consequence

An army can contain excellent HMGs and Spitfires yet have no weapon that makes the opponent afraid to cross a lane in the Reactive Turn.

One inexpensive Missile Launcher, Feuerbach, Panzerfaust, Flash Pulse, mine layer, or template defender may add more defensive value than another active gun.

---

## No close-range secondary weapon on expensive gunfighter

A premium HMG / sniper / Feuerbach platform without a good short-range option can be vulnerable to:

- warbands;
- Parachutists;
- Camo flankers;
- motorcycles;
- Impersonators;
- smoke-assisted assault.

Treat the secondary weapon package as part of the attack piece's real profile.

---

# 22. Order efficiency and weapon choice

The best weapon is often the one that reduces the **Orders required to create the engagement**.

Examples:

- HMG attacks from current position -> 1 Order.
- Spitfire must cross two streets first -> 3 Orders before first useful attack.
- Airborne Boarding Shotgun appears inside ideal band -> very high positional efficiency.
- E/M grenade disables TAG in one successful attack -> no need to grind through STR.
- White Noise lets a medium-range attacker walk past an MSV ARO -> the best "weapon" may be not shooting at all.

This is why weapon evaluation must include movement cost.

### Practical formula

Think roughly in terms of:

**Orders to reach engagement + Orders to win engagement + Orders to reach safety / objective.**

A weapon that looks weaker on paper may produce the better attack run because it fits the carrier's starting position.

# 23. Active versus Reactive Turn changes weapon value

Weapon value changes dramatically between turns.

## Active Turn favors

- high Burst;
- flexible range;
- weapons capable of repeated Face to Face wins;
- mobility;
- ammunition that remains useful over many targets.

Typical examples:

- HMG;
- Spitfire;
- Red Fury;
- Marksman Rifle;
- AP HMG;
- Burst-mode Feuerbach.

## Reactive Turn favors

- single-hit consequence;
- long range;
- templates;
- EXP / DA;
- disposable anti-armour weapons;
- Stun;
- E/M;
- weapons that force Dodge or route changes.

Typical examples:

- Missile Launcher;
- sniper;
- EXP Feuerbach mode;
- Panzerfaust;
- Flash Pulse;
- Direct Templates guarding corners.

Do not evaluate a weapon's value without asking **which turn is supposed to justify its cost**.

# 24. Engagement selection examples

## Example A: TAG at 28 inches

Available options:

- HMG in good range;
- Boarding Shotgun far outside ideal range;
- E/M trooper three Orders away.

Usually start with the HMG if it can get a favourable Face to Face roll.

If the HMG matchup is poor because the TAG has superior MODs, consider spending Orders to reach the E/M solution instead of repeatedly taking a bad gunfight.

---

## Example B: TAG around a corner at 6 inches

The HMG may now be the wrong weapon.

Better options may include:

- E/M template;
- PARA;
- Boarding Shotgun;
- CC;
- Direct Template trade from a cheap model.

The target did not change.

**Position changed the correct answer.**

---

## Example C: linked Missile Launcher on rooftop

Do not automatically answer with your own Missile Launcher.

Possible superior approaches:

- HMG from favourable range;
- Smoke + MSV;
- White Noise;
- Camo attacker from side angle;
- airborne flank;
- Guided;
- ignore the lane and score elsewhere.

The correct weapon may be the one that makes the Missile Launcher unable to use its strengths.

---

## Example D: cheap rifleman beside expensive Engineer

An Impact Template may be better than shooting the Engineer directly if the cheap rifleman provides an easier Main Target and the template can catch both.

Target selection and weapon selection interact.

---

## Example E: elite Mimetism gunfighter at 5 inches

A normal BS attack may be unnecessarily difficult.

Possible better answers:

- Direct Template;
- shotgun with massive close-range MOD;
- PARA;
- CC;
- E/M;
- trade with a cheap model.

At close range, **bypassing the gunfight** can be much stronger than improving the gunfight.

---

# 25. Compact doctrine

When deciding **"Given this target and position, what weapon / mode should I want?"**, use this sequence:

1. **Find the engagement distance where your modifier advantage is greatest.**
2. **If opposed, prioritize winning the Face to Face Roll: Burst and MODs first.**
3. **Attack the target's weakest relevant defence: ARM, BTS, or PH.**
4. **Against armour, reduce or bypass defence with AP, K1, E/M, PARA, hacking, or combined ammunition.**
5. **Against lightly defended or multi-Wound targets, multiply consequences with DA, EXP, Continuous, or Shock where relevant.**
6. **Use Direct Templates when you want guaranteed pressure or a trade.**
7. **Use Impact Templates / Blast when enemy spacing gives you secondary targets.**
8. **Use low-Burst high-consequence weapons more readily in ARO than in the active turn.**
9. **Evaluate the weapon together with carrier mobility and Orders required to reach its good range.**
10. **Build overlapping range coverage so losing one weapon does not delete an entire engagement band.**

The best Infinity weapon is usually not the weapon with the most frightening ammunition.

It is the weapon that lets you make the opponent fight **on the wrong defensive axis, in the wrong range band, with the wrong response available**.
