# List-Building Strategy — Close Combat and Short-Range Assault

## N5.3 Warbands / cheap trade cross-reference (2026-10-23)

[N5.3 Warbands/Cheap Traders apex/value](warbands-and-cheap-trading-units-apex-value.md) compares **Diablo8 Berserk+Trench-Hammer**, **Morlock6 E/M CC** (requires base contact), **Makaul14 Viral/DA+Eclipse** (MSV-blocking route) and **Dāturazi14 MA4/Warhorse** (Regular Smoke infrastructure). The **delivery/survival** test matters more than bare CC stat.

Close Combat in Infinity is not simply a contest between models with high CC Attributes. Its strategic value is that it can **change the type of contest**, shut down a powerful shooter, exploit vision control, convert cheap bodies into control pieces, or finish a target that has already been disabled by hacking, E/M, PARA, or other effects.

The central question is therefore not:

> **Is this trooper good at CC?**

It is:

> **Is reaching CC a more efficient way to solve this target than shooting it?**

## 1. When CC is preferable to shooting

CC is usually attractive when it **turns an unfavorable gunfight into a favorable melee contest**.

Typical good targets include:

- elite BS attackers whose CC defense is mediocre;
- long-range ARO pieces that become much less threatening once reached;
- models relying on Mimetism, rangebands, Partial Cover, or other advantages that matter far less in CC;
- expensive models that can be disabled simply by becoming Engaged;
- Hackable heavy targets that can first be Immobilized or Isolated;
- targets carrying strategically important weapons, equipment, or command roles that must be neutralized quickly.

Do not enter CC merely because a model has Martial Arts or a good CC Attribute. If the opponent is also a serious melee fighter, has Natural Born Warrior, Guard, multiple Wounds/STR, or an excellent CC weapon, a pistol, shotgun, template, hacking attack, or other short-range tool may be the safer solution.

A useful comparison is:

> **Expected Orders to solve the target at range vs expected Orders to deliver the assault piece and solve it in CC.**

If reaching the target costs four Orders merely to avoid taking a reasonable gunfight, CC is probably the wrong answer.

## 2. Delivery is often more important than raw CC ability

Many excellent CC profiles fail if they cannot reach the enemy efficiently.

The strongest delivery tools include:

- Smoke;
- Eclipse;
- Camouflage and Impersonation;
- Infiltration and Forward Deployment;
- Dodge movement;
- high MOV;
- motorcycles and other fast platforms;
- Climbing Plus;
- Super-Jump;
- Airborne Deployment;
- Berserk threat extension;
- terrain that permits movement entirely in Total Cover.

When evaluating a close-assault profile, consider **delivery package + attack quality**, not only CC.

A slightly weaker fighter that starts in the midfield or carries Smoke may be a much better practical assassin than an exceptional fighter starting deep in the Deployment Zone with no delivery tool.

## 3. Smoke / Eclipse into CC

Smoke and Eclipse are not merely defensive tools. They can manufacture a safe assault route.

The usual sequence is:

1. identify the target and the models supporting it;
2. place Smoke/Eclipse to block the most dangerous supporting AROs;
3. move the assault piece through Total Cover or the blocked firelane;
4. reach a position from which the final approach costs as few Orders as possible;
5. enter CC or attack from short range;
6. use the resulting position to continue the attack, score, or retreat.

The best Smoke placement is often **not directly on the intended victim**.

Instead, use the template to remove:

- a sniper watching the approach;
- a nearby Fireteam member;
- an MSV gunfighter where ordinary Smoke would otherwise fail;
- a template weapon covering the doorway;
- another ARO piece that would create an unfavorable multi-ARO approach.

The principle is:

> **Use vision control to isolate the target before committing the assault piece.**

Eclipse is particularly valuable for this role because it can deny LoF even against many MSV-based defenses that would defeat ordinary Smoke.

## 4. CC as a way to disable shooters

A model does not always need to die for the assault to succeed.

Putting a premium shooter into Engaged State can be enough to remove its normal battlefield function. A sniper, HMG, Feuerbach, missile launcher, or similar model that is tied up in CC is no longer covering the table in the way its owner intended.

This can be worthwhile even when the engaging model is cheap and unlikely to win the melee quickly.

Good targets include:

- expensive long-range ARO pieces;
- key Fireteam gunfighters;
- attack pieces covering an objective;
- models whose value comes primarily from BS Attack rather than CC;
- models that the opponent will be forced to spend several Orders rescuing.

This creates a different value calculation:

> **A cheap model tying up an expensive gunfighter can be a successful trade even if neither model dies.**

## 5. Cheap troops as control pieces

A low-cost troop can contribute to CC without being a dedicated melee specialist.

Useful jobs include:

- physically screening a premium model;
- threatening to Dodge into Silhouette contact;
- tying up an attacker that moves too close;
- joining an existing melee to increase the friendly side's Burst;
- forcing the opponent to spend Orders clearing a doorway or objective room;
- acting as the first body in a later coordinated or multi-model assault.

This is especially effective against models whose points are invested mainly in shooting, mobility, hacking, or other non-CC functions.

Do not evaluate these models by whether they are likely to **kill** the target. Evaluate whether they can force the enemy to spend Orders, lose movement freedom, or surrender an important firing lane.

## 6. Reactive engagement: Dodge into Silhouette contact

There is no separate old-style Engage ARO in current N5.

Instead, successful Dodge movement can place a trooper into Silhouette contact and therefore into Engaged State.

This makes cheap high-PH troops, warbands, and other strong Dodging models dangerous around corners, doorways, objectives, and narrow approaches.

A useful defensive setup is to position a cheap model where an attacker cannot pass without giving it an opportunity to Dodge into contact.

The threat is not necessarily damage. The threat is:

> **If you pass this point, I may attach a cheap model to your expensive attacker and force you to solve it.**

This can protect more important pieces behind the screen.

## 7. Multi-model CC and N5.3

N5.3 corrected the multiple-combatant wording so the relevant Close Combat support interaction applies with **2 or more Troopers** in Silhouette contact.

This makes extra bodies in melee significantly more valuable.

When allied troops join the same combat, the active attacker can gain additional Burst from those assisting models. Therefore:

- the first model can establish the engagement;
- the second model can turn a B1 attack into a much more reliable attack;
- additional cheap models can continue increasing the pressure;
- mediocre CC troops become substantially more threatening when they gang up on one target.

The tactical principle is:

> **The first body pins the target; additional bodies turn the pin into a kill mechanism.**

This makes nearby cheap troops, Peripherals where permitted by their activation structure, and clustered midfield pieces more relevant to close-assault planning than their individual CC values suggest.

## 8. Coordinated CC

Coordinated Orders provide a particularly strong way to mass several models against one target.

Up to four eligible troops can participate. When a Coordinated Order declares CC Attack:

- only the Spearhead performs the CC Attack;
- each allied coordinated trooper that reaches Silhouette contact with the target gives the Spearhead +1B;
- the reactive target can choose only one of the coordinated troopers as the target of its ARO.

This can allow several otherwise mediocre fighters to threaten a valuable target.

A classic use is:

> **Several cheap troops → Coordinated Move + CC Attack → one premium target.**

With four models successfully reaching contact, even a basic B1 CC weapon can become a B4 attack from the Spearhead.

This is situational because it costs a Command Token, requires compatible troops in the same Combat Group, and depends heavily on geometry. But when the board state presents the opportunity, it can convert spare bodies into a credible anti-TAG or anti-gunfighter attack.

Do not force this setup during list construction. Recognize it when several friendly models already happen to be near the same valuable enemy.

## 9. Berserk is a threat-extension tool, not an automatic trade button

Current N5 Berserk should not be treated as the old shorthand **"Berserk = guaranteed trade."**

Berserk is a Long Skill that combines Move + CC Attack and lets the user move up to the sum of both MOV values to reach Silhouette contact. Any listed Berserk MOD applies to the CC Attack.

Because the complete movement route and target are declared before enemy AROs, Berserk trades flexibility for reach.

Use Berserk when:

- the extra movement reaches a target that standard Move + CC Attack cannot reach efficiently;
- the target is a weak melee opponent;
- the assault piece has enough resilience to accept the incoming ARO;
- the target is sufficiently important that committing the model is worthwhile;
- the target cannot easily exploit your declared route with a devastating response.

Avoid Berserk when:

- the target is itself an elite CC fighter;
- multiple overlapping AROs cover the route;
- a normal Move would let you wait for the opponent's ARO before choosing your second Short Skill;
- failure to reach Silhouette contact would leave the model exposed;
- the assault piece is too valuable to commit blindly.

The main principle is:

> **Berserk buys threat range at the cost of declaration flexibility.**

## 10. Martial Arts: evaluate Burst and dice quality, not only CC

Martial Arts levels do more than raise the CC Attribute.

Current Martial Arts progression includes combinations of:

- positive CC MODs for the user;
- negative MODs to the opponent;
- +1 SD at higher levels;
- +1B at the upper levels.

This means two models with similar printed CC values may have very different practical melee performance.

In particular:

- MA1 mainly imposes a negative MOD on the opponent;
- MA2 adds a positive CC MOD for the user;
- MA3 adds +1 SD;
- MA4 adds +1B;
- MA5 combines +1B and +1 SD.

The biggest jump for active-turn killing is often not one additional point of CC. It is gaining **better dice quality or additional Burst**.

When comparing melee specialists, evaluate:

1. final success value;
2. enemy success value after MODs;
3. Burst;
4. +1 SD or similar dice manipulation;
5. weapon ammunition/effects;
6. delivery cost;
7. durability if the fight takes more than one Order.

## 11. Martial Arts vs Natural Born Warrior

Natural Born Warrior does not simply "cancel Martial Arts."

When the NBW model is the **target of a CC Attack** and declares CC Attack in return, it ignores negative MODs imposed by the opposing trooper.

This means NBW can ignore effects such as:

- the opponent MOD from Martial Arts;
- Surprise Attack negative MODs;
- CC Attack (-X) effects;
- negative MODs imposed by some PARA or similar CC interactions.

However, NBW does **not** remove the attacker's positive bonuses.

For example, an MA4 attacker may still retain:

- its own +3 CC;
- its +1B.

NBW therefore makes a model much harder to bully through stacked negative modifiers, but it does not automatically make that model superior to a top-tier Martial Artist.

Practical rule:

> **Check NBW before assuming your normal MA/Surprise Attack MOD stack will work.**

## 12. Short-range assault should be multi-modal

The strongest close-assault pieces often threaten more than CC.

Useful combinations include:

- shotgun + CC;
- direct template + CC;
- pistol + CC;
- E/M + CC;
- hacking + CC;
- Smoke/Eclipse + CC;
- marker state + short-range weapon + CC.

This creates declaration forks.

A model approaches at short range. The defender must decide whether to:

- shoot;
- Dodge;
- Reset;
- declare CC Attack if already engaged;
- use another defensive option.

The active piece can then choose the second Short Skill that best exploits the declared response when using a normal two-Short-Skill Order.

For example:

- enemy Dodges → shoot with a high-Burst short-range weapon;
- enemy shoots → attack with the favorable weapon or continue an approach where legal;
- Hackable target Resets → shoot;
- defender prepares for shooting → enter CC if the geometry permits;
- clustered defenders → use a template rather than forcing the melee.

This is usually stronger than a model whose only meaningful plan is **run forward and use sword**.

## 13. Immobilize, Isolate, or E/M first; CC afterward

Do not require the melee specialist to defeat every layer of a premium target by itself.

Against Hackable targets such as many HI, REMs, Vehicles, and TAGs, first consider:

- hacking;
- E/M ammunition;
- PARA;
- Repeaters/Pitchers;
- other Immobilized or Isolated effects.

Then send the assault piece once the target's options are reduced.

A strong attack sequence can therefore be:

> **Project network → Immobilize/Isolate → approach safely → CC or short-range finish.**

This is particularly powerful against TAGs and expensive HI because the control effect may already have removed much of the model's value before the melee begins.

It also creates an important stopping rule:

> **If the target is already effectively neutralized, do not spend extra Orders killing it unless the mission or board state requires the kill.**

An Immobilized/Isolated TAG with no practical Engineer support may already be a solved problem.

## 14. CC against TAGs

TAGs are attractive close-assault targets because they are expensive and often derive most of their value from shooting, mobility, and resilience rather than elite melee performance.

But reaching a TAG with a weak normal CC Weapon is not automatically useful.

Evaluate the assault piece's actual anti-armor weapon:

- AP;
- DA;
- EXP;
- AP+DA;
- AP+EXP;
- E/M CC weapons;
- PARA or other disabling effects.

There are two distinct qualities to look for:

### Duelist

Wins the Face-to-Face Roll reliably through high CC, Martial Arts, Surprise Attack, Burst, NBW, or similar advantages.

### Can-opener

Has a weapon capable of converting the successful hit into meaningful damage or disabling effects against high ARM/BTS/STR.

The best TAG hunters combine both.

If your model is only a duelist, consider disabling the TAG first. If it is only a can-opener, look for ways to create a Normal Roll or heavily favorable Face-to-Face Roll before committing.

## 15. Do not automatically finish a disabled heavy target

A common Order-efficiency mistake is continuing to attack a model after its strategic value has already collapsed.

Suppose a TAG is:

- Immobilized;
- Isolated;
- badly positioned;
- cut off from its Engineer;
- unable to affect the relevant objectives.

Spending another three Orders to destroy it may produce fewer benefits than using those Orders to:

- kill its Engineer;
- remove its Order support;
- score;
- attack Specialists;
- establish midfield control;
- reposition the assault piece safely.

The purpose of CC is not necessarily to collect the target's points. It is to **remove the target's ability to influence the game**.

## 16. Guarding against enemy CC specialists

The best defense against an elite melee piece is usually to prevent it from reaching the intended target efficiently.

Layer defenses with:

- disposable screens;
- Mines;
- Perimeter weapons;
- direct-template weapons;
- Repeaters and Hackers against Hackable assault pieces;
- cheap AROs;
- overlapping short-range defenders;
- awkward movement geometry;
- doors, corners, stairs, rooftops, and other terrain that increases approach cost.

The goal is not necessarily to kill the CC specialist with the first defense.

The goal is to turn:

> **3 Orders to reach and kill my premium piece**

into:

> **6–8 Orders to clear screens, cross the approach, solve the defense, and finally attack the real target.**

In Infinity, extra distance and disposable bodies function as armor because every additional step costs Orders.

## 17. Screen valuable models with expendable ones

Do not leave a TAG, Lieutenant, Engineer, Hacker, or other valuable model as the first thing an enemy assassin can touch after rounding a corner.

Instead, where practical, create:

**assassin → cheap screen → valuable target**

The screen may force the attacker to:

1. kill or bypass the cheap model;
2. spend another Order repositioning;
3. survive another round of AROs;
4. only then reach the valuable model.

Even a weak screen can therefore prevent an efficient assassination run.

This is especially useful against fast CC pieces that depend on reaching several valuable targets in sequence during one turn.

## 18. Protect against chain-kill routes

When deploying against strong short-range assault pieces, look at the route they could take **after the first kill**.

Avoid configurations where a single attacker can move naturally from:

- gunfighter;
- to Lieutenant;
- to Engineer;
- to Specialist;
- to Order generators

without ever crossing a defended lane.

Spread valuable models so each new target requires another movement problem.

A CC assassin that kills one important model can be acceptable. A CC assassin that kills four important models because they were arranged along one protected corridor is a deployment failure.

## 19. Ending the assault in a useful position

A successful melee attack should leave the assault piece somewhere useful whenever possible.

Good end positions include:

- Engaged with another valuable enemy;
- behind Total Cover;
- inside or near an objective room;
- covering a doorway with a template;
- threatening another short-range target;
- forcing the opponent to spend Orders dealing with it next turn.

A surviving assault piece in the enemy backfield can remain valuable even if it has no good immediate attack.

It may force the opponent to:

- change movement routes;
- spend Orders clearing it;
- abandon a support model;
- delay scoring;
- keep troops facing or positioned defensively.

This makes **post-assault positioning** part of the attack calculation.

## 20. Practical CC decision tree

Before committing to close combat, ask:

1. **Can I solve this efficiently by shooting instead?**
2. **Is the target's CC defense meaningfully worse than its ranged defense?**
3. **How many Orders does delivery cost?**
4. **Can Smoke, Eclipse, Camo, Impersonation, movement skills, or terrain reduce that cost?**
5. **Does the target have Martial Arts, Natural Born Warrior, Guard, Berserk, or a dangerous CC weapon?**
6. **Can hacking, E/M, PARA, or another control effect disable it first?**
7. **Can a second cheap troop join and increase Burst?**
8. **Would a Coordinated Order create a worthwhile gang-up?**
9. **Do I actually need the target dead, or is Engaged/Immobilized/Isolated enough?**
10. **Where will my assault piece finish if the attack succeeds?**
11. **Does spending more Orders on this target still improve the mission position?**

The main doctrine is:

> **Use CC when it converts an unfavorable problem into a favorable one. Delivery is usually more important than raw CC, Burst and weapon quality often matter more than tiny Attribute differences, and disabling or tying up a valuable model can be just as useful as killing it.**
