# List-Building Strategy — Hacking and Network Projection

## Hacking is hacker quality multiplied by network quality

Evaluate hacking as a **package**, not by looking only at the Hacker's WIP or device.

A strong Hacker with no meaningful Repeater projection can be easy for the opponent to avoid. A huge Repeater network with only one mediocre Hacker can also fail to capitalize on the coverage.

A good hacking package balances:

- Hacker quality and program coverage;
- Repeater density;
- forward or mobile network projection;
- anti-hacker defence;
- enough relevant Hackable targets or mission utility to justify the investment.

Do not heavily buy one side of the package without enough of the other.

## Network projection

Network projection determines where the hacking game actually exists.

Useful projection methods include:

1. **Forward or deployment-position Repeaters.** Infiltration, Forward Deployment, Minelayer plus Deployable Repeater, and forward REMs are particularly valuable because they create useful Hacking Areas before spending many Orders.
2. **Pitchers.** Excellent long-range active projection. Their value rises with good BS, Fireteam bonuses, +B/+SD, and Baggage support because they require rolls and use Disposable ammunition.
3. **FastPandas.** Shorter ranged but highly reliable because they are placed rather than fired. Particularly good on frontline Hackers or Fireteams that already want to advance.
4. **Deployable Repeaters.** Reliable but require the carrier to physically reach the desired location. Best on Camo, Infiltration, Forward Deployment, or very fast troops.
5. **Fast native-Repeater REMs.** Mobile network nodes that can physically move the Hacking Area into useful positions, at the cost of exposing the REM itself.

Forward network pieces are especially valuable because they create **Turn-0 area denial** and make the opponent account for hacking before the active player spends Orders building the network.

## Multiple hackers behind one network

The value of additional complementary Hackers rises as Repeater coverage improves because **every Repeater serves all of them simultaneously**.

A broad network can support:

- a primary HD or HD+;
- a KHD or Trinity-equipped hybrid for anti-hacker defence;
- a secondary Hacker with complementary programs;
- Spotlight or Guided support;
- reactive hacking AROs from several models.

Once substantial points are invested in Repeaters, adding a second Hacker can be more efficient than adding more network projection, provided the second Hacker meaningfully expands program coverage or defensive redundancy.

## Combat Instinct on Hackers

**Combat Instinct is unusually valuable on a Hacker when the list has broad Repeater coverage.**

Its anti-Surprise protection is useful, but the major network interaction is that it prevents **Stealth** from bypassing the Hacker's reactive presence. A Stealth Hackable trooper cannot simply cross the relevant Repeater area while suppressing that Hacker's AROs.

Combat Instinct therefore scales with network size:

- little or no Repeater coverage: mostly a niche self-protection skill;
- strong midfield network: a major reactive-area-denial upgrade.

A Combat Instinct Hacker is particularly suitable as a **network sentry** behind aggressive Repeaters.

## Killer Hacking Devices

A standard KHD is primarily an **anti-Hacker and network-defence device**. Trinity gives it a strong way to kill enemy Hackers, while Cybermask provides protection and positional utility.

A normal KHD does relatively little directly to ordinary non-Hacker HI, TAGs, REMs, or VH. Therefore its value rises when:

- the list already has meaningful Repeater coverage;
- enemy Hackers are likely to contest that network;
- important HI/TAG/VH need protection from enemy cyber pieces;
- Cybermask supports the model's physical specialist or infiltration role.

Do not evaluate a KHD as if it were a general-purpose HD unless the profile adds additional programs.

## KHDs with normal Hacker programs

A KHD that gains **Carbonite, Spotlight, Oblivion, or another normal Hacker program** is substantially more flexible than a standard KHD.

Examples of what the extra program changes:

- **KHD + Carbonite:** can control ordinary Hackable HI, REMs, TAGs, and VH rather than only attacking Hackers;
- **KHD + Spotlight:** gains useful interaction against essentially any target and can support conventional or Guided attacks;
- **KHD + Oblivion:** becomes a serious anti-HI/TAG network attacker while retaining Trinity and Cybermask.

These profiles often provide excellent role compression because they remain dangerous to hostile Hackers while also becoming productive against non-Hacker targets.

## HD or HD+ with Trinity

The reverse hybrid is also valuable. A normal HD already handles Hackable troops with programs such as Carbonite, Oblivion, Spotlight, and Total Control, but adding **Trinity** gives it a decisive anti-Hacker weapon.

A premium **HD/HD+ + Trinity** profile can sometimes replace the traditional package of one general Hacker plus a separate KHD.

This is especially attractive when the hybrid Hacker is resilient, high-WIP, has good defensive MODs, or already occupies a central role in the list.

## HD+

HD+ is not merely a numerically better HD. Its major strategic additions are **Cybermask and White Noise**.

Cybermask helps an expensive or important Hacker survive, reposition, and approach mission areas safely.

White Noise makes network projection even more valuable because Repeaters extend not only offensive hacking reach but also the area from which the Hacker can project vision-control effects.

Therefore:

> **HD+ turns a strong Repeater network into both a hacking network and a vision-control network.**

HD+ becomes particularly attractive in lists that already have strong Pitcher, FastPanda, Deployable Repeater, or forward-Repeater infrastructure.

## Apex and hybrid hackers

A Hacker with broad device access plus anti-Hacker programs can act as an **apex Hacker**: one model capable of attacking Hackable troops, killing hostile Hackers, supporting the network, and often protecting itself with Cybermask or defensive programs.

This role compression can justify a high price, but only if the profile is competent enough to survive and exploit all of that program coverage. Avoid paying premium points for a long hacking program list on a model that cannot safely reach or remain connected to the relevant network.

## +B and +SD on hacking programs

Hacking-program Burst and Special Die modifiers should be evaluated differently.

**+B** is primarily an **active-turn offensive upgrade**. It increases the number of actual attack dice, making programs such as Trinity or Oblivion substantially more reliable when the Hacker is initiating the engagement.

**+SD** improves reliability in both the **Active and Reactive Turns** because the Hacker rolls an extra die and discards one rather than increasing Burst. This is particularly valuable for B1 hacking AROs and for network guardians expected to defend through Repeaters.

Useful heuristic:

- **+B:** prioritize on aggressive primary Hackers that will initiate many attacks;
- **+SD:** prioritize on hybrid Hackers or network defenders that must remain dangerous in both turns.

Program-specific implications include:

- **Trinity +B:** excellent active anti-Hacker hunter;
- **Trinity +SD:** excellent duelist/network defender because the bonus remains relevant in ARO;
- **Oblivion +B:** particularly strong active anti-HI/TAG control because multiple attack dice increase the chance of forcing Isolated;
- **Carbonite +B/+SD:** improves the reliability of disabling premium Hackable attackers;
- **Spotlight modifiers:** become more valuable when the whole list can exploit Targeted rather than only a Guided platform.

## Zero Pain

Zero Pain is a dedicated **defensive hacking tool**. It can be more valuable than offensive anti-Hacker programs when the primary requirement is to keep an important utility Hacker alive and connected to the network.

A useful distinction is:

- **Trinity / Trinity +SD:** defend the network by threatening to kill the enemy Hacker;
- **Zero Pain:** defend the Hacker by making hostile Comms Attacks fail.

Choose according to whether preserving the Hacker or eliminating the hostile cyber piece matters more to the list's plan.

## EVO criteria

EVO Hackers become more attractive when several systems benefit simultaneously.

Strong reasons include combinations of:

- multiple combat REMs that use Supportware;
- several HI/REM/VH that benefit from Fairy Dust;
- multiple Combat Jump troops that benefit from Controlled Jump;
- reactive REMs **without native Total Reaction** that benefit from Enhanced Reaction;
- enough REM infrastructure that the EVO's own Repeater/support role matters.

One isolated beneficiary rarely justifies the EVO by itself. The more of these systems overlap, the more efficient the EVO becomes.

A Total Reaction bot already has its reactive-Burst solution, so **Enhanced Reaction should not be counted as a reason to pair an EVO with a TR bot**. If the TR bot is part of the EVO case, the relevant interaction is usually active-turn Supportware such as Assisted Fire or simply the fact that other REMs benefit too.

## Guided missile REMs

Treat a Guided missile REM primarily as a **payoff for an already-strong Spotlight/network package**, not as the reason to build that package.

A missile REM is much more attractive when the list already wants:

- strong Repeater coverage;
- reliable Spotlight;
- Pitchers or forward Repeaters;
- Forward Observers or Sensor;
- Baggage or other support that improves the projection pieces.

If the hacking/network infrastructure is useful even when Guided never fires, adding the missile REM can be efficient. If the entire network exists only to enable the missile bot, the list is more fragile and easier for the opponent to disrupt.

For the detailed REM-side decision rules, see [REMs](rems.md).

## Hacking-area denial package

One particularly strong archetype combines:

- forward or mobile Repeaters;
- a Combat Instinct Hacker;
- broad HD/KHD program coverage;
- a second complementary Hacker or KHD.

This closes several common escape routes at once:

- Stealth is less effective against the Combat Instinct Hacker;
- hostile Hackers entering the network face Trinity/KHD threats;
- ordinary Hackable HI/TAG/REM/VH face Carbonite, Oblivion, or other HD programs;
- the same network can support Spotlight, White Noise, and other utility.

At that point the hacking package becomes genuine **area denial** rather than merely another attack option.
