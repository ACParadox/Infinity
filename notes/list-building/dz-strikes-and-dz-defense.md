# List-Building Strategy — DZ Strikes and DZ Defense

## Current N5.3 airborne / Enemy-DZ attacker profile tiers (2026-10-23)

The new [N5.3 cross-game airborne and Enemy-DZ **apex/value tier guide**](airborne-and-enemy-dz-attackers-apex-value.md) compares actual **Rasyat, Fiday/Speculo, Mirage-5, Van Zant, Bashi, Banshee, Hellcat, Garuda, Teishin, Tiger, Ragik, Caskuda and fast/hidden attackers**, distinguishing **delivery reliability, lethal ceiling, after-entry Order economy and useful expected trades**. The **current Combat Jump rule fails to own-DZ table edge as a Model**—not scatter. Continue using the doctrine below for breach and layered DZ defence; use the tier guide for concrete current profiles and transcript-backed opposing opinions.


Deployment-zone attacks are not one tactic. They are a family of penetration methods that defeat normal deployment geometry in different ways.

The main question is not only **how a unit reaches the enemy DZ**, but also **what tools it has once it gets there**.

A useful model is therefore to evaluate a DZ striker on two axes:

1. **Delivery method** — Parachutist, Combat Jump, Impersonation, deep Infiltration, very high movement, Super-Jump, Climbing Plus, Aerial, etc.
2. **Breach package** — Smoke/Eclipse/White Noise, close combat, templates, Mimetism, hacking, deployables, Sensor/MSV, Surprise Attack, etc.

The strongest DZ strikers combine a difficult delivery vector with a breach package that invalidates the obvious defensive answer.

## Rules assumptions

- **Parachutist** normally enters from a table edge and cannot enter through the enemy Deployment Zone.
- **Parachutist (Deployment Zone)** explicitly allows entry through the enemy Deployment Zone.
- **Infiltration** can deploy in the enemy half with the appropriate roll, but **cannot deploy inside the enemy Deployment Zone**. There is no current N5.3 `Infiltration (Deployment Zone)` equivalent to Parachutist (Deployment Zone).
- **Impersonation** can attempt to deploy inside the enemy Deployment Zone using its special deployment procedure.
- **Combat Jump** can attack the DZ from arbitrary landing vectors, subject to its landing rules and roll.

Deep Infiltration is therefore best understood as a **launch point close to the DZ**, often requiring only one or two Orders to breach it, rather than literal deployment inside the DZ.

## General DZ-strike doctrine

### Attack army structure, not necessarily the strongest model

A deep striker often gains more by removing the pieces that make the army function than by fighting its premier gunfighter.

Common priorities include:

1. Lieutenant;
2. critical Fireteam member;
3. exposed Order generators;
4. Engineer or Doctor;
5. EVO or support Hacker;
6. mission specialist;
7. expensive model exposed from the rear.

The exact order depends on the opponent and mission.

### Look for a kill chain

A good DZ strike is rarely about reaching one isolated target. Before committing, identify whether the attacker can move from one favorable target to another without repeatedly fighting through hard AROs.

The dangerous deployment pattern is not simply "one support piece is vulnerable." It is:

> attacker reaches the DZ, kills one cheap model, then can continue through Hacker → Engineer → Lieutenant → Order generator with each new target exposed at favorable range.

### Open a seam first

A DZ strike becomes much stronger when another piece removes one mine, repeater, sentry, or ARO that was protecting the intended route.

Do not require the DZ attacker to solve every defensive layer itself if another model can cheaply remove the one piece creating the bottleneck.

### Prefer safe insertion plus attack

A Combat Jumper or other deep piece often gains more from landing safely behind Total Cover and then attacking than from attempting an exposed high-risk insertion directly beside the target.

The same principle applies to fast ground pieces: reaching a safe staging point in one Order can be better than ending the first move exposed merely because the model can move farther.

### Use close-range weapons aggressively

DZ attackers often want weapons whose best range is normally difficult to reach from the front:

- Boarding Shotguns;
- SMGs;
- Direct Template Weapons;
- pistols;
- close combat;
- mines and other deployables.

A deep attacker is valuable partly because it can deliver these tools directly into their ideal range bands.

### Exploit verticality and facing

A rear attack is not only about the back table edge. Rooftops, opposite sides of buildings, elevated walkways, and Climbing Plus/Super-Jump paths can create angles that bypass the direction in which the defender expected to receive an attack.

The objective is usually to expose **one target at a time**, not the entire DZ.

### Budget an exit for valuable strikers

For expensive Combat Jumpers, bikes, Vehicles, Super-Jump pieces, and other premium attackers, reserve Orders for survival when practical:

- retreat behind Total Cover;
- move out of a predictable counterattack lane;
- re-enter Camouflage when possible;
- deploy mines or other protection;
- return to a Fireteam or support position.

A deep run that kills less but leaves the attacker alive can be more valuable than an overextended run that trades down.

## Parachutist (Deployment Zone)

### Offensive use

Parachutist (Deployment Zone) is a reliable way to attack the rear or side of the enemy DZ without making a Combat Jump roll.

A strong pattern is:

1. enter from a side or rear edge where the model is initially protected;
2. move parallel to the back edge;
3. attack exposed support troops, cheerleaders, and specialists in sequence.

It becomes even stronger when paired with a frontal attacker. The opponent must choose facings and positions that respect both the normal frontal threat and the possibility of edge entry.

### Counters

Do not try to cover the entire table edge equally. Instead, make the most profitable entry points bad.

Useful counters include:

- cheap rear or side sentries;
- mines and other deployables near likely entry lanes;
- cross-covering defenders rather than a single model watching one direction;
- **360° Visors**, especially on very cheap troops;
- spacing support pieces so one successful entry does not create a long kill chain.

### 360° Visors as DZ defence

Cheap 360° pieces are unusually efficient DZ defenders because they remove much of the normal reward for attacking from an unexpected facing.

A low-cost model such as a Warcor can cover:

- rear-edge Parachutists;
- Combat Jumpers that land behind the main line;
- motorcycles or Vehicles that race past normal facings;
- Climbing Plus/Super-Jump pieces manufacturing rear angles.

A cheap Flash Pulse model does not need to kill the attacker to be useful. Forcing a Face-to-Face roll, imposing Stunned, or simply demanding an additional Order from a much more expensive DZ striker can already be a favorable exchange.

Cross-coverage remains important: a single 360° model can still be removed. It is strongest as one layer of a broader DZ defence.

## Combat Jump

### Offensive use

Combat Jump should be used primarily for **positional freedom** rather than stunt landings.

The strongest landing point is often:

- a rooftop behind the DZ;
- a courtyard or alley outside LoF;
- the opposite side of a wall from support pieces;
- a position that gives a clean Boarding Shotgun, template, or rear shot after one Short Skill of movement.

Multiple valuable Combat Jumpers make Controlled Jump and EVO support more attractive.

### Counters

Useful counters include:

- enemy Controlled Jump;
- cheap 360° sentries;
- mines or templates covering good landing/extraction areas;
- rooftop and elevated coverage;
- dispersed deployment;
- short-range defenders watching the places where a jumper would emerge after a safe landing.

When deploying, look at the DZ from above and ask:

> Where can an S2 model land completely unseen and then reach my support structure in one Move?

Those locations deserve protection even if no enemy begins the game near them.

## Deep Infiltration

Infiltration cannot place a model directly inside the enemy DZ, but a successful deep deployment can begin close enough that the DZ is only one or two Orders away.

### Offensive use

Deep infiltrators are strongest when they begin on a side of the DZ where terrain lets them round a building or cross one short corridor instead of fighting through the main defensive line.

Camouflage, Surprise Attack, mines, and short-range weapons make this approach particularly effective.

### Counters

The important defensive principle is to **connect midfield defence to DZ defence**.

Useful counters include:

- mines and Camo markers just outside the DZ;
- defenders covering the corridor between advanced deployment areas and the DZ;
- Sensor when appropriate;
- templates near likely breach points;
- avoiding a clean 8–12 inch corridor that leads directly from midfield into the support area.

A midfield mine can often protect the DZ more efficiently than another rifle standing on the back table edge.

---

## Impersonation

Impersonation is different from most penetration methods because conventional ARO geometry does not solve the problem by itself.

### Offensive use

The Impersonator should normally remain protected by the marker state for as long as practical and reveal only when the attack becomes highly profitable.

Strong sequences include:

- Move + Move past the outer defensive shell, then attack on the following Order;
- position for a template that catches multiple support models;
- walk close enough that the target must deal with both shooting and CC threats;
- force the defender to spend Discover attempts while the Impersonator controls the engagement timing.

### Counters

Useful counters include:

- **Biometric Visors**;
- layered Discover opportunities;
- cheap sacrificial screens;
- a second defender covering the square where the Impersonator will likely reveal;
- spacing valuable pieces so one reveal cannot cripple several systems at once.

The objective is not always to prevent the assassination. Often the realistic goal is to make the Impersonator trade for only one valuable model rather than destroy an entire support cluster.

---

## Motorcycles, Vehicles, and other extreme-mobility attackers

Very fast pieces can perform a DZ strike without any deployment skill at all.

Mobility functions as Order economy: if a model crosses in one Order what normal infantry would require three Orders to traverse, the remaining pool can be spent on attacks inside the DZ.

### Offensive use

Look for a seam in the defensive line rather than fighting the strongest ARO directly.

Once the attacker passes the last piece facing forward, continue exploiting movement and rear angles instead of stopping for unnecessary fights.

### Counters

The best defence is often to force changes of direction and additional Orders.

Useful tools include:

- staggered defenders;
- mines and deployables;
- cheap Direct Template Weapons;
- hacking and Repeaters against Hackable attackers;
- 360° Visors;
- terrain and deployment that prevent one straight movement corridor from reaching the support area.

A defensive piece does not need to defeat the fast attacker outright. Making it spend two extra Orders reaching the valuable targets may be enough to collapse the attack run.

---

## Super-Jump, Climbing Plus, Aerial, and vertical penetration

These pieces punish deployment that is safe only in two dimensions.

A model hidden behind a building may still be vulnerable from above, from a vertical wall, or from the opposite side of terrain that a Climbing Plus or Super-Jump piece can traverse efficiently.

### Offensive use

Use vertical mobility surgically.

With Super-Jump in particular, remember that the complete trajectory matters and the model cannot rely on Partial Cover while jumping. The goal is usually to expose the attacker to only the intended victim rather than jumping into the view of the whole enemy army.

Climbing Plus often provides more controlled vertical attack angles because the model can use walls as normal movement surfaces.

### Counters

Useful counters include:

- defenders on different elevations;
- crossfire from opposite sides of buildings;
- 360° Visors;
- rooftop sentries;
- deployment that does not assume a roof or back wall is inherently safe.

# Breach packages

## Vision control

Smoke, Eclipse, and White Noise can radically change a DZ strike because the standard answer to a deep attacker is often "make it cross multiple firing lanes."

Vision control can turn those firing lanes off.

### Smoke

A very fast attacker with Smoke can build a temporary corridor into the DZ:

1. Smoke the first dangerous lane;
2. move through it;
3. Smoke the next corner or gap;
4. enter the DZ at close range.

MSV can counter ordinary Smoke where applicable, so smoke-heavy DZ attackers become stronger when they can choose routes that avoid the MSV defender.

### Eclipse

Eclipse is stronger as a penetration tool because normal MSV-based shooting does not simply solve it.

Against Eclipse, the defender needs more non-shooting area denial.

### White Noise

White Noise can remove MSV-based sentries from the engagement and can create a safe path against pieces that would otherwise dominate a firing lane through visual technology.

### Counters to vision-control penetration

When the attacker can switch off LoF-based defence, rely more heavily on tools that defend **space rather than sight lines**:

- mines;
- Perimeter weapons;
- Repeaters and hacking against Hackable models;
- Direct Template Weapons positioned at emergence points;
- overlapping defence beyond the footprint of a single Smoke/Eclipse template.

A useful principle is:

> **Smoke defeats firing lanes; area denial defeats movement corridors.**

---

## Close combat

High mobility plus strong CC is a distinct DZ-strike package.

A conventional gunfighter still needs good range and Face-to-Face odds after entering the DZ. A strong CC attacker may only need to reach one corner.

Once in silhouette contact, the target's premium long-range gun and high BS may contribute very little.

Good CC targets include:

- Lieutenants hiding behind terrain;
- expensive long-range ARO or attack pieces;
- Hackers;
- Engineers and Doctors;
- isolated TAG/HI support pieces;
- REMs vulnerable to being tied up or destroyed at close range.

Berserk and similar trade-oriented mechanics can make this even more dangerous when a cheap attacker can force an exchange against a much more expensive target.

### Counters to CC penetration

Useful counters include:

- **depth** — do not place the valuable target immediately behind the first corner;
- **cheap sacrificial bodies** — make the attacker spend an Order killing a low-value screen first;
- **Direct Template Weapons** — punish the final approach;
- **mines and Perimeter weapons** — force Dodge or other defensive actions before contact;
- **mutual support** — another defender should threaten the attacker after it engages or kills the first target;
- **spacing** — do not allow one successful CC breach to roll directly into several valuable models.

## Other breach tools

| Capability | DZ-strike value |
|---|---|
| **Mimetism** | Makes cheap sentries and reactive gunfighters much less reliable |
| **Direct Templates** | Punish clustered cheerleaders and support troops; bypass BS quality |
| **Mines / Drop Bears** | Let the attacker damage something and then make removal or pursuit expensive |
| **Camouflage / Surprise** | Helps the attacker cross the final defensive layer and win the first engagement |
| **Hacking / KHD** | Lets the striker attack Hackable targets or defensive Hackers without relying only on guns |
| **Sensor / MSV** | Helps the striker hunt Camo or visual-modifier defenders that would otherwise stall the run |
| **Durability / Dogged / NWI** | Lets the attacker continue a run after taking a hit and supports deliberate trade sequences |

# DZ-strike / counter matrix

| DZ attack vector | Main strength | Strong counters |
|---|---|---|
| **Parachutist (Deployment Zone)** | Reliable rear/side insertion | 360° Visor, edge sentries, mines, crossfire, spacing |
| **Combat Jump** | Ignores normal approach geometry | Controlled Jump, 360° sentries, protected landing zones, mines, vertical coverage |
| **Deep Infiltration** | Starts one or two Orders from the DZ | Midfield/DZ-boundary screen, mines, Sensor, corridor denial |
| **Impersonation** | Bypasses ordinary defensive shell while protected by marker state | Biometric Visor, Discover layers, sacrificial screens, separation |
| **Motorcycle / fast MOV** | Converts movement into extreme Order efficiency | Mines, templates, hacking, choke points, 360° Visor |
| **Vehicle / Aerial attacker** | Huge movement plus unusual attack angles | Hacking, vertical crossfire, 360°, area denial |
| **Super-Jump / Climbing Plus** | Bypasses walls, roofs, and normal facing assumptions | 360° Visor, vertical crossfire, rooftop coverage |
| **Smoke/Eclipse striker** | Switches off conventional shooting defence | Mines, Perimeter, hacking, DTWs at emergence points, defence in depth |
| **High-CC striker** | Deletes pieces whose value depends on ranged combat | screens, templates, mines, spacing, disposable blockers |

These categories stack. For example:

- `Parachutist (Deployment Zone) + Mimetism + Boarding Shotgun` is substantially more dangerous than a generic Parachutist.
- `very high MOV + Smoke + strong CC + Dogged/NWI` can function as a **ground-launched DZ missile** despite having no advanced-deployment skill at all.

# Building DZ defence in layers

The best DZ defence is usually **cheap and layered**, not one expensive dedicated bodyguard.

## Outer layer — midfield and approach denial

Use:

- Camo;
- mines;
- Repeaters;
- cheap AROs;
- narrow corridors and choke points.

Purpose: make fast ground and infiltrating attackers spend resources before reaching the DZ.

## Edge and vertical layer

Use:

- cheap sentries;
- **360° Visors**;
- rooftop/elevated coverage;
- crossfire over likely Parachutist and Combat Jump routes.

Purpose: punish attacks that bypass the normal front line.

## Inner layer — reveal and breach punishment

Use:

- Boarding Shotguns;
- Direct Template Weapons;
- cheap sacrificial bodies;
- mines/Perimeter weapons;
- Biometric Visors where available.

Purpose: make the final approach to the Lieutenant and support structure expensive.

## Structural defence

Use:

- Lieutenant decoys;
- Chain of Command where appropriate;
- dispersed Orders and support pieces;
- redundant support;
- spacing that prevents one template or one attack route from reaching several critical models.

Purpose: limit the damage even when the opponent successfully penetrates the DZ.

# DZ-defense block checklist

When constructing a modular **DZ Defense block**, test whether it has answers to these penetration vectors:

1. rear/side edge entry;
2. arbitrary airborne landing;
3. marker-state / Impersonation penetration;
4. high-speed ground penetration;
5. vertical penetration;
6. vision-control penetration;
7. close-combat penetration.

A compact block does not need a unique model for every threat. Prefer pieces that overlap roles.

Especially efficient multi-role defenders include:

- cheap **360° Visor** troops;
- mines and other deployables;
- cheap DTWs;
- Repeaters when the likely attackers are Hackable;
- Biometric Visors in metas where Impersonation is a major concern;
- models that can both defend the DZ and contribute an Order, specialist function, Flash Pulse, or other utility.

The objective is not necessarily to make DZ penetration impossible.

The objective is to prevent penetration from creating a long chain of low-risk kills.

A good defensive result may be:

> enemy airborne attacker spends its insertion Order, another Order killing a cheap sentry, then dies or runs out of useful Orders.

A bad defensive result is:

> attacker lands safely and chains through Order generator → Hacker → Engineer → Lieutenant → REM because every model was facing forward and clustered together.
