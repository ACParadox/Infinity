# Zero Pain

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)
 SHORT SKILL / ARO
Comms Attack, Non-Lethal. 

Troopers can only perform Zero Pain if at least one of these is true:

- They are the Active Trooper.

- In the Reactive Turn, if they are allowed to declare an ARO.

- In the Active or Reactive Turn, this Program allows the user to make two WIP Rolls in a Face to Face Roll to evade all enemy Comms Attacks.

- A -3 WIP MOD is applied to the enemy Comms Attacks.

- If the user wins with either Roll, the Comms Attack fails, the user does not have to make any Saving Rolls, and they avoid any Special Effects of the Attack.

| Zero Pain | 0 | -3 | -- | 2 | -- | Short Skill / ARO | Nullifies Comms Attack. B 2 in ARO, Non-Lethal. |


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Zero Pain is a **reactive anti-Comms defense program**, not a Saving Roll modifier and not a passive protection. It functions as an **evasion Face to Face Roll** that can completely nullify an incoming Comms Attack before any Saving Rolls or States are applied.

This makes Zero Pain fundamentally different from Firewall bonuses or defensive Equipment: it does not mitigate consequences, it attempts to **prevent the attack from resolving at all**.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Core Mechanical Identity
Zero Pain allows the user to roll **two WIP dice in a Face to Face Roll** against *any* enemy Comms Attack, while also imposing a **–3 WIP MOD** on the attacker.

If the Zero Pain user wins with **either die**:
- The Comms Attack fails entirely
- No Saving Rolls are made
- No States or Special Effects are applied

This means Zero Pain directly contests the attack itself, rather than reacting afterward.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### What Counts as a Valid Trigger
Zero Pain can be declared when **any enemy Comms Attack** is directed at the user, provided one of the normal declaration conditions is met:
- Active Turn (user is the Active Trooper)
- Reactive Turn (user is eligible to declare an ARO)

Key clarification:
Zero Pain does **not** require the attacker to be a Hacker. Any Comms Attack qualifies, including non-hacking sources.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Zero Pain vs Reset
Zero Pain and Reset are often compared, but they are not interchangeable.

Key differences:
- Reset uses **one die**
- Zero Pain uses **two dice**
- Reset does not impose a MOD on the attacker
- Zero Pain applies **–3 WIP to the enemy**

Strategically:
- Reset is a general-purpose defensive skill
- Zero Pain is a **specialized, high-reliability counter** to Comms Attacks

In most cases, Zero Pain is statistically superior when facing dedicated Comms pressure.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Interaction with Burst and Multiple Attacks
Zero Pain is resolved as a **single Face to Face Roll**, even if:
- The enemy Comms Attack has Burst higher than 1
- Multiple dice are rolled by the attacker

Winning with either Zero Pain die cancels **all effects** of that Comms Attack declaration.

However:
- Separate Comms Attacks (from different Orders or different attackers) each require a new Zero Pain declaration
---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Criticals and Zero Pain
If the attacker scores a Critical:
- It behaves as normal within the Face to Face Roll
- Zero Pain can still cancel it by winning the Face to Face comparison

This is a major strength of Zero Pain:
It can negate even high-impact Critical-based Comms effects outright.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Zero Pain and ARO Pressure
Zero Pain having **Burst 2 in ARO** is one of its strongest features.

Practical consequences:
- It discourages speculative or low-odds ARO hacking
- It makes single-hacker ARO traps significantly less reliable
- It forces opponents to commit multiple Orders or multiple attack vectors

This shifts hacking from a low-risk control tool into a **resource-intensive commitment**.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### What Zero Pain Does NOT Do
Zero Pain does not:
- Provide ongoing protection outside declared Orders
- Affect non-Comms Attacks
- Prevent multiple sequential Orders from eventually succeeding
- Protect against attacks that do not allow a Face to Face Roll

It is **reactive and momentary**, not persistent.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Tactical Uses
Zero Pain excels on units that:
- Must push through hostile hacking or jammer networks
- Are expected to absorb repeated ARO pressure
- Cannot rely on Firewalls or TinBots

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Strategic Evaluation
Zero Pain is best understood as **Comms Counterplay**, not defense.

It:
- Converts enemy control tools into contested engagements
- Raises the Order cost of disabling key pieces
- Forces attackers to coordinate or abandon Comms-based denial plans

Used correctly, Zero Pain turns the psychological advantage of Comms Attacks against the attacker—every attempt becomes a gamble rather than a certainty.
