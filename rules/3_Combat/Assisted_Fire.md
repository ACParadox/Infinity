# Assisted Fire

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)
 LONG SKILL
Supportware, No Roll. 

Hacker | Firewall | Hacking Area | Repeater | Deployable Repeater | Hacking Programs Chart | Hacking Device

- The target must be a REM from the same Army List as the user, and must be inside the user's Hacking Area.

- Assisted Fire grants the target REM the Marksmanship Special Skill.

- When the user activates this Program, which does not require a Roll, place an Assisted Fire Token (SUP: ASSISTED FIRE) beside the user and beside the REM.

- Once activated, this Program's range covers the entire game table.

SUPPORTWARE

- A Trooper can benefit from the effects of only one Program with the Supportware Label at a time.

- Each Hacker can only sustain one active Supportware Program at a time.

- A Hacker can cancel an active Supportware Program by declaring another Program and expending the corresponding Order or ARO.

- A Supportware Program is automatically cancelled if the target of the Supportware is targeted by a new Supportware Program, or if the Hacker running the Program enters Isolated State or any Null States.

During their Active Turn, a Player that has two Troopers with EVO Hacking Devices on the table, declares Fairy Dust with one of them and chooses REMs as the Troop Type that will benefit from this Supportware.

For tactical reasons, they decide to spend the last Order on the other Trooper with an EVO Hacking Device to declare Enhanced Reaction on a REM located in the vanguard.

By designating a REM that was already benefitting from Fairy Dust, the new Supportware Program deactivates the Fairy Dust Program and the corresponding Token is removed from the table.

A Supportware Program is automatically cancelled if the target of the Supportware is targeted by a new Supportware Program, or if the Hacker running the Program enters Isolated State or any Null States.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The program does not change the weapon profile itself. Range Bands, Damage, Ammunition type, Traits, and Disposable values remain unchanged. Any confusion usually comes from players assuming Assisted Fire “upgrades” the weapon rather than modifying the Trooper’s BS Attack.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because Assisted Fire is Supportware, it follows the standard Supportware limitations:
- Only one instance of the same Supportware program can be active at a time per Hacker and only one Supportware buff active on a Trooper.
- Losing the Hacker, entering certain States, or voluntarily cancelling the program will immediately remove its effects.
This makes Assisted Fire vulnerable to counterplay via Hacking, even if the REM itself is hard to dislodge.

## Subtle Rules Interactions

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If the REM enters a Null State or a State that prevents declaring BS Attacks, Assisted Fire remains technically active but functionally useless until the REM can legally declare BS Attacks again. This distinction matters for timing interactions and State cancellation sequencing.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If multiple REMs could theoretically benefit, remember that Assisted Fire targets a single REM. There is no splash, aura, or shared effect, even if REMs are in base contact, in the same Combat Group, or activated consecutively.

## Tactical and Strategic Uses

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Assisted Fire shines brightest on REMs with:
- High Burst primary weapons
- Good range bands
- Access to long firelanes
It turns these units into highly order-efficient attack pieces that can challenge elite enemy shooters at a fraction of the cost.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Order efficiency is the real gain. By improving hit reliability, Assisted Fire reduces the number of Orders needed to win key Face to Face Rolls, which is often more valuable than raw lethality.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A common advanced tactic is to activate Assisted Fire *after* the REM has already been positioned. This minimizes wasted Orders if the REM is immediately Neutralized or Isolated before it can exploit the buff.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Defensively, Assisted Fire can act as a deterrent rather than a kill tool. An opponent may avoid challenging a boosted REM directly, choosing indirect approaches that cost them more Orders and time.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
From a strategic perspective, Assisted Fire pressures the opponent’s Hacking game. Even factions that normally ignore enemy Hackers may be forced to engage them to shut down Supportware, indirectly protecting your other assets.

## Design Intent (Unofficial Interpretation)

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Assisted Fire appears designed to:
- Elevate REM gunfighters into credible Active Turn threats
- Reward combined arms play between Hackers and attack pieces
- Create meaningful counterplay through Hacking rather than direct firepower alone

Used correctly, it is not just a damage boost, but a tempo control tool that shapes how both players allocate Orders during a Turn.

Also, since it needs to be applied first before being active, this means it won't protect against a first-turn alpha strike.
