# Template Weapons and Equipment

Combat Module

BS Weapons and Equipment that use a Template have an Area of Effect that affects not only the main target, but also an area of the game table determined by the size of the Template.

The Main Target of a Template Weapon or Equipment is a Game Element that must be able to be designated as a valid target for the BS Attack, from which the MODs applied to the Roll are determined, if a Roll is required. This Roll will be opposed separately, one by one, by the rest of the Enemies affected by the template.

There are two types of Weapons and Equipment Templates:

- Direct Template.

- Impact Template.

If the Main Target is outside the Area of Effect of the Template Weapon or Equipment, the BS Attack is automatically considered a failure and no Game Element will be affected by the Template.

## Types of Templates

In this rulebook, there are three kinds of Templates used by Weapons and pieces of Equipment: the Circular Template, the Large Teardrop Template and the Small Teardrop Template.

The height of the Templates used by Template Weapons and Equipment is equal to their radius, or half their width (except for those Ammunitions whose Traits otherwise specify it).

## Area of Effect

The Area of Effect of a Template is the area it covers with a single declaration of use. For example, if you declare an Attack using a Template Weapon, all Troopers or targets in Silhouette contact or inside the Area of Effect of the Template are affected by the Attack.

### Area of Effect of Template Weapons and Equipment

- When using a Template Weapon or Equipment, any Trooper whose base or Silhouette Template is in contact with the Template, or is partially or fully inside it, is affected by the Weapon or Equipment.

- Any Trooper affected by a Template Weapon or Equipment can declare Dodge as their second Short Skill or ARO, even if they do not have LoF to the Trooper declaring the Attack.

- As a general rule, Dodging an Attack with a Template requires a successful PH Roll.

- As an exception to this, in the following cases the Dodge requires a PH -3 Roll to avoid an Attack with a Template Weapon: If the Trooper affected by the Template Weapon Attack has no LoF to the attacker. If the Template Weapon Attack is from a Deployable Weapon (a Mine, for example).

- Once placed, the Template remains on the game table until the Resolution step of the Order.

- The Area of Effect of a Template can affect Allied Troopers as long as the Template has no PS value and does not inflict any State.

- When several Troopers are activated at the same time (a Coordinated Order or Fireteam, for example), the Template affects every Trooper that was in Silhouette contact with its Area of Effect at any time during that Order, since everything happens simultaneously during the Order.

- Critical Hits with Template Weapons: When a Critical is rolled when using a Template Weapon, it counts as a Critical against the Main Target of the attack, and only the Main Target. Against any Secondary Targets affected by the Template, it is a successful Roll instead of a Critical.

- Responding to a Template Weapon Roll: Against an Attack with a Template Weapon that required a Roll to hit whether it was an Impact Template Weapon or an Intuitive Attack, etc., each affected enemy may make a separate Face to Face Roll against the attacker.

- If the Trooper affected by the Template Weapon Attack has no LoF to the attacker.

- If the Template Weapon Attack is from a Deployable Weapon (a Mine, for example).

- When a Critical is rolled when using a Template Weapon, it counts as a Critical against the Main Target of the attack, and only the Main Target. Against any Secondary Targets affected by the Template, it is a successful Roll instead of a Critical.

- Against an Attack with a Template Weapon that required a Roll to hit whether it was an Impact Template Weapon or an Intuitive Attack, etc., each affected enemy may make a separate Face to Face Roll against the attacker.

Troopers affected by a Template Weapon do not benefit from the +3 MOD to the Saving Roll (SR) for Partial Cover.

## Template Weapons, and Allied and Neutral Troopers

Declaring Attacks against Allied or Neutral Troopers is not allowed, nor is it allowed to perform Attacks that may affect them, unless the Template lacks a PS Attribute value and does not inflict States. Therefore, if when declaring an Attack with a Template Weapon, an Allied or Neutral Trooper would be affected by the Template, that shot is cancelled (but not other shots of that same Burst that have no Allies or Neutrals within their Area of Effect). Any reactive Troopers within the Area of Effect of the cancelled shot can still declare AROs.

In this case of a cancelled shot, if a Weapon with the Disposable Trait was used, the initially declared use is still consumed.

### Template Weapons, and Allied and Neutral Troopers Example

Trooper 1 has B 2 with their Pulzar. When declaring a BS Attack against Troopers 2 and 3 it is found that Template B affects Trooper 4 ( Allied or Neutral ). Template B is therefore cancelled.

Troopers 2 and 3 may declare AROs normally.

## Template Weapons Into Close Combat

Template Weapons placed on a group of Troopers engaged in Close Combat will always affect every Trooper involved, even if, due to the Template’s placement, it contacts only some of them. Players must take this into account, since Attacks cannot be performed against Allied Troopers.

## Template Weapons, Scenery Elements and Total Cover

A Template’s Area of Effect may be determined by the presence of scenery elements on the game board, as seen in the graphics.

In Infinity, different Templates have a Blast Focus, which is used to determine if a Trooper is affected by the Template, or if the Template’s Area of Effect has been blocked by Total Cover from intervening scenery elements. For a Trooper to be affected by the Template, it must be possible to trace a LoF from the Template’s Blast Focus to the Trooper, without being blocked by Total Cover.

The Blast Focus is the center of the Circular Template and the narrow end of the Teardrop Templates.

LoF from the Blast Focus to a Trooper may only be drawn within the Area of Effect of the Template itself. (See FAQs.)

The Scenery element completely covers the enemy Trooper. Therefore, LoF cannot be traced from the Blast Focus so the Trooper is not affected by the Area of Effect. (See FAQs.)

## FAQs

Q: When firing a Direct Template Weapon, can you fire 'round' corners to hit secondary targets that are not in LoF from the firing Trooper?

A: No.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Template Weapons are one of the most misunderstood and misplayed elements in Infinity. Many errors come not from the weapon profiles themselves, but from how templates interact with ARO generation, Face to Face Rolls, movement declaration, and positioning along a movement path.

---
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Template placement is locked at declaration, not resolution. The Active Player must place the template when declaring the attack, choosing its orientation and point of origin based on the declared position. You cannot “adjust” the template later after seeing AROs or Dodge movement.

Common pitfall: Players sometimes try to rotate or slide the template after measuring Dodge movement. This is illegal and can drastically change outcomes.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Movement-based AROs are especially dangerous against Template Weapons. Because AROs can be declared at any point along the movement path, a Trooper can provoke a template ARO even if they end their movement out of the template’s final area.

Tactical implication: When advancing into narrow corridors or around corners, always consider the entire movement path, not just the final position.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Templates generate AROs even without Line of Fire. A Trooper affected by a Template Weapon is eligible to declare an ARO regardless of LoF or facing. This catches many players off guard, especially when using Intuitive Attack or speculative placements.

Tactical use: Templates are a reliable way to force AROs from Camouflage or Marker State Troopers that would otherwise remain passive.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Templates and multiple targets resolve independently. Each Trooper affected by the template resolves their own interaction. A successful Dodge by one Trooper does not cancel the attack against other Troopers under the same template.

Strategic value: This makes template weapons extremely efficient against Fireteams and clustered models, since a single Order can force multiple independent defensive rolls.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cover does not protect against template hits, but it still applies to Saving Rolls if the rules for that weapon allow it. Players often forget that while the BS MOD from Cover is irrelevant, the Saving Roll MOD is still applied if the target meets Cover conditions.

Practical reminder: Being “in Cover” still matters even when hit automatically.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Template Weapons are excellent anti-Surprise tools. Since they do not rely on BS rolls, negative MOD stacks from Mimetism, Surprise Attack, or poor range bands are irrelevant. This makes them ideal for clearing midfield threats and entrenched skirmishers.

Tactical advice: Low-BS warbands with templates punch far above their cost when used aggressively and supported by smoke or coordinated movement.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Templates are positional weapons, not attrition weapons. Their true strength lies in board control rather than raw lethality. The threat of a template often matters more than its actual damage output.

Strategic use: Use templates to deny corners, ladders, objectives, and approach routes, forcing opponents to reroute or waste Orders on cautious movement.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Deployable template-like Equipment (such as mines or similar effects) follows many of the same interaction principles, but players often forget they are separate game elements once placed. They generate their own ARO interactions and do not “belong” to the firing Trooper for Line of Fire purposes.

Common mistake: Treating deployables as if they share facing, LoF, or positioning flexibility with the deploying Trooper.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Finally, templates reward precise table discipline. Small errors in base placement, silhouette alignment, or template centering can radically alter outcomes. High-level play assumes exact positioning and mutual agreement before dice are rolled or effects are applied.

Best practice: Pause, confirm template placement with your opponent, then proceed. This avoids disputes and preserves game flow.

# Direct Template Weapons vs Impact Template Weapons
# Differences, Similarities, and Common Interactions

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Both Direct Template Weapons (DTW) and Impact Template Weapons (ITW) use templates to affect an area of the table and potentially multiple Troopers. They are often grouped together in casual discussion, but they behave very differently in terms of declaration, resolution, and tactical role.


### Similarity: 
- both weapon types can affect multiple Troopers with a single Order. Each affected Trooper resolves the attack independently, meaning Dodges, Saves, and States are checked per model, not per template.

Key takeaway: A single template never results in a single “group” resolution — Infinity always resolves per Trooper.

- both types generate AROs from any Trooper affected by the template, even without Line of Fire. This makes them powerful tools for forcing reactions from hidden, camouflaged, or out-of-LoF enemies.

Tactical overlap: Both DTWs and ITWs are excellent for breaking static defenses and punishing poor positioning.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Core difference: 
Direct Template Weapons do not make Attack Rolls. The template is placed and all affected Troopers are automatically hit unless they successfully oppose the attack (most commonly with Dodge).

Impact Template Weapons require a successful BS Attack Roll against a declared target. The template is only applied if the attack succeeds.

This single distinction drives most downstream rule differences.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Face to Face interaction differs sharply.  
Direct Template Weapons usually create Face to Face Rolls only through the defender’s Dodge (or similar opposing Skill). The attacker never rolls dice.

Impact Template Weapons almost always involve a Face to Face Roll if the target can react with a BS Attack, Dodge, or other opposing Skill, because the attacker is rolling normally.

Practical result: DTWs are reliable but low ceiling; ITWs are swingy but potentially devastating.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Template placement timing is different.  
Direct Template Weapons place the template immediately upon declaration, based on the user’s position and facing at that moment.

Impact Template Weapons place the template centered on the target only after a successful hit is confirmed during Resolution.

Common mistake: Players sometimes try to pre-place an Impact Template before rolling. This is incorrect and can influence movement and ARO decisions unfairly.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Range and MOD relevance diverge strongly.  
Direct Template Weapons largely ignore range bands, Cover BS MODs, and most negative MOD stacks, since no BS roll is made.

Impact Template Weapons fully interact with range bands, Cover, Mimetism, Surprise Attack, Visibility Zones, and Fireteam bonuses.

Strategic implication: DTWs are ideal for low-BS units and close-range brawls; ITWs reward high BS and strong MOD stacking.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Collateral risk is higher with Impact Template Weapons.  
Because the template is centered on the target after a successful hit, friendly Troopers or neutral elements near the target may be affected.

Direct Template Weapons are easier to “aim safely” because the template orientation is chosen directly and visibly at declaration.

Positioning lesson: ITWs punish careless clustering on both sides of the table.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### ARO deterrence profiles differ.  
Direct Template Weapons strongly discourage Dodge-less or low-PH Troopers from reacting aggressively, since they risk automatic hits.

Impact Template Weapons discourage AROs primarily through Burst and Face to Face pressure, not certainty of damage.

This makes DTWs psychologically oppressive in tight spaces, while ITWs dominate open firelanes.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategic roles are complementary, not redundant.  
Direct Template Weapons excel at:
- Clearing corners and doorways
- Trading up with cheap units
- Forcing movement and breaking stalemates

Impact Template Weapons excel at:
- Punishing clustered Fireteams
- Leveraging high BS platforms
- Projecting threat beyond close range

High-level armies often want access to both, using each where its interaction profile is strongest.
