# Total Control

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)
 SHORT SKILL / ARO
Comms Attack, Non-Lethal. 

- The target must be an enemy TAG, or a TAG in Possessed State.

- In the Active or Reactive Turn, this Program has a Burst of 1, allowing the user to perform one WIP Roll against the target.

- Each successful Roll, due to DA Ammunition, forces the target to make two Saving Rolls against BTS, with PS 4.

- Any failed Saving Roll causes the target to enter Possessed State, placing a Possessed State Token (POS) by them. If this Program is used against a TAG in Possessed State, any failed Saving Roll cancels the target's Possessed State, causing them to enter Normal State, and the Possessed State Token (POS) is removed.

- A Critical with Total Control forces its target to perform an additional Saving Roll.

- The range of this Program is the Hacker 's Hacking Area.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## TOTAL CONTROL — INTERACTION NOTES & COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS

Total Control is a non-lethal Hacking Program that applies the Possessed State to enemy TAGs. Despite being listed alongside disabling programs like Carbonite and Oblivion, its effect is fundamentally different: it temporarily transfers control of a TAG rather than restricting it.

It is a high-risk, high-reward tempo play. The TAG remains fully functional, armed, and order-efficient — just for the opposing player. This makes positioning, order timing, and follow-up planning far more important than the hacking roll itself.

Another easily missed point is that Total Control works both ways: using it on a TAG already in Possessed State removes that State and returns the TAG to Normal. This makes Total Control the primary *counter* to enemy possession attempts.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## ACTIVE VS REACTIVE USE — WHY ARO TOTAL CONTROL IS RARE BUT POWERFUL

Total Control can be declared in ARO, which is often overlooked. Because it is Burst 1 in both Active and Reactive Turns, it does not gain efficiency from Active Turn order stacking the way Carbonite or Oblivion do.

In ARO, Total Control is best understood as a *deterrent*, not a reliable plan. Most TAGs will prefer to shoot, Reset, or avoid entering Hacking Areas rather than risk losing control entirely. Even a low-probability ARO can force suboptimal movement or cautious play from a TAG-heavy opponent.

Reactive Total Control is strongest when layered with Repeaters, forcing TAGs to either stay out of key lanes or commit Orders to clearing the hacking net before advancing.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## TOTAL CONTROL VS RESET — FACE TO FACE REALITY

Reset is the universal defense against Total Control, and many players underestimate how often it will be declared. TAGs tend to have respectable WIP, and Firewalls or Tinbots further tilt the odds.

This means Total Control is rarely about “one clean roll.” It is usually a resource drain:
- Forcing Reset instead of attacks
- Pulling Orders out of the main attack piece
- Creating windows for follow-up Carbonite, Oblivion, or Spotlight

Treat Total Control as part of a hacking *sequence*, not a standalone solution.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## POSSESSED TAGS — TACTICAL EXPLOITATION

Once a TAG is Possessed, the goal is not subtlety — it is damage and disruption before the State is inevitably removed.

Key priorities when you gain control:
- Expend the TAG’s Orders immediately
- Move it into exposed or disadvantageous positions
- Force it to attack its own army, especially clustered links or cheerleaders
- Spend Orders inefficiently (Move into bad arcs, break Fireteams)

Do not try to “preserve” the TAG. Expect it to be Reset, hacked back, or repaired. Every Order you extract is profit.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## INTERACTION WITH ENGINEERS AND REPAIRS

Engineers are the natural counterplay to Total Control. Clearing Possessed State restores full functionality without Order loss beyond positioning the Engineer.

This creates a tactical triangle:
- Hackers threaten TAGs
- Engineers protect TAGs
- Killers and direct fire protect Hackers

A Total Control attempt that forces an Engineer to move forward is often already successful, even if possession never lands. It exposes valuable support pieces and breaks defensive formations.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## REPEATERS, FIREWALLS, AND RISK MANAGEMENT

Using Total Control through enemy Repeaters is especially dangerous. Firewalls penalize the attack and increase BTS resistance, making possession unlikely and exposing your Hacker to retaliation from Killer Hackers.

Best practice is to:
- Use friendly Repeaters
- Clear or bypass enemy Repeater nets first
- Pair Total Control Hackers with Killer Hackers or Cybermask protection

Total Control is one of the most punishing hacking programs when it succeeds — and one of the most dangerous when attempted recklessly.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## STRATEGIC USE — WHEN TO CHOOSE TOTAL CONTROL OVER OTHER PROGRAMS

Choose Total Control when:
- The enemy TAG is isolated from Engineer support
- The TAG is central to the opponent’s plan
- You can immediately exploit possession with Orders
- Mission tempo rewards disruption over permanent disable

Avoid Total Control when:
- Engineers are nearby and safe
- Firewalls heavily protect the TAG
- You cannot capitalize on possession immediately
- A simple Isolated or IMM-B result would already achieve your goal


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## SUMMARY — TOTAL CONTROL IN PRACTICE

Total Control is not a shutdown button. It is a tempo weapon.

Used well, it:
- Forces defensive play from TAGs
- Extracts Orders and positioning errors
- Punishes unsupported or overextended centerpieces
- Creates psychological pressure in TAG-heavy lists

Used poorly, it:
- Exposes Hackers to Killer retaliation
- Wastes Orders against Firewalls
- Achieves nothing if not immediately exploited

Mastery of Total Control comes from understanding *when* possession matters more than disabling — and having a plan ready before you roll.
