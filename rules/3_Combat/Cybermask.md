# Cybermask

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)
 LONG SKILL
NFB, No Roll. 

- The user must be outside the LoF of enemy Markers or Troopers.

- The user enters the Impersonation-2 state, which does not require a Roll, replacing the user's model with an Impersonation-2 Marker (IMP-2).

- The effects of this Program persist until the user reveals themselves as per the Impersonation-2 state rules.

- This Program affects only the Hacker himself and therefore has no range.

While the Hacker is in IMP-2 State, the Program’s NFB Label applies, so they cannot use or apply any other Skill, piece of Equipment, or Hacking Program that has the NFB Label, for example Mimetism, Albedo, Holoprojector etc.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cybermask is often misunderstood as “just another Marker state,” but its real power comes from how it **overwrites the Trooper’s presence on the table**. Once Cybermask is successfully applied, the Trooper is replaced by a Marker and is no longer treated as a Model for most interaction purposes. This immediately cuts off many enemy options that require a valid Trooper target, not just Line of Fire.

A frequent rules pitfall is forgetting that Cybermask is a **Hacking Program**, not an Automatic Skill. This means:
- It is declared as a Skill that can generate AROs.
- It can be opposed by Reset or other valid Hacking AROs.
Players sometimes assume Cybermask is “safe” because it does not directly attack the enemy, but it is still an interactive declaration that can be contested.

Cybermask interacts strongly with **ARO generation**. While in Impersonation-2 state, enemies cannot declare BS Attack or most other AROs unless they Discover This makes Cybermask one of the a strong tool for **order-efficient repositioning** in the midfield, especially through fire lanes controlled by conventional shooters.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
One easily missed interaction is that Cybermask does **not grant Camouflaged State**. Effects that explicitly reference Camouflage (bonuses, penalties, or automatic interactions) do not apply unless the rule explicitly affects all Markers.

Cybermask is often used defensively, but its offensive utility is underestimated. Entering Cybermask allows a Hacker to:
- Safely reposition into optimal ZoC coverage.
- Threaten enemy Hackable units without revealing too early.
This creates psychological pressure, forcing opponents to spend Orders on Discover or repositioning rather than advancing their own plan.

A subtle interaction occurs with **Zone of Control AROs**. A Cybermasked Trooper can still generate hacking pressure while remaining difficult to directly respond to. Opponents may have valid AROs (such as Reset), but these are often inefficient compared to the cost of the Cybermask Order itself.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cybermask synergizes extremely well with objectives and scenario play. Because many mission actions only require a Trooper to be present and active, Cybermask allows Hackers to approach objectives with reduced risk, then reveal only when the objective interaction is declared. This is particularly effective late in the Turn when the opponent has limited Orders left to respond.

Be mindful of cancellation timing. Cybermask is typically cancelled when the Trooper declares an Attack or other revealing action. Planning Orders so that movement, positioning, and final objective interaction or attack happen in a tight sequence is key. Poor sequencing can accidentally expose the Hacker earlier than intended, wasting the advantage Cybermask provides.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cybermask is  powerful on a Killer Hacker because it directly supports the Killer Hacker’s core role: **hunting enemy Hackers while avoiding conventional threats**. 
Killer Hackers are optimized for infowar duels, not gunfights, and Cybermask minimizes the situations where they can be attacked by non-hacking pieces.

A Cybermasked Killer Hacker can move through fire lanes and contested midfield areas with far less risk. 
This allows the Killer Hacker to **choose when and where hacking fights happen**, instead of being forced into bad trades by ARO pieces or random Discover + Shoot attempts.

Cybermask enables the Killer Hacker to **close distance safely**. Many hacking programs are strongest or only usable within Zone of Control. Cybermask lets the Killer Hacker walk into optimal range without exposing themselves early, turning short-range hacking into a deliberate ambush rather than a gamble.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Once revealed, a Killer Hacker is often exposed and vulnerable. Cybermask helps ensure that when the Killer Hacker reveals, it is:
- On favorable terms
- Against the correct target
- With maximum lethality and minimum retaliation

Cybermask also protects the Killer Hacker from enemy Killer Hackers. While Cybermasked, the Killer Hacker is harder to directly engage in hacking duels unless the opponent commits to specific counterplay. This reduces the chance of losing an expensive or mission-critical piece to a reactive hacking ARO.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
From a tactical sequencing perspective, Cybermask lets a Killer Hacker operate in a **two-Order strike pattern**:
1. Advance and position safely under Cybermask.
2. Reveal to execute a decisive hacking attack or objective action.
This sequencing is far more reliable than trying to brute-force positioning through enemy control zones.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategically, Cybermask turns the Killer Hacker into a **midfield predator** rather than a backline support piece. Instead of waiting for enemies to enter hacking range, the Killer Hacker actively shapes the battlefield by threatening large areas of the table, forcing the opponent to respect hacking danger even when no Hacker is visibly present.

Also, since it needs to be applied first before being active, this means it won't protect against a first-turn alpha strike.
