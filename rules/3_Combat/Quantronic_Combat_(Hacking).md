# Quantronic Combat (Hacking)

Combat Module

In Infinity, infowar and cyberwarfare tasks, defined as Hacking, are shouldered by the Hackers, specialized Troopers who can use the different Hacking Programs which define quantronic combat.

According to their nature, Hacking Programs can either be aggressive to affect enemy Troopers, defensive to protect Allied Troopers, supportive to boost Allied Troopers or affect scenery items and objectives in scenario missions.

When making your Army Lists in Infinity Army, a summary of the Hacking Programs will be included, in the same way that a summary of your Troopers’ weapons is included.

## Upgrade Programs (UPGRADE)

Custom-made software tailored to the style and preference of specific infowar operatives. If available, they will be listed in the Unit Profile, in round brackets next to the name of the Hacker or the Device.

## Supportware Programs

These are programs of extended duration which are mainly designed to support allied Troopers on the battlefield by granting them bonuses to improve their combat capabilities. Likewise, they may also be designed to hinder enemy Troopers, applying negative MODs to them.

SUPPORTWARE

- A Trooper can benefit from the effects of only one Program with the Supportware Label at a time.

- Each Hacker can only sustain one active Supportware Program at a time.

- A Hacker can cancel an active Supportware Program by declaring another Program and expending the corresponding Order or ARO.

- A Supportware Program is automatically cancelled if the target of the Supportware is targeted by a new Supportware Program, or if the Hacker running the Program enters Isolated State or any Null States.

During their Active Turn, a Player that has two Troopers with EVO Hacking Devices on the table, declares Fairy Dust with one of them and chooses REMs as the Troop Type that will benefit from this Supportware.

For tactical reasons, they decide to spend the last Order on the other Trooper with an EVO Hacking Device to declare Enhanced Reaction on a REM located in the vanguard.

By designating a REM that was already benefitting from Fairy Dust, the new Supportware Program deactivates the Fairy Dust Program and the corresponding Token is removed from the table.

A Supportware Program is automatically cancelled if the target of the Supportware is targeted by a new Supportware Program, or if the Hacker running the Program enters Isolated State or any Null States.

## Hacking Devices and Hacking Programs: Characteristics

Hacking Devices and Hacking Programs have the following characteristics:

- They act in the user's Hacking Area.

- They do not require LoF to act, unless the Hacking Program's own description states otherwise.

- Only Troopers in Model form may be targeted by Hacking Programs.

- They benefit, if applicable, from the target's Targeted State.

## Example of Quantronic Combat (Hacking)

Step 1: The Active Trooper declares a Move from point A to point B.

The Reactive Trooper declares a Carbonite Hacking Program ARO.

The Active Trooper declares Reset for their second Skill of the Order. They do not declare a BS Attack against the Hacker because it is clear that they lack LoF to the Hacker along their entire movement path.

Step 2: The Face to Face Rolls are performed.

Active Trooper, Reset ( WIP Roll, in this case there are no MODs to apply): 1 d20 with Success Value (SV) = 13.

Reactive Trooper, Carbonite (WIP Roll, in this case there are no MODs to apply): 1 d20 with SV = 13.

They both succeed, but the Reactive Trooper's 7 cancels the Active Trooper's 3.

Step 3: Therefore, as the Hacker wins the Face to Face Roll, the Active Trooper must perform 2 Saving Rolls due to DA Ammunition, against PS 7.
Saving Roll = 13 (PS 7 + BTS 6).

Due to the 14, the Active Trooper enters Immobilized-B State, placing an Immobilized-B Token next to them.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Quantronic Combat is not just a “third attack type”; it is a parallel combat layer that overlaps movement, AROs, States, and Order efficiency. Many misunderstandings come from treating hacking like shooting-with-WIP instead of as an information-control and state-application system.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Hacking Attacks are Attacks for all core purposes. This matters more than it first appears: they generate AROs, can be Face to Face, can be affected by Surprise Attack, and interact with skills that trigger on “being attacked”, even though no physical weapon is involved.

Hacking Area is frequently misunderstood as “ZoC only”. In practice, it is ZoC plus any valid Repeaters. This means a Trooper can be attacked or forced into a Face to Face Roll by a Hacker who is physically far away, as long as the digital path exists.

Because Repeaters project hacking presence without projecting Line of Fire, hacking often creates AROs that feel “invisible” to newer players. The Active Player must always consider whether movement will enter an enemy Hacking Area even when no enemy Model is visible.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Reset is not a universal defense against hacking. Reset only becomes Face to Face if it directly affects the hacking attack being declared. If the hacker’s program does not oppose Reset (or the Reset user is not the target), the hacker may be rolling Normal Rolls.

This makes positioning critical: a Hacker attacking multiple targets through Repeaters can force some targets into Normal Rolls while others contest Face to Face. 

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Hacking programs that impose States (Isolated, Immobilized, Targeted, etc.) are often stronger than those that cause direct damage. State application bypasses ARM and Cover and instead attacks Order economy and positioning.

Isolated in particular is a strategic State rather than a lethal one. Removing a Trooper from the Order Pool can be more impactful than inflicting a Wound, especially on elite or Order-generating units.

The Burst of hacking programs in ARO is always 1 unless explicitly modified. This leads to a common mistake: players assume multiple Repeaters or multiple Hackers stack Burst. They do not. Each ARO is still a single declaration with B1.

However, multiple Hackers can declare separate AROs against the same activation if each is eligible. This increases the chance of at least one successful program without violating Burst limits.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Face to Face hacking is resolved exactly like BS or CC Face to Face, including Criticals. A Critical hacking success can apply additional effects even when no Saving Rolls are involved, depending on the program.

This makes high-WIP Hackers disproportionately dangerous in reactive play, especially when protected by Repeaters that force attackers into multiple unfavorable Face to Face rolls.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Stealth interacts with hacking in a subtle but important way. A Stealth Trooper moving inside ZoC does not automatically generate hacking AROs unless the movement meets the normal requirements to break Stealth.

This makes Stealth one of the strongest counters to defensive hacking nets, particularly for midfield specialists and attack pieces trying to cross repeater-heavy zones.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Hacking Attacks do not require Line of Fire, but they do require valid targets. Not every Trooper is hackable, and not every program can target every Troop Type. Forgetting target restrictions is one of the most common illegal plays.

From a tactical standpoint, this means mixed armies (Hackable and non-hackable Troopers advancing together) can dilute the effectiveness of enemy hacking defenses.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Supportware programs are often undervalued because they do not immediately affect the opponent. Their real strength lies in Order efficiency: one successful program can multiply the effectiveness of several Orders spent later.

Because Supportware is usually declared without an opposing Face to Face Roll, protecting your Hacker during setup and early turns is essential. Losing the Hacker often collapses the entire support plan.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
White Noise enable safe movement lanes for otherwise vulnerable Troopers and can temporarily reshape the table in ways no other attack type can.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Quantronic Combat rewards pre-measuring mentally, not physically. You must plan where Repeaters will project control before Orders are spent, because once movement is declared, hacking AROs may already be locked in.

Players who think of hacking as an area-denial tool rather than a direct-damage option tend to extract far more value from it over the course of a game.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
In summary, hacking is about control: control of movement, Orders, States, and risk. Its power is often indirect, delayed, or positional, which makes it easy to underestimate—but devastating when used deliberately.
