# Controlled Jump

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)
 SHORT SKILL / ARO
Supportware, No Roll. 

- When the user declares the use of this Program, which does not require a Roll, the Program's Effects are applied immediately, instead of in the Resolution step, and a Controlled Jump Supportware Token (Sup: Controlled Jump) is placed beside the user.

- If declared as an ARO to an enemy Trooper declaring Combat Jump, this means that the -3 MOD will affect the enemy Trooper's PH Roll.

- It is not mandatory to declare Controlled Jump as an ARO to an enemy Trooper declaring Combat Jump, instead the Hacker can choose to wait and declare an ARO in the normal way.

- As long as Controlled Jump is active, when performing the Combat Jump Skill every Allied Trooper applies a +3 MOD while every Enemy Trooper must apply a -3 MOD. If both players have Controlled Jump active, the effects of both Programs are cancelled.

- A player cannot have more than one active Controlled Jump Program.

- This Program's range covers the entire game table.

- This Program affects Troopers even if they don’t have the Hackable Characteristic.

- Controlled Jump can be declared as an ARO against a Combat Jump Long Skill anywhere on the table.

SUPPORTWARE

- A Trooper can benefit from the effects of only one Program with the Supportware Label at a time.

- Each Hacker can only sustain one active Supportware Program at a time.

- A Hacker can cancel an active Supportware Program by declaring another Program and expending the corresponding Order or ARO.

- A Supportware Program is automatically cancelled if the target of the Supportware is targeted by a new Supportware Program, or if the Hacker running the Program enters Isolated State or any Null States.

During their Active Turn, a Player that has two Troopers with EVO Hacking Devices on the table, declares Fairy Dust with one of them and chooses REMs as the Troop Type that will benefit from this Supportware.

For tactical reasons, they decide to spend the last Order on the other Trooper with an EVO Hacking Device to declare Enhanced Reaction on a REM located in the vanguard.

By designating a REM that was already benefitting from Fairy Dust, the new Supportware Program deactivates the Fairy Dust Program and the corresponding Token is removed from the table.

A Supportware Program is automatically cancelled if the target of the Supportware is targeted by a new Supportware Program, or if the Hacker running the Program enters Isolated State or any Null States.

## FAQs

Q: Does Controlled Jump affect the deployment of Speedballs?

A: No. Controlled Jump only applies to Troopers.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Controlled Jump is a Supportware Hacking Program available exclusively to EVO Hacking Devices. It is a table-wide effect that modifies the PH Roll of every Trooper that performs the Combat Jump Special Skill, regardless of faction, position, or Hackable status.

Unlike most Hacking Programs, Controlled Jump does not require a Roll and applies its effects immediately when declared. This timing is critical: the modifier is in place before any Combat Jump PH Rolls are resolved, and it persists until the Supportware is cancelled or replaced.

Controlled Jump applies a +3 MOD to all Allied Troopers performing Combat Jump and a –3 MOD to all Enemy Troopers performing Combat Jump. If both players have an active Controlled Jump at the same time, the effects cancel out completely for both sides

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A frequent misunderstanding is that Controlled Jump only applies to the next Combat Jump. This is incorrect. As long as the Supportware Token remains on the table, it affects every Combat Jump attempted by either player.

Controlled Jump may be declared as an ARO in direct response to an enemy Trooper declaring Combat Jump, even though the Hacker is not in LoF, ZoC, or Hacking Area of the deploying unit. The Program explicitly ignores normal range and targeting limitations

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Declaring Controlled Jump as an ARO is optional. The Reactive Player may instead choose to delay and declare a different ARO if eligible. This allows mind games when an enemy reveals Combat Jump but you are unsure whether to commit your EVO’s Supportware slot.

Controlled Jump affects Troopers even if they do not have the Hackable Characteristic. This means it applies equally to Heavy Infantry, Warbands, TAG Paratroopers, and any other unit using Combat Jump

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Only one Controlled Jump Program may be active per player at any time. Declaring another Supportware Program (such as Assisted Fire, Fairy Dust, or Enhanced Reaction) automatically cancels Controlled Jump, removing its Token immediately

Because Controlled Jump is Supportware, each EVO Hacker can only sustain one active Supportware Program. This creates a strategic tradeoff between improving Airborne Deployment reliability and buffing REMs elsewhere on the table.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Interaction with Combat Jump resolution:
The PH MOD from Controlled Jump directly modifies the Combat Jump Roll. A failed Combat Jump can result in deviation and unsafe placement, making the –3 penalty from enemy Controlled Jump especially punishing for low-PH Troopers.

If multiple Combat Jump Troopers are deployed in the same Turn, Controlled Jump applies to all of them without additional Orders. This makes it disproportionately strong when supporting lists that rely on multiple airborne units.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Controlled Jump does not affect Parachutist deployments, as Parachutist does not require a Roll. This distinction is easily overlooked and often misplayed in casual games.

Strategic use – Active Turn:
Activating Controlled Jump before attempting several Combat Jumps dramatically increases reliability, especially for PH 10–11 units. This is often worth the Order expenditure if at least two airborne units are planned.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategic use – Reactive Turn:
Keeping an EVO Hacker safe and reserving Controlled Jump as an ARO can deter enemy Combat Jump entirely. The threat of a –3 MOD may force opponents to delay or avoid airborne insertion altogether.

Psychological pressure:
Even if the enemy never attempts Combat Jump, the presence of an EVO Hacker capable of Controlled Jump shapes deployment and list-building decisions. Opponents may choose safer Parachutist profiles or avoid Airborne Deployment entirely.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
List design considerations:
Armies with access to EVO Hackers gain disproportionate value from Combat Jump units. Conversely, armies that rely heavily on Combat Jump should plan redundancy or accept higher risk when facing factions commonly fielding EVO Hackers.

Common pitfall:
Players sometimes forget that Controlled Jump affects enemy Combat Jump during their own Active Turn as well. If you leave Controlled Jump active, you are also protecting yourself defensively without further Orders.
