## BS Attack Into a Close Combat

If a BS Attack is declared against an enemy Trooper that is engaged in CC Combat a -6 MOD must be applied for each Allied Trooper engaged in the CC (in addition to any MODs for Range, Cover, Mimetism... for the target).

Every failed BS Attack Roll will hit the Allied Trooper, forcing them to perform a Saving Roll. If there are several Allied Troopers locked in that Close Combat, then the Trooper’s player chooses which one of them receives each hit.

## BS Attack Modifiers (MOD)

Before performing the BS Attack Roll (or Rolls), the player must check every MOD they must apply. There are MODs that will affect the numerical value of the roll, the Attribute and others may affect the BS Attack Roll's Burst (B). Existing MODs are:

- Cover

- Range

- Special Skills, Weapons, pieces of Equipment, or States.

Modifiers Explained

### Cover

The term Cover refers to all pieces of scenery that partially or completely obstruct LoF, thus preventing the attacker from making a clean BS Attack.

- If the target is in Total Cover, the attacker may not perform a BS Attack with Weapons, Special Skills, or Equipment, that requires LoF.

- If the target is in Partial Cover, the attacker will apply a -3 MOD to their BS Attack Roll, and the target of the BS Attack applies a +3 MOD to its Saving Roll (SR), if necessary.

### Types of Cover

Total Cover completely blocks the attacker’s vision of his target, obstructing any LoF to their Silhouette.

The target is in Total Cover when one or more scenery items completely blocks LoF to its Silhouette.

Partial Cover does not allow the attacker to see the whole Silhouette of their BS Attack ’s target.

The target is in Partial Cover when they are in contact with a piece of scenery that partially obscures their Silhouette.

Climb, restrictions for Troopers and vertical surfaces:

- A Trooper who is on a vertical surface cannot benefit from Partial Cover MODs.


### Range

All BS Weapons, pieces of Equipment, and Special Skills that can make BS Attacks apply a Range MOD that depends on the distance between the attacker and the target.

If the target is beyond the maximum Range of the BS Weapon, piece of Equipment or Special Skill, then the BS Attack automatically fails (the Order is spent, Disposable weapons lose a use, etc.).

For example, for the Combi Rifle we have the following values:

| Name | 8" | 16" | 24" | 32" | 40" | 48" | 96" | PS | B | Ammo | Save Attr. | Save Rolls | Traits |

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Combi Rifle | +3 | +3 | -3 | -3 | -6 | -6 | -- | 7 | 3 | N | ARM | 1 | Suppressive Fire |

- If the distance between the Trooper and the Target is from 0 to 16 inches, the Trooper has a +3 MOD to their BS Attack Roll due to Range.

- If the distance is greater than 16 inches and equal to or less than 32 inches, the Trooper has a -3 MOD to their BS Attack Roll due to Range.

- If the distance is greater than 32 inches and equal to or less than 48 inches, the Trooper has a -6 MOD to their BS Attack Roll due to Range.

- The BS Attack automatically fails if the Range is more than 48 inches.

### Special Skills, Weapons, Equipment, or States

Certain Special Skills, Weapons, Equipment or States can apply a MOD to BS Attack Rolls. These Special Skills, Weapons, pieces of Equipment, or States are explained in the respective sections of this rulebook.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
BS Attack is a Common Skill, which means it is extremely flexible but also very strict in timing and declaration. A frequent mistake is treating BS Attack as “just shooting” instead of a fully declared Skill with multiple locked-in decisions. When you declare a BS Attack, you must already specify weapon, ammo, targets, burst split, and the point of origin for the attack. You cannot adjust these later after seeing AROs or measurements.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The declaration point matters more than many players realize. If you Move + BS Attack, you must declare from which exact point along the movement path the shots are fired. This determines Range MODs, Cover, and even whether some AROs are valid. You cannot “float” the attack along the movement; it is a single, fixed firing point chosen at declaration.

BS Attack does not automatically generate a Face to Face Roll. It only becomes Face to Face if the target declares a Skill that directly affects the outcome of the attack (such as BS Attack, Dodge, etc). If multiple enemies react, some rolls may be Face to Face while others are Normal Rolls in the same Order, which is a common source of confusion.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Burst division is all-or-nothing per target. Once Burst is split, each target is resolved independently, and losing dice due to unmet Requirements does not let you reassign them. This makes over-splitting Burst risky, especially when engaging enemies with different MOD stacks or visibility conditions.

In ARO, BS Attack is always Burst 1 unless a rule explicitly modifies it. Players sometimes forget that bonuses which increase Burst in the Active Turn often do nothing in ARO, while bonuses that grant Special Dice can still apply. This makes Active Turn gunfights fundamentally asymmetric even before MODs are considered.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Template weapons used via BS Attack still follow all normal BS Attack declaration rules. However, Direct Template Weapons bypass the roll entirely and therefore ignore many interactions that normally apply to BS Attacks, such as Special Dice or certain negative MODs. This distinction is easy to miss when switching between weapons on the same trooper.

Criticals in BS Attack are often underestimated. A Critical does not just guarantee a hit; it adds an additional Saving Roll unless the ammunition says otherwise. This means high-Burst, high-Success-Value shooters dramatically increase lethality not just by landing hits, but by fishing for extra Saves through Criticals.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cover interacts with BS Attack in two separate ways: it applies a negative MOD to the attacker’s BS Roll and a positive MOD to the target’s Saving Roll. Players sometimes mentally compress this into “Cover is -3,” which leads to tactical errors when evaluating AP, Shock, or high-Damage weapons.

BS Attack is often best used as a positioning tool, not just a killing tool. Forcing an enemy to declare Dodge instead of BS Attack can turn a Face to Face into a favorable exchange or even a Normal Roll for you against other targets. Sometimes the value of BS Attack is in controlling enemy reactions rather than inflicting wounds.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Coordinating BS Attacks across multiple Orders is more important than maximizing Burst in a single Order. Stripping enemy MODs (by removing Mimetism sources, forcing Prone, or denying Cover angles) often yields better long-term results than pushing unfavorable Face to Face Rolls early.

Finally, remember that BS Attack is just one tool in the Combat Module. Over-reliance on straight gunfights is often a losing strategy in Infinity. BS Attack becomes far more effective when combined with movement, angles, hacking pressure, and template threats that limit or dictate the opponent’s ARO choices.
