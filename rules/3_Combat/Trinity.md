# Trinity

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)
 SHORT SKILLS / ARO
Comms Attack 

- The target must be an enemy Hacker.

- In the Active Turn, this Program's Burst of 3 allows the user to perform three WIP Rolls, which can be distributed among one or more targets as usual.

- The user applies a +3 WIP MOD to their Roll.

- Each successful Roll forces the target to make a Saving Roll against BTS, with PS 6.

- For each failed Saving Roll, the target receives 1 Wound.

- A Critical with Trinity forces its target to perform an additional Saving Roll.

- The range of this Program is the Hacker 's Hacking Area.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Trinity is a Hacking Attack designed purely for cybercombat between Hackers. Unlike most other Hacking Programs, Trinity does not impose States like Immobilized or Isolated; its sole purpose is to inflict direct damage on enemy Hackers. This makes it the primary “killer app” in hacking confrontations, especially when contesting objectives or defending the Hacking Area.

Trinity can only be declared against valid enemy Hackers within Hacking Area and has no effect on REMs, HI, TAGs, or other troopers unless they possess the Hacker Special Skill. This sharply limits its applicability, but also defines its role very clearly.

Trinity interactions with AROs are critical to understand. Enemy Hackers can respond to Trinity with Reset or with their own Hacking Attacks, including Trinity itself. This often results in Face to Face Rolls between Hackers, turning hacking duels into high-risk, high-reward exchanges rather than guaranteed effects.

One easily missed interaction is that Trinity does not automatically shut down enemy hacking defenses. If multiple Hackers cover the same Hacking Area, declaring Trinity against one does nothing to prevent others from declaring AROs such as Oblivion, Spotlight, or even Trinity themselves, as long as requirements are met. This makes “stacked” hacking nets very resilient.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because Trinity causes direct damage, it interacts strongly with BTS and multiple Wound structures. Hackers with low BTS or single Wound profiles are extremely vulnerable, while elite Hackers with higher BTS or multiple Wounds can often risk Face to Face rolls and survive. This asymmetry strongly influences hacking tactics and list design.

Trinity is often underestimated as an ARO. Even with Burst reduced to 1, the threat of a damaging Face to Face roll can deter enemy Hackers from aggressive Spotlight or Oblivion attempts. Simply having a visible or well-positioned Hacker can exert strong area denial through the threat of Trinity alone.

Strategically, Trinity shines as a counter-hacking tool rather than an objective enabler. Its best use is clearing enemy Hackers that protect key lanes, Repeaters, or objectives, thereby opening the board for TAGs, Heavy Infantry, or aggressive movement without fear of debilitating hacking programs.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A subtle tactical use of Trinity is order efficiency. Removing a single key enemy Hacker can collapse an entire Repeater network, effectively neutralizing multiple hacking threats with one successful engagement. This indirect payoff is often far more valuable than the immediate damage itself.

Trinity should rarely be your first hacking declaration if the enemy has multiple ARO-capable Hackers. In those situations, baiting Reset, forcing movement, or attacking Repeaters directly can be safer approaches before committing to a Trinity exchange that might expose your Hacker to multiple Face to Face rolls.

Finally, Trinity reinforces the idea that hacking in Infinity is not just about States and board control, but also about lethal duels. Players who ignore Trinity often find their Hackers eliminated quickly, while players who respect its threat shape their movement, positioning, and hacking declarations much more carefully.
