# CC Attack

Common Skills
 SHORT SKILL / ARO
Attack 

Skill that allows Close Combat fighting, striking an adversary in Silhouette contact.

- Be using a Melee Weapon, a Skill, or piece of Equipment that allows for a CC Attack.

- Be in Silhouette contact with an enemy Model or Target.

- Allows use of the CC Attribute to fight in Close Combat (CC).

- By declaring a CC Attack, Troopers may only use those Weapons with the CC Trait.

- If a Trooper has several targets and a Burst value that is higher than 1, the player must clarify how they are dividing the Burst when declaring the CC Attack.

- In the Resolution step, check which MODs to apply, and make the CC Attack Rolls using the corresponding Attribute.

- For each success obtained in CC Attack Rolls, the target must perform a Saving Roll SR: Success Value: Success Value = ARM/BTS + PS + Cover (+3 if Partial Cover) + other MODs

### Coup de Grâce

- Coup de Grâce: When declaring the CC Attack, if the Enemy Target is in Unconscious State (including if it is represented by a Shasvastii-Embryo Token), then, without needing to perform a roll, the target automatically goes from Unconscious to Dead State without the possibility of making a Saving Roll. Coup de Grâce cannot be used against a Trooper who has activated the Dogged or No Wound Incapacitation Special Skill. Therefore, the CC Attack is resolved normally.

Modifiers Explained Examples

- CC Attack (-3) means that, when the user declares CC Attack, their targets apply a -3 MOD to any Face to Face Rolls.

- CC Attack (SR-1) means that, when the user declares CC Attack, their targets apply a -1 MOD to their Saving Rolls against the user's hits.

- CC Attack (Shock) means that the user adds Shock Ammunition to all their CC Attacks.

- CC Attack (Continuous Damage) means that the user applies the Continuous Damage Trait to all their CC Attacks.

- PARA CC Weapon (-3) means that, when the CC Weapon is being used, the user's targets apply a -3 MOD to any Face to Face Rolls.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Engaged states:
- Troopers in Engaged State are considered to be in Close Combat.

- Troopers in this state can only declare Berserk, CC Attack, Dodge, Idle, Reset, and Skills that specify that they can be used in Close Combat or in Engaged State.

- Troopers that are in Engaged State may not trace LoF outside their Close Combat.

- By declaring BS Attack on a Trooper in Engaged State, the BS Attack Into a Close Combat rule must be applied.

- Template Weapons placed on a group of Troopers in Engaged state will always affect all participating Troopers, even if the placement of the Template would only affect one of them.

- This state is canceled when the Trooper is no longer in Silhouette contact with any Enemy Troopers.

- A Trooper's Engaged State is automatically canceled if, in the 5.1 Effects step of any Order, all enemies in Silhouette contact are in an Immobilized or Null State (except for Sepsitorized or Possessed ).

- A Trooper may also cancel the Engaged State if they pass a Dodge Roll, either Normal or Face to Face. In this case, the Trooper must leave Silhouette contact, moving up to 2 inches to separate themselves from the enemy.

- If they cannot move to a valid position that is outside Silhouette contact, the Trooper will remain in Engaged State without moving.

Line of Fire

- Troopers who are in Engaged State cannot draw LoF to Troopers or game elements that are not in Engaged State with them. In addition, Troopers who are in Engaged State are not affected by Visibility Conditions or by other rules affecting LoF.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
CC Attacks are almost always resolved as Face to Face Rolls, because the target normally declares a CC Attack, Dodge, or other Skill that affects the outcome. However, CC Attacks can be Normal Rolls if the target cannot react (for example the target is in a State that prevents reactions).

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A common mistake is assuming Dodge always cancels CC Attacks entirely. Dodge only cancels the CC Attack against the Trooper who Dodges; it does not cancel CC Attacks against other targets if multiple engagements occur.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Engaged State is central to CC Attack interactions. Troopers in Engaged State:
- Cannot draw Line of Fire outside the engagement.
- If targeted by BS Attacks from outside the engagement, there's a good chance the attack will hit an ally, making it very risky.
- Are immune to many LoF- or visibility-based MODs.

This makes CC a powerful defensive state, not just an offensive one.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
CC MODs listed on Unit Profiles (such as CC Attack (-3)) only apply during Face to Face Rolls. They do nothing in Normal Rolls. This is often overlooked and can significantly change expected odds when attacking targets that cannot react.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Burst in CC is normally B1, even in the Active Turn, unless modified by a Special Skill, Weapon, or piece of Equipment. Players coming from BS-centric play often assume Active Turn Burst advantages exist in CC by default — they do not.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Multiple CC Weapons and modes can create confusion. The attacker must declare:
- Which CC Weapon is used
- Which ammo or mode is used (if applicable)

This declaration is locked in before Rolls. You cannot switch weapons after seeing MODs or ARO declarations.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Surprise Attack MODs apply in CC just like in BS or Hacking, as long as their conditions are met. This can dramatically swing CC engagements, especially against elite CC specialists who rely on high Attributes rather than Burst.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
CC Attacks interact strongly with movement skills:
- Move + CC Attack allows you to reposition into contact and strike in a single Order.
- A failed CC Attack does not break Engaged State unless a rule explicitly causes disengagement.

This means repeated CC Attacks can be made without re-entering contact.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Replacing Game Elements (such as transforming into a different Silhouette or replacing a Marker with a Model) can cause immediate Engaged State if base edges are aligned appropriately. This can result in an unexpected CC situation without movement.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Criticals in CC follow the standard Critical rules: they automatically succeed and impose additional Saving Rolls unless the weapon or ammo specifies otherwise. High CC values dramatically increase Critical threat, making CC a lethal equalizer even against heavily armored targets.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategic use: CC Attack is not only about killing. Entering Engaged State can:
- Lock down powerful long-range ARO pieces
- Protect your Trooper from enemy fire
- Force inefficient responses from the opponent

Sometimes the goal is control, not damage.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Tactical use: CC is strongest when combined with:
- Smoke or visibility denial to safely approach
- Marker States to enable Surprise Attack
- Coordinated pressure that forces bad ARO choices
- Mobility or Stealth to avoid LoF.

Using CC as part of a combined-arms plan is far more reliable than relying on raw CC stats alone.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## CC attack in Smoke, Eclipse or Zero Visibility zone
Smoke creates a Zero Visibility Zone, which blocks Line of Fire.

Close Combat does not use Line of Fire at all.

Once two Troopers are in Silhouette contact, they are in Engaged State.

Troopers in Engaged State:

Can freely declare CC Attack

Ignore Visibility Conditions, including Smoke

✅ Therefore: Smoke has no effect on Close Combat.
