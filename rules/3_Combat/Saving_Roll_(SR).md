# Saving Roll (SR)

Combat Module

The Saving Roll (SR) lets you know if the target's armor or protections save it from suffering damage from an attack.

Saving Rolls work like any other Roll in the Infinity game mechanics.

To calculate the Success Value of the Saving Roll, the player must take the target's ARM or BTS Attribute value, as appropriate, and add the following to it:

- The Weapon's Possibility of Survival (PS) value.

- The (+3) MOD due to Partial Cover, if applicable.

- Apply other MODs (+ or -) due to Special Skills or rules, if any.

Once the Success Value of the Saving Roll is determined, the player rolls a d20 for each hit and compares the result:

- Saving Roll passed: If the final result is equal to or lower than the Success Value of the Saving Roll, the impact does not cause damage, so the target does not receive any Wounds, and its state is not altered.

- Saving Roll failed: If the final result is higher than the Success Value of the Saving Roll, the impact causes damage. The most common consequence is that the target receives a Wound or is affected by a detrimental state.

Some Attacks, Special Ammo, or Weapons require a different type of Saving Roll. They have no PS value and instead list an Attribute with a MOD (such as PH -6), which requires a Normal Roll against the specified Attribute, applying the listed MOD.

## FAQs

Q: What happens if the target of a Protheion user fails more Saving Rolls than are needed for it to enter Dead State? For example, if an Unconscious target fails two or more Saving Rolls.

A: Any additional failed Saving Rolls will have no effect, so will not benefit the Protheion user.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Saving Rolls are resolved after a Trooper has been successfully hit and the Attack generates one or more Saving Rolls. This step is entirely separate from the Attack Roll itself. Always remember: hits are confirmed first, then Saving Rolls are made, then States and Wounds are applied.

A Saving Roll is always a Normal Roll. It is never Face to Face, cannot be opposed. Once you reach Saving Rolls, the attacker’s job is done; only the defender’s Attributes, MODs, and ammunition effects matter.

Cover does not increase ARM or BTS. It applies a +3 MOD directly to the Saving Roll. This is a very common legacy mistake from older editions. Because it is not an ARM bonus, AP ammunition does not reduce the Cover MOD. A Trooper in Cover always benefits from +3 to the Saving Roll unless the Attack explicitly ignores Cover SR Mods.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The Attribute used for the Saving Roll (ARM or BTS) is defined by the weapon, ammunition, or effect that caused the hit. If an effect causes multiple types of Saves, each one is resolved separately, even if they originate from the same hit.

Multiple Saving Rolls from a single Order are resolved one by one, but the order rarely matters because all results apply at the end of the Resolution step. However, when both positive and negative effects occur in the same Order, positive effects are applied first, then negative ones. This can matter for edge cases involving healing, state cancellation, or simultaneous effects.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Each failed Saving Roll inflicts its listed effect, usually a Wound or Structure loss, and may also impose a State (such as Unconscious or Dead). Passing one Saving Roll does not protect against other Saving Rolls generated by the same Attack.

Critical Hits add an extra Saving Roll with the same Attribute and Traits as the original hit, unless explicitly stated otherwise. This extra Save is not optional, not reduced, and not a separate hit for targeting purposes. Players sometimes forget this additional roll, especially when multiple dice are involved.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If a Trooper is required to make Saving Rolls while already in a Null State, the rolls are still made if the rules instruct it, but additional Wounds beyond Dead have no further effect. This mainly matters for rules interactions, campaign play, or effects that care about “failed Saves” rather than damage dealt.

Saving Rolls are never rerolled unless a Special Skill, Equipment, or scenario rule explicitly allows it. Rerolls from Skills that affect Attacks do not apply here unless they specifically mention Saving Rolls.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
From a tactical perspective, forcing multiple low-quality Saving Rolls is often more effective than forcing a single high-quality one. Weapons or tactics that generate multiple Saves exploit statistical variance and can overwhelm high ARM or BTS values, especially when the target relies heavily on Cover.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategically, Saving Rolls are where Infinity’s lethality truly manifests. Winning Face to Face Rolls is only half the battle; choosing ammunition, angles, and targets that reduce the enemy’s effective Saving Rolls is what converts Orders into permanent advantage on the table.
