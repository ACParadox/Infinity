# Possibility of Survival (PS)

Combat Module

In Infinity, the Possibility of Survival (PS) is the capacity of a weapon, Attack, Special Skill, piece of Equipment, Hacking Program, etc. to harm or impair its target. The PS value, represented by a number, is indicated on the weapon’s profile or in the rules for the specific Special Skill, Equipment, etc.

However, this PS value is not usually applied to the target as is. Targets often have the right to a Saving Roll that determines if their Armor ( ARM ) or Biotechnological Shield ( BTS ) protects them from this damage.

Generally, each uncancelled success in an Attack Roll forces the target to perform one Saving Roll.

The descriptive text of the type of Attack, Ammunition, Special Skill, Equipment, Hacking Program, etc., used in an Attack indicates whether the target must use their ARM, BTS, or any other Attribute (or combination of them) when performing a Saving Roll.

The PS value indicates the lethality of a weapon, based on the probability of the target surviving the hit. The lower the PS value, the more lethal the weapon.

Saving Roll SR Success Value:
Success Value = ARM/BTS + PS + Cover (+3 if Partial Cover) + other MODs


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
PS is not “damage” in the traditional sense. It is a modifier that directly influences the Saving Roll target number.
Lower PS = deadlier attack.
Higher PS = more survivable hit.
This inversion is frequently misunderstood by new and even veteran players, especially when comparing weapons across ammo types.

PS never causes effects by itself. It only matters when a Saving Roll is required.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
PS Is Added, Not Compared
Correct process:
ARM or BTS + PS + Cover (+3) + other MODs = Saving Roll Success Value.
You roll under that number to survive.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
AP Ammunition Does Not Reduce Cover
AP modifies ARM/BTS, not PS and not Cover.
Players often assume AP “helps against everything defensive,” but Cover always applies unless explicitly bypassed.
This makes PS and AP orthogonal tools:
AP weakens stats.
PS defines baseline lethality.
Cover always stacks unless denied.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Multiple Saves Multiply PS Impact
Weapons or ammo that force multiple Saving Rolls (DA, EXP, T2, etc.) do not increase PS, but they compound its effect.
Even moderate PS values become extremely lethal when multiple saves are required.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
PS and Non-Lethal Effects
Many Hacking Programs and Non-Lethal Attacks still use PS.
Failing a Saving Roll may cause States instead of Wounds, but PS still governs how hard it is to resist.
This is why “low PS but non-lethal” effects can still be devastating to elite troops.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
PS vs Burst — A Subtle Tradeoff
High Burst weapons often have higher PS (less lethal per hit).
Low Burst weapons often have lower PS (more lethal per hit).
This creates a meaningful decision:
Burst is about winning Face to Face Rolls.
PS is about converting hits into consequences.
Winning the roll means nothing if the target shrugs off the saves.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Close Combat and PS Misconceptions
In CC, players often over-focus on CC Attribute and ignore PS.
However:
A successful CC hit with poor PS may fail to do anything.
Special CC weapons with altered Save Attributes (ARM=0, BTS, etc.) are effectively PS amplifiers.
This is why Monofilament and similar traits feel disproportionately deadly.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
PS and High-ARM Targets (HI, TAGs)
Against high-ARM units:
Small PS differences matter less than ammo traits and save count.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
PS and Mission Design Implications
Objective-heavy missions reward consistency over spike lethality.
Moderate PS + reliable Burst is often better than ultra-low PS glass cannons.
Conversely, assassination missions reward stacking low PS with multiple saves.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Tactical Uses and Exploits
Use PS-efficient weapons to punish exposed or out-of-cover enemies.
Force enemies into Coverless positions before committing low-PS attacks.
When trading AROs, remember: surviving the hit is as important as winning the roll.
