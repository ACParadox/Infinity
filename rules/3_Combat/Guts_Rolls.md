# Guts Roll

Combat Module

When Troopers survive a successful Attack, it means their body armor took the brunt of the impact, keeping them from serious injury. This triggers a primal fear of death, and survival instincts kick in. The Trooper's survival instincts can force them to recoil from danger and dive for cover.

- The Trooper is not in an Engaged, Null or IMM State.

- The Trooper had suffered one or more successful Attacks that cause the Trooper to enter any States or to perform any Saving Rolls.

- The Guts Roll is performed even if all the attackers are in a Null State at the end of the Order.

- A Guts Roll consists in a single Normal WIP Roll, always performed at the very end of the Order, after performing any Saving Rolls.

- If the Guts Roll is successful, the Trooper must stand its ground and must do nothing else.

- If a Trooper would have to make a Guts Roll, its owner can instead choose to fail it automatically.

- If the WIP Roll is failed, it is compulsory to perform the first of these options that the Trooper can complete: The Trooper must move up to 2 inches to completely leave: The LoF of the Enemy or Enemies (for example, in the case of a Coordinated Order, or multiple AROs ) that have performed Attacks against the Trooper. The danger zone or Area of Effect of Attacks that did not require LoF. If this is not possible, the Trooper must move up to 2 inches to gain Total or Partial Cover from all attackers. If the two previous options are not possible, the Trooper must go Prone, facing in any direction its player chooses

- Check the enemy LoF or Area of Effect from the Trooper's final position at the end of the Order, when the Guts Roll is made.

- When checking the enemy's LoF or Area of Effect, if the Trooper is already outside them and the Guts Roll is failed, the Trooper enters Prone State, facing in a direction of the player's choice.

- This movement of a failed Guts Roll: Does not generate AROs or activate Deployable Weapons or Equipment. Must follow the General Movement Rules as well as the Moving and Measuring rules. Does not allow the Trooper to enter Silhouette contact with enemy Troopers, or with Scenario Objectives.

- If this is not possible, the Trooper must move up to 2 inches to gain Total or Partial Cover from all attackers.

- The danger zone or Area of Effect of Attacks that did not require LoF.

- Must follow the General Movement Rules as well as the Moving and Measuring rules.

Attacks that have no PS value and do not cause any State do not require a Guts Roll.

Troopers in Engaged State do not make Guts Rolls.

## Guts Roll - Stun Ammunition

- Additionally, failing the Saving Roll causes the target to automatically fail the subsequent Guts Roll for having survived an Attack, unless he has the Courage Special Skill or an equivalent.

## Guts Roll - Climbing Plus

- Climbing Plus also allows the user to move on vertical surfaces as if performing normal Movement on a horizontal surface when declaring Move or Dodge, or during movement due to a failed Guts Roll. The General Movement Rules and Moving and Measuring chapter must be applied.

## Guts Roll - Courage

- Courage allows its user to choose to automatically pass any Guts Rolls.

## Guts Roll - Religious Troop

- The user automatically passes all their Guts Rolls without requiring a Roll. This means that the user is forced to hold their position and cannot withdraw or seek cover after surviving an Attack.

- However, if the player wants the user to withdraw or gain Cover after surviving an Attack, then they may make a WIP Roll. If the Roll is a success, the user can apply the Effects of failing the Guts Roll.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## CORE CONCEPT – WHAT GUTS REALLY REPRESENTS

Guts Rolls are not a “morale check” in the traditional sense.
They are a forced positional consequence for surviving danger.
The rule exists primarily to:
- Break static gunfights
- Introduce involuntary movement after Resolution

A Guts Roll happens AFTER all rolls, damage, and states are resolved,
and even if the attacker is no longer alive at that point.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## FREQUENTLY MISSED TRIGGERS

1. You only roll Guts if:
   - You were hit successfully AND
   - You had to make a Saving Roll OR entered a State

This means:
- Missed attacks never trigger Guts
- Purely “harmless” or beneficial effects do nothing

2. Guts is rolled even if:
   - The attacker is Unconscious or Dead at Resolution

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## AUTO-FAIL IS A FEATURE, NOT A PENALTY

Choosing to automatically fail a Guts Roll is often advantageous.

Tactical uses of intentional failure:
- Step out of Line of Fire without spending an Order
- Drop Prone to deny further AROs
- Break enemy firelanes safely
- Reposition into Partial or Total Cover
'
This is NOT considered voluntary movement and:
- Generates no AROs
- Does not trigger Mines, Perimeter Weapons, or Deployables


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## ORDER OF FORCED MOVEMENT (MANDATORY PRIORITY)

When failing Guts, the Trooper MUST attempt the following IN ORDER:

1. Leave all enemy LoF or Area of Effect using 2 inch movement, note that going prone to leave LoF can satisfy this as well.
2. If impossible, gain Partial or Total Cover from ALL attackers
3. If still impossible, go Prone

You cannot choose between these freely.

Important nuance:
- If you are already outside LoF/AoE when Guts is checked,
  failing Guts forces you Prone immediately.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## GUTS DOES NOT:

- Generate AROs
- Activate Deployable Weapons or Equipment
- Allow Silhouette contact with enemies or Objectives

This makes Guts movement one of the safest repositioning tools in the game.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## INTERACTION: ENGAGED STATE

Troopers in Engaged State NEVER make Guts Rolls.

This prevents:
- Forced disengagement from Close Combat
- Accidental breaking of Silhouette contact

Common mistake:
Being shot or templated while Engaged does NOT cause fallback.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## INTERACTION: COURAGE, RELIGIOUS, AND SIMILAR SKILLS

Courage:
- Allows automatic success on Guts Rolls
- Still allows the player to choose to fail if desired


Religious Troop:
- Removes the choice entirely
- The Trooper never retreats due to Guts

Practical difference:
- Courage = flexibility
- Religious = predictability (good or bad depending on position)


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## INTERACTION: STUN AMMUNITION

Failing a Stun Save automatically fails the subsequent Guts Roll
unless the Trooper has Courage or equivalent.

Tactical implication:
- Stun is a displacement weapon, not just a shutdown tool
- Excellent for forcing ARO pieces out of position


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## INTERACTION: CLIMBING PLUS

Troopers with Climbing Plus may:
- Perform Guts movement on vertical surfaces
- Treat walls as horizontal ground during fallback

This enables:
- Forced drops behind cover
- Vertical disengagement without Orders

Non–Climbing Plus Troopers cannot do this.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## FIRETEAMS AND COHERENCY

Guts movement happens AFTER Resolution and BEFORE Coherency check.

Consequences:
- Members can involuntarily break Fireteam coherency
- Foxhole / Suppressive Fire States may be canceled

Fireteams are especially vulnerable to forced Guts displacement.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## STRATEGIC & TACTICAL USE CASES

### DEFENSIVE PLAY:
- Place ARO pieces so failing Guts moves them into better cover
- Use corners and ladders to create “safe fallback vectors”

### OFFENSIVE PLAY:
- Use Stun, low-damage hits, or speculative fire to trigger Guts
- Force defenders to abandon rooftops or suppressive fire lanes

### POSITIONAL WARFARE:
- Guts is one of the only movement effects that ignores AROs
- Smart terrain usage turns enemy success into your repositioning

### FINAL THOUGHT:
Guts Rolls are not a punishment for being hit —
they are a positional mini-game that rewards foresight.
