# Oblivion

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)
 SHORT SKILL / ARO
Comms Attack, Non-Lethal. 

- The target must be an enemy with the Hackable Characteristic ( HI, REM, TAG, VH...) or an enemy Hacker.

- In the Active Turn, this Program's Burst of 2 allows the user to perform two WIP Rolls, which can be distributed among one or more targets as usual.

- Each successful Roll, due to AP Ammunition, forces the target to make a Saving Roll against their halved BTS, with PS 4.

- Any failed Saving Roll causes the target to enter Isolated State, placing an Isolated State Token (ISOLATED) by them.

- A Critical with Oblivion forces its target to perform an additional Saving Roll, applying their halved BTS value.

- The range of this Program is the Hacker's Hacking Area.

| Name | Attack MOD | Opp. MOD | PS | Burst | Target | Skill Type | Special |

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Oblivion | 0 | 0 | 4 | 2 | TAG, HI, REM, VH, Hacker | Short Skill / ARO | AP Ammo, Non-Lethal, State: Isolated. |



**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A single failed Saving Roll against Oblivion is enough to apply Isolated. Multiple successful WIP rolls do not “stack” the state, but they do increase the odds of forcing at least one failed Save. Burst 2 is about reliability, not escalation.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Oblivion and Order Economy
Isolated is primarily an Order-denial effect and debuff.
A Trooper that becomes Isolated:
- Immediately stops receiving Orders from the Order Pool.
- If still Isolated at the start of their next Active Turn, becomes Irregular and contributes no Regular Order.
- Cannot convert or share Orders in any way.
- Has -9 to all Reset rolls

This makes Oblivion especially strong against elite, low-model-count armies where each Regular Order is critical.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Oblivion vs Fireteams
- A Trooper entering Isolated State immediately leaves their Fireteam.
- If the Isolated Trooper was the Team Leader, the Fireteam breaks entirely.

This can collapse Fireteam positioning, remove Burst bonuses mid-turn, and force unexpected Coherency failures on following Orders.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Oblivion and Lieutenants
Oblivion is a direct threat to command structure.
- If the Lieutenant becomes Isolated and the state is not cancelled before their next Active Turn, the army enters Loss of Lieutenant.
- Cancelling Isolated later does not undo the Loss of Lieutenant state already triggered.

This makes Oblivion a powerful indirect assassination tool, especially against obvious or exposed Lieutenants.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Oblivion and Reset – Why It’s So Oppressive
Isolated imposes a severe WIP penalty to Reset.
- Reset remains the primary self-help option, but the large negative MOD makes success unreliable.
- This often forces the opponent to spend Orders repositioning an Engineer instead.

The real tax of Oblivion is not the state itself, but the Orders required to clear it.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Interaction with Engineers
Engineers are the most reliable counter to Oblivion.
- Engineer cancellation does not suffer the WIP penalty imposed by Isolated.
- However, it requires Silhouette contact, often costing multiple Orders and exposing the Engineer.

This creates a secondary tactical objective: isolating a key piece to draw Engineers out of position.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Oblivion vs Carbonite – Common Confusion
Oblivion and Carbonite do very different jobs:
- Oblivion attacks Orders, Fireteams, and command.
- Carbonite attacks movement and declarations.

A Trooper can be both Isolated and Immobilized, but cancelling one does not cancel the other. Using Oblivion first often makes Carbonite follow-ups safer and more punishing (and almost impossible to remove without an Engineer, at -12 MOD Reset).

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Oblivion in Active turn
Using Oblivion actively can disable a future attack run by a main active gunfighter

## Oblivion in ARO
Using Oblivion reactively is even better than active turn.
- Even a single ARO success can end an attack run by disallowing receiving Orders and removing Fireteam bonuses.
- It can leave the target vulnerable by being in a dangerous location, if it was planning to move to cover or out of LoF in a subsequent order.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Firewalls, TinBots, and Why Oblivion Sometimes “Fails”
Firewalls significantly reduce Oblivion’s effectiveness.
- They penalize the attacker’s WIP.
- They improve the target’s BTS for Saving Rolls.

This does not make Oblivion bad, but it shifts its role from reliable control to pressure and attrition. Against Firewalled targets, Oblivion is often about forcing suboptimal decisions rather than expecting immediate results.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Oblivion vs E/M
Oblivion and E/M produce similar outcomes but through different vectors.
- Oblivion requires Hackable targets and hacking access.
- E/M often has shorter range or riskier delivery but can affect a broader target set.

Use Oblivion when you want projection and safety.
Use E/M when you want certainty at close range.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Strategic Uses
- Isolate high-cost attack pieces to strand them with no Order support.
- Break Fireteams before engaging them with gunfire.
- Pressure Lieutenants without needing to expose assassins.
- Force Engineers into dangerous positions.W
- Control midfield movement through Repeater networks and ARO threats.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Final Takeaway
Oblivion is not a damage tool. It is a control weapon.
Its value is measured in Orders lost, Fireteams broken, and attack plans abandoned. Players who evaluate Oblivion only by immediate effects will underestimate it; players who plan around its long-term impact will find it one of the most efficient tools in Infinity N5.
