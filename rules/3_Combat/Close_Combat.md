## Close Combat (CC)

Urban combat, attempting to take a building, or ship to ship space-boarding often occur in reduced spaces, which favors close combat with bladed weapons (knives, machetes, sabers, swords, claws, etc.) which we generically call Melee Weapons.

## Interacting with a Close Combat

Any Trooper in Silhouette contact with an Allied Model that is in the Engaged State may only declare CC Attack, Dodge or other related Skills that may be used in CC. For example, in these circumstances declaring Skills such as Doctor or Engineer on a Trooper in Engaged State is not allowed.

## Movement Sequence and CC

Close Combat is often associated with a Movement, the most common game sequence in this case is the following:

Step 1: The Active Player (Trooper A) consumes an Order and declares Move.

Step 2: The Reactive Player (Trooper B) declares CC Attack as their ARO.

Step 3: The Active Player (Trooper A) declares CC Attack as the second Short Skill of the Order.

Step 4: The corresponding Face to Face Roll is performed. Face to Face Roll:

- Active Player: 1 Die in CC.

- Reactive Player: 1 Die in CC.

## CC Attack Modifiers (MOD)

Before performing the CC Attack Roll (or Rolls), the player must check every MOD they must apply. The MODs are:

- Close Combat against several enemies.

- Special Skills, Weapons and Equipment.

### Close Combat With Multiple Troopers

This rule is applied when more than 2 Troopers are in Silhouette contact with the target and any of them is activated.

When declaring a CC Attack, the Player must select one Trooper or Peripheral activated by the Order. That Trooper or Peripheral performs the CC Attack and has a +1 MOD to Burst (B) for each Allied Trooper or Peripheral that is not in a Null or Immobilized State and is in Silhouette contact with their target.

When declaring AROs, if the Reactive player decides to declare CC Attack they must select only one Trooper or Peripheral in Silhouette contact with the target. The selected Trooper performs a CC Attack and has a +1 MOD to B for each Allied Trooper or Peripheral in Silhouette contact with the target. The MOD is only applied for those allies that are not in a Null or Immobilized State, and have not declared Dodge, Idle or Reset.

## Close Combat - Other CC Restrictions

- When in Close Combat, Troopers can only declare: Berserk CC Attack Dodge Idle Reset Those Skills that specify that they can be used in Close Combat or in Engaged State.

- The maximum number of Models that may be in Silhouette contact with a Model on a 25 mm base is 4.

- The maximum number of Models that may be in Silhouette contact with a Model on a 40 mm or larger base is 6.

## Close Combat (CC) - Special Skills and Equipment

Certain Special Skills, Weapons, and Equipment impose MODs to CC Attack Rolls, as seen in the rulebook (see Special Skills, Weaponry, and Equipment, although it is recommended that these concepts be applied as they appear in the game).

- Success Values Above 20

- Engaged State

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Entering CC automatically places both Troopers in Engaged State. This is critical: while Engaged, Troopers cannot draw Line of Fire to anything outside the combat, and are riskier to attack from the outside (risk of hitting an ally). New players often miss that simply *being* in CC is already a strong defensive state, even before rolling dice.

CC Attacks are Face to Face rolls if the target declares CC Attack, Dodge, or another Skill that directly affects the attacker. If the target does not meaningfully oppose the CC Attack, the roll becomes Normal. This means catching enemies unaware, isolated, or unable to declare a proper ARO can drastically swing CC outcomes.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Berserk is used to extend the range of movement to get into silhouette contact, instead of a Move + CC Attack.

Multiple enemies in base contact do not form a “group CC.” Each CC Attack is resolved against a single target. However, positioning matters: engaging one enemy can prevent nearby enemies from shooting you, forcing them into inefficient Dodges or CC declarations. This makes CC specialists excellent at dismantling clustered fireteams or defensive nests.

Weapons and Ammunition in CC are easy to overlook. Traits like Shock, DA, AP, or special effects can matter more than raw CC value. High CC without lethal ammo can stall an enemy; lethal ammo without sufficient CC can fail to land hits. Always evaluate both together.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Surprise Attack and Marker States strongly favor CC play. Decloaking directly into base contact often denies effective AROs and stacks MODs heavily in your favor. This is one of the safest ways to deliver CC specialists and explains why many elite CC units also have Marker deployment or mobility tricks.

Engaging TAGs and Heavy Infantry in CC is usually about neutralization, not immediate destruction. Even if you cannot reliably wound them, keeping them Engaged denies their main weapons and often costs the opponent multiple Orders to escape, especially if they lack strong CC or PH.

Dodging in CC is frequently underestimated. A successful Dodge can break Engaged State and reposition, sometimes more valuable than attempting a low-probability CC Attack. Reactive players should consider Dodge not as “giving up,” but as a tactical reset tool.

Once out of CC, the enemy needs to move again to try a CC attack, meaning the Trooper can now use a BS attack ARO, which might be stronger.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Verticality and movement skills interact subtly with CC. Climbing, Jumping, or dropping into base contact can bypass expected fire lanes. Measuring movement carefully to end in Silhouette contact is essential; a few millimeters can be the difference between safety and death.

Strategically, CC specialists are best used as scalpels, not hammers. They excel at removing key ARO pieces, pinning Lieutenants, and breaking defensive formations. Throwing them headlong into open fire without setup wastes their true strength.

Finally, CC creates psychological pressure. Opponents often overcommit Orders to avoid or escape CC threats, even when statistically unnecessary. Leveraging this fear—by threatening CC rather than always executing it—is an advanced but highly effective tactical approach.
