# Carbonite

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)
 SHORT SKILL / ARO
Comms Attack, Non-Lethal. 

- The target may only be an enemy Trooper with the Hackable Characteristic ( HI, REM, TAG, VH...) or an enemy Hacker.

- In the Active Turn, this Program's Burst of 2 allows the user to perform two WIP Rolls, which can be distributed among one or more targets as usual.

- Each successful Roll, due to DA Ammunition, forces the target to make two Saving Rolls against BTS, with PS 7.

- Any failed Saving Roll causes the target to enter Immobilized-B State, placing an Immobilized-B State Token (IMM-B) by them.

- A Critical with Carbonite forces its target to perform an additional Saving Roll.

- The range of this Program is the Hacker's Hacking Area.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** 
Carbonite is a Short Skill / ARO Hacking Program that inflicts Immobilized-B (IMM-B) through DA Ammunition on Hackable targets. Its role is board control and tempo denial rather than direct removal (although IMM-B can be hard to remov, especially combined with Isolated state). While its rules text is straightforward, its interactions are frequently misunderstood or underutilized.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Active Turn Burst and Target Splitting**

Carbonite has Burst 2 in the Active Turn, allowing two separate WIP Rolls that may be split between targets.

Commonly missed points:
- You may declare Carbonite against two different valid targets in the same Order.
- Each successful Roll forces two BTS Saving Rolls (DA Ammunition).
- A single failed Saving Roll is sufficient to apply IMM-B.

This makes Carbonite an efficient tool to “fish” for Immobilization across multiple HI/REM/TAG targets rather than overcommitting against a single one.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Face to Face vs Reset — Why Carbonite Is So Strong in ARO**

Carbonite is frequently declared in ARO against:
- Movement inside Hacking Area
- Attacks declared without LoF to the Hacker

Key interaction:
- Reset is the *only* meaningful defensive response most Hackable units can declare.
- Reset is Face to Face, but offers no damage or positional threat to the Hacker.
- If the Hacker wins the FtF Roll, the target must immediately make Saving Rolls and risks IMM-B.

This creates a lose-lose situation:
- Declare Reset → risk Immobilization
- Declare anything else → Normal Roll Carbonite


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Immobilized-B Is More Punishing Than It Looks**

IMM-B is one of the most restrictive non-Null States in N5.

Important consequences:
- The Trooper cannot declare any Skill or ARO except Reset (at –3 WIP).
- Dodge is not allowed.
- The Trooper still generates Orders, but cannot meaningfully use them.

This makes an immobilized enemy extremely vulnerable, as it cannot dodge, nor counter-attack.
The fact that it cannot counter-attack also makes it worthwhile for weaker troopers to attack it.
---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Carbonite vs Engineers — Order Drain by Design**

IMM-B can be cleared by:
- Reset at –3 WIP (often low odds, especially under Firewalls or Surprise MODs)
- Engineer in base contact with a successful WIP Roll

Strategic implication:
- Carbonite converts a single Order into multiple forced Orders:
  - Orders spent Resetting
  - Orders spent repositioning an Engineer
  - Orders spent protecting that Engineer

Even if IMM-B is cleared, Carbonite has already achieved value by taxing the opponent’s Order economy.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Carbonite Does Not Remove Orders or Fireteam Status**

Unlike Isolated:
- IMM-B does NOT remove the Trooper’s Regular Order.
- IMM-B does NOT, by itself, break Fireteams.

However:
- A Fireteam member in IMM-B cannot Move, Dodge, or meaningfully contribute.
- This often functionally “pins” a link team in place until repaired.

Carbonite is therefore best used to freeze positioning, not to collapse Order structure.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Synergy with Other Hacking Programs**

Carbonite shines brightest when layered with other infowar effects:
- Use Spotlight to gain Targeted bonuses on later Carbonite attempts
- Use Oblivion to apply Isolated, making Reset much harder
- Repeaters to extend threat without exposing the Hacker

A common pattern:
- Oblivion to cripple Orders and Reset
- Carbonite to fully lock the unit in place


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Tactical Use Cases**

Carbonite excels at:
- Area denial against TAGs and Heavy Infantry
- Locking midfield guards in place to open movement lanes
- Punishing aggressive advances through Repeater networks
- Forcing suboptimal Resets during the opponent’s Active Turn

Less effective when:
- Facing heavy Firewall saturation
- The opponent has multiple Engineers positioned nearby
- Used as a lone effect without network support

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Mental Model for Carbonite**

Think of Carbonite as:
- A cyber “bear trap”
- A way to dictate where the enemy *cannot* go next Order

Used patiently and layered with Repeaters, Carbonite turns the table into hostile terrain for Hackable units rather than merely a source of attrition.
