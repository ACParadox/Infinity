# Direct Template Weapons

Combat Module

As their name implies, these are the ones where the Template is placed directly touching the edge of the attacking Trooper's Silhouette, or centred on the attacker for the Circular Template.

- They do not require a BS Roll to hit.

- When declaring the Attack, the Template must be placed on the table to check that the Main Target is within the Area of Effect, and to determine which Troopers or game elements (Markers, Deployable weapons or Equipment...) are affected by the Attack.

- The placement of the Direct Template depends on their type: Direct Teardrop Template: The narrow end of the Teardrop Template (Blast Focus) must be placed in contact with the edge of the Silhouette of the Trooper declaring the Attack. Every Trooper affected by the Template will suffer the Attack. The Template cannot be placed so that it would go through the Silhouette of the Trooper that is declaring the Attack (see example). (See FAQs.) Direct Circular Template: The center of the Circular Template (Blast Focus) must be placed over the center of the base of the Trooper declaring the Attack, and every Trooper affected by the Template will suffer the Attack.

- In the following cases the Dodge requires a PH -3 Roll to avoid an Attack with a Template Weapon: If the Trooper affected by the Template Weapon Attack has no LoF to the attacker. If the Template Weapon Attack is from a Deployable Weapon (a Mine, for example).

- Dodging a Direct Template Weapon requires a Normal PH Roll (or PH-3 as mentioned previously).

- If a Trooper affected by a Direct Template Weapon declares an Attack against the user of the Direct Template Weapon, their Attack will be a Normal Roll (if the Weapon, Special Skill, etc., requires a Roll). In the Resolution step of the Order, the Trooper will need to make a Saving Roll, since this type of Template Weapon affects targets directly, without needing to Roll to hit.

- In ARO, any Active Trooper in the Area of Effect of the Template during the Order counts as a Main Target when checking whether the Template is cancelled.

- Those Direct Template Weapons that have a Burst (B) value higher than 1, allow the Template to be placed as many times per Order as their Burst (B) value indicates, and to distribute this B among different targets if so wished.

- Direct Teardrop Template: The narrow end of the Teardrop Template (Blast Focus) must be placed in contact with the edge of the Silhouette of the Trooper declaring the Attack. Every Trooper affected by the Template will suffer the Attack. The Template cannot be placed so that it would go through the Silhouette of the Trooper that is declaring the Attack (see example). (See FAQs.)

- Direct Circular Template: The center of the Circular Template (Blast Focus) must be placed over the center of the base of the Trooper declaring the Attack, and every Trooper affected by the Template will suffer the Attack.

To Dodge an Attack with several Direct Templates in the same Order, a single Normal PH (or PH-3) Roll must be passed, allowing the Trooper to Dodge all Templates with one Roll.

Despite being in contact with the Template, Troopers declaring an Attack with a Direct Template will not be affected by it, unless otherwise specified in the Weapon’s, Skill’s or Type of Ammunition’s description.

# Direct Template Weapons — Interactions Notes and Unofficial Commentary

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Direct Template Weapons (DTWs) are one of the most misunderstood weapon categories in Infinity N5 because they deliberately bypass several “normal” shooting assumptions: they do not roll to hit, they do not use Burst in the conventional way, and they interact with AROs, Dodge, and positioning in unique ways. Their real strength is not raw lethality, but control of space, order efficiency, and forcing unfavorable choices.

A very common mistake is treating DTWs as “automatic hits that ignore everything.” They do not ignore everything. They ignore BS Rolls, but not Saving Rolls, Cover bonuses to Saving Rolls, or states and traits that affect being targeted (such as Total Immunity, specific ammo interactions). 
Understanding what DTWs skip and what they still respect is critical.

DTWs generate AROs in a special way. A trooper affected by a template is always eligible to declare an ARO, even without Line of Fire. This is extremely important: placing a template around a corner or through smoke does not “surprise” the target into helplessness. They still get to Dodge, Reset (if applicable), or declare a valid Attack if requirements are met.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
DTWs never creates  Face to Face Rolls since it's not a roll! This is one of their defining traits. The attacker does not roll, and the defender’s Dodge Roll does not cancel the attack in the usual sense, only avoids it. 
A successful Dodge avoids the template’s effects on that trooper only; it does not negate the attack itself or protect other troopers under the same template.

But, if the enemy ARO is a BS Attack, the enemy attack is also a normal roll, which means can mean lead to mutual destruction.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
When multiple troopers are under a single Direct Template, each trooper resolves their own Saving Roll or Dodge independently. There is no “shared outcome.” This makes DTWs extremely effective against tightly packed Fireteams, Coordinated Orders, or troops clustering near objectives.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cover does not prevent being hit by a Direct Template Weapon, but it still provides its Saving Roll bonus if applicable. This is often missed. DTWs punish cover-dependent units less than people expect; they punish positioning and proximity much more than defensive modifiers.

---
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Engaged State interactions are particularly dangerous. A DTW hitting any model in CC affects all models in CC, even allies. Which makes it also one of the easiest ways to accidentally torch your own models if you are careless.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
DTWs are one of the strongest deterrents to ARO pieces holding corners. Because the attacker does not need to roll, even heavily modified reactive troopers cannot “win” the exchange—at best, they Dodge and reposition. This makes DTWs ideal for clearing short-range roadblocks without risking high-value attackers.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
DTWs synergize extremely well with smoke, visibility zones, and corner movement. Since they do not require Line of Fire in the traditional sense, a trooper can move into template range through smoke and still threaten enemies who cannot meaningfully oppose them with Face to Face Rolls.


One of the most overlooked tactical uses of DTWs is order trading. Even if the DTW user is almost guaranteed to die afterward, forcing multiple enemy Saving Rolls with a single Order is often an excellent exchange, especially against elite or order-generating troops. 

Even if the DTW trooper is weak, it allows it to trade up by wounding high power targets (but dying while doing it), which they would never be able to do in a Face-to-Face roll.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
DTWs strongly influence table psychology. Players tend to spread out more, avoid stacking behind corners, and hesitate to push Fireteams aggressively when DTWs are present. This indirect control effect is often more valuable than the actual damage caused.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Finally, DTWs reward aggressive, disposable units and punish static defense. Warbands, cheap assault troops, and close-range specialists extract maximum value from DTWs, while expensive gunfighters gain comparatively less. 
If you are paying a premium for high BS and modifiers, DTWs are not your primary tool—but they are often the tool that enables those gunfighters to operate safely.
