# Aerial

Special Skills
 AUTOMATIC SKILL
Obligatory 

Troopers possessing this Special Skill have flight capabilities. While in combat, Aerial Troopers stay as close to the ground as possible to avoid being an easy target.

- Restriction: While this Skill is active, the Trooper cannot be in Prone State or Engaged State.

- Restriction: While this Skill is Active, the Trooper is never in Silhouette contact with other Models, Objectives, scenery items, or game elements, and vice versa, even if physically touching them.

- Restriction: Troopers with this Special Skill cannot declare Cautious Movement.

Unconscious State Automatic Special Skills and Automatic Equipment have no effect while in Unconscious State.

Aerial and Guard:

The Guard Special Skill may not be used against Troopers with the Aerial Skill, unless they are in Unconscious State.

The aerial movement of Troopers with this Skill is represented using the Super-Jump and Terrain (Total) Special Skills, which are listed in their Unit Profiles. Similarly, the fact that these Troopers are harder to hit due to their higher speed and the height they can fly at is represented by Mimetism, or other Special Skills.

In addition, it is impossible to enter Silhouette contact with these Troopers unless they are in Unconscious State, so if they are not Unconscious any Wounds they have received can only be removed by using a GizmoKit.

## FAQs

Q: Do Troopers with Aerial Special Skill activate Weapons with the Boost Trait?

A: No, as it is not possible to enter Silhouette contact with Aerial Troopers.

### Interaction Note
Related rules: ARO, Face-to-Face, Partial Cover.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

**Subject: Aerial**

This term does not exist as an official Infinity N5 rule, skill, or state.  
The following commentary treats **“Aerial”** as a *tactical category of attacks from elevated positions or descending vectors* (e.g., Super-Jump firing angles, Combat Jump arrival zones, rooftop fire lanes).

---
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### How “Aerial” Approaches Influence Rules Interactions

#### 1. ARO Interactions
- Elevated or descending lines often produce **unexpected LoF**, granting AROs earlier or from angles the opponent didn’t anticipate.
- Aerial vectors commonly **deny Partial Cover** if the shooter can see the target’s base through an angle that avoids touching scenery.
- ARO pieces on roofs may gain **extended reach**, but lose protection if an aerial attacker approaches from behind or from a blind angle.

#### 2. Face-to-Face Interactions
- When shots come from above, defenders may have **restricted LoF arcs**, making it easier for the aerial attacker to force uncontested Normal Rolls.
- Conversely, committing to an aerial angle may expose the attacker to **multiple AROs from different elevations**, leading to multi-way Face-to-Face engagements.
- Troopers attempting to Dodge aerial attacks may suffer from **poor placement options** (e.g., Dodge movement cannot move them off a rooftop or out of vertical danger zones effectively).

#### 3. Partial Cover Interactions
- Many aerial lines naturally bypass cover:
  - If the scenery piece is shorter than the attacker’s firing vector, the target often **loses cover entirely**.
  - Even when cover exists, unusual angles may create **ambiguous situations**, requiring players to carefully check LoF using silhouettes.
- However, aerial shooters may themselves be more exposed:
  - Elevated positions often **lack cover** unless specifically designed.
  - Dropping down with Super-Jump may **forfeit cover** for the duration of the Order.

---
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Pros & Cons of Aerial Attack Styles (UNOFFICIAL)

#### **Pros**
- **Bypass Cover:** Greatest advantage—vertical angles frequently eliminate Partial Cover bonuses.
- **Surprise Lines:** Can attack from unexpected directions, forcing suboptimal ARO choices.
- **Zone Control Breach:** Can leap over mines, repeaters, and zones that normally protect ground-level approaches.
- **High Ground Dominance:** Improved vision over cluttered boards.

#### **Cons**
- **Exposure:** Elevated pieces often have fewer defensive options.
- **Multiple ARO Risks:** Being “in the open” in mid-air or on rooftops often creates many simultaneous threats.
- **Dodge Limitations:** Aerial routes limit defenders’ movement, but **also** limit the attacker’s escape after losing a Face-to-Face.
- **Order-Intensive:** Gaining aerial positions frequently costs extra movement or Super-Jump orders.

------

### Tactical Commentary (UNOFFICIAL)
- Use aerial vectors to pick off entrenched targets, especially those relying on cover or watching predictable lanes.
- Avoid lingering: aerial aggression is strongest during the **first exposure**, weakest during the **counterattack**.
- Combine aerial strikes with **Smoke**, **White Noise**, or **coordinated distractions** to survive the ARO web.
- Beware enemy **360º Visors** or **Sixth Sense** units, which can negate directional advantages.
