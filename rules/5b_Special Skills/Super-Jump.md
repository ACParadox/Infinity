# Super-Jump

Special Skills
UTOMATIC SKILL
Movement, Optional. 

Troopers with Super-Jump have the ability to perform super-human jumping feats thanks to special gear, augmentations, or evolutionary advantages.

- With this Special Skill, the Jump Long Skill is turned into a Basic Short Skill, if declared as the first Basic Short Skill of the Order.

- The Super-Jump Special Skill allows its user to perform other Basic Short Skills or Short Skills while performing a Jump (for example: Jump + BS Attack ) (see Trooper Activation ).

- Restriction: The Trooper can only declare Jump as a Basic Short Skill once per Order. If the Trooper then declares Move as their second Basic Short Skill, this uses the second value of their MOV Attribute.

- If the user declares Jump as a Long Skill, they can Jump up to their first MOV value plus 4 inches, instead of the usual 2 inches of the Jump Skill.

- Restriction: When they declare Jump, Troopers with Super-Jump cannot benefit from Partial Cover MODs anywhere along their trajectory.

- If the Trooper is or becomes Impetuous (due to having the Frenzy Characteristic or any other effect), they can use Super-Jump in the Impetuous Phase.

- Some Troopers have the Super-Jump Skill listed in their Unit Profile with a distance shown in round brackets. When Jumping, the Trooper can add this distance to their Jump, instead of the usual 2 inches of the Jump Skill.

### Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion)

- Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion): Troopers with this Skill listed in their Unit Profile s are able to control the trajectory of their Jumps, which allows them to change the direction and heading of the jump before they land on a surface (see the examples).

When declaring the use of Super-Jump, the complete and exact trajectory that a Trooper will perform must be specified so the rival player may declare all their AROs.

During their entire trajectory, Troopers have a 360° LoF.

When Jump is declared, the Prone State is automatically cancelled.

## Super-Jump Examples

During their Active Turn, a Trooper with Super-Jump needs to get past an obstacle. As the obstacle is higher than their Silhouette Template, the Trooper must declare Jump in order to pass over it. Thanks to their Special Skill, the Trooper may declare another Short Skill, such as BS Attack during the Jump.

In the Active Turn, several Troopers have been activated with the same Order ( Fireteams, Peripherals, Coordinated Orders ) and need to clear an obstacle. They declare Jump as the first Skill of the Order. Any of the Troopers who have the Super-Jump Special Skill can declare Jump as a Basic Short Skill, while the other Troopers each have a choice of declaring Jump as a Long Skill, or declaring an Idle.

For the second Skill of the Order, any of the Troopers who declared an Idle, or Jump as a Basic Short Skill, can declare a second Basic Short Skill or Short Skill as normal. While the Troopers who declared Jump as a Long Skill have already declared their entire Order and cannot declare a second Skill.

In his Active Turn, a Trooper with Super-Jump wants to Attack an enemy Trooper, but without leaving themselves exposed in the next Order. In addition, an obstacle is blocking his LoF. So, he decides to use his Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion) Special Skill to perform a diagonal Jump, tracing a path that clears the obstacle blocking his LoF allowing him to shoot at the enemy Trooper, and then chooses to return back to the starting position of his Jump.

As shown in the picture, a Trooper with Super-Jump (Jet Propulsion) can freely change their trajectory through the air until they reach the designated landing spot.

## Super-Jump FAQs

Q: Can you vault while Climbing or Jumping?

A: Yes. Climb and Jump follow the General Movement Rules, including vaulting.

**Super-Jump — Unofficial Commentary and Interaction Notes**

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Super-Jump dramatically changes how a Trooper interacts with verticality, LoF, and exposure. The ability to declare other Short Skills during a Jump (for example Jump + BS Attack) makes it one of the most aggressive mobility tools in the ruleset.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Rule Interpretation]**
Because the rule explicitly grants 360° LoF during the entire trajectory, models can be attacked from any angle while airborne. They cannot claim Partial Cover at any point of the Jump, including start, trajectory, and landing. Even if the Trooper begins behind Cover, the act of declaring Jump removes the benefit once the movement is resolved.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Trajectory Declaration]**
Super-Jump requires the full trajectory to be declared before AROs. This means the Active player cannot probe or pre-measure multiple arcs; they must choose a line, then face all AROs generated by that path. Jet Propulsion versions allow curved paths, which increases flexibility but still requires a fully declared route.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Comparison: Super-Jump vs. Climbing Plus]**
Both Skills enable attacking from unusual angles, but they encourage opposite risk profiles.

Super-Jump:
- Extremely fast repositioning.
- Optimal for popping up over rooftops or hopping across gaps.
- Loses Partial Cover during Jump.
- Generates broad ARO exposure because the model is elevated and visible from many angles.
- Can add extra distance if noted on the profile or when used as a Long Skill.

Climbing Plus:
- Maintains Partial Cover only when not on a vertical surface (vertical walls provide no Partial Cover).
- Safer for controlled LoF management, since the Trooper keeps to surfaces and does not gain height rapidly.
- Far fewer unexpected AROs; often only from the specific angle you expose.
- Cannot attack mid-Climb if not in contact with a surface? (Climbing Plus explicitly allows other Short Skills while on vertical surfaces, but not while “airborne” as Jump does.)

Super Jump is for explosive tempo and sudden lines; Climbing Plus is for careful positioning and layered exposure.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Comparison: Super-Jump vs. Standard Jump]**
Standard Jump:
- Always a Long Skill unless vaulting low obstacles.
- Cannot combine with a second Skill.
- Limited vertical distance.

Super-Jump:
- Can be a Short Skill, allowing Jump + BS Attack, Jump + Discover, Jump + Dodge, etc.
- Can be used in Impetuous Phase.
- Long Skill version increases jump height/distance.
- May include additional inches depending on profile.
- Jet Propulsion variant allows directional changes.

Super-Jump is massively superior to basic Jump for tactical purposes; basic Jump is only a fallback movement option.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Key Interaction: ARO Generation]**
Declaring Jump as a Short Skill often reveals the Trooper to multiple enemy positions simultaneously. Because the full route and landing point must be declared, all possible AROs become known before dice roll. Super-Jump is therefore best used when:
- Only a single enemy can draw LoF.
- The Trooper intends to shoot from an unexpected angle.
- The route crosses a gap rather than elevating above cover.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Jump + BS Attack Timing]**
Jump + BS Attack is resolved like any other Movement + Attack Order:
- First Short Skill: Jump. Declare full route.
- AROs generated.
- Second Short Skill: BS Attack declared if legal along the route.
Because the Trooper has 360° LoF, they may choose the point along the route where LoF is drawn. However, the Trooper is still targetable for the entire trajectory. Opponents’ AROs are declared based on the declared route, not the eventual firing point.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Vulnerability]**
A Trooper using Super-Jump is more fragile when exposed:
- No Partial Cover.
- No Prone.
- Total exposure in mid-air.
- Often grants multiple AROs.

This makes Super-Jump an aggressive tool that punishes poor timing. Using it late in an order group, after removing major ARO threats, is usually optimal.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Pros and Cons Summary]**

Pros:
- Lets the Trooper attack over obstacles without climbing.
- Makes unexpected lateral and vertical repositioning possible.
- Short Skill version enables Jump + BS Attack.
- Jet Propulsion allows curved jumps and returning to the start point.
- Works during Impetuous Phase.
- Sometimes adds extra measured inches depending on profile.

Cons:
- No Cover anywhere on trajectory.
- High ARO exposure, often from units otherwise blocked by terrain.
- Requires exact path declaration, reducing adaptability.
- Can leave the Trooper stranded in a bad angle if the Attack fails.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT — Tactical Notes]**
- Use rooftops or parapets to “minimize exposure time”: declare a short pop-up jump where the Trooper rises just high enough to see a target, fires, and drops back down into total Cover.
- Clean long-range AROs before using Super-Jump aggressively.
- Jet Propulsion users can “hook” around obstacles to draw LoF while avoiding some angles.
- Super-Jump is extremely good with high-Burst, good-range weapons or templates (shotguns, flamethrowers) because they benefit from surprise angles.
