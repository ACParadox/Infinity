The term Hacker designates a set of Troopers who have received specific training in Infowar. This training allows them to make use of Hacking Devices, and the programs specifically designed for them.

- Hackers may be equipped with a Hacking Device that will grant them access to certain Hacking Programs, depending on the type of Device they are using, or to make direct use of certain Programs without requiring a device.

- Hackers may have Upgrade Programs assigned to them.

- This Special Skill remains active even when its owner is in a Null State.

The Hacker Special Skill defines a Trooper trained in Quantronic combat, allowing use of Hacking Devices and Programs. Because hacking interacts with many subsystems—Repeaters, Firewalls, Supportware, Targeted, states like Immobilized, and the TAG possession cycle—it is central to Infinity's infowar layer.

## Hacker Interaction Notes

### Hackers & Hacking Area
Hackers act within their Hacking Area (Zone of Control + Repeater network).  
This creates a “projected influence” that shapes movement lanes. Troopers entering or moving inside a Hacker’s area risk AROs such as **Carbonite**, **Spotlight**, or **Oblivion**.

### Hackers & Repeaters
Repeaters extend a Hacker's threat bubble. Because Repeaters create symmetrical Hacking Zones, placing one aggressively risks giving enemy Hackers direct access to your own forces. Using them requires planning and screening with Firewalls or Killer Hackers.

### Hackers & Firewalls / Tinbots
Firewalls grant +3 WIP and -3 Damage against Hacking Attacks that target the protected Trooper.  
This dramatically shifts the odds in Face-to-Face Rolls for Carbonite or Oblivion and is the main defensive counter to enemy Hackers. Tinbots creating permanent Firewalls make HI links much harder to subdue.

### Hackers & Reset
Any Trooper may declare Reset against a Hacking Program or Comms Attack—even without LoF.  
This means Hackers rarely catch enemies “helpless”. However, Reset is usually lower odds than shooting or Dodging, so hacking can still force suboptimal decisions.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Hackers vs. Hackable vs. Non-Hackable Targets
Programs divide into:

- **Against Hackable only:** Carbonite (IMM-B), Oblivion (Isolated), Total Control (TAG possession), Trinity (vs Hackers).  
- **Against anything:** Spotlight.  
Spotlight therefore becomes a universal control tool, not limited to HI/REM/TAG.

### Hackers & Targeted State
Spotlight’s Targeted State synergizes strongly with Guided Missile attacks, speculative fire, and general BS attacks.  
Targeted also enables some Classifieds. Because it is Non-Lethal and works in ARO, it is one of the most efficient ways to generate mission value.

### Hackers & Supportware
EVO Hackers enable Supportware such as **Assisted Fire**, **Controlled Jump**, **Enhanced Reaction**, and **Fairy Dust**.  
Supportware provides persistent table-wide buffs but is limited to *one Supportware per Hacker and one per target*.  
Cancelling existing Supportware by applying a new one to the same target is a key tactical lever.

### Hackers & States: IMM-A / IMM-B / Isolated / Possessed
- IMM-B (Carbonite) shuts down movement and attacks, forcing Engineers or Reset attempts.  
- Isolated (Oblivion) removes Regular Order generation and breaks links.  
- Possessed (Total Control) temporarily gives an enemy TAG to the Hacker’s player.  
These states are among the most order-effective ways to control elite units.

### Hackers vs. Killer Hackers
KHDs counter normal Hackers with **Trinity**, doing real Wounds instead of States.  
Using Repeaters aggressively without KHD coverage invites retaliation.

### Hackers & Cybermask / White Noise
- **Cybermask** improves survivability, providing Marker State defense and avoiding enemy targeting.  
- **White Noise** blocks MSV lines, opening safe advance routes for non-MSV troops.  
These utility programs are often more important than offensive ones in objective missions.

## Hacker Comparisons (Pros & Cons)

### Standard Hacker vs. Hacker Plus (HD+)
**Hacking Device**  
- Pros: Core suite—Carbonite, Spotlight, Oblivion, Total Control.  
- Cons: No defensive or positioning tools.

**Hacking Device Plus**  
- Pros: Adds **White Noise** and **Cybermask**, giving powerful board manipulation.  
- Cons: Still vulnerable to Killer Hackers; pricier.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Standard Hacker vs. Killer Hacker
**Hacker**  
- Pros: Mission utility (Spotlight, Oblivion, Total Control).  
- Cons: Vulnerable to Trinity.

**Killer Hacker**  
- Pros: Superior in Hacker-on-Hacker conflict; safer inside enemy Repeater nets.  
- Cons: Lacks general mission tools like Spotlight and Oblivion.

### EVO Hacker vs. Other Hackers
**EVO**  
- Pros: Unique Supportware (Assisted Fire, Controlled Jump, Enhanced Reaction, Fairy Dust).  
- Cons: No attack programs; requires protection; offers no direct offense.

## Hacker Tactical Notes

### Using Hackers Aggressively
- Project Repeaters forward with fast REMs or Deployable Repeaters.  
- Spotlight in ARO creates constant pressure on enemy movement.  
- Oblivion key targets (Lieutenants, link leaders, heavy hitters).

### Using Hackers Defensively
- Place Hackers behind total cover, exerting table-wide influence through Repeaters.  
- Use Tinbot-equipped Fireteams to resist enemy hacking.  
- Keep a Killer Hacker hidden to punish enemy attempts to penetrate your network.

### Multi-Hacker Synergy
- Combine an EVO for buffs with a normal Hacker for offense.  
- Maintain overlapping Repeater coverage to create “zones of denial”.  
- Use Cybermask to reposition Hackers safely.

### Protecting Your Own Network
- Avoid exposing your army through overly aggressive Repeater placement.  
- Remove enemy Repeaters proactively.  
- Use White Noise and terrain to break Hacker sightlines and approach angles.

### Risks of Hackers
- Fragile and often high-value targets.  
- Vulnerable to dedicated Hunter units and KHDs.  
- Require careful management of Repeater nets to not backfire.

## Summary  
Hackers are force multipliers and control pieces. They shape the flow of the game, force suboptimal enemy decisions, and unlock multiple mission paths. Their weakness lies in fragility, vulnerability to Killer Hackers, and poor performance when left exposed or unsupported.
