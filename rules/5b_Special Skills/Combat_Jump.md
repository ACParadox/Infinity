# Combat Jump

Special Skills 
 LONG SKILL
Airborne Deployment (AD), Private Information, Optional. 

Some Troopers, equipped with special jump gear, have been trained to dive from the sky directly onto the battlefield, falling upon the enemy in devastating surprise strikes.

- This Trooper is not required to deploy during the Deployment Phase, remaining off-table until their player decides to deploy them during their Active Turn.

- While off-table, the Trooper will not add their Order to the Order Pool during the Tactical Phase. However, they may enter the table using their own Order to use this Special Skill, when the player chooses.

- By using this Special Skill, the player cannot place the Trooper in Prone State, or in Silhouette contact with enemy and neutral Models, Markers, or Tokens, or in contact with any scenario objective.

- The player may deploy the Trooper on any horizontal surface of the game table, as long as the size of the surface is equal to or larger than their base.

- Deploying inside buildings or enclosed pieces of Scenery is not permitted, even if they have an open roof or open doors or windows like, for example, an Objective Room.

- After placing the Trooper on their landing spot, with any Deployable Weapons or Equipment that they can deploy, the player performs a PH Roll. If the Roll is passed, the Trooper lands successfully and remains where the player has placed them. Then, the Reactive Player declares all their AROs.

- If the user fails the Roll, they must be placed within their player's Deployment Zone, always in contact with the edge of the game table. Additionally, after failing the Roll, the user loses the option to deploy in a Marker State or as a Decoy and is always deployed as a Model. Any Deployable Weapons and Equipment deployed alongside him are removed from the game table. Then, the Reactive Player declares all their AROs.

- Restriction: During the Order this Special Skill is used, the Trooper cannot benefit from Partial Cover.

- When a player moves the Trooper into a different Combat Group, while it is off the table, they must inform their opponent that the Trooper exists. The Trooper's information remains Private, until the Trooper is deployed.

- This Special Skill can be used during the Impetuous Phase, which requires removing both the Trooper's Impetuous Token and their own Order.

- While off-table, Troopers with this Special Skill can be selected to deploy as part of a Coordinated Order. However, in this situation they will lose their own Order.

### Combat Jump (Explosion)

- Combat Jump (Explosion) has the following additional rules:

- After performing the PH Roll and determining the Trooper's final location, the Trooper makes a Direct Template Attack, placing the center of the Circular Template (Blast Focus) over the center of their base.

- This Attack has PS 8 with Normal Ammunition, against the ARM Attribute.

- The Trooper is not affected by this Attack.

- This is a single-use Special Skill, and its user cannot perform it again.

Troopers cannot be deployed in contact with enemy and neutral Models, Markers, or Tokens, or in contact with any mission objective, no matter the Special Skills they have.

Controlled Jump

- As long as Controlled Jump is active, when performing the Combat Jump Skill every Allied Trooper applies a +3 MOD while every Enemy Trooper must apply a -3 MOD.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

Combat Jump is the most aggressive form of Airborne Deployment. It allows a Trooper to enter the battlefield by dropping directly onto the table at the moment of use, rather than starting on the table during Deployment. This makes Combat Jump ideal for striking deep into the opponent’s formation or accessing hard-to-reach objectives.

## Combat Jump Interactions:
- Combat Jump interacts strongly with the Controlled Jump Hacking Program. If the opponent has Controlled Jump active, your Combat Jump Roll suffers a penalty, making failure far more likely. If your own Hacker has Controlled Jump active for your turn, you gain a bonus when attempting the Combat Jump Roll.

- Failing a Combat Jump Roll normally forces the Trooper to deviate and land somewhere else. This risk makes MODs from Controlled Jump significant.

- Troopers deploying with Combat Jump and also using States such as Decoy must still follow Airborne Deployment Coherency rules when placing replicas. This limits how deceptive or safe the initial landing can be.

- Combat Jump cannot be used by Troopers in a Fireteam. Members of a Fireteam may not have Skills with the Airborne Deployment Label, so Combat Jump Troopers are always operating independently.

- Coordinated Orders may include off-table Troopers with Combat Jump, but they will lose their own personal Order for that turn. The Coordinated drop can create simultaneous threats but comes at considerable Order cost.

## Pros:
- Enables placing a Trooper exactly where they can cause maximum disruption.
- Bypasses enemy LoF and terrain to immediately threaten vulnerable targets.
- Can appear behind Total Cover, breaking normal approach limitations.
- Highly effective for assassinations, objective grabs, and forcing reactive repositioning.

## Cons:
- Requires a Roll, which, if failed, risks disadvantageous placement.
- Vulnerable to enemy Controlled Jump, which imposes a penalty directly on the PH Roll.
- If discovered and prepared for, opponents may set layered AROs around key zones, reducing safe landing options.
- After dropping, the Trooper is usually alone and lacks Fireteam bonuses or support.
- Deviated landings may put the Trooper inside enemy LoF or in poor cover.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Comparison: Combat Jump vs Parachutist
- Combat Jump allows entering almost anywhere on the battlefield, while Parachutist restricts drop locations to the table edge or designated zones. Combat Jump offers far greater reach and ambush potential.

- Parachutist has no landing Roll and therefore no risk of deviation. In contrast, Combat Jump is more powerful but riskier.

- Parachutist is more predictable for both players; Combat Jump threatens the entire table, forcing the opponent to defend multiple angles.

- Combat Jump is generally better for surprise attacks, Parachutist for reliable reinforcement.

## Comparison: Combat Jump vs Infiltration
- Infiltration places the Trooper during Deployment, not during the game. Combat Jump allows deployment at any moment during the Active Turn.

- Infiltration is more stable on the table from the start; Combat Jump arrives later but exactly when and where needed.

- Infiltration has its own Roll if attempting to deploy in the opponent’s half; Combat Jump always risks a Roll regardless of landing position.

##  Combat Jump Tactical Notes:
- Use friendly Controlled Jump to improve reliability. Consider spending the Order on an EVO Hacker before attempting multiple Combat Jumps.

- Aim for rooftops, behind walls, or inside blind spots where opponents cannot immediately retaliate during your landing.

- Consider landing near objectives to pressure the opponent’s scoring. Even a single Trooper can force reallocations of Orders.

- If possible, drop out of enemy Hacking Areas to avoid AROs from enemy Hackers with Controlled Jump.

- Use Coordinated Combat Jump sparingly. While powerful, the loss of each Trooper’s personal Order can heavily impact your turn economy.

- Landing after your opponent's major attack pieces have moved into the midfield can create devastating counter-attacks.

## Multiple Drop Strategy:
- Deploy the first Combat Jump Trooper conservatively to secure a safe foothold.
- Pair Combat Jump with Smoke or other visibility modifiers from on-table units to further protect landing zones.
