# Forward Deployment

Special Skills
 DEPLOYMENT SKILL
Superior Deployment, Optional. 

This Special Skill marks Troopers who move before the bulk of their own forces and take positions beyond the established allied lines.

- The users of this Special Skill can be deployed beyond the limit of their Deployment Zone. The number of inches that those Troopers may deploy beyond their Deployment Zone appears between round brackets in their Unit Profile.

Troopers cannot be deployed in contact with enemy and neutral Models, Markers, or Tokens, or in contact with any mission objective, no matter the Special Skills they have.


## How Forward Deployment Interacts with Core Deployment Rules
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
- Troopers with Forward Deployment may deploy outside the normal Deployment Zone, up to the distance allowed by their level of the Skill.
- They must still obey all standard Deployment restrictions:
  - Cannot deploy in contact with enemy models, tokens, or objectives.  
  - Must be placed in a legal location where their base fits entirely.
  - May deploy Prone.
  - Distances may be measured for Deployment Zones and areas where Forward Deployment can be used.


## Forward Deployment vs. Infiltration (Comparison)
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
**Forward Deployment**
- Always succeeds; no roll is required.
- Grants a fixed forward extension (e.g., +4", +8").
- Cannot cross the table’s centerline unless the bonus reaches or exceeds it.
- No risk of “failed deployment.”

**Infiltration**
- Allows deployment up to or across the centerline.
- Crossing the centerline requires a PH Roll.
- Failing the roll forces the Trooper to deploy on the *back edge* of their own Deployment Zone.
- Potentially grants far more aggressive forward placement than any level of Forward Deployment, but at higher risk.

**Pros of Forward Deployment**
- Reliability—no roll, no risk.
- Good for mid-range shooters, specialists, and pieces needing early board presence.
- Helps Fireteams via Strategic Deployment.

**Cons of Forward Deployment**
- Less aggressive reach than Infiltration.
- Predictable; enemy will know the forward boundary.
---

## Forward Deployment vs. Strategic Deployment
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategic Deployment allows an entire Fireteam to deploy as if they all had Forward Deployment (+4").  
This provides:
- Strong early board control for Fireteams.
- Cohesive team positioning in midfield without risk.

Forward Deployment alone affects only the individual Trooper possessing the Skill.

**Pros of Strategic Deployment (relative to individual FD)**
- Multiplies effect across multiple troopers.
- Increases Fireteam influence and potential early objective pressure.
- Allows link teams to gain terrain advantage earlier.

**Cons of Strategic Deployment**
- Requires Fireteam composition.
- Predictable spacing—entire team starts further forward, potentially exposed to enemy alpha strikes.
---

## Interactions With Minelayer
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
- Forward Deployment greatly increases the threat zone of Minelayers.
- A Mine or deployable can be placed anywhere within the Trooper’s Zone of Control, as long as it remains within the area in which the Minelayer is allowed to Deploy.
- Combining both typically creates strong early deterrent zones, shapes enemy movement during Turn 1, and protects objectives before the first Order is spent.

---

## Interactions With Hidden Deployment
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
- A Trooper with both Hidden Deployment and Forward Deployment would theoretically write down a Hidden Deployment location anywhere allowed by both rules.
- Since Hidden Deployment already allows deployment anywhere legal (including forward), Forward Deployment mainly matters if the Trooper’s Hidden Deployment rules or mission restrictions limit locations.

---

## Pros and Cons of Forward Deployment in Army Building
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

### Pros
- **Board Control**: Immediate presence near objectives and midfield lanes.
- **Order Efficiency**: Saves Orders that would otherwise be spent on early repositioning.
- **Aggressive Specialists**: Ideal for Forward Observers, Hackers, and button-pushers needing immediate objective access.
- **Synergy with Mimetism/Marker States**: Strong when combined with survivability tools, making midfield skirmishers very dangerous.

### Cons
- **Exposure to Alpha Strike**: Being farther up the board can put units inside enemy threat ranges.
- **Limited Flexibility**: Cannot exceed the assigned distance; infiltration units can outperform FD in raw reach.
- **Predictable Lines**: Opponents often measure and prepare counter-deployments at the FD boundary.

---

## Example Tactical Uses
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
- Deploying a Forward Deployment specialist near central objectives to score immediately on Turn 1.
- Setting up overlapping fields of fire with mid-range weapons (Spitfires, Marksman Rifles).
- Using FD Minelayers to protect consoles, entrances, or firelanes before the game even starts.
- Shaping enemy approach lanes by placing ARO pieces on rooftops or corners further upfield.

---

## When to Choose Forward Deployment Troopers
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Pick FD models when:
- You intend to fight for mid-board control.
- You rely on early scenario scoring.
- You need efficient Order use.
- You expect heavy midfield pressure and want to contest space immediately.

Avoid or minimize FD models when:
- Opponent has strong alpha-strike capability (e.g., Combat Jump + MSV units).
- Missions favor defensive posture over early aggression.
- You are playing a highly reactive gunline where forward placement offers little benefit.
