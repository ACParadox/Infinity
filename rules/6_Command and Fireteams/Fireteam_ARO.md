# Fireteams in the Reactive Turn

Fireteams Module

## Fireteam ARO

Players must consider the Fireteam's ARO to be the Skill declared by more than half of the Fireteam members that declare an ARO. In any other case, the player decides which Skill is the Fireteam's ARO. The consequence for declaring a Skill that is different from the Fireteam's ARO is that:
    - if the Fireteam Leader ARO is different than Fireteam's ARO, it cancels the Fireteam.
    - if not the FT leader, he leaves the FT.

- During the Reactive Turn, all the Fireteam members gain their own separate AROs. However, any Skills declared as AROs must be the same for all of the reactive members, which is called the Fireteam's ARO.

- During the Reactive Turn, all the Fireteam members have all the bonuses that being part of a Fireteam provides.

- During the Reactive Turn, all Fireteam members perform their own Rolls and apply the corresponding effects on the enemy. Close combat is an exception (see the Fireteams and CC Combat section).

- Each Fireteam member must observe the Coherency rule in regard to the Fireteam Leader. The player must perform a Coherency Check at the start and end of each ARO, to know how many members the Fireteam has. If a Fireteam member fails a Coherency Check regarding the Fireteam Leader, they will not count as part of the Fireteam and will immediately stop being part of it.

- When facing multiple Active Troopers ( Coordinated Orders, another Fireteam, Troopers with Peripherals, and so on), each Fireteam member must choose only one of the Troopers activated by the Order as their target, but they are not obliged to choose the same target. However, the Skill declared in ARO must be the same for all members of the Fireteam.
    


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteams do not generate “group AROs”. Each Fireteam member is still an individual Trooper for ARO purposes. The main difference is that Fireteam bonuses and restrictions can apply when those AROs are declared, resolved, or lost. Many players instinctively assume Fireteams act as a single reactive entity—this is incorrect and leads to frequent mistakes.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Every eligible Fireteam member may declare an ARO when an enemy Order grants it, even if the Team Leader is not the one with Line of Fire or Zone of Control. However, only members that meet normal ARO requirements (LoF, ZoC, Hacking Area, template, etc.) may react. Fireteam membership never grants “shared” perception.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteam AROs are declared simultaneously, but the Fireteam does not need to maintain Coherency to declare AROs. Coherency is only checked at specific moments. A member who is temporarily out of Coherency can still declare an ARO if they are still part of the Fireteam at that moment.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If Coherency is broken at the start of an Order in the Active Turn, out-of-Coherency members immediately leave the Fireteam and will not receive Fireteam bonuses. In the Reactive Turn, this usually matters only if a rule explicitly forces a Coherency check. This subtle timing difference is often overlooked.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because ARO Burst is normally B1, Fireteam bonuses that add Burst do not function the way many players expect. The Special Die is not Burst and therefore bypasses the usual ARO Burst limitation. This makes Fireteams better at “quality defense” rather than volume of fire.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Only one Fireteam member can be the Team Leader, but in ARO the Team Leader concept usually does nothing by itself. Players sometimes assume only the Team Leader can react—this is false. Team Leader matters mainly for movement, bonuses, and coherence, not ARO eligibility.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
When facing template weapons, multiple Fireteam members may be affected by the same template and all may declare AROs. However, Dodge AROs are resolved individually. A successful Dodge by one member does not protect the others.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategically, Fireteams excel as layered ARO defenses. Stagger members so that different enemy movement paths trigger different ARO pieces. This forces the Active Player to deal with multiple Face to Face rolls across multiple Orders.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A common tactical use is designating a high-BS or long-range weapon as the primary ARO piece while other Fireteam members cover alternate angles with cheaper weapons. The Fireteam bonus improves the main shooter while the others tax enemy Orders.
