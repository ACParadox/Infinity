# Fireteams Chart

Fireteams Module

This section explains how to read the information provided in the Fireteams Chart. To do so, we will use the following Chart as an example, but it is important to remember that the official and current Charts are those shown in the Infinity Army app.

1. Name of the Army or Sectorial.

2. The maximum number of each Type of Fireteam that may be on the game table at the same time. This is checked every time a Fireteam is created. In this case, the Morat Aggression Force may have no more than one Fireteam Core, and no more than two Fireteam Haris, but has no limit on the number of Fireteam Duos.

3. When Creating a Rodoks Fireteam, the player may choose to create a Fireteam Duo and/or a Fireteam Haris. Once created, the Fireteams follow the Fireteam Integrity rules. If the Availability of the Troopers and the rules for creating Fireteams allow it, the player may have a Fireteam Haris and a Fireteam Duo of Rodoks on the game table at the same time.

4. When Creating a Fireteam, you must observe the minimum and maximum number of Troopers that can be part of that Fireteam. In this case, a Hungries Fireteam must include at least one Oznat FTO. Fireteam Bonuses apply based on the number of Troopers in the Fireteam who are from the same Unit, or list the name of the Unit or Fireteam in a note or in brackets.

5. Different Units that can form the Morat Fireteams. The asterisk (*) in the Min column indicates that, when you create any Morat Fireteam, you must choose at least 1 Trooper with an asterisk (*) in the Chart. [5N] Note: The Fireteam Bonus level of a Morat Fireteam depends on how many Troopers in the Fireteam have "(Morat)" or are from the same Unit.

6. In some cases, there may be additional conditions for the creation or operation of Fireteams. These notes take priority over the General Rules of Fireteams.

7. The 'FTO' term in the chart indicates that, in a Unit, only those options with 'FTO' in their option name can be part of a Fireteam. This includes options such as the FTO Lieutenant, the FTO Hacker, or the FTO-2. However, if a specific FTO is mentioned, for example, the FTO-2, only that option can be part of the Fireteam.

8. Wildcards can be part of any Fireteam. The maximum column shows the maximum number of Troopers of that Unit that can be included per Fireteam.

- [5N] Note: The Fireteam Bonus level of a Morat Fireteam depends on how many Troopers in the Fireteam have "(Morat)" or are from the same Unit.

The following Troopers cannot be part of a Fireteam:

- Peripherals and their Controllers.

- Troopers in Marker form.

- Troopers with the Infiltration Special Skill.

- Troopers possessing Skills with the Airborne Deployment Label.

- Troopers that activate or are in the Suppressive Fire State.

- Troopers who deploy in Decoy State.

- Troopers in Isolated State or any Null State.

Additionally, members of a Fireteam cannot be part of a Coordinated Order.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteams Chart entries define *permission*, not obligation. If a Trooper appears in the chart, it only means it is allowed to form or join that specific Fireteam type; it does not override other requirements such as Sectorial restrictions, AVA limits, or special conditions stated elsewhere. A common mistake is assuming the chart alone grants universal compatibility.

The Fireteams Chart is *hierarchical*, not additive. If a Trooper appears in multiple Fireteam entries (for example Core and Haris), it must still obey the limits of each Fireteam type independently. Being eligible for Core does not imply eligibility for Haris unless explicitly listed.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
 “Wildcard” does not mean “counts as any troop type” for all purposes. It only means the Trooper may join any Fireteam allowed by the Sectorial, but it does not grant special bonuses, Fireteam skill access, or override composition caps unless explicitly stated.

Named characters listed in the Fireteams Chart often have *asymmetric permissions*. Some characters may join a Fireteam but prevent it from being formed unless other requirements are met. Always verify whether the chart entry allows “forming” a Fireteam or only “joining” an existing one.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Mixed Fireteams created via chart permissions still use a single Team Leader. Bonuses are calculated from Fireteam size, not homogeneity. Players sometimes incorrectly assume mixed teams lose bonuses faster or gain partial bonuses; this is not supported unless a rule explicitly states so.

The Fireteams Chart does not bypass Coherency rules. Even if a Trooper is chart-legal, failing Coherency immediately removes that Trooper from the Fireteam at the relevant check. This is especially easy to miss with slower or support-focused members in mixed teams.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteam composition legality is checked continuously, not only at creation. If a Trooper leaves the Fireteam due to Coherency, State, or death, the remaining Fireteam must still satisfy the chart. If it does not, the Fireteam is immediately cancelled.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Some charts allow Fireteams that look inefficient on paper but are designed for order efficiency or protection rather than offense. A Fireteam with one strong attacker and several cheap bodies is often a deliberate design choice, not a trap.

The chart does not imply balance between options. Just because two units can form the same Fireteam does not mean they are meant to perform the same role. Players should treat the chart as a *toolbox*, not a tier list.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategic use: Fireteams built from chart flexibility excel at role compression. Mixing specialists, gunfighters, and support pieces into a single activation group reduces Order expenditure, even if raw firepower is lower than a pure combat team.

 Charts that allow cheap fillers are ideal for absorbing casualties while maintaining Fireteam bonuses. Losing a low-cost member instead of a key attacker often preserves offensive momentum.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteams with chart-enabled Wildcards are excellent for disguising the true Team Leader. Swapping the Leader between similar-profile members mid-turn can reduce the impact of enemy ARO planning.

Tactical use: Chart-legal support pieces (Doctors, Engineers, Hackers) inside Fireteams gain survivability through shared movement and positioning, even if they rarely attack. This is often stronger than running them solo.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Design note: If a Fireteams Chart entry feels oddly restrictive, it is usually intentional to preserve faction identity or internal balance. Treat restrictions as signals about intended playstyle rather than oversights.
