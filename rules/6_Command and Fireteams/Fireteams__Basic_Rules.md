## Fireteam Creation

Each Army has a Fireteams Chart that indicates the quantity and type of Fireteams that the player may create during the game.

- During the Deployment Phase, place a Fireteam Leader Token (FIRETEAM LEADER), or equivalent, beside one of the Troopers that meet the Requirements of this rule. In that moment, the player must make a Coherency Check relative to the chosen Fireteam Leader.

- During the game, spend a Command Token and place a Fireteam Leader Token (FIRETEAM LEADER) beside one of the Troopers that meet the instructions of the Fireteams Chart of their Army.

Creating a Fireteam during the game requires the Operational Use of a Command Token.

When a Fireteam is created, the player must declare which Troopers are part of it, and must follow all the rules and instructions in the Fireteams Chart.

The following Troopers cannot be part of a Fireteam:

- Peripherals and their Controllers.

- Troopers in Marker form.

- Troopers with the Infiltration Special Skill.

- Troopers possessing Skills with the Airborne Deployment Label.

- Troopers that activate or are in the Suppressive Fire State.

- Troopers who deploy in Decoy State.

- Troopers in Isolated State or any Null State.

Additionally, members of a Fireteam cannot be part of a Coordinated Order.

## Rejoining the Fireteam in the States Phase

At the end of the next States Phase, any Troopers who left a Fireteam may automatically rejoin it if they are in Coherency with the Fireteam Leader.

## Fireteam Types

The types of Fireteams that the player may use during a match are:

### Fireteam Duo

When created, a Fireteam Duo must contain two Troopers.

### Fireteam Haris

When created, a Fireteam Haris must contain three Troopers.

### Fireteam Core

When created, a Fireteam Core must contain a minimum of three and a maximum of five Troopers.

Some Sectorials can modify the conditions for creating a Fireteam, which will be listed in the Sectorial’s Fireteams Chart.

## General Rules for Fireteams

- The members of a Fireteam must observe the Coherency rule in regard to the Fireteam Leader.

- A Trooper cannot simultaneously be part of more than one Fireteam.

- All members of a Fireteam must belong to the same Combat Group.

- When a Regular Order is spent on a member of a Fireteam, they automatically become the Fireteam Leader. Additionally, they activate the other members of the Fireteam (see Fireteam Order ).

- Fireteams must contain a minimum of two Troopers and a maximum of five Troopers (always observing the Army's Fireteams Chart ).

- Fireteams allow the use of the Fireteam Bonuses for two, three, four, or five members, depending on the Fireteam Level.

## Fireteams and CC Combat

- In Close Combat, whether in the Active or Reactive Turn, when several members are engaged in the same CC, only the Fireteam Leader can be chosen to perform the CC Roll, applying the Close Combat with Multiple Troopers rule.

- In the Reactive Turn, if the Fireteam Leader is not engaged in that CC, the player must choose one of the Fireteam members who are engaged in that CC to perform the CC Roll, applying the Close Combat with Multiple Troopers rule.

- In ARO, the enemy will be able to target any Fireteam member engaged in the CC, but only one of them.

## Fireteams and Suppressive Fire

- A Trooper stops being part of a Fireteam if he declares the Suppressive Fire Common Skill. In addition, a Trooper’s Suppressive Fire State is automatically cancelled if they join a Fireteam.

## Fireteams and Comms Attack

- In the Reactive Turn, if the Fireteam has more than one Hacker and the player declares a Hacking ARO, every reactive Hacker may use a different Hacking Program.

## Fireteams FAQs

Q: During your main part of the Deployment Phase, can you reposition Troopers, Deployables, etc?

A: In their main Deployment Phase step, players can reposition Troopers, Deployables, etc. As soon as a Roll or measurement is made for those game elements, their position is fixed, and they cannot be repositioned during the Deployment Phase.

For example, you can reposition a Trooper as many times as you want, but after you create a Fireteam and adjust the placement of the Fireteam members during the Coherency Check, you can no longer reposition any member of that Fireteam.


# Fireteams – Interaction Notes and Unofficial Commentary (Infinity N5)

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
This document intentionally focuses on interactions, edge cases, and commonly misunderstood points rather than restating the official Fireteam rules. All commentary below is unofficial, interpretive, and meant as explanatory annotations for learning, reference, and tactical understanding.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## 1. Fireteam Creation

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A common oversight is that Fireteam creation is not just a list-building concept: the Fireteam only truly exists once deployed and coherency is checked.

Fireteam creation locks in positioning. After performing the Coherency adjustment for a newly created Fireteam, those models cannot be freely repositioned anymore. This matters especially when deploying near cover edges, ladders, or elevation changes.

Strategically, Fireteam creation is often about *future order efficiency*, not immediate power. Creating a Fireteam early allows coordinated movement later, but it also ties troopers together, making them more vulnerable to templates and hacking zones.


## 2. Rejoining the Fireteam in the States Phase

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteam members that were previously separated (due to loss of coherency, unconsciousness, or similar effects) may rejoin the Fireteam during the States Phase if they meet all requirements. This is frequently missed, especially by newer players.

Rejoining does not retroactively grant bonuses during the turn in which the trooper was absent. Fireteam bonuses only apply once the trooper has successfully rejoined and coherency is restored at the correct timing window.

Tactically, this allows intentional temporary splitting. A trooper can break coherency to accomplish a short-term task (button pushing, angle guarding) and then rejoin without permanently dissolving the Fireteam.

---

## 3. Fireteam Types

### 3.1 Fireteam Duo

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Duo Fireteams are ideal for mobile attack pairs, specialist escorts, or synchronized advances through dense terrain. Because only two models are involved, maintaining coherency is significantly easier and less order-intensive.

Also, the best FT bonus (+1 SD) can be achieved with a FT Duo.

---

### 3.2 Fireteam Haris

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteam Haris sits at a middle ground and is frequently misunderstood as a “mini-Core.” In practice, it is best treated as a flexible assault or midfield control unit.

The Haris Skill requirement means the Fireteam has a built-in lynchpin. Losing the Haris trooper can immediately dismantle the Fireteam, which is an important risk to account for in planning.

From a tactical standpoint, Haris Fireteams excel at flanking, pushing objectives, and operating outside the main battle line where a Core Fireteam would be too cumbersome.

The level 3 FT bonus isn't great, but having three members means that even if a Trooper is lost, the FT still have +1 SD.


### 3.3 Fireteam Core

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteam Core is the most powerful and the most restrictive Fireteam type. Its strength comes from stacking bonuses, but those bonuses are conditional and fragile.

A common mistake is assuming a Core Fireteam should always stay together. In reality, Core Fireteams often benefit from controlled fragmentation, temporarily losing members to gain board coverage, then reforming later.

Core Fireteams are also priority targets for the opponent. Expect hacking pressure, template weapons, and coordinated attacks aimed at breaking coherency or removing key members.

The level 4-5 FT bonus are good, but not great, so having a 4-5 member Core is optional, three is often fine.

## 4. General Rules for Fireteams

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Only the Team Leader performs attacks, but all members contribute to movement and positioning. This asymmetry is a frequent source of confusion, especially when dealing with Line of Fire and ARO generation.

When coherency is broken at the start of an Order, out-of-coherency troopers immediately leave the Fireteam and are not activated. This can happen unintentionally through cautious movement or vertical displacement.

Strategically, Fireteams reward precise movement discipline. Pre-measuring ZoC relationships mentally before declaring movement is a learned skill that separates experienced Fireteam users from beginners.

---

## 5. Fireteams and Close Combat

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Only the Team Leader can participate in Close Combat as the active attacker, even if multiple Fireteam members are in base contact. Other members do not add extra attacks or modifiers unless a specific rule states otherwise.

This often surprises players who expect multiple troopers to “pile in.” Fireteams do not function as CC swarms by default.

Tactically, Fireteams should avoid prolonged Close Combat unless the Team Leader is explicitly built for it. Engaging in CC with a Fireteam can also freeze multiple valuable troopers in place, reducing overall board control.

---

## 6. Fireteams and Suppressive Fire

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A Fireteam automatically dissolves when a member declares Suppressive Fire. This is one of the most commonly forgotten interactions.

Suppressive Fire is a deliberate trade-off: personal area denial in exchange for losing Fireteam bonuses and structure. This is not a drawback, but a tactical choice.

Strategically, breaking a Fireteam into individual Suppressive Fire pieces late in the turn can be extremely powerful, especially when locking down objectives or fire lanes after the Fireteam has completed its main task.

---

## 7. Fireteams and Comms Attacks

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteams are particularly vulnerable to Comms Attacks due to shared positioning and overlapping Zones of Control. A single successful hacking program can neutralize the Team Leader and effectively shut down the Fireteam.

A key point often missed is that Fireteam bonuses do not protect against hacking effects. The Fireteam structure offers no inherent defensive benefit in Quantronic Combat.

From a tactical perspective, Fireteams should either include hacking defense (Repeaters, TinBots, Firewall effects) or actively avoid known hacking lanes.
