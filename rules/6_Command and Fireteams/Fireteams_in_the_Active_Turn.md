# Fireteams in the Active Turn

Fireteams Module

During the Active Turn, Fireteams behave differently from other Infinity Troopers, as explained below:

- During the Orders Phase of the Active Turn, the Fireteam Order requires spending 1 Regular Order from the Order Pool of the Fireteam membersCombat Group.

- The player must nominate one member of the Fireteam as the Fireteam Leader, placing a Fireteam Leader Token (FIRETEAM LEADER) beside them.

- Members of a Fireteam must observe the Coherency rule regarding the Fireteam Leader. If they do not, see Fireteam Integrity.

- All Fireteam members are activated with the Fireteam Order.

- If the Fireteam declares Reset or any Skill with the Movement Label, both the Fireteam Leader and the other Fireteam members declare and perform that Skill. If a different Skill is chosen, only the Fireteam Leader declares and performs it, while the rest of the members of the Fireteam declare and perform an Idle, while granting the Fireteam Leader a set of Bonuses determined by the Fireteam Level.

- If any of the Fireteam members does not meet the Requirements of a Skill declared during the Order or does not have the Skill, then they perform an Idle instead of that Skill, while the others may act normally.

- Each Reactive Trooper can choose only one of the Troopers (whether the Fireteam Leader or one of the other Fireteam members) as their target, but they are not obliged to choose the same target.

## Fireteams, NCO and Tactical Sense

If a Fireteam member has the NCO or Tactical Awareness Special Skill and spends a Tactical Order, they are automatically designated as the new Fireteam Leader, and the Fireteam is activated as if a Regular Order had been spent.

Any member that breaks Coherency is no longer part of the Fireteam (see Fireteam Integrity ).

During a Fireteam Order, the Skills that are performed by both the Fireteam Leader and the rest of the members are:

- Reset

- Skills with the Movement Label (except Berserk )

- Dodge

- Move

- Jump

- Climb

## Fireteams and Berserk

If the Fireteam Leader declares the Berserk Skill, only the Fireteam members who have that Skill will be able to declare and perform it, the rest of the Troopers will declare and perform an Idle.

Super-Jump

In the Active Turn, several Troopers have been activated with the same Order ( Fireteams, Peripherals, Coordinated Orders ) and need to clear an obstacle. They declare Jump as the first Skill of the Order. Any of the Troopers who have the Super-Jump Special Skill can declare Jump as a Basic Short Skill, while the other Troopers each have a choice of declaring Jump as a Long Skill, or declaring an Idle.

For the second Skill of the Order, any of the Troopers who declared an Idle, or Jump as a Basic Short Skill, can declare a second Basic Short Skill or Short Skill as normal. While the Troopers who declared Jump as a Long Skill have already declared their entire Order and cannot declare a second Skill.

## Fireteams in the Active Turn FAQs

Q: If several Troopers are activated at once (for example a Coordinated Order or a Fireteam) and generate AROs, can any of the AROs target Active Troopers that are using Stealth?

A: Yes, but the ARO will become an Idle if the Stealth user does not declare a Skill that allows AROs.

Fireteams in the Active Turn – Interactions Notes and Unofficial Commentary (Infinity N5)


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteam Orders are still Orders spent on a single Trooper

A Fireteam does NOT activate “as a group” in the same way as a single multi-model entity.
You always activate one Trooper: the Team Leader.
The rest of the Fireteam members are activated only because a Fireteam rule explicitly
allows them to be activated together.

This matters because:
- Anything that checks “the activated Trooper” usually only refers to the Team Leader.
- Anything that checks “Troopers activated by the Order” includes all Fireteam members.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Skill declaration is shared, execution is individual

When the Team Leader declares a Short Skill or Long Skill, all Fireteam members
must declare the same Skill.

However:
- Each member checks Requirements independently.
- Each member resolves movement, LoF, range, and MODs independently.

This means it is legal (and common) for:
- Some members to Move less (or not at all).
- Some members to Idle because Requirements are not met.

A very common mistake is assuming that if one member can perform the Skill,
they all can. The opposite is also true.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Only one Team Leader, but many attackers

Even though there is a single Team Leader:
- If FT is declaring a Reset or any Skill with the Movement Label, all members do it.
- Else only the FT leader does the action.

This means a FT is more of a movement tactic, to save orders moving multiple units to a similar location.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Coherency is checked twice, not continuously


Fireteam Coherency is checked:
- After declaration (before movement measurement)
- At the end of the Order

During movement, temporary coherency breaks are allowed.

Tactical exploitation:
- You can “slingshot” members forward or sideways as long as final positions
  re-establish coherency.
- This allows wider angles, better corner coverage, and safer stacking behind terrain.

Tactical risk:
- Forgetting the final coherency check can silently eject members from the Fireteam.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Losing members recalculates bonuses immediately


If Fireteam members leave the Fireteam during the Order
(due to coherency loss, States, or other effects):
- Fireteam bonuses are recalculated immediately.

This can retroactively affect:
- Burst values
- Availability of Special Dice

Subtle consequence:
- Dice assigned earlier in the declaration step may become illegal and be lost.

This is one of the most punishing Fireteam mistakes at higher-level play.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Guts Rolls are individual, not collective


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteams amplify efficiency, not resilience


Fireteams do not make Troopers tougher.
They make Orders more efficient.
'
A Fireteam concentrates value:
- Losing one Order can expose multiple models.
- Losing one key member can collapse bonuses.

Strategic mindset:
- Fireteams win games by decisive, controlled aggression.
- Overextending a Fireteam often loses games faster than losing solo pieces.
