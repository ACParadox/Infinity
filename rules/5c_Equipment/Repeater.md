# Repeater

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)

Equipment

This is a range amplifier for Hackers.

- Repeaters extend the Hacking Area of all Hackers in the same Army List.

- Hackers within the Zone of Control of an enemy Repeater may use it to apply Hacking Programs against any enemy Hacker, but applying Firewall MODs (-3).

- There can be no reaction against a Repeater that is being used by an enemy Hacker, only against the Hacker, if possible.

This piece of Equipment is automatically active and cannot be deactivated by its owner, unless its carrier is in Isolated State or any Null States.

Firewall imposes an additional MOD (-3, -6...) to the WIP Roll of the Trooper declaring a Comms Attack against a Trooper benefitting from a Firewall, for example if attacking through an enemy Repeater.

The Trooper benefiting from the Firewall also applies a MOD of +3 to their Saving Rolls (SR) against the Comms Attack.

Only the MODs of a single Firewall can be applied, even if the Trooper benefits from more than one. In the case of the Trooper benefitting from more than one Firewall, their player will decide which one to apply.

## 1. Interaction Notes

### Repeater & ARO Generation
- Entering a Repeater’s ZoC **does not itself generate an ARO**.  
-  The ARO is generated by **the Hacker**, if the Hacker has LoF or the acting trooper declares a Skill that allows them to react.

### Repeater & Stealth
- A model with Stealth can move inside a Repeater’s ZoC **without provoking Hacking AROs**, unless a Hacker has LoF.  
- This makes Stealth one of the strongest tools for bypassing “Reapter nets,” allowing assassins or heavy hitters to slip into strike range.

### Repeater & Firewall
- When attacking *through an enemy Repeater network*, Hackers suffer Firewall MODs (–3 or –6 depending on equipment).  
- The defender gains +3 to Saving Rolls.  
- Only one Firewall applies, even if multiple sources exist.

### Repeater & Target Selection
- A Hacker using an enemy Repeater inside ZoC can attack **any enemy Hacker**, regardless of distance, terrain, or LoF.  
- This is the core of “mutual hacking destruction”—entering an enemy Repeater net often exposes both sides to risk.

### Repeater & Order Timing
- Deployable Repeaters (e.g., from Pitchers, FastPandas) **become active at the end of the Order** that placed them.  
- Until then they project no Hacking Area and do not enable AROs.

### Repeater & Camouflage / Hidden Movement
- Repeater effects do **not** reveal Camo or HD units.

## 2. Strategic Interactions
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

### Area Denial
Repeater networks force opponents to plan movement around hacking bubbles. High-value targets (HI, TAGs, REMs) often must spend Orders Resetting or maneuvering around zones to avoid Oblivion or Spotlight.

### Cyber-Projection via Pitchers / FastPandas
- Pitchers project Repeaters at range, enabling “turn-one lockouts” or rapid Objective control denial.
- FastPandas provide fast, short-range deployment to reshape hacking zones instantly.

### Repeater Chains
Repeaters can “daisy chain” a Hacker’s influence across the entire table. A single well-placed Deployable may convert the midboard into a hacking trap.

### Defensive Layering
Repeater zones combined with:
- **EVO Supportware** (Enhanced Reaction)  
- **Spotlight AROs**  
- **Oblivion**  
create dangerous zones that punish even cautious advances.

------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Pros and Cons of Repeaters
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

### Pros
- Huge **area control** potential without LoF requirements.  
- Enables **long-range hacking** across the entire table.  
- Threatens enemy Hackers due to unlimited targeting within network.  
- Enhances **ARO presence** in the midfield and DZ.  
- Cheap, often Disposable equipment with high tactical payoff.

### Cons
- Your own Hackers become **vulnerable** inside the enemy’s Repeater net → “mutual assured hacking.”  
- Can be bypassed by **Stealth**, Camo infiltration, or aggressive non-hackable troops.  
- Requires Hackers to be alive 
- Overextending Repeaters may give *opponents* better attack vectors than you receive.
- Repeaters can be shot easily

## 5. Example Use Cases
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

- **Midfield denial:** Place Repeaters near objectives to force opponents to fight through hacking AROs.  
- **Counter-hack:** Allow your Killer Hacker to strike enemy Hackers hiding deep in DZ.  
- **TAG control:** Spotlight/Oblivion through Repeaters to suppress TAGs without needing LoF.  
- **Alpha-strike protection:** Layer Repeaters around DZ to punish Impersonators or Skirmishers.  
- **Supportware delivery:** Provide Assisted Fire or Enhanced Reaction to REMs anywhere on the board.

------------------------------------------------------------

# Summary
Repeaters enable powerful, flexible table control but bring inherent risk by opening two-way hacking lines. Their strength lies in their ability to project non-LoF threats and reshape battlefield movement. Understanding Repeater networks—both offensive and defensive—is essential for mastering Infinity’s infowar layer.
