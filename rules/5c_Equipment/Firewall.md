# Firewall

Quantronic Combat (Hacking)

Equipment

Some Troopers or Hacking Devices have pieces of Equipment which provide extra protection against Comms Attacks. These defenses are codified in the Firewall rule, which applies a series of MODs to hinder the Attack and enhance the target's protection.

- Any enemy that declares a Comms Attack against a Trooper benefitting from a Firewall must apply a negative MOD to his WIP Attribute, as indicated between brackets: Firewall (-3), Firewall (-6)...

- A Trooper benefiting from a Firewall also applies a MOD of +3 to their Saving Rolls (SR) against Comms Attacks.

- Troopers can only benefit from one Firewall at a time. If a Trooper can benefit from more than one Firewall, their player will decide which one to apply.

The Firewall in a Comms Attack is the equivalent of Cover in a BS Attack, it hinders the Attack and aids the target’s Saving Roll.

Although the MOD to the Attack varies (-3, -6…), the MOD to the Saving Roll is always fixed (+3), unless a specific rule indicates otherwise.

Examples of Tinbot Types TinBot: Firewall (-3) Indicates that, when the owner of the TinBot is targeted by a Comms Attack, the Enemy applies a -3 Firewall MOD.

## What Firewall Represents in Gameplay
Firewall is the Quantronic equivalent of **Cover for Hacking**:  
it imposes offensive penalties on the attacker (–3 or –6 to WIP) while giving the defender a **+3 BTS save bonus**.

## [UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]
## Typical Interactions

### Firewall stacking limitations
- Even if a Trooper is inside a Fireteam with a TinBot, protected by Fairy Dust, and being attacked through a Repeater, **only ONE Firewall** is chosen and applied.  
- A player chooses the **strongest** available (e.g., Firewall –6 instead of –3).

### Firewall and Repeater networks
- Using an **enemy** Repeater imposes Firewall –3 on the attacker.  
- This discourages aggressive hacking through hostile zones unless the odds remain favorable (e.g., high-WIP KHD using Trinity vs a low-WIP hacker).


## [UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]
## Comparative Analysis of Firewall Sources

### 1. **TinBot Firewall (–3)**  
**Pros:**  
- Cheap and common in Fireteams.  
- Entire Fireteam benefits.  
- Always on.

**Cons:**  
- Only as strong as the TinBot printed (usually –3).  
- Lost if the model carrying it becomes Isolated or Null.

### 2. **Fairy Dust Firewall (–3)**  
**Pros:**  
- Army-wide protection for a chosen troop type.  
- Excellent for HI/REM/TAG-heavy lists.  
- Stacks with TinBot availability (but only one Firewall applies).  
- Table-wide effect.

**Cons:**  
- Costs an Order and requires an EVO Hacker.  
- Cancels if that Hacker becomes Isolated or Null.  
- Only one Supportware allowed per Trooper.

### 3. **Firewall from Enemy Repeaters (–3)**  
(A negative penalty imposed **on the attacker** only.)  
**Pros:**  
- A passive defense that punishes hackers entering an enemy’s network.  
- Strong deterrent for KHDs diving through repeaters.

**Cons:**  
- Only applies when attacking **through** the repeater, not normally.

## [UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]
## Pros and Cons of Firewall Overall

### **Pros**
- Strongest universal defense against hacking.  
- Cheap (TinBots) or army-wide (Fairy Dust).  
- +3 to BTS saves is significant vs Oblivion and Total Control.  
- Forces enemy to over-invest in hacking or avoid the matchup entirely.  
- Essential for Fireteams containing vulnerable, high-value Hacker targets.

### **Cons**
- Does nothing against **non-Comms Attacks**.  
- Cannot stack: you choose only one Firewall source.  
- If your Firewall source is removed/isolated, protection instantly collapses.  
- High-WIP, Burst-boosted hacking can still push through Firewall –3.  
- Offers only +3 to BTS, not more (even if the MOD to attacker is –6).

## [UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]
## Common Interaction Questions

### **Q: Does Firewall help against Spotlight?**  
**A:** Yes—Spotlight is a Comms Attack. Enemy suffers the MOD (–3 or –6), and defender gains +3 BTS to resist if the roll succeeds.

### **Q: Does Firewall help against Jammers?**  
**A:** Yes—Jammers are Comms Attacks that require WIP rolls. Firewall applies normally.

### **Q: Does Firewall work against Guided Attacks?**  
**A:** No. Guided Attacks are BS Attacks with the Guided trait, NOT Comms Attacks.  
TinBot: Guided (–6) is a separate piece of Equipment with its own rule.

### **Q: Does Firewall protect Hackers themselves?**  
**A:** Yes—if they are inside a unit with a TinBot, under Fairy Dust, or otherwise benefiting from a Firewall source.

## [UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]
## Tactical Application Notes

### 1. For Fireteams
- Always consider including a TinBot in any Fireteam containing a Hacker or valuable HI/REM model.  
- Firewall dramatically increases the survival of Fireteam Hackers when enemy Killer Hackers attempt to alpha-strike the link.

### 2. For TAG and REM lists
- Fairy Dust is almost mandatory for armies relying on **expensive hackable units**.  
- Many TAGs with BTS 6 effectively reach BTS 9 against Comms Attacks under Firewall.

### 3. For hacking-heavy matchups
- Deploy Repeaters to force enemies to hack **through** your network, imposing Firewall –3 on their attacks.  
- Combine Forward Observers and Repeaters to create hacking traps defended by Firewalls.

### 4. For aggressive Killer Hackers
- Know when Firewall flips a statistically favorable engagement into an unfavorable one.  
- Trinity against Firewall –6 (rare but possible) becomes extremely risky.
