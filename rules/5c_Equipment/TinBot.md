# TinBot

Equipment

A TinBot is a semi-autonomous piece of Equipment that remains near its owner and provides him with some bonus or benefit.

- A TinBot grants its MODs or additional advantages as long as its owner is not in any Null State ( Unconscious, Dead, Sepsitorized, etc.) or Isolated State.

- TinBot Models and Tokens are State Tokens, so they cannot block movement or LoF, or be targeted by any Skill.

- If the owner of a TinBot enters Dead State, remove the TinBot from the game alongside its owner.

- A TinBot grants its owner with different MODs, Special Skills, or pieces of Equipment. The player will apply the one specified next to the TinBot, in the Unit Profile.

- If the owner of a TinBot is part of a Fireteam, every member of the Fireteam benefits from the TinBot.

- If the members of a Fireteam have more than one TinBot between them that grants the same advantages, they can only use one of them per declared Order or ARO. If the TinBots grant different MODs for the same advantage, only the most advantageous MOD may be used.

TinBot: Firewall (-3) Indicates that, when the owner of the TinBot is targeted by a Comms Attack, the Enemy applies a -3 Firewall MOD.

TinBot: Guided (-6) Indicates that, when the owner of the TinBot is targeted by a Guided Attack, the Enemy applies a -6 MOD.

TinBot: Discover (+3) Indicates that, when the owner of the TinBot declares the Discover Skill, they apply a +3 WIP MOD.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
TinBots in Infinity N5 are passive bonus-providers attached to a trooper.  
They never act, never block LoF, and never roll dice. Their entire gameplay impact is through MODs they grant their owner (or the Fireteam).

TinBots exist in different variants (Firewall -3, Guided -6, Discover +3), each granting a specific defensive or utility advantage.

---

## 1. Key Rule References (Summary)

- TinBot grants its effects **as long as the owner is not Null or Isolated**.
- TinBots are **State Tokens**:  
  They cannot be targeted, attacked, blocked, or interacted with directly.
- **Fireteam sharing:**  
  If the owner is in a Fireteam, **the whole Fireteam benefits**.
- **TinBot stacking limitation:**  
  If multiple TinBots in the Fireteam grant the *same type* of advantage, **only one** can be used per Order/ARO.  
  If they grant *different values of the same advantage*, only the **best** applies.
- TinBot Effect Types:  
  • Firewall (-3) — enemy applies -3 when targeting you with a Comms Attack.  
  • Guided (-6) — enemy applies -6 MOD when making a Guided Attack against you.  
  • Discover (+3) — user gains +3 WIP on Discover declarations.

## 2. Interactions With Game Systems

### 2.1. TinBots and Fireteams
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
TinBots are most impactful when the owner participates in a link:

- Fireteam-wide coverage dramatically increases value, especially Firewall (-3).  
- Because Fireteams are common hacking targets, Firewall TinBots are a near-mandatory inclusion for many sectorials.
- Discover (+3) TinBots in a Fireteam create reliable, order-efficient anti-camouflage capability.

**Interaction Notes:**
- Fireteam bonuses themselves do not change TinBot rules.  
- TinBots apply regardless of who in the Fireteam is the *target* (for Firewall/Guided) or who is the *actor* (for Discover), because *all* members benefit.

---

### 2.2. TinBots vs. Comms Attacks and Hacking
Firewall (-3) applies when the trooper is targeted by **any Comms Attack**, including:
- Hacking Programs (e.g., Oblivion, Total Control, Spotlight)
- Non-hacking Comms Attacks, if applicable

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Firewall (-3) is the most strategically meaningful TinBot effect:
- It increases resistance to enemy hacking both in Active and Reactive turn.
- It discourages attacks through Repeaters because MOD stacking gets unfavorable for the attacker.
- It synergizes strongly with Non-Hackable units (TAGs, REMs, HI under Fairy Dust) by pushing defense values even further.

**TinBot stacking with Supportware / Fairy Dust:**
- You cannot “double Firewall” your defense.  
- The *best* Firewall modifier applies between TinBot and Fairy Dust.  
- For example:  
  • TinBot Firewall (-3) + Fairy Dust Firewall (-3) = still only -3.

---

### 2.3. TinBots and Guided Missiles
Guided (-6) TinBot reduces the attacker’s WIP by 6 for Guided Attacks.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
This is extremely potent but niche:
- If your meta relies on Spotlight → Guided play, this TinBot variant can dramatically reduce missile efficiency.  
- The modifier applies even if the trooper is Prone or out of LoF, because Guided Attacks ignore LoF by rule.

---

### 2.4. TinBots and Discover Rolls
Discover (+3) increases the user’s WIP when declaring Discover.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
This works especially well when:
- The Fireteam spreads this bonus across multiple members.  
- Stacking Discover bonuses with Sensor units or MSV-equipped shooters.  
- Fighting camo-heavy armies like Ariadna, Shasvastii, or Haqq’s infiltrators.

## 3. Pros & Cons of TinBot Variants

### Firewall (-3)
**Pros:**
- Most universally useful TinBot.  
- Protects against hacking lists and repeater nets.  
- Boosts Fireteam survivability dramatically.

**Cons:**
- Worthless vs. armies with minimal hacking (warbands, low-tech lists).  
- Does not prevent non-WIP-based isolation or effects from templates.

---

### Guided (-6)
**Pros:**
- Heavily reduces viability of Guided missile play.  
- Fireteam-wide coverage makes your whole link hard to pick off remotely.

**Cons:**
- Only useful if opponents use Guided.  
- Ineffective if Guided attacks are not part of the mission or meta.

---

### Discover (+3)
**Pros:**
- Ideal for camo-heavy environments.  
- Fireteam sharing increases reliability enormously.  
- High synergy with MSV or Sensor members.

**Cons:**
- Provides no defense and no hacking protection.  
- Wasteful in metas without significant camouflage presence.

---

## 4. Comparison: Firewall vs. Guided vs. Discover TinBots

**Firewall (-3)**  
- Best general-purpose choice.  
- Provides the strongest defensive utility in the most matchups.  
- Mandatory in some hacking-heavy sectorials (Nomads, CA, O-12, Aleph matchups).

**Guided (-6)**  
- Reactive defense against a single attack mode.  
- Perfect for missions where troopers must sit on objectives in the open.

**Discover (+3)**  
- Pure offense/utility.  
- Turns a Fireteam into reliable camo-crackers.

**Which to choose?**
- Play into the mission and the known meta.  
- Firewall is almost always useful; Discover is mission-dependent; Guided is meta-dependent.

---

## 5. Tactical Notes (Unofficial)

- **Tank the hacking game:**  
  Firewall TinBots reduce the enemy’s hacking efficiency enough to cross repeater grids without immediate risk.
  
- **Control Guided missile lists:**  
  Guided (-6) TinBots force enemy missile carriers to waste Orders or hunt new targets.

- **Anti-camouflage Fireteams:**  
  Discover (+3) makes even average WIP troops extremely reliable scanners. Pair with Sensor for overwhelming detection capability.

- **Protect your TinBot carrier:**  
  If the owner becomes **Isolated or Null**, the TinBot stops working immediately.  
  Opponents may deliberately attack the TinBot carrier rather than higher-value models.

- **TinBots do not stack:**  
  Always check which TinBot gives the strongest modifier; tactically position the carrier accordingly.

---

# Overall Evaluation

TinBots are **cheap, passive, high-impact upgrades** that dramatically influence hacking duels, Guided missile defense, and camo-countering. Their value increases further in Fireteams, where a single TinBot can harden an entire group.

They are one of the strongest “invisible” force multipliers in N5 and should be selected based on:
- Expected threats (hackers, camo, missiles)
- Mission requirements (area control, objective holding)
- Fireteam composition and survivability
