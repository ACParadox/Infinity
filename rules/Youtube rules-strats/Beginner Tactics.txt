Slicing The Pie
First tactic is slicing the pie and  learning to peek. Why well it comes  
up alot and it's a great example of a  rules understanding and applying that. 
So what is slicing the pie and peaking? Well in  short it's moving from being in total cover to  
just coming out enough to see exactly what you  want you to see and nothing more and taking a  
shot. With a cooperative opponent, which should  be most of the time, this means you can usually  
find the right angle to get what you want. The tactic here is not you gotta slice every  
pie and peek every corner but more understanding  when you gotta do it and why you gotta do it.  
So when do you want to do it. Simply when  you don't want to take 2 fights at once,  
that's a trick for when you start saying “yeah  this will work”. You’ll know when this as you  
would have two aro pieces looking at one spot  where you need to remove them one by one. Since  
LOF is open information you can find a line or  angle where you can only take one fight unless  
the models are vertically stacked. Now obviously  you don’t just slide out constantly visible  
and take each fight. If you're worried about  running out of orders, or hidden ARO pieces you  
may want to slide out, then slide in to prevent  retaliation in the reactive turn. Avoiding those  
shots you didn’t need to take is always nice. I mentioned teaching rules and well what rules  
do they teach. It's a simple but important one.  Everything in infinity happens all at once and  
when you declare move + action that action, be it  a hack, or importantly a BS attack that happens  
at any single point in your movement. Now  all the burst needs to happen at that one  
point but it means you move back and forth and  take the shot inbetween. This is also useful  
to know if you're making an attack in the open,  which is not always advisable, as you can pick  
the best spot in your movement to maybe get a  better range band. However, and this is key,  
it goes both ways so your opponent can also pick  at what point they shoot you so they can't get a  
pretty advantageous shot. These two points don't  need to be the same point so keep this in mind. 
So as you can see this can be an important  one to know as you play the game. It’s one  
you’re very likely to learn from just playing  the game but know the rules and getting the  
reps in will help you understand the  finer points of moving and shooting. 
Movement
From one movement tip to the next but this  one is far simpler and I've talked about  
it before and it's worth knowing. So it's  doing fun movement tracks like dodge move,  
cautious move, and climbing and jumping  and how to get the most out of them. 
So what do I mean by all this? Well let's start  from the first one. Dodge movement, what’s special  
about that, well this is where knowing the OES  comes in. So you declare dodge at the given time,  
then you roll, contesting or normal roll, and  then you move/apply the effects of the roll in  
the resolution phase. The main benefit of this  is that it does not provoke any aros since there  
is no space in which your opponent can declare  one. This can set up nice long skill combos like  
berserk, triangulated fire or doing some kind of  stage dive hacking attempt which is really nice. 
Second one is cautious move, which has a nice  wiki page to explain it but the long and short  
of it is if you start out of LOF, you can long  skill move your first move value and you provoke  
no AROs assuming you end outside the line  of fire. This can be used to cross gaps,  
and combined with smoke and stealth can be a  really nice way to get around tricky situations  
that maybe only camo or guns would have resolved. Next up is knowing that jumping and climbing while  
being a long skill without super jump and  climbing + can still be useful. Sometimes  
you just have to get up a container and the  order cost is secondary. Fortunately it’s  
not as inefficient as before as now you get your  silhouette for free since you can measure from any  
point in your silhouette so going up something  1.5 times your size should be a lot easier now. 
So just remember there is more to movement  than running between cover and shooting on  
the way. Think about the table and if there  is a better angle you can take and if you can  
get there. If that answer is yes, or yes  with very few buts then might be consider  
doing that rather than the direct approach. Forking is the next tactic and I'm looking  
Forking
forward to the auto generated subtitles on this  one. So what is forking, forking is when you  
create only bad decisions for your opponent when  making an active turn play. You may have heard  
of a similar phrase called create dilemmas not  problems. A problem can be solved, a dilemma can  
not. Your opponent is not going to like getting  forked, ha, since there is not an easy answer. 
I'll give an example to put this explanation  to the end. Let’s say I have a warband with  
some CC skills, an SMG and a chain rifle, like  a varangian, and I charge it into b2b contact  
in that first move. Now in ARO my opponent is  present with 3 aro options. Shoot me as I run  
into melee to contest the SMG burst or CC attack,  dodge to avoid my templates or CC me back for when  
I attempt to melee him or shoot him.whatever they  choose is not advantageous unless they are pretty  
stack as my opponent could declare dodge, that  would be the safest but usually is not a great  
role as its burst 1 on usually no more than 13s,  but then i would just CC them on 23, maybe i  
have MA and then their dodge is worse or i shot  them with an SMG so that's 3 dice vs 1 which is  
not great odds either. My opponent could shoot me  which is effective but I could contest that with  
3 dice vs 1 or use CC which is very likely to win  and even impose negative mods from MA or CC-3. I  
could if I'm feeling spicy just template them and  eat the potential wound on my end cause he could  
wiff the shot or I make the save, I have 2 chances  to succeed he has the arm save to not fail and eat  
a wound. I would also now be in melee which is  where I want to be. This is also true with melee,  
i can just eat the wound and template him. What I've done here is taken a unit that has  
multiple tools and found the best situation to  put them in. I’ve offered my opponent nothing  
great but still they have choices, which is also  a positive in terms of game designs. This is the  
philosophy behind forking. Warbands, but anything  with a shotgun or a template and burst weapon can  
do it fairly well. Some of the best forkers in the  game are varangians, half of SP, the seraph tag,  
and the Stigmata in nomads with its hacking  rather than BS attacks. It's a basic tactic  
that you should know that sometimes your best  play is to be aggressive and to realise that  
in the active turn you need to be the one making  decisions to win. If your opponent has to make a  
decision it's the ones you want them to make. Keep  this in mind with skirmishers and other pieces in  
camo which can offer similar choices with delay  dodge and discover. You can really react depending  
on what they do to your benefit. Keep in mind your  opponents ARO options and you can slowly use that  
to turn it in your favour. So always keep forking  in mind, and the dirty version out of your mind. 
Hidden Deployment
Effective use of HD is something that’s really  key in getting the most out of those units and  
some of the most feared units in the game are  HD users. Cutters, notificers, anthermatics and  
nadhirs are all feared for a reason. Why well it’s  because while in HD they’re basically immune to  
everything except BTV and being sensored. Even  then its largely up to you to reveal them which  
can make devastating AROs, lethal attack runs or  sneak skirmisher actions. These three are pretty  
self explanatory but they have a good amount of  though that goes into them, let's start with AROs. 
So HD aros are always fun, or not very fun if you  get gated by them. So how do you get the most out  
of your HD AROs? Well step one uses a template  weapon, ML noticifer, flammensphere Nadhir and  
swiss miss are the famous ones that just work  because you can catch things that are bunched  
up unexpectedly. Which can and will be devastating  as there are only a few fireteams that won't mind  
dodging this such as tuts but they don't want to  risk it. Templates aren’t required but it helps. 
So a good gun is nice but placement is the real  key thing to know. So what is a good placement?  
Well that's a video in of itself but there  are 2 big ones. Watching a good lane that is  
not already covered. This is more obvious and  a good player may be able to out your pieces  
and approach more cautiously as a result could  nullify the effect. HD hacker can also cause  
your opponent to pause for thought as every  repeater is just that little bit more spicier. 
The other place to put them is along the same line  as another ARO piece so that when something nasty
