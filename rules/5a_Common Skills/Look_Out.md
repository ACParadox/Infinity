# Look Out

Common Skills
 ARO
No Roll 

This Skill allows the user to warn all allies of imminent danger so that they can turn to face new threats.

Players must check the Requirements of this Skill when declaring it.

- The user must have LoF to an enemy Trooper or Marker who declares or performs an Order.

- Allows all other Troopers of the same player to modify their LoF without displacing, if the player so chooses. This will be considered as if the Troopers modifying their LoF had declared an ARO.

- This modification to the LoF is made through a Normal or Face to Face Dodge (PH-3) Roll. This will be a Normal Roll, unless the alerted Trooper has been targeted by an Attack that can be evaded with a Face to Face Dodge Roll.

- The alerted Troopers may perform the Dodge (PH-3) Roll as long as they had not declared any other previous ARO.

- The Reactive Player must indicate which Troopers will modify their LoF after declaring their Look Out! ARO.

- All the other Troopers' Dodge (PH-3) Rolls will be made in the same Order as the Trooper using the Look Out! ARO.

During his Reactive Turn, Trooper A gains LoF to an enemy that has declared Move as the first Basic Short Skill of the Order, which allows him to approach Trooper B. As Trooper B is looking the other way, the enemy is outside his Line of Fire and Zone of Control.

Since the enemy is too far from Trooper A for him to shoot effectively, Trooper A decides to declare the Look Out! ARO, and the Reactive Player indicates that Trooper B will make a Dodge (PH-3) Roll as their ARO because he is alerted. The enemy declares a BS Attack against Trooper B.

Thanks to Look Out!, Trooper B may make his Dodge (PH-3) Roll. If the Trooper wins the Face to Face Roll, in addition to avoiding the Attack, he may turn around to face the enemy, if he wishes so. In that case, in the next Order that the enemy declares, Trooper B will already have LoF to react by declaring, for example, BS Attack against his enemy.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Look Out! is a Reactive-only Common Skill that is frequently overlooked or misapplied. It represents a Trooper warning nearby allies of an imminent threat, allowing them to react even though they would normally be unable to declare an ARO. The key point many players miss is that Look Out! does not grant a free or additional ARO; it merely enables eligible Troopers to declare one they would otherwise be denied.

Look Out! can only be declared as an ARO by a Trooper that is already able to react to the Order being spent. If the warning Trooper does not have a valid ARO itself, it cannot use Look Out! to “bootstrap” reactions for others. This prevents chain reactions and keeps the skill firmly anchored to standard ARO rules.

A common misunderstanding is assuming Look Out! bypasses Line of Fire requirements entirely. In reality, it only allows Troopers within Zone of Control of the Look Out! user to react to an enemy that is inside the Look Out! user’s Line of Fire. The warned Troopers still do not gain Line of Fire; they are simply allowed to declare AROs that do not require LoF, such as Dodge or Reset.

Look Out! interacts tightly with the Order Expenditure Sequence. The Look Out! ARO is declared at the normal ARO declaration step, and the benefiting Troopers must immediately declare their AROs at that same timing point. You cannot wait to see how the Active Trooper completes their Order before deciding whether the warning “matters.”

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Troopers benefiting from Look Out! must still meet all the requirements of the ARO they declare. For example, Dodge still requires the enemy to be within Zone of Control or affecting the Trooper in some way. Look Out! does not waive distance, state, or eligibility requirements; it only unlocks the opportunity to react.

One easily missed limitation is that Look Out! does not allow attacks. Since most attacks require Line of Fire, and Look Out! does not provide it, the practical ARO options are usually Dodge or Reset. This makes Look Out! primarily a defensive and positional tool rather than a direct offensive enabler.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
From a tactical standpoint, Look Out! shines when protecting clustered or mission-critical pieces. A single sentry with good Lines of Fire can effectively extend a “warning bubble” to nearby specialists, Lieutenants, or fragile units, allowing them to Dodge out of danger instead of being caught helpless.

Look Out! synergizes strongly with tight formations, Fireteam-adjacent positioning, or objective rooms where multiple Troopers operate within mutual Zone of Control. Even outside formal Fireteams, this skill rewards disciplined spacing and overlapping coverage rather than wide dispersal.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
On the Active Player’s side, remembering Look Out! is crucial when engaging defensive nests. Declaring a seemingly safe attack against a visible guard may unexpectedly provoke multiple Dodges from nearby Troopers, increasing Order expenditure and reducing attack efficiency. Clearing or isolating the Look Out! Trooper first is often the correct solution.

In summary, Look Out! is not flashy, but it is a powerful force multiplier for defense. Its value lies in denying the Active Player easy, uncontested attacks and forcing them to respect positioning, Zones of Control, and layered reactions even when only one enemy model appears to have Line of Fire.
