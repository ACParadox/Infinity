# Dodge

Common Skills
SHORT SKILL / ARO
Movement 

This Common Skill allows the user to move and to attempt to evade an Attack against them. It also gives the user the opportunity to turn around to face an enemy who acts outside their LoF.

Troopers can only Dodge if at least one of these is true:

- They are the Active Trooper.

- In the Reactive Turn, if they are allowed to declare an ARO.

- Allows the user to make a Face to Face Roll to evade all enemy Attacks during an Order or ARO, regardless of the Burst (B) value (for example, Dodging every strike in Close Combat, or shots from several opponents).

- This Face to Face Roll pits the user's PH Attribute against whichever Attribute the attacker uses (BS, CC, PH or WIP).

- If the user is not making a Face to Face Roll (for example if they have not been Attacked or have been affected by a Direct Template Weapon ), they will instead make a Normal PH Roll.

- Dodge does not allow the user to evade Comms Attacks, but the Reset Common Skill does.

- A successful Normal or Face to Face Dodge Roll allows the user to move up to 2 inches. This movement: Is measured, declared, and the Trooper moves, during the Effects step of the Order Expenditure Sequence. If both players have Troopers that successfully Dodged, the Active Player will move their Troopers first, then the Reactive Player will move theirs. Does not generate AROs or trigger Deployable Weapons or Equipment.

- Dodge follows the General Movement Rules as well as the Moving and Measuring sidebar, both of which are explained in the Movement Module.

- Dodge allows the user to enter Engaged State with an enemy, as long as the movement is enough to reach Silhouette contact with that enemy.

- A successful Dodge Roll, whether it is a Normal or Face to Face, allows the user to cancel their Engaged State and IMM-A State, applying any State-specific MODs.

- Is measured, declared, and the Trooper moves, during the Effects step of the Order Expenditure Sequence. If both players have Troopers that successfully Dodged, the Active Player will move their Troopers first, then the Reactive Player will move theirs.

- Does not generate AROs or trigger Deployable Weapons or Equipment.

A -3 PH MOD must be applied to the Dodge Roll in the following circumstances. Even if several of these circumstances apply, only one -3 MOD is applied.

- In ARO, if the Active Trooper is inside ZoC and outside LoF.

- If Dodging a Template Weapon without LoF to the attacker.

- If Dodging the Template of a Deployable Weapon.

Modifiers Explained Examples

- Dodge (-3) means that, when the user declares Dodge, the opponents in any Face to Face Rolls suffer a -3 MOD.

## Dodge - Examples

Step 1:

The Active Trooper declares Move as the first Basic Short Skill of their Order.

The enemy declares Dodge as their ARO, attempting to reach close combat, to halt the Active Trooper's advance.

The Active Trooper declares BS Attack as the second Skill of their Order. The Active Trooper's Success Value will be 15 (12 + 3):

- BS = 12.

- +3 for Range.

The Reactive Trooper's Success Value will be 10:

- PH = 10

Step 2:

The corresponding Face to Face Roll is performed.

The Reactive Trooper wins the Face to Face Roll and dodges the Active Trooper's shots. If the player chooses to, they may now move the Reactive Trooper up to 2 inches in any direction.

Step 3:

During step 5.1 of the Order Expenditure Sequence, the Reactive Trooper checks how far he can get with his 2 inches of movement. After checking that the Active Trooper is within that distance, he decides to move into contact, and ends up in Engaged State.

During his Active Turn, the Trooper declares Move as the first Basic Short Skill of their Order.

The Reactive Trooper is probably within a Mine 's Trigger Area. If he declares an ARO, the Mine will detonate. Since the alternative to not declaring an ARO is to suffer three unopposed shots from the enemy, the Reactive Trooper declares Dodge as their ARO. The Active Trooper declares BS Attack as the second Skill of their Order.

Declaring the Dodge ARO gives the Reactive Trooper a Face to Face Roll against the 3 enemy shots, and also an attempt to avoid being hit by the Mine.

The Reactive Trooper's Success Value will be 10:

- PH = 10

The Active Trooper's Success Value will be 15:

- BS = 12

- +3 for Range

Result:

The Reactive Trooper manages to avoid the Active Trooper's shots by winning the Face to Face Roll with his 8.

However, the Mine hits the Reactive Trooper. The Mine is a Deployable Weapon, and it imposes a -3 MOD to Dodge. Therefore, the Reactive Trooper would only avoid the mine if his die's result was 7 or less. By failing to Dodge the Mine's impact, the Reactive Trooper will also be unable to move up to 2 inches, even though he succeeded against the Active Trooper's Attacks.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Core Function and Common Misconceptions**

Dodge is not just a “defensive roll”; it is one of the most flexible and abusable Common Skills in Infinity N5. It can oppose BS Attacks, and it may also grant movement even when no attack is cancelled.

A very common mistake is assuming Dodge is only reactive. Dodge can be declared in the Active Turn as the second Short Skill, enabling repositioning, engagement control, and baiting AROs.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Dodge vs Face to Face and Normal Rolls**

Dodge becomes a Face to Face Roll only when the Trooper’s action directly affects the attacker’s action against that same Trooper.

Key point often missed:
A successful Dodge only cancels Attacks against the Dodging Trooper, not against other targets of the same Order.

This is critical when:
- Facing Split Burst attacks
- Being one of several targets of a BS Attack
- Being affected by coordinated or multi-target effects

Your Dodge success is personal, not global.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Dodge vs Template Weapons**

Dodge is the primary defense against Templates, but it behaves differently than against direct attacks.

Important interaction:
If a Trooper is affected by both a Template Weapon and a non-Template Attack in the same Order, failing the Dodge against the Template cancels the movement portion of Dodge, even if the Trooper succeeded in Face to Face against the non-Template Attack.

This means:
- Mixed AROs (gun + template) are extremely strong against Dodging units
- Dodging “through” Templates is unreliable unless PH is very high


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Dodge Movement Timing and Positioning**

Dodge movement (up to 2”) is resolved during the Resolution step, after rolls, and follows normal movement rules.

Tricky but legal uses:
- Breaking Silhouette contact to cancel Engaged State
- Sliding into or out of Cover
- Entering Silhouette contact to force Close Combat in the opponent’s Active Turn
- Repositioning without provoking new AROs (movement is not declared, it is resolved)

You cannot:
- Move through impassable terrain
- End in an illegal position
- Use Dodge movement to retroactively invalidate LoF that was valid when Skills were declared


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Dodge in Close Combat and Engaged State**

In Engaged State, Dodge is often the safest declaration.

Key interactions:
- A successful Dodge can cancel Engaged State if the Trooper can move out of Silhouette contact
- If no valid landing position exists, the Trooper remains Engaged even if the Dodge Roll succeeds
- Dodge does not impose CC MODs like CC Attack does, making it preferable for low-CC units

This makes Dodge the primary escape tool from melee for non-specialist fighters.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Dodge vs States**

Dodge has special importance for clearing or mitigating States:

- Immobilized-A: Dodge is the only allowed declaration and may cancel the State
- Targeted: Dodge does not cancel Targeted (Reset is required)
- Engaged: Dodge may cancel Engaged if movement is possible
- Stunned: Dodge remains fully usable
- Isolated: Dodge remains usable, unlike Reset which becomes heavily penalized

Players often overvalue Reset and undervalue Dodge when under multiple States.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Dodge and MOD Application Pitfalls**

Negative MODs imposed by Dodge apply only in Face to Face Rolls.

This means:
- If the enemy Attack becomes a Normal Roll, Dodge does not impose its negative MOD
- Automatic Skills still apply regardless
- Some equipment or skills grant Dodge MODs that only apply against specific attack types or origins (LoF/ZoC)

Always check whether the roll is actually Face to Face before applying Dodge penalties to the attacker.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Dodging multiple mines**
Each Mine is its own independent attack.
When you Move + Dodge and trigger two Mines, both Mines detonate.
You roll one Dodge, but that roll is checked separately against each Mine.
Each Mine applies –3 PH to your Dodge.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Strategic and Tactical Uses**

Defensive uses:
- Surviving high-Burst AROs where returning fire is unlikely to win
- Escaping Close Combat with superior enemy fighters
- Negating speculative or indirect attacks

Offensive and positional uses:
- Dodging into base contact to force CC in the Reactive Turn
- Dodging into Cover while in the Active Turn
- Baiting AROs by declaring Move, then Dodging instead of attacking

Advanced play:
High-PH units effectively “spend Orders to reposition” using Move + Dodge with relatively low risk, especially when enemy AROs are weak or template-dependent.
This is especially true for units with Dodge (+2 inch), where they might move faster doing a Move + Dodge than a Move + Move.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT] Summary**

Dodge is not a passive survival tool — it is a positional, tempo-controlling skill.

Mastery of Dodge means:
- Knowing when NOT to shoot back
- Exploiting Dodge movement for board control
- Understanding exactly which attacks your success actually cancels

Players who treat Dodge as a last resort miss one of Infinity N5’s most powerful mechanics.
