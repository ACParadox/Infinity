# BS Attack

Combat Module

Common Skills
SHORT SKILL /ARO
Attack. 

Common Skill employed to attack in ranged combat using a BS Weapon or another suitable weapon or piece of Equipment.

- Be using a BS Weapon, or a Skill or piece of Equipment capable of making a BS Attack.

- Be able to draw Line of Fire ( LoF ) from the position of your Attack to the position of the target, unless the BS Weapon, Skill or piece of Equipment used doesn't require LoF.

- The user employs his BS Attribute (or Attribute specified by the weapon) to fire upon one or more enemies.

- When declaring a BS Attack, the user must choose which of his BS Weapons, Special Skills or pieces of Equipment to use, from those available in his Unit Profile.

- If the attacker has more than one target and a BS Weapon, Special Skill or piece of Equipment with a Burst value higher than 1, they must distribute their attacks as part of the BS Attack declaration.

- All shots must be declared from the same point.

- If the chosen weapon has more than one Type of Ammunition available, you must choose one for the entire Burst, as part of the BS Attack declaration.

### Combat - BS Attack (Guided)

- BS Attacks (Guided) must be carried out with the weapon's Blast Mode if it has one, or a Mode with the Impact Template (Circular) Trait. If the weapon does not have a Mode with the Impact Template (Circular) Trait, the player may choose any of the weapon's Modes but they will always apply the Circular Template Trait, placing a Circular Template centered on the target.

- BS Attacks (Guided) must be carried out with the weapon's Blast Mode if it has one, or a Mode with the Impact Template (Circular) Trait. If the weapon does not have a Mode with the Impact Template (Circular) Trait, the player may choose any of the weapon's Modes and apply the Trait to that Mode.

- A Trooper can avoid a BS Attack (Guided) with a successful Dodge or Reset Face to Face Roll. If a Reset Roll is successful, in addition to avoiding the Attack, the Trooper's Targeted State is cancelled.

- Restriction: Unless the Skill itself indicates otherwise, the B will always be 1 regardless of the actual B value of the Weapon or any MODs applied to the B.

- Restriction: The distance to the target cannot exceed the weapon's maximum Range, applying MODs as usual.

## BS Attack Modifiers Explained Examples

- BS Attack (-3) means that, when the user declares BS Attack, a -3 MOD applies to the opponent in any Face to Face Rolls. BS Attack (-X) has no effect against Troopers with the Warhorse Special Skill.

- BS Attack (SR-1) means that, when the user declares BS Attack, their targets apply a -1 MOD to their Saving Rolls against the user's hits.

- BS Attack (Shock) means that the user adds Shock Ammunition to all their BS Attacks.

- BS Attack (Continuous Damage) means that the user applies the Continuous Damage Trait to all their BS Attacks.

Guts Roll When Troopers survive a successful Attack, it means their body armor took the brunt of the impact, keeping them from serious injury. This triggers a primal fear of death, and survival instincts kick in. The Troopers' survival instincts can force them to recoil from danger and dive for cover.

Alert Troopers are aware of what is happening around them and are also connected to their comrades, so attacks do not go unnoticed.

The target must apply a -3 MOD to Dodge if they have no LoF towards the Attacker.

The Targeted State gives the target a -3 MOD to Reset Rolls, also any BS Attack against them gets a +3 MOD.

Since the Skills used in an Order are simultaneous, a BS Attack can be declared at any point during the route followed by the Move Skill. If the Burst is divided among several targets, all shots must be made from a single point. Usually, the attack is made from the position that offers the biggest advantage: a clear LoF, targets out of [[Cover], optimal range, etc.

To determine the Range MOD when performing a BS Attack (Guided), measure the distance from the Attacker to the target in a straight line. Any other possible trajectories of this attack are not taken into account.

The Missile Launcher Remote declares a BS Attack (Guided) against a Trooper with the Mimetism (-6) Special Skill, who is in Targeted State.

The following MODs apply to the Remote's BS Attack (Guided) Roll:

- +3 for Range.

- +3 for the target's Targeted State.

As the Remote is using BS Attack (Guided), they do not have to apply the target's Mimetism (-6) MOD, nor any MODs for Cover. In addition, of the two firing modes available to the Missile Launcher, the Attack must be made with Blast Mode (EXP ammunition).

The target of the Attack has two ARO options:

- Dodge with a -3 MOD due to Dodging a Template Weapon without LoF to the Attacker.

- Reset with a -3 MOD due to being in Targeted State.

Therefore, the REM makes a Face-to-Face BS Attack Roll (BS +6) against the target's Dodge Roll (PH -3) or Reset Roll (WIP -3) Roll.

## BS Attack Into a Close Combat

If a BS Attack is declared against an enemy Trooper that is engaged in CC Combat a -6 MOD must be applied for each Allied Trooper engaged in the CC (in addition to any MODs for Range, Cover, Mimetism... for the target).

Every failed BS Attack Roll will hit the Allied Trooper, forcing them to perform a Saving Roll. If there are several Allied Troopers locked in that Close Combat, then the Trooper’s player chooses which one of them receives each hit.

## FAQs

Q: Do Skills that let you perform a 'BS Attack Roll' (for example Intuitive Attack and Speculative Attack), or have the BS Attack Label, count as a BS Attack for MODs? For example, if the Trooper has 'BS Attack (SR-1)', 'BS Attack (+1 SD), or 'BS Attack (AP)'.

A: Yes. Note that +1 SD MODs don't apply to Long Skills.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A frequent mistake is treating BS Attack as a single “shot” rather than a declaration that creates one or more attack rolls. Each die rolled is a separate attack instance, but all are declared at once, from a single point, using a single weapon and ammo choice. 
This matters greatly when dividing Burst across multiple targets or resolving partial invalidation of dice.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
BS Attack always checks its Requirements at Resolution, not at declaration. This means players may legally declare a BS Attack even if Line of Fire, range, or visibility is uncertain at that moment. If the Requirements are not met during Resolution, the Skill becomes Idle. This is particularly important when interacting with movement, corner-peeking, or Zero Visibility Zones.

Because of this timing, declaring BS Attack can be used as a “threat declaration” to force AROs. Even if the attack later becomes Idle, the Reactive player may already have committed to a Dodge, Reset, or BS Attack ARO, which can be tactically significant.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The point of origin for a BS Attack must be declared explicitly. When combined with Move, this allows the attacker to choose whether range bands, cover, and visibility are calculated from the start, end, or any intermediate point of movement. This choice is locked in and cannot be changed after AROs are declared.

This leads to a common pitfall: moving into a better range band but accidentally exposing oneself to additional AROs at the chosen firing point. The declared firing position defines who can react with BS Attack, not the final position of the model.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
When splitting Burst between multiple targets, each target generates its own independent Roll (Face to Face or Normal). Success or failure against one target has no effect on the others. This is often missed when players assume a single high roll “wins the whole exchange.”

Strategically, splitting Burst is usually inferior unless one or more targets cannot oppose the attack. High Burst concentrated into a single Face to Face Roll is one of the strongest advantages of the Active Turn.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
In ARO, BS Attack is always Burst 1 unless a specific rule states otherwise. This is true even if the reactive model has a weapon with a higher printed Burst. Players often forget that bonuses to Burst usually only apply in the Active Turn.

This asymmetry is intentional and defines Infinity’s lethality curve: the Active player presses advantages with Burst, while the Reactive player relies on positioning, MOD stacking, and threat density rather than raw dice volume.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cover applies two distinct effects during a BS Attack: a negative MOD to the attacker’s roll and a positive MOD to the target’s Saving Roll. These are separate and cumulative. Ignoring one but not the other is a common rules error.

From a tactical standpoint, forcing enemies out of Cover is often more valuable than improving your own BS. Even high-BS attackers lose efficiency rapidly when shooting into Cover, especially against ARM-heavy targets.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
BS Attack interacts strongly with Face to Face mechanics. Any opposing Skill that can affect the outcome of the attack (such as Dodge or another BS Attack) converts the roll into a Face to Face Roll. Skills that do not directly oppose the attack result in Normal Rolls.

This distinction enables deliberate targeting choices: attacking an enemy already engaged in another Face to Face Roll, or one that cannot meaningfully oppose, can dramatically increase lethality without increasing risk.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Criticals in BS Attack are not just “better hits”; they fundamentally alter probability. A single Critical automatically cancels all non-critical opposing successes in a Face to Face Roll, regardless of value.

High Burst does not merely increase hit chances—it increases Critical density. This is why even modest weapons become deadly when supported by Fireteams/Special Dice bonus.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Direct Template Weapons bypass the Roll step entirely. They still count as BS Attacks for interaction purposes (ARO generation, Skill interactions), which is frequently overlooked.

This makes templates a powerful counter to high-MOD defensive stacks. When accuracy becomes unreliable, removing the Roll entirely is often the most efficient solution.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
BS Attack can be declared against Markers using Intuitive Attack or similar rules, but otherwise cannot affect Camouflage or similar states without first resolving their specific interactions. Forgetting this restriction is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

Tactically, this encourages layered defenses: Marker States protect against BS Attack unless the attacker invests Orders, positioning, or specific Skills to bypass that protection.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Experienced players treat BS Attack as a tool to shape the battlefield, not just remove models: forcing Dodges, breaking Suppressive Fire, draining Orders, and controlling movement lanes are often more important than immediate kills.
