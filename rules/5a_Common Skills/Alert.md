# Alert

Common Skills

AUTOMATIC SKILL
Optional, No Roll. 

Troopers are aware of what is happening around them and are also connected to their comrades, so attacks do not go unnoticed.

- The Trooper has not have been activated by an Order or ARO in the same Order.

- The Trooper, or an Allied Trooper within their Zone of Control, has been the target of an Attack.

- At the end of the Order, during its Conclusion, and after performing any Saving Rolls, Troopers that meet the Requirements may turn on the spot, without changing position, to modify their LoF angle as their player chooses.

- This movement is automatic, it does not require a Roll and does not generate AROs, since it happens during the Conclusion Step of the Order.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Alert is frequently misunderstood as a “free ARO generator”. It is not. Alert does not grant an ARO against the current Order, nor does it retroactively allow reactions to the activating enemy Trooper. Instead, it is a communication tool that prepares nearby allies for future Orders. Think of it as changing facing and awareness, not immediate retaliation.

A common mistake is assuming Alert allows Troopers to react even if they were previously ineligible for ARO. Alert does not bypass Line of Fire, Zone of Control, or ARO timing rules. It only affects the facing of allied Troopers and their readiness for subsequent enemy activations.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Alert is especially easy to miss in its timing. It is declared as an ARO, meaning it consumes the reacting Trooper’s single ARO for that Order. Choosing Alert means giving up shooting, dodging, resetting, or any other ARO option during that Order.

Facing changes caused by Alert are immediate and resolved during the Order resolution, but their practical impact is felt on later Orders. This often leads to players overvaluing Alert when the enemy Trooper is about to complete their run and undervaluing it when defending against multiple coordinated attacks.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Alert interacts subtly with Stealth. If a Stealthy Trooper moves without generating AROs, Alert cannot be declared unless some other condition allowed the ARO in the first place. Alert does not “break” Stealth by itself, because you must already have a valid ARO to declare it.

Alert does not Discover, does not reveal Markers, and does not allow allies to delay or retroactively declare AROs. If a Camouflage Marker moves past your lines, Alert can help set up facing to respond later, but it will not expose or stop that Marker during the current Order.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A frequently missed interaction is with Fireteams. Alert does not grant Fireteam bonuses, does not allow coordinated facing changes beyond the defined area, and does not replace proper Fireteam positioning. However, using Alert with a backline Fireteam member can prevent the entire link from being caught facing the wrong direction on the next Order.

Alert is one of the few defensive tools available to Troopers who lack strong ARO weapons or mods. Line infantry, cheerleaders, and backfield specialists can use Alert to contribute defensively without risking low-odds Face to Face Rolls.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategically, Alert shines against attack pieces that rely on chaining multiple Orders through your backfield. Spending one Order to force multiple enemy Troopers to turn and cover angles can dramatically increase the Order cost of an aggressive run.

Alert is weak against single, decisive alpha strikes. If the enemy intends to kill or disable key pieces in the current Order, Alert is almost always inferior to Dodge or a direct ARO. Its value increases the longer the enemy Trooper is expected to remain active in the area.

A useful tactical pattern is to Alert with a disposable or low-value Trooper to protect a high-value piece that is currently facing away. This trades one Trooper’s ARO for improved survivability of a more important model on subsequent Orders.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Turning models to face an approaching threat often forces the attacker to slow down, reposition, or split Burst inefficiently.

From a design perspective, Alert reinforces Infinity’s emphasis on information warfare and positioning. It rewards players who think ahead one Order rather than focusing exclusively on immediate dice outcomes.
