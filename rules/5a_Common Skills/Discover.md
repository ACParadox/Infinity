# Discover

Common Skills
BASIC SHORT SKILL / ARO

This Skill allows the user to locate enemies hidden in the form of a Marker, forcing them to reveal the Trooper, Weapon, or Equipment that the Marker represents

- The user must be able to draw LoF to the target.

- This Skill cannot be used against the same Target twice in the same Order.

- If the user passes a Normal WIP Roll, applying the same MODs as in a BS Attack ( Cover, Range, Mimetism...), the target Marker is removed, and must be replaced with the actual Trooper Model, or Weapon or Equipment Token.

- A Trooper that fails a WIP Roll to Discover a Marker cannot attempt to Discover the same Marker until the next Player Turn.

- The Trooper may still attempt to Discover a different Marker that Player Turn, and its player may still attempt to Discover the first Marker using a different Trooper.

- Note that a Trooper that has been revealed, and re-entered Camouflaged State or Impersonation-1 or 2 State again, does not count as the same Marker.

- A Trooper that fails the WIP Roll attempting to Discover a Marker, may attempt to Discover another different Marker.

- The Discover Skill has its own Range MODs, as if it were a BS Weapon

Discover is a Basic Short Skill, and it may therefore be combined with any other Basic Short Skill or Short Skill (see Trooper Activation ).

A common combination in an Order is Discover + Attack. This tactical maneuver allows you to declare an Attack against a Camouflaged enemy, which cannot normally be done.

- If the Camouflaged target declares an ARO, revealing themselves, there is no need to perform the Discover Roll before resolving the Attack.

- If the Camouflaged target does not declare an ARO, the Trooper will need to pass the Discover Roll before resolving any Attack against that target.

- Remember that the Active Trooper may Discover one target and Attack a different target if they choose to do so.

## FAQs

Q: Can the BS Attack (+1 SD) and +1 BS Fireteam Bonuses be applied to Discover?

A: No.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A frequent misunderstanding is assuming Discover is only relevant against Camouflage. In reality, it interacts with multiple Marker States (Camouflaged, Holoechoes, Decoys, etc.). Any time a Trooper is represented by a Marker whose identity matters, Discover is the primary, rules-supported way to convert uncertainty into actionable information.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Discover is often underestimated because it does not directly cause damage. However, it directly enables lethal actions. A successful Discover allows subsequent Attacks to bypass Marker protections, prevents Surprise Attack, and removes negative MOD stacking that Marker States typically provide. Treat Discover as a “setup attack” rather than a wasted Order.

One easily missed interaction is that Discover can be declared as an ARO. This makes careless Marker movement extremely dangerous. Moving a Camouflage Marker through open LoF can invite multiple Discover AROs, and because AROs are simultaneous, the Active Player cannot “wait to see” if the Marker is revealed before resolving other AROs.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A common mistake is attempting to mix Discover and BS Attack sequencing across different Troopers in the same Order. You cannot Discover with one Trooper and then immediately shoot with another Trooper in reaction to the same movement, hoping the Discover succeeds first. All AROs resolve simultaneously, and the game explicitly disallows conditional declarations based on Discover success.

Discover versus Marker-heavy defenses is often an Order-efficiency puzzle. If you can stack multiple Discover attempts in a single Order (multiple AROs, or coordinated positioning), you dramatically increase pressure on the Marker player. Even failed Discover rolls can be successful strategically if they force cautious repositioning or delayed advances.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Discover interacts strongly with Zone of Control mechanics. Marker States often rely on avoiding Line of Fire, but Discover does not inherently require an attack roll that causes damage, making it a low-risk declaration when you suspect hidden threats near objectives or chokepoints. Using Discover defensively can prevent nasty short-range surprises later in the turn.

One subtle interaction is that Discover removes not only the Marker but also the ambiguity of Silhouette, facing, and equipment. This matters immediately for Line of Fire calculations, future ARO eligibility, and template placement. Revealing a Trooper can suddenly open LoF lanes your opponent thought were safe.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Against Holoecho or Decoy-style effects, Discover is less about removal and more about validation. Forcing your opponent to confirm which elements are real can collapse layered defenses. Even if the real Trooper remains hidden, stripping away false markers simplifies the tactical situation dramatically.

From a tactical standpoint, high-WIP Troopers are natural Discover specialists, but positioning matters more than raw stats. A mediocre WIP Trooper with clean LoF and good angles can outperform an elite specialist stuck at poor visibility or awkward arcs.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Discover pairs extremely well with Intuitive Attack, Sensor, and template-based area control. Even when those tools are not immediately available, Discover can be used to force the opponent into revealing a threat early, allowing the rest of your Order pool to be spent efficiently elsewhere.

Defensively, Marker users should assume that Discover attempts are inevitable. Smart play involves layering positions so that a discovered Trooper is still in Cover, still protected by overlapping AROs, or still able to trade favorably if immediately attacked. Discover is not failure — being discovered in a bad position is.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
At a strategic level, Discover is an information economy mechanic. Every successful Discover shifts the balance of Open vs Private Information. Players who consistently manage Discover pressure tend to dominate mid-game tempo, as they reduce uncertainty faster than their opponent can replace it.

House-rule note for teaching games: it is often helpful to explicitly remind players that Discover is an attack-like declaration in timing but not in effect. Clarifying this early prevents many sequencing and “can I wait to see?” arguments later, especially when Marker-heavy factions are involved.
