# ARO: Automatic Reaction Order

Basic Rules

In Infinity games, thanks to the Automatic Reaction Order (ARO) mechanic, the action and decision-making never stops. Even during their opponent's Active Turn, a player's Troopers, Models and Markers can react each time the opponent activates one of his Troopers by spending an Order.

The Reactive Player's Troopers may declare an ARO whenever they meet any of the following criteria:

- An enemy Trooper activates within its Line of Fire (LoF).

- An enemy Trooper activates within its Zone of Control (ZoC) or Hacking Area.

- It has a Special Skill, weapon, or piece of Equipment that specifies that the Trooper can react to enemy actions without LoF or ZoC.

- It is affected by a Template Weapon, or is the target of a Comms Attack.

The Reactive Player must declare AROs for all eligible Troopers, Models, or Markers immediately after the Active Player declares his Long Skill, or the Basic Short Skill they intend to perform first (see: Order Expenditure Sequence ). Troopers that fail to do so lose their ARO against that Order. If, after declaring the second Short Skill of their Order, the Active Trooper gives an ARO to any enemy Troopers that did not have a valid ARO against the Active Trooper’s first Basic Short Skill, those enemy Troopers can now declare their AROs.

Each time the Active Player activates a Trooper by spending an Order, each eligible enemy Trooper gets one single ARO, regardless of the number of Skills the Active Player declares during that Order.

The target of an ARO must always be one of the Troopers that have been activated by the Order.

Troopers may not react to AROs, since only the Active Player's Orders give AROs and only the Reactive Player may declare AROs.

There is no limit to the number of Troopers that may react to an Order being spent on an enemy Trooper.

Using an ARO, the Reactive Player can only declare Skills that specifically state they are usable in ARO (check the Summary Chart ).

Unless a Special Skill, Equipment or scenario rule states otherwise, AROs always have Burst B1. In such a situation, if the Reactive Trooper has a Burst value higher than one in ARO, they cannot split it amongst different targets.

## All at Once

In Infinity, Orders and AROs are simultaneous regardless of the Skills declared.

For example, if an activated Trooper declares Move + BS Attack and chooses to shoot from its starting position (to take advantage of a favorable Range), and its target reacts with a BS Attack, choosing to shoot at the end of the Movement (again, for Range purposes), then both actions are still considered to be simultaneous for all game purposes.

All AROs are also simultaneous. This means that, if a Camouflage Marker declares a Move, the opponent can declare Discover with Troopers that have LoF to it, but they cannot declare Discover with one of the Troopers and BS Attack with the rest, waiting for the Discover Roll to be successful (see: Camouflaged State ).


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
AROs are granted *whenever the Active Trooper activates*. This means a Trooper may gain an ARO even if the Active Trooper’s first Short Skill is something non-interactive (e.g., Idle) as long as the activation meets one of the ARO conditions (LoF, ZoC, Hacking Area, being targeted by a Template, etc.).

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
ARO opportunities are checked twice per Order:
1. Immediately after the declaration of the first Short Skill / Long Skill.  
2. Immediately after the declaration of the second Short Skill for Troopers who had no valid ARO previously. 
If the second Short Skill newly satisfies an ARO requirement, Troopers who had no valid ARO previously may now declare an ARO.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Even if multiple things about the Active Trooper’s Order would normally grant AROs (e.g., Move and BS Attack), *each Reactive Trooper still receives only one ARO per Order*. You never get two AROs against the same Order from one model.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Reactive Troopers may only declare Skills permitted in ARO. Many common mistakes happen when players attempt to declare non-ARO legal Skills reactively.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Declaring an ARO that fails its Requirements at Resolution (e.g., you Discover a Camouflage Marker but end up not having LoF due to angles or scenery) causes the ARO to become Idle. Order-expending effects such as Disposable ammunition are still lost if their use was declared.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because AROs are simultaneous, *you may not “wait to see if another Trooper succeeds first”*. For example, you cannot have one model Discover a Camo Marker and wait to declare BS Attack with another only if the Discover succeeds.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If the Active Trooper declares a Move, Reactive Troopers may declare their ARO from *any point along the route* the Active Trooper travels. This allows the Reactive Player to choose the most advantageous position (e.g., best Range Band, exposing the Trooper to LoF only briefly).

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Troopers inside a Zero Visibility Zone **do not gain LoF** and therefore cannot declare LoF-based AROs. However, they may still declare non-LoF AROs (e.g., Reset, Dodge, BS Attack with Speculative Fire, depending on Skill Requirements).

A Trooper without LoF but inside ZoC may declare non-LoF AROs (typically Dodge, Reset, or Hacking). This interaction is easy to forget: *ZoC is often just as relevant as LoF*.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A Trooper may *forfeit* its ARO by not declaring it immediately when prompted. If the Reactive Player chooses not to declare an eligible ARO in the window allowed, they lose the opportunity for that Order entirely.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Troopers in Engaged State cannot draw LoF outside their Close Combat but *can* still declare certain ARO options (Dodge, CC Attack, Reset) according to the State’s permissions. 

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If a Template affects a Trooper, they gain an ARO *even without LoF or ZoC*. This means a Trooper can always Dodge and template weapon.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteam Bonuses may impact ARO (e.g., Special Die from Fireteam Level 2). The Burst in ARO is *normally* B1, but Special Dice (SD) can be added.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A Reactive Trooper may choose to *delay* their ARO only when a rule explicitly allows it (e.g., Sixth Sense against an attacker outside LoF, or when interacting with a Marker following those specific rules). Outside these exceptions, delaying results in forfeiting the ARO.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A common misunderstanding: A successful Dodge/Reset in a Face to Face Roll cancels the attack **only against the dodging/resetting Trooper**, not against other targets. This can matter greatly when several Troopers engage a single Active model.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A Trooper may declare an ARO to an enemy in Hidden Deployment, Camouflage, or Holo states *only if* the enemy action satisfies an ARO requirement. These states do not inherently prevent AROs when they perform actions that reveal or interact.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because ARO declarations must be done without measuring except where explicitly allowed, players must make their best assessment of LoF and ZoC before deciding. Incorrect assumptions may result in failed Requirements and Idle AROs.

## Timing
The Reactive Player must declare AROs for all eligible Troopers, Models, or Markers immediately after the Active Player declares his Long Skill, or the Basic Short Skill they intend to perform first. 

They must declare the type of ARO, and all details and choices related to the execution of a Basic Short Skill,
Short Skill, or Long Skill must be specified when it is declared. Ex. for a BS attack: pecify which Weapon, piece of
Equipment, Special Skill, Ammunition, or other option will be used.

Since most AROs will be declared after the first short skill, Troopers choosing their ARO skill must predict what the enemy will declare as a second short skill.
This also means that the enemy may decide what to do as a second skill basedon the ARO (called fork or forking). 

## Fork / Forking
Forking in Infinity — ARO Interaction Explained

Forking is a tactical situation created by the Active Player in which they declare skills or position their troopers in a way that forces the Reactive Player to face multiple simultaneous threats, each requiring a different optimal ARO.

Ex. 1: Trooper A declare moves as short skill, Trooper B declare ARO Dodge, in anticipation of an BS Attack, then Trooper A declares a Hacking Attack, where Dodge is useless.

Ex. 2: Trooper A declare moves as short skill, turns a corner and encounters Trooper B (a Hacker) and an enemy. Trooper B declares a Hacking attack and the mine triggers a template attack. 
Trooper A can now choose for his second short skill between Dodging the mine, or Reset for the Hacker attack.
