# Silhouettes

In game terms, all Troopers occupy an unvarying volume on the table. This volume is cylinder-shaped, with its width determined by the base size and its height by the type of Trooper.

Silhouette Templates are a game aid designed to help players determine the in- game volume and height and width of a Trooper.

If doubts about the volume of a Trooper arise during a game, players can use the Silhouette Templates to decide exactly what cylinder-shaped space it takes up on the game table. Any part of the model that juts out of the perimeter of its base is ignored for game purposes.

Depending on their unit type, Troopers have in their Profile an Attribute called Silhouette (S) that indicates which Silhouette Template they should use.

## [UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]

### Declaring LoF from Silhouette Volume
Even though minis may lean or crouch, LoF always uses the full Silhouette cylinder. This means you cannot "hide" by physically posing a model smaller, and you can sometimes see over objects even if the miniature's sculpt looks too low.  
New players often incorrectly try to determine LoF based on the model’s physical pose.

### Being Seen While Moving
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because movement is continuous and the Silhouette volume moves with every infinitesimal point of the declared route, a Trooper is considered visible from any angle along the entire path, not only at the start and end.  
This causes many “surprise” AROs when newer players think they are only visible at the endpoints.

### Check Distances From Silhouette Base
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Distance measurements are always base-edge to base-edge (except in rare vertical or template checks). Players sometimes mistakenly measure from the miniature’s extremities.

### Silhouette Determines Cover Interaction
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cover checks (partial obstruction of Silhouette volume) are based on the theoretical cylinder, not the sculpt. A model leaning sideways does not change what counts as Covered.

### Replacing Models With Different Silhouette Values
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
When a Trooper changes Silhouette (e.g., states, equipment, or special effects), use the replacement rules:
- You may center the new base or match edges.
- Matching edges often gives a micro-positional advantage (gaining cover or reaching melee base contact).
This is a commonly forgotten optimization, and entirely legal.

### Template Interactions With Silhouettes
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Templates check contact with the Silhouette cylinder — not the miniature. Sometimes a model’s physical pose looks “safe” even though the Silhouette extends into a blast. Always test against the template + silhouette, not the sculpt.

### Silhouettes and Prone
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Prone reduces Silhouette height but not diameter. This means:
- Prone Troopers become easier to fully hide behind low terrain.
- However, they still maintain horizontal footprint for LoF and template intersections.
Players often assume Prone makes the base smaller — it does not.

### Verticality and Climb Constraints
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Troopers cannot be deployed on vertical surfaces, and Silhouettes must fully fit in any location they occupy. This means:
- You cannot end movement on ledges too small to contain the base.
- A Trooper may Move along a wall only during a Climb but must still have valid final placement.

### Being in / out of LoF due to Silhouette Height
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because Silhouette values are standardized, two Troopers of the same Silhouette always see each other over equal-height obstacles. Sculpt height is irrelevant.

### Silhouette and Engaged State Restrictions
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
When Engaged, Troopers lose normal LoF but still occupy their full Silhouette. This sometimes prevents enemy placement or movement even though the miniatures appear to not physically block the area.

### Placing Models Under Overhangs
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A Trooper may not be placed or finish movement in a position where the Silhouette cannot physically fit under a ceiling/overhang. A common misunderstanding occurs when players assume the sculpt height matters — only the Silhouette height matters.

### Silhouette Templates in Ambiguous Situations
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If players cannot agree on LoF or volume, the Silhouette Template MUST override the miniature. Many conflicts disappear once templates are used consistently.

### Movement Through Gaps
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A Trooper can only move through gaps wider than its base diameter, regardless of the sculpt's pose. This prevents unrealistic movement exploitation.

### Height for Jump and Climb
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
All height calculations use Silhouette height, not the miniature. This is crucial for:
- Declaring Jump distances.
- Checking if ledges are reachable.
- Determining vertical LoF.
