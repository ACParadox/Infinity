# Coherency

Coherency is the game mechanic which allows management of several linked troopers acting together and simultaneously in the same Order while applying a rule, Special Skill, piece of Equipment, Game State... that requires those troopers to stay in close proximity.

Coherency is determined by the Zone of Control of a specific trooper known as the Reference Trooper. The Reference Trooper can vary and is defined by each rule, Special Skill, piece of Equipment... which requires Coherency (for example, the Team Leader of a Fireteam, the Controller of a Peripheral or an HVT...).

To be in Coherency, the rest of the linked troopers must be inside the Zone of Control of the Reference Trooper.

## Coherency Check

### During the Deployment Phase

The player can check Coherency, measuring the Zone of Control of the Reference Trooper to be sure the rest of the linked troopers are inside it.

After having checked Coherency, the player can adjust the placement of those troopers so they are all inside the Zone of Control.

However, this adjustment is not allowed regarding the last figure kept back as reserve, nor during the deployment of that Trooper (or Troopers if any rule, Special Skill, piece of Equipment... allows several to kept back in reserve).

### At the start or end of an Order

The player must check Coherency at the start of the Order, after having declared the first Short Skill of the Order, the Long Skill, or an ARO, but before measuring movement, if any has been declared. The player must also check Coherency at the end of the Order, at the end of the Conclusion step, after having performed any movement due to a failed Guts Roll or any other rule.

## Breaking Coherency

When a Coherency Check is failed, if a trooper is out of the Reference Trooper’s ZoC, then Coherency is broken and its consequences are applied immediately, as Stated by the rule, Special Skill, piece of Equipment... the player is applying.

In the Active Turn, after declaring a Fireteam ’s first Short Skill of the Order, or Long Skill, any members out of Coherency with the Team Leader would leave the Fireteam and not be activated. Fireteam Bonuses would then be recalculated.

To make the game faster, in those situations in which it is clear and evident that Coherency is kept (when the troopers are close to the Reference Trooper, and clearly inside his Zone of Control ), it is not necessary to perform a Coherency Check, unless the adversary asks for it.

## FAQs

Q: During your main part of the Deployment Phase, can you reposition Troopers, Deployables, etc?

A: In their main Deployment Phase step, players can reposition Troopers, Deployables, etc. As soon as a Roll or measurement is made for those game elements, their position is fixed, and they cannot be repositioned during the Deployment Phase.

For example, you can reposition a Trooper as many times as you want, but after you create a Fireteam and adjust the placement of the Fireteam members during the Coherency Check, you can no longer reposition any member of that Fireteam.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Coherency underpins Fireteams, Peripherals, Decoys/Holoechoes and some scenario conditions. Because it is checked *at the start* of an Order (after declaring the first Short Skill), *and again at the end* of the Order, many timing-based interactions can surprise players.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Coherency is always measured from the designated **Reference Trooper** of the rule being applied.  
Examples:  
- Fireteam → Team Leader  
- Peripheral → Controller  
- Decoy & Holoecho → Host Trooper  
- Inspiring Leadership → The Trooper with the Skill  
- Scenario conditions → As defined by mission text  

This means different rules may require different coherency anchors at the same time.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Coherency must be measured **before moving the models** when the first Short Skill is declared.  
This means:  
If a model begins outside Coherency at declaration time, it immediately counts as breaking Coherency and suffers the rule’s consequence even if movement would have brought it back in.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Breaking Coherency in a Fireteam occurs immediately when detected on the first Short Skill declaration. Troopers that fail Coherency *do not activate* and *do not declare Skills* for that Order. They are simply not part of the Fireteam for that activation.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because Coherency is checked *again* at the end of the Order, a model may start in Coherency, activate normally, then end out of range after movement → This usually disbands the Fireteam or applies other rule-specific consequences. This is intentional: Infinity encourages careful positioning.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Zone of Control is a **true radius** based on Silhouette volume, not base edge, so slight differences in placement matter. This can cause legal but unintuitive outcomes such as:  
- A Trooper with a larger Silhouette being easier to keep inside odd Fireteam shapes.  
- Vertical distance affecting Coherency because ZoC is a 3D sphere.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because you cannot pre-measure movement before declaration, players must estimate whether their movement path will maintain Coherency. This is a common source of errors and accidental Fireteam breaks.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Coherency does **not** require Line of Fire, visibility, or even being on the same elevation. Only distance matters.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Markers can be subject to Coherency rules (Decoy, Holoecho, Peripherals in Marker States). When checking Coherency that would reveal Private Information (e.g. Minelayer, Holoecho during Deployment), the opponent must look away.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
In Fireteams, Coherency is ignored for ARO declaration timing — members may declare AROs even if spread out. However, if the team breaks at the *start* of the Active team’s Order, only members in Coherency with the Leader activate; others remain capable of issuing standard AROs but no longer benefit from Fireteam bonuses.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Movement caused by Guts Rolls is checked for Coherency at the **end** of the Order. This means a model failing Guts may unintentionally break a Fireteam or detach from an Inspiring Leadership bubble.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Peripherals share their controller’s Order expenditure, so a break in Coherency prevents them from activating. This often causes players to underestimate how tightly Peripherals must stay close to their Controller.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If several rules depend on Coherency and use different Reference Troopers, you must evaluate each independently. A model may remain in Coherency for one rule but break another simultaneously.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because Coherency is purely distance-based, Troopers that end movement in mid-air (Climbing, Jump path points) still count as occupying that position for Coherency checks.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Fireteam Coherency can be deliberately broken as a tactic. For example:  
- Allowing expendable members to drop from the team by moving the Leader aggressively.  

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Common mistakes players make with Coherency include:  
- Measuring after movement instead of before.  
- Forgetting to check at the end of the Order.  
- Assuming Coherency is a “during movement” requirement rather than a start/end requirement.  
- Thinking Coherency requires visibility.  
- Forgetting that Peripherals replicate actions only if Coherency is satisfied.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
In practice, Coherency is the main balancing mechanism for Fireteams and Peripherals. It rewards positional discipline and punishes sloppy spacing. Proper spacing, turn planning, and awareness of movement distances dramatically reduce unintended rule breakages.
