# Combat Module

In Infinity, combat allows Troopers to perform Attacks. There are three kinds of Attacks: Ballistic Skills (BS), Close Combat (CC) and Quantronic Combat (Hacking). This section details every rule necessary to resolve Attacks.

BS, CC, or Hacking combat takes place within the normal Order Expenditure Sequence and it is based on the different Rolls in Infinity.

## Combat - Types of Weapon

Each weapon in Infinity has its own game profile, and some of them have special rules that are explained in the Ammunition and Weaponry chapter. This rulebook also contains a Weapons Chart for you to refer to during games.

Weapons in Infinity are divided as follows:

- BS Weapons: Those weapons having Ranges and Range MODs or using a Template to affect an area of the game table.

- Melee Weapons: Close-quarters weapons and those that can only be used in CC and possess the CC Trait.

- Mixed Weapons: Those weapons having Ranges and Range MODs but also the CC Trait. So, they have a Use Mode as a Melee Weapons and another Use Mode as a BS Weapon

- Deployable Weapons: Those weapons that can be placed on the game table whereupon they become an independent element. They possess the Deployable Weapon Trait.

- BS Weapons (PH): These weapons are BS Weapons but are used with the PH Attribute instead of BS. When using these weapons, any rules or MODs that apply to BS Attacks or to the BS Attribute are applied to the PH Attribute instead. These weapons have the BS Weapons (PH) Trait.

- BS Weapons (WIP): These weapons are BS Weapons but are used with the WIP Attribute instead of BS. When using these weapons, any rules or MODs that apply to BS Attacks or to the BS Attribute are applied to the WIP Attribute instead. These weapons have the BS Weapons (WIP) Trait. The BS Attack ( Shock ) Skill cannot be combined with these weapons.

## Combat - Burst (B)

In Infinity, when a Trooper performs an Attack during their Active Turn they must roll as many dice as indicated by their Weapon, Skill or piece of Equipment.

During their Active Turn, Troopers must use their full Burst (B) value including all Modifiers (MODs). However, MODs from Skills and Equipment with the Optional Label, such as the Fireteam Burst Bonus, do not have to be applied during the Active or Reactive Turn.

When declaring the Attack, the player must specify:

- The Weapon, Equipment, or Special Skill that they will use.

- The way they will divide the Burst (B) of the Weapon, Equipment, or Special Skill between one or more targets. In this situation, all the Attacks must be declared from the same point.

- Additionally, if the Weapon, Equipment, or Special Skill has different types of ammunition or options, the player must then declare which one they will use.

If, during the Resolution of the Order, it is verified that some of the dice of the Burst (B) assigned to a target do not meet the necessary Requirements, then those dice will be lost. The rest of the dice of the B that do meet the Requirements will be resolved normally.

## Combat - Burst During the Reactive Turn (ARO)

In ARO, the B value is always reduced to 1; however, some rules or Special Skills can modify this value. If the target declared Move plus any Short Skill or Basic Short Skill, the Reactive Player may declare the ARO at any point along the Active Trooper’s movement.

## Combat - Modifiers (MOD)

Both during the Active and Reactive Turns, there may be Modifiers ( MODs ) that affect the Burst (B). They are applied when declaring the Attack. There are also MODs to the Attack Rolls, which are applied in the Resolution step of the Order. These MODs are explained in the sections for their respective Types of Combat ( BS, CC, or Hacking ).

Modifiers Explained

Any MOD or value in round brackets next to a Special Skill, Weapon, or Equipment —such as CC Attack (+3), Combat Jump (PH=10), BS Attack (+3), CC Weapon (PS=6), Combi Rifle (+1B), etc.— only applies when using the Skill, Weapon, or Equipment.

- Positive MODs only apply to the user.

- Negative MODs only apply to enemies. Negative MODs for Automatic Skills and Equipment such as Mimetism (-6), or Surprise Attack (-3) always apply the MOD as specified in their rules. Negative MODs for other Skills, Weapons, and Equipment such as Dodge (-3), or CC Attack (-3) only apply during Face to Face Rolls.

- " ReRoll " in round brackets next to a Skill, Weapon, or Equipment will allow the user to reroll one die from the Roll, only when using that Skill, Weapon, or Equipment.

- "+1 SD" or "+1 Special Die" in round brackets next to a Skill, Weapon, or Equipment allows the player to roll one extra die, and then select and discard one die from the Roll. This extra die does not increase the Burst value of the Special Skill, Weapon, or Equipment, and does not expend additional Disposable uses. If distributing Burst between several targets, the extra die must be allocated to one of those targets, and a die discarded from the Roll against that target. If used in a Face to Face Roll, a die is selected and discarded after both players have rolled. If both players have to select and discard a die, the Active Player selects first. This MOD applies in both Active and Reactive Turns. This MOD cannot be applied to Long Skills. Also, it cannot be applied to Skills, Weapons, or Equipment that do not require a Roll, for example, Direct Template Weapons.

- Negative MODs for Automatic Skills and Equipment such as Mimetism (-6), or Surprise Attack (-3) always apply the MOD as specified in their rules.

- Negative MODs for other Skills, Weapons, and Equipment such as Dodge (-3), or CC Attack (-3) only apply during Face to Face Rolls.

- If used in a Face to Face Roll, a die is selected and discarded after both players have rolled. If both players have to select and discard a die, the Active Player selects first.

- This MOD applies in both Active and Reactive Turns.

- This MOD cannot be applied to Long Skills. Also, it cannot be applied to Skills, Weapons, or Equipment that do not require a Roll, for example, Direct Template Weapons.

As a general rule, if these Modifiers (MODs) affect Burst (B), they are only applied during the Active Turn.

The maximum Burst (B) value of any Attack is 6, no matter how many MODs to B are applied. The extra dice for "+1SD" do not count towards this maximum, as they do not increase the Burst value.

MODs imposed by Skills, Special Skills, or pieces of Equipment may be applied to their users, or to those enemy Troopers acting against them, in several different ways. For example:

Dodge (-3) means that, when the user declares Dodge, the opponents in any Face to Face Rolls suffer a -3 MOD.

## Combat - Attack Roll

Once all MODs have been established, players perform the BS Attack, CC Attack, or Hacking Rolls using the corresponding Attribute which, as a general—but not exclusive—rule, is BS, CC, PH, or WIP. The order in which the Attack Rolls are performed is irrelevant.

## Combat - Normal Roll

If the Skill declared by the target does not affect the outcome of the Attack, the attacker can make a Normal Roll (see Rolls ).

The player performs as many Rolls as indicated by the Burst (B) value assigned to each target.

## Combat - Face to Face Roll

If the Skill declared by the target does affect the outcome of the Attack (for example declaring a BS Attack ), then a Face to Face Roll is made (see Face to Face Rolls ).

Both players make one Roll for each point of Burst they assigned to that target.

## Combat - Criticals

A Critical is an automatic success. Unless otherwise specified, each Critical rolled in an Attack causes the target to make an additional Saving Roll.

If the Attack does not cause the target to make Saving Rolls, the effect of the Critical will be specified in the rules for that Attack, Ammunition etc.

Some weapons use certain Types of Ammunition capable of altering the effects of an Attack, causing more than one Saving Roll for each success, reducing the ARM or BTS of the target, etc. This can also alter the way Criticals work.

The additional roll gained by scoring a Critical Hit will retain both the Attribute used to make the Saving Roll and the Traits of the weapon used in the attack.

In a Face to Face Roll, Criticals always win, trumping any non-critical result rolled by the opponent. If both players roll one or more Criticals, the Face to Face Roll is a tie and both Troopers fail.



**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
BS Weapons (PH) and BS Weapons (WIP) still count as **BS Attacks** for all rule interactions except that the **roll Attribute changes**. This means MODs like Cover (to-hit), Mimetism, Visibility penalties, etc. still apply, even though the roll uses PH or WIP.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Deployable Weapons are often misplayed as “existing inside the trooper.” Once placed, they become **independent elements** and no longer benefit from the trooper’s LoF, MODs, or Skills. All interactions treat the Deployable itself as the origin of effects.

## 2. Burst (B)
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Many players forget that Burst must be declared **from a single point along the movement**. If the trooper moves, they cannot assign some dice from the starting point and others from the end point. You must pick one shooting point per declared Attack.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If any portion of the declared Burst becomes illegal (target leaves LoF, enters Total Cover, or fails Requirements), **only the affected dice are lost**, not the whole Attack.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Special Dice (+SD) never increase Burst. They behave like “roll one extra; keep one fewer” and do not count toward the maximum Burst 6 limit.

## 3. Burst in ARO
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Even when multiple enemies activate, each reactive trooper receives **only one ARO**, and therefore one Burst (normally B1). Declaring BS Attack against several active troopers is impossible.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Automatic Skills that provide extra Burst in ARO (e.g., Enhanced Reaction) modify the base ARO Burst but still follow all ARO restrictions: you still only get one ARO, but that ARO may have B2 or more.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Choosing the point along the enemy’s movement to react is critical. The reactive trooper effectively picks **one LoF snapshot** where the shot is taken. Visualizing this as a single instant prevents mistakes about changing Range Bands halfway through the enemy’s movement.

## 4. Modifiers (MOD)
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The MOD cap of ±12 is applied **after everything is added together**, not per-source. Multiple extreme visibility situations rarely exceed this, but it matters for stacking Surprise Attack, Mimetism, Range, and Cover.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Negative MODs that apply **only during Face to Face** (for example Dodge -3) are frequently misapplied to Normal Rolls. If the opponent’s declaration does not oppose your action, these modifiers do not apply at all.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
+1SD effects function after the roll; they do not bypass ARO Burst limitations, nor interact with Disposable uses. They also can be applied in ARO because they do not modify Burst itself.

## 5. Attack Roll
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Attack Rolls are always performed using the Attribute defined by the weapon mode or Skill — which can mean BS, PH, WIP, or occasionally others. Changing ammo type never changes the Attribute.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If multiple identical Attacks occur (same weapon, same MODs, same target), they may be rolled together, but if any target or MOD differs, you must roll separately for each Burst allocation.

## 6. Normal Roll
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The most common misconception: a Normal Roll does *not* mean “unopposed by default.” A face-to-face can only occur if the target’s declared Skill **affects the outcome** of the Attack. 

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If an Attack has multiple targets, some troopers may roll face-to-face while others roll Normal, depending entirely on whether their declaration interacts with the Attack.

## 7. Face to Face Roll
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A roll only becomes Face to Face if each declaration can **prevent** the other. Discover vs. BS Attack is not Face to Face because Discover cannot prevent a BS Attack.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Movement does not create a Face to Face Roll by itself. Move + BS Attack vs. BS Attack is FtF because the Attack affects the Attack; Move + Dodge vs. BS Attack is FtF because Dodge can cancel the Attack; but Move alone vs. BS Attack is never FtF.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If a trooper splits Burst, each target gets its own separate Face to Face Roll. The results of one FtF never spill over to the others.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If both sides roll Criticals, the entire FtF nullifies — including any non-critical successes. This is often forgotten and changes outcomes dramatically.

## 8. Criticals
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Criticals never “auto-wound”. A Critical simply forces an additional Saving Roll using the same ammo effects.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
AP ammo interacts with Criticals exactly like normal rolls — meaning the additional Save caused by the Critical is also AP.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Criticals in Normal Rolls still add extra Saves. Because Normal Rolls do not compare results, there is no cancelation step — multiple Criticals simply mean multiple additional Saves.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A Critical in a Face to Face automatically beats *all* non-critical successes, even a success rolled on a 1 when the attacker’s SV is 1. If both roll Criticals, no effect is applied at all.
