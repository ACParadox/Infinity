# Alignment

Game States and Glossary

In Infinity it is important to define which game elements belong to a players’ side, and which belong to no side. This determines if those game elements are capable of being attacked, and if so, from whom these attacks may come.

The Alignment terms help the players know the extent of the rules over certain game elements.

## Contents

### Ally

Game elements that belong to the player’s Army List, or to those of their teammates if the game is played in pairs or groups.

### Enemy

Game elements that belong to the opposing player’s Army List or to any of their teammates if the game is played in pairs or groups.

### Hostile

A game element with its own Attributes that does not belong to the Army List of any of the players. It is considered an Enemy by all the players and is able to declare and receive Attacks.

### Neutral

Game elements with Attributes that do not belong to the Army Lists of either player.

# Ally Interactions

An Ally is anything belonging to your Army List. This includes:
- Troopers
- Peripherals
- Deployable Equipment
- States produced by your own units (example: Deployable Repeater)

Common reminders:
- You cannot target Allies with Attacks unless a rule explicitly allows or requires it (example: hitting them with a Template to affect an Enemy behind them is still legal, but they will take the hit).
- Support Skills (Doctor, Engineer, Reset to break States) normally only work on Allies.
- Many scenario rules explicitly ignore Allies when determining scoring zones.

Tricky cases:
- Peripherals are Allies but do not generate Orders. Players often forget that Alignment is totally independent of Order contribution.
- Decoy and Holoecho markers count as Allied game elements even though they are “fake units”.

# Enemy Interactions
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Enemy is straightforward: anything belonging to the opponent’s Army List. Most Attacks, ARO triggers, and detection mechanics reference “Enemy Troopers”.

Common misunderstandings:
- Enemy Markers are still Enemies. Alignment does not change during Marker States.
- Mines and Perimeter weapons your opponent placed are Enemies and will react to you even though they are not Troopers.
- Effects like Discover, Hacking AROs, and Template triggers rely on this definition.

Pros for understanding:
- Clean targeting rules: if it’s the opponent’s piece, treat it as Enemy.

Cons:
- Some scenario elements treated as Neutral or Hostile break this simple assumption.

# Neutral Interactions
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Neutral elements have Attributes but do not belong to either Army List and are *not* considered Enemies. They cannot declare Attacks unless a mission states otherwise.

Examples: Civilians, consoles, HVTs, environmental pieces.

Important differences from Hostile:
- Neutral elements do not provoke AROs.
- You cannot Attack Neutral elements unless a mission explicitly allows it.
- Neutral objects do not count for most skills requiring an Enemy target (Discover, BS Attack, Engage, etc.).

You absolutely can. Templates and random effects can damage Allies; you simply cannot *declare* Attacks *against* them unless permitted.

# Strategic Notes
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
- Use Neutral objects as movement blockers. Opponents cannot legally attack them to clear a lane.
- Be cautious around Hostile elements—they create crossfire zones you cannot ignore.
- When planning templates, remember Allies are legal collateral.
- Understanding the Alignment of scenario elements can determine whether a unit is free to move safely or will provoke dangerous AROs.

# Example Synergies
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
- Deploying mines near a Hostile can catch the opponent when the Hostile triggers movement.
- Using Neutral scenery pieces as cover works without worrying about them reacting.
