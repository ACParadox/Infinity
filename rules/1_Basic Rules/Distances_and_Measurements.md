# Distances and Measurements

Distances on the game table are measured in inches, using a tape measure or ruler.

When measuring the distance between two Troopers, players must measure between the closest parts of their Silhouettes.

To determine what the distance is between two objects or scenery items, players must measure a straight line between them.

In Infinity, it is advised to use the base for measuring distances. By doing so, it is easier to determine the reference point for the distance and speed up the game. Only on rare occasions it will be necessary to use the Silhouette Template to know the exact range between two Troopers.

As a general rule in Infinity, the Skills that compose an Order must be declared before taking any measurements (see Order Expenditure Sequence ).

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Subject: Distances and Measurements – Core Concepts

The official rule establishes three key points:
- Distances are measured in inches.
- Between Troopers, you measure between the closest parts of their Silhouettes.
- Between scenery or other objects, you measure a straight line between them.
- Players are advised to use the base as the main reference for distance, using the Silhouette Template only when necessary for precision. 

In practice:
- For most movement and range checks, just measure base-edge to base-edge; this is quicker and usually accurate enough.
- When a precise Silhouette volume matters (tight corners, weird terrain, prone on rooftops, etc.), switch to a Silhouette Template to avoid arguments.
- “Closest point” means you do not pick arbitrary points on the base – you look for the shortest possible line between the two Silhouettes, usually approximated by the base rims.

Common misunderstandings:
- Using the base does not mean you ignore the Silhouette volume; it’s a shortcut that is assumed to tightly approximate the Silhouette unless the situation is ambiguous.
- You always measure the *shortest* legal distance, not a curved or “favourable” path, unless a movement rule specifically tells you to follow the route (e.g., movement path along a wall).


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Subject: Declaring Skills Before Measuring

The rule explicitly states that, as a general rule, the Skills that compose an Order must be declared before taking any measurements. 

Important interactions:
- Order Expenditure Sequence requires you to fully declare weapons, targets, Burst allocation *before* measuring. 
- If, during Resolution, the measurement shows a Requirement is not met (out of Range, etc.), the Trooper performs Idle and any Disposable uses spent are lost. 

Practical consequences:
- You cannot “pre-measure” weapon ranges to pick the best gun or decide whether to split Burst; you must commit, then check.

Note: this is not the case for movement: You can measure Movement distances immediately after declaring any Skill with the Movement Label and before determining where the Trooper ends his Movement

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Subject: What You May Measure Freely vs. What You May Not

Distances and Measurements work alongside a number of exceptions where the rules *do* allow measurement without committing to a Skill.

Examples where free/early measurement is explicitly allowed:
- Deployment:
  - Players may measure Deployment Zones, Forward Deployment areas, the central line of the table, and any Exclusion Zones stated by the mission. 
- Zone of Control checks:
  - After the Active Trooper has completed a movement and is placed at the final point, both players may measure the Active Trooper’s entire 8" ZoC along its entire route to check who gained AROs. 
- Coherency checks:
  - Coherency is checked at the start and end of an Order (or during Deployment for Fireteams and similar), *before* measuring movement, to see if models are within 8" ZoC of their Reference Trooper. 

Key principle:
- “Declare before measuring” applies to Skill *decisions* (weapons, targets, paths).
- “You may measure” applies to game structure checks: Deployment Zones, ZoC, Coherency, and other explicitly listed exceptions.

If the rule does not *explicitly* grant you permission to measure something, assume you must declare first and measure at Resolution.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Subject: Zone of Control, AROs, and Measurement Direction

Zone of Control is an 8" cylinder from the outer edge of a Trooper’s Silhouette.

Interactions with measurement:
- During Orders, ZoC measurements are always performed from the *Active* Trooper, checking a maximum of 8" from any point along their path. 
- This means you do not arbitrarily measure from Reactive Troopers to see if they “might” get an ARO; you only check once the Active Trooper’s route is fixed and placed on the table.
- The ZoC measurement tells you which Reactive Troopers may declare AROs even without LoF (Reset, Change Facing, Hacking Programs, etc.).

Common traps:
- You cannot “probe” ZoC in advance by measuring from a Reactive Trooper to see if it reaches 8" to an enemy model that *might* move; you must wait until the Active Trooper has actually moved.
- If the Active Trooper’s path never comes within 8" of a particular enemy, that enemy does not get an ARO, even if they are close at the end of a later Order.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Subject: Line of Fire, Silhouettes, and Measurement Synergy

Distances and Measurements work together with LoF and Silhouette rules:
- LoF is any unobstructed straight line between any two points of the Silhouettes of Trooper and target. 
- You may use Silhouette Templates, rulers, and laser pointers to check LoF and clarify whether anything obstructs it. 

Synergy with distance:
- The same “closest point between Silhouettes” logic applies when deciding whether a Trooper is within a weapon’s Range band or ZoC.
- Because Troopers gain 360° LoF while performing Movement-labeled Skills, you often need to think about *where along the measured path* they first see or are seen by enemies, not only their starting/ending positions. 

Practical takeaway:
- When in doubt, lay down the Silhouette Template and draw the shortest line between the two volumes; that same line gives you both LoF and the distance to check Range bands.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Subject: Fair Play and Shared Measurements

Infinity explicitly encourages players to cooperate when checking distances and LoF:
- The Fair Play section states that opponents may assist with measurements and LoF checks, and that both players should work together to correct mistakes.

Usage guideline:
- If both players agree to a loose, time-saving approach (e.g., quickly checking if something is “about 8 inches”), that is fine as long as it is applied consistently.
- However, when a precise interaction matters (e.g., ZoC AROs, exact Range bands, or silhouette contact to enter Close Combat), you should switch to strict, rules-accurate measurements.

Tournament-friendly habit:
- Clarify at the beginning of the game how strict you both intend to be with measuring (especially around ZoC and “declare before measure”).
- Use shared tools (laser LoF, silhouette markers) to avoid conflicts, and rely on the written rule that Skills are declared before measurement whenever there is a dispute.
