## Army List

The Army List is the list of Troopers that make up the combat forces the player will be using during the game.

In order to play a game of Infinity, the first step will be to determine the number of Army Points with which the Army Lists will be created. This number will be decided by mutual agreement between the players, or it could be determined by a specific mission or scenario you are playing, or by the organizer if you are playing at an event.

An Army List may include a maximum of 15 Troopers.

A standard Infinity game is organized with 300 Points and 6 SWC, which would let players include up to 15 Troopers for an average duration game.

### Sectorial Army Lists

The Sectorial Armies are specific sections of a faction, or small territorial armies from a specific area, region, or planet of the faction to which they belong. Each faction in Infinity may have one or more Sectorial Armies.

The Sectorial Armies have their own distinct Army Lists with Trooper Availabilities that are different from the generic Army of the faction they belong to. They will often have greater access to specific units, at the cost of not being able to access other units from their generic Armies, which in turn belong to other Sectorial Armies of the same faction.

## Army Points and Value

The total sum of the Cost of the Troopers that make up an Army List must always be equal to or lower than the number of Army Points agreed upon for the game.

In order to create an Army List, the player needs to select his Troopers from the ones available for his faction (PanOceania, Yu Jing, Combined Army...) and add up their Cost.

## Combat Group

A Combat Group is a closed group of Troopers with a maximum number of members no greater than 10.

When the total number of Troopers making up an Army List is more than 10, the player must organize the Troopers into separate Combat Groups.

When creating the Army List, the player must assign each Trooper to a Combat Group; he can create extra Combat Groups as needed, as long as the total number of Troopers in each one is no greater than 10.

A Combat Group cannot normally be reorganized during the game. Because of this, players are not allowed to transfer Troopers or their Orders from one Combat Group to another.

Executive Use of Command Tokens For each Command Token expended, the player may do one of the following:

- Permanently move an Allied Trooper and all their Peripherals to a different Combat Group, keeping in mind the 10-member limit.

Dead State

- The Trooper is removed from the game table and their Combat Group.

Unconscious State

- Troopers in Unconscious State still belong to their Combat Group.

Only those Troopers who contribute or spend Orders take up space in the Combat Group. Peripherals do not contribute or spend Orders, so they do not count towards the maximum limit of 10 Troopers in a Combat Group.

Although the content of a Marker is Private Information, which Combat Group they belong to is Open Information.

If the Marker is a Deployable Weapon or piece of Equipment, it belongs to the Combat Group of the Trooper that deployed it.

## Availability (AVA)

All Troopers have an Availability (AVA) value on their Unit Profile. The Availability Attribute determines the maximum number of Troopers from the Unit that can be included in the player’s Army List.

An Availability of Total allows the player to field as many Troopers from that Unit as desired, without exceeding the agreed Army Points for the game, or the number of Troopers allowed in the Army List.

## Support Weapons Cost (SWC)

Support Weapons are the weapons or Special Equipment not included in the basic or standard equipment. These Support Weapons have a specific cost named Support Weapons Cost (SWC).

On each Unit Profile, the SWC for each option for that Trooper is specified, taking into account the weapons and the equipment at their disposal.

In game terms, each 50 Army Points will provide 1 point of SWC to spend on Troopers with Support Weapons.

Troopers whose SWC value has the + symbol will provide the player that many extra SWC points to be added to the total of the Support Weapons Points available for his Army List. In addition, it will not cost the player any SWC to field these Troopers, as their SWC points value is considered to be 0.

## Lieutenant

Army Lists must have one Trooper with the Lieutenant Special Skill.

However, players are not allowed to field more than one Trooper with the Lieutenant Special Skill in their Army List.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

## Maximum of 15 Troopers
A common mistake is assuming Peripherals count toward the 15-trooper cap. They do not. Only Troopers that generate or spend Orders count. Peripherals, because they generate no Orders, never occupy Army List slots.

Deployable weapons and equipment also never count as Troopers, even if they appear as Markers on the table.

## Sectorial Army Lists and Availability (AVA)

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Players sometimes misread Sectorial AVA changes, assuming the Sectorial may take *more* of every troop. In reality, Sectorials often *lose* access to some generic units. Always check if a unit is even present in the Sectorial before checking its AVA.

AVA Total does not bypass the 15-Trooper Army List limit. You may have AVA Total Warbands, but still only 15 Troopers total.

## Combat Groups (max 10 per group)

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The most common Combat Group error is forgetting that *Peripherals do not count* toward the 10-Trooper limit. A model with two Peripherals still only occupies one Combat Group slot.

Transferring Troopers between CGs during the game always costs a Command Token and is permanent. Even advanced players forget that you cannot “move them back later.”

Units in Hidden Deployment still occupy their assigned Combat Group. 

## Order Generation Interactions

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Troopers not deployed on the table generate *zero* Orders. This includes:
- Hidden Deployment
- Airborne Deployment (Combat Jump / Parachutist)
- Troopers not yet placed by scenario rules

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Unconscious Troopers *still contribute* to Combat Group size but do not generate Orders, which can cause odd mid-game CG compositions (e.g., 9 active + 1 unconscious). They still block adding new members because they occupy a slot.


## Lieutenant Restrictions & Interactions

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Your Lieutenant’s identity is Private Information but the army *must contain exactly one*. Doing “zero” is illegal.

## SWC (Support Weapon Cost)

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Some units list “+X SWC”. This is often misunderstood: the unit *gives you additional SWC*, and its own SWC cost becomes 0. It is not paying SWC—it is enhancing the pool.

SWC is capped by Army Points (1 SWC per 50 pts). Extra SWC granted by units can exceed this cap.

## Army Points & List Legality

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Infinity allows *underspending* on Points and SWC. There is no minimum requirement.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A list with 15 Troopers but only 14 appropriate models is still legal. 


## Hidden Information & Deployment Interactions

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Because many elements in Army Lists are Private Information (Cost, SWC, AD Troopers, Hidden Deployment, Lieutenant, etc.), opponents cannot ever challenge a list’s legality during the game. Only after the match is over does Private Information become Open and discrepancies may be seen.

## Trickiest Common Misunderstandings

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
- Thinking a 2-model Fireteam uses 2 Army List slots *plus* Peripherals. It is still only 2 Troopers + free Peripherals.
- Forgetting that Hidden Deployment Troopers still occupy Combat Group slots.
- Assuming “must deploy as one” units (Synced, Buddies, etc.) count as multiple list entries. They don’t.
- Confusing Troopers and Models: a single Trooper may create multiple Markers, but it is still a single Army List entry.

## Designer-Intent Commentary (unofficial)

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Army List restrictions enforce the game’s pacing and tactical economy. The 15-Trooper limit caps Order saturation. The 10-Trooper-per-Group limit keeps decision load manageable. These limits are as much balance tools as they are structural parts of the rules.
