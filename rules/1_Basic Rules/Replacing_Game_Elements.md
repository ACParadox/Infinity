# Replacing Game Elements

Occasionally, during a game, it is necessary to replace a Game Element like a Trooper, or a piece of Equipment, with another Model or Marker due to a Special Skill, piece of Equipment, game condition, or scenario condition. For simplicity, these definitions always refer to the term Trooper.

## Matching Diameters

If the replacement Trooper possesses the same base size, then the base of the replacement Trooper must match the position of the original Trooper’s base.

## Different Diameters

However, some Special Skills, pieces of Equipment, game conditions, or scenario conditions require that the replacement Trooper to have a different Silhouette Attribute value and base size.

In this case, the player has two options to choose from:

- The player can match the center of the replacement Trooper's base with the center of the original Trooper's base.

- The player can make the edge of the replacement Trooper's base match the edge of the original Trooper's base. As seen in the graphic, the arc of the circumference of the borders must coincide.

This allows, for example, the replacement Trooper to gain contact with Cover that the original Trooper didn't have. In a similar way, this allows the replacement Trooper to enter Engaged State. However, this rule cannot be used to cancel the Engaged State.

Where the replacement is mandatory and inevitable, because of a rule, Special Skill, or piece of Equipment that has the Obligatory Label, the following must be taken into account:

- If the space available is less than the volume determined by the silhouette template, the replacement Trooper will enter the Immobilized-A State. This State can only be cancelled by the conditions of the space containing the Trooper changing, and may not be cancelled in the usual manner defined by the State itself. Pieces of Equipment will be removed from the gaming table instead of entering the Immobilized-A State.

## Relevant Rule Text (for reference when attaching to Knowledge)
Replacing Game Elements

Occasionally, during a game, it is necessary to replace a Game Element like a Trooper, or a piece of Equipment, with another Model or Marker due to a Special Skill, piece of Equipment, game condition, or scenario condition. For simplicity, these definitions always refer to the term Trooper.

Matching Diameters  
If the replacement Trooper possesses the same base size, then the base of the replacement Trooper must match the position of the original Trooper’s base.

Different Diameters  
However, some Special Skills, pieces of Equipment, game conditions, or scenario conditions require that the replacement Trooper to have a different Silhouette Attribute value and base size.  
In this case, the player has two options to choose from:  
- Match the centers of the bases.  
- Match the edges of the bases so that the circumferences align.

This can allow the replacement Trooper to gain Cover that the original Trooper did not, or to enter Engaged State. It cannot be used to cancel Engaged State.

If the replacement is mandatory due to an Obligatory rule and the physical space is too small for the silhouette, the Trooper enters Immobilized-A instead. Equipment is removed instead of entering Immobilized-A.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Replacing a game element is *not* movement. No LoF checks, ZoC checks, AROs, or Order Expenditure Sequence elements trigger from the act of replacement. Treat it as an instantaneous substitution.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Because this is not movement, skills like Sixth Sense, Stealth, or effects that trigger “when a Trooper moves into ZoC/LoF” do **not** apply here.

---

### **Center-to-Center vs. Edge-to-Edge Consequences**
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Center-to-center placement guarantees the replacement model remains as close as possible to the original location, but may accidentally remove Cover or push the model out of base contact with scenery.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Edge-to-edge placement is often tactically advantageous and is intentionally allowed—this is not considered exploitation. Gaining Cover, entering Engaged State, or improving angles is *explicitly legal*. The only restriction is that it cannot be used to *break* existing Engaged State.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Players should expect that models with transformation mechanics (TAG ejection systems, Seed-Embryo <-> Adult forms, etc.) may “snap” into better defensive positioning through legal edge-matching.

---

### **Engaged State Interactions**
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** A Trooper may enter Engaged due to an edge-aligned swap. This can occur even if the original Trooper was not Engaged.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** However, if the original Trooper *was* Engaged, replacement cannot be used as a loophole to escape CC. The replacement must remain Engaged unless physical space makes this impossible (rare).

---

### **Silhouette and Space Constraints**
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** When up-sizing (S1 → S2, S2 → S5, etc.), edge alignment often prevents accidental Immobilized-A results. If neither alignment option fits, only then does Immobilized-A apply.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** When down-sizing (e.g., TAG ejection), the smaller model always fits, so Immobilized-A should almost never occur.

---

### **Hidden Deployment, Markers, and Replacement**
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Emerging from Hidden Deployment is *not* a replacement action: it is a reveal during declaration. Therefore, the rules of this section do not apply.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** However, *Marker to Model* transitions (e.g., Camouflage breaking) *are* replacements and should use these rules.

---

### **Transformations & States**
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Any special rule that causes a Trooper to change form uses Replacing Game Elements unless its own rules override this section.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** If a transformation would cause illegal placement *and* there is no optionality (Obligatory), then the Immobilized-A safeguard prevents the game from breaking. This prevents soft-locking the player in terrain.

---

### **Common Misunderstandings**
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Replacement is *not* deployment, so deployment restrictions (like exclusion zones) do not apply.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Replacement does *not* allow a Trooper to bypass enemy ARO presence. Only the position changes, not turn order or activation rules.

### **Practical Tactical Notes**
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Use edge alignment to gain Cover when emerging from Marker State—this is one of the strongest defensive uses of the rule.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Pilots ejecting from a TAG can intentionally place edge-aligned to avoid bad LoF or to gain Cover.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Shasvastii players can often choose better angles when a Seed hatches by using edge matching.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]** Players should pre-measure edge vs center positions to avoid disputes, since both placements are valid options.
