## Game Round

Infinity games are divided into Game Rounds, or Rounds, during which both players have the chance to take an active role. This means each Round is divided into two Player Turns, or Turns, one for each player.

At the start of a Round, a new Player Turn begins, following the turn order determined during the Initiative Phase.

## Player Turn

During each Turn there is an Active Player and a Reactive Player. The Active Player can activate his Troopers and execute actions, while the Reactive Player can react to the activation of the Active Player’s Troopers (see ARO: Automatic Reaction Order ).

## Start of the Turn: Tactical Phase

The Tactical Phase is the step when the Active Player makes a series of quick checks before they start activating their Troopers.

### Executive Use of Command Tokens

The Active Player may make Executive Use of their Command Tokens.

### Retreat! Check

The Active Player counts his Victory Points to see if he enters a Retreat! situation (see End-Game Conditions ).

### Loss of Lieutenant Check

The Active Player checks whether he is in Loss of Lieutenant.

### Order Count

The Active Player counts how many Orders he has at his disposal for this Turn, by counting the number, type and States of his Troopers.

For each Regular Trooper deployed on the table, as a Model or Marker, that is not in a Null State ( Unconscious, Dead …), the Active Player adds one Regular Order to the corresponding Order Pool.

For each Irregular Trooper deployed on the table, as a Model or Marker, that is not in a Null State, the Active Player places an Irregular Order Token on the table, making it clear which Combat Group it belongs to.

If the Active Player has a Lieutenant deployed on the table, as a Model or Marker, that is not in a Null State, the Active Player places the Special Lieutenant Order in a visible place.

For each Trooper with Tactical Awareness deployed on the table, as a Model or Marker, that is not in a Null State, the Active Player places a Tactical Order on the table, next to the Trooper.

Each Combat Group has its own independent Order Pool, Irregular Orders, and Tactical Orders.

Troopers that have yet to be deployed on the table as Models or Markers (for example, due to the Combat Jump Special Skill) do not contribute their Order to the Order Pool. Undeployed Troopers' Orders are Private Information, so their player can keep their Orders secret and out of sight of the opponent.

## Impetuous Phase

During this phase, the Active Player may activate each Trooper with the Impetuous Special Skill once, without spending an Order.

## Orders Phase

This is the main phase of the Player Turn, when the Active Player gets to use their Order Pool, Irregular Orders, Tactical Orders, and the Lieutenant Special Order to activate their Troopers. The Active Player has no obligation to expend all Orders. However, unused Orders cannot be reserved for subsequent Turns and are lost.

## States Phase

Once the Active Player runs out of Orders, or decides not to use the remaining ones, both players will carry out any checks for those States or Skills that require it. These rolls do not generate AROs.

## End of the Turn

Once all checks are made, the Active Player Turn ends.



**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
## Start of the Turn: Tactical Phase
*Everything in this phase happens before any Impetuous or Regular Orders are spent*.  
Double-checking the following avoids many disputes:
- Is the Lieutenant alive and not Isolated?  
- Are new States affecting Order generation?  
- Are there AD troops still off-table whose Orders remain Private Information?

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
If a Trooper enters or leaves a Null State *at the end of the previous turn*, the Order count changes immediately for the next Tactical Phase.  
For example: If a Trooper was healed during the opponent’s State Phase, it contributes an Order again this turn.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Command Token “Executive Use” happens here only.  
A frequent oversight: you must move Troopers between Combat Groups **before** counting Orders.  
If you count first and then move them, you must recount.  
This matters for Order efficiency and for preventing confusion about who actually generated which Order.


## Impetuous Phase

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Impetuous activations occur *after* Order counting but *before* spending Regular or Irregular Orders.  
This can cause tricky sequencing when an Impetuous Trooper is in a dangerous position.  

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
If a Trooper becomes Impetuous by entering a State or effect during the previous turn, it will activate here immediately unless some rule explicitly forbids it.
## Orders Phase

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
A common misunderstanding:  
You may alternate which Trooper is activated at any time.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Tactical Order management interacts strongly with Fireteams and NCO.  
Remember: Tactical Orders are **not** added to the Order Pool but remain visible and usable only by specific Troopers or abilities.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Using the Lieutenant Special Order is Open Information even though the identity of the Lieutenant is Private Information.  
This frequently leads to “soft reveals,” but it is intentional within the rules system.

## States Phase

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
The States Phase is the resolution point for effects like:
- End-of-turn Dodge movement
- Clearance rolls for certain Immobilized or Targeted effects
- Automatic cancellations based on timing

These checks **do not generate AROs**, and this is a commonly misunderstood detail.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Because many State cancellation rolls occur here, the positioning of Troopers at the *end* of the last Order of the turn often matters more than players realize.  
For example, ending in Total Cover may protect a Trooper who attempts Reset rolls for Immobilized later.

## End of the Turn

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
This step rarely has explicit mechanical effects beyond concluding the sequence, but it is important for memory consistency.  
Some groups adopt a ritual checklist:
- All State rolls done?
- All Tokens removed?
- All ARO-triggering declarations from the final Order resolved?

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
If the Lieutenant died during the turn and no Chain of Command triggered, the new Lieutenant is appointed **now**, not during the Tactical Phase.  
This timing matters: the army will spend the *next* Tactical Phase in Loss of Lieutenant unless Chain of Command prevented it at the moment of the check.

# General Interactions & Common Pitfalls

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Because each player has an “Active Turn” within a Round, timing disputes often arise about when Orders refresh.  
Remember:
- Order Pools refresh at the **start of *your* Tactical Phase**, not globally each Round.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
States that suppress Order generation (Null, Isolated, Dead) are checked only at the Tactical Phase, but their effects can begin earlier.  
Example: A Trooper rendered Isolated during the opponent’s Active Turn will not contribute an Order next turn.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Reactive decision-making is influenced heavily by the Game Sequence.  
For example, successfully forcing the opposing Lieutenant into Null during your Active Turn gives you a full Round where they are either:
- In Loss of Lieutenant, or
- Forced to reveal Chain of Command

# Short Tactical Commentary

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Understanding Game Sequence interactions is essential for predicting how many resources an opponent will have in the next turn.  
Eliminating or disabling several Order contributors near the end of your own Active Turn is often more efficient than early aggression, because the Order Pool reduction is felt immediately next Round.
