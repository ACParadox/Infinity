# Order Expenditure Sequence

Each time the Active Player decides to spend an Order (of whatever type) to activate a Trooper, follow these steps:

1. Activation: The Active Player declares which Trooper will activate.

2. Reactive Player's AROs

3. Declaration of the Second Skill: The Active Player declares the second Short Skill of the Order, if applicable. If movements are declared, the player measures where the Trooper can move, chooses the route, and places the Trooper at the final point of its movement.

4. Reactive Player's AROs

5. Resolution: Check that the Skills and pieces of Equipment whose use has been declared meet their respective Requirements, make all necessary range measurements, determine which MODs apply, and then both players make their Rolls. If any Skill or piece of Equipment doesn't meet its Requirements, the Trooper instead performs an Idle.

All details and choices related to the execution of a Basic Short Skill, Short Skill, or Long Skill must be specified when it is declared.

For instance, if you declare a movement, you must specify the entire route.

If you declare an Attack, you must specify which Weapon, piece of Equipment, Special Skill, Ammunition, or other option will be used, who the targets are, the specific point where the shots are taken from, how the Burst is divided between targets, etc.

When declaring Alert!, any Basic Short Skill, or the Long Skills Jump or Climb, their Requirements must be checked when the Skill is declared. For all other Skills that are declared as part of an Order or ARO, their Requirements must be checked during step 5 of the Order Expenditure Sequence: Resolution.

If the use of a Skill, weapon, or a piece of Equipment is declared and when the Requirements are checked they are not met, the action is cancelled and the Trooper instead performs an Idle. Additionally, any declared uses of Disposable items are expended and lost.

The type, status, and expenditure of any Order ( Regular, Irregular, Tactical, the Lieutenant Order, which Combat Group it belongs to, whether it has been spent or not...) is Open Information. Players must place the Tokens for each and every one of their Orders in a visible place on the gaming table, and they must inform their opponent every time they spend an Order to activate a Trooper, even if it is the Lieutenant Order.

If a Trooper receives and removes Wounds or States in the same Order, the positive effect is always applied first, followed by the negative effect.

Infinity. Fair Play

To streamline the game, the opponent may assist the Active Player with measurements, checking LoF, etc. This will make the game more exciting and dynamic. Fair play is a win-win for both players and is essential for a good game atmosphere. If mistakes are made, ideally both players should come to an agreement to fix the problem.

The tagline “in Infinity it's always your turn” isn't just about using AROs to react during your opponent's turns, but that it is also a tool to interact continuously with your opponent, creating a constant dialogue that makes the games more engaging and enjoyable. This makes it easier to keep learning and improving your game skills, and has helped create a tight community of players, whose friendship reaches across borders and oceans.

## ORDER EXPENDITURE SEQUENCE — INTERACTIONS & COMMONLY MISUNDERSTOOD POINTS

 The first step: Activation: The Active Player declares which Trooper will activate. The player also declares the first skill.

• **Declare → AROs → Declare → AROs → Resolve**  
  Players must strictly follow the 5-step sequence. The most common mistakes happen when players try to “pre-measure”, “pre-commit”, or treat the steps as flexible. They are not.  
  Infinity is designed so that *information is revealed gradually* through declarations.

• **Skill declarations happen BEFORE measurements**  
  This is one of the most important fundamentals of Infinity: players must declare Skills *without* measuring LoF, distance, ZoC, or ranges (except when the rules explicitly allow it, such as Deployment Zone size).  
  Many misunderstandings come from assuming you can check viability before declaring; however, *you are allowed to declare illegal Skills* — they simply become **Idle** during Resolution if Requirements are not met.

• **Illegal declarations are NOT corrected mid-order**  
  If a declared weapon cannot draw LoF, if a Hacking Program is out of range, or if a target was invalid:  
  → The declaration stands, becomes **Idle**, and **Disposable items are still spent**.  
  This is intended — declaring “blind” is part of Infinity’s tactical uncertainty.

• **FULL information about an Attack must be declared immediately**  
  When declaring any Attack, the Active Player must state:  
  – Which weapon/program/equipment  
  – Ammunition type  
  – Exact target(s)  
  – The point of origin of the attack  
  – Burst distribution  
  Players may not add or adjust this information later in the Order.

• **Movement declarations must specify the entire path**  
  You cannot “see how it goes” or decide mid-movement.  
  The route is fixed at declaration, but you can measure Movement distances immediately after declaring any Skill with the Movement Label 
  This matters for ARO eligibility, template coverage, and LoF timing.
  Ex.: If you move a certain path, where in that path a hidden unit now gains ARO and gets reveal, it'd be impossible to un-do the reveal of the unit by changing the movement path.

  Note: Declaring a movement and stating purpose helps communication, things like: I move here while hugging the wall to get limited cover. 

• **AROs must be declared immediately when gained**  
  AROs are not optional delays; if a Trooper had a valid ARO opportunity and the player did not declare it, it is lost.  
  A common mistake: “I’ll wait to see the second Short Skill.”  
  That is illegal unless the Trooper received no ARO during the first declaration.

• **Second ARO window exists only for Troopers that did NOT gain ARO in the first step**  
  If you already had an ARO opportunity but chose not to react, you do NOT gain a second chance when the second Short Skill is declared.

• **All decisions in an Order are simultaneous**  
  Even if a Trooper declares a Move + Attack and shoots from the starting point, and the enemy reacts by shooting at the end of the movement, the actions are still mechanically simultaneous.  
  This is often misunderstood: *the declared timing affects modifiers and LoF, but not the simultaneity of resolution.*

• **Idle does NOT break Stealth or Marker States on its own**  
  If a declaration becomes Idle because Requirements were not met, the Trooper still reveals itself if the *declared* Skill would have revealed it.  
  Example: declaring BS Attack through smoke (illegal due to no LoF) still breaks Camouflage.

• **Order type, origin, and expenditure are ALWAYS Open Information**  
  Players must track Orders visibly. The Active Player must clearly announce the expenditure of each Order—this prevents hidden manipulation of order economy.

• **Positive effects before negative effects**  
  If a Trooper heals itself and is then hit during the same Order, the healing applies first, then the damaging effect.  
  This interaction is frequently forgotten in complex multi-effect orders.

• **Disposable items are lost even if the declaration fails**  
  This rule is very often missed:  
  – Grenades thrown at out-of-range targets  
  – Mines placed illegally  
  – Hacking Programs declared with no valid target  
  All expend charges even if the final action is Idle.

• **Template coverage is only checked at Resolution**  
  You may declare a template even if it is unclear whether the template will cover the target.  
  This prevents “pre-checking” template viability and keeps the fog-of-war element intact.

• **Misdeclared Short Skills are NOT interchangeable**  
  Example: Declaring Dodge as the first Short Skill does not let you convert it into Move or Idle voluntarily.  
  Only Requirements failure causes Idle.

• **“Hidden retroactive undo” is not allowed**  
  Once anything is declared in an Order, you cannot retract it, even if you immediately realize an oversight  
  — unless both players agree to Fair Play correction.  
  But the rules themselves do not allow retraction.
