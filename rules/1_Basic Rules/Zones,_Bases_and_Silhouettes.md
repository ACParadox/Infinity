# Zones, Bases and Silhouettes

This ruleset frequently uses terms that define the interaction between Troopers and other game elements.

## Base Contact/Silhouette Contact

The Troopers are positioned with their Silhouettes in contact with each other. This term also applies when the Trooper’s Silhouette is in contact with a game element that may or may not have a base, like a piece of terrain.

A Trooper is always in Silhouette contact with themselves.

## Inside

To consider a Trooper to be inside a defined game area (for example a Zone of Control, a Zero Visibility Zone, or a Difficult Terrain Zone ) its Silhouette must be in contact with the zone, or be at least partially within the zone.

## Totally Inside

When a rule specifies “totally inside”, then the Trooper’s entire Silhouette must be inside the defined area.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Zones are defined independently of bases and Silhouettes, but many players subconsciously tie them together. A Zone (such as Zone of Control, Hacking Area, or scenario Zones) is always measured from a reference point defined by the rule, most commonly the Trooper’s base edge or Silhouette volume. The Zone does not “follow” the model dynamically during an Order unless the rule explicitly says it does; checks are made at specific timing points (start/end of Order, declaration, or resolution).

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
Zone of Control (ZoC) is measured horizontally from the Trooper’s Silhouette, not from the base top or weapon. Vertical distance matters only insofar as the straight-line distance exceeds the ZoC radius. This is frequently missed on multi-level tables, where players intuitively project ZoC as a vertical cylinder; it is actually a sphere measured in 3D space.

A Trooper’s base is a gameplay convenience, not the authoritative representation of volume. The Silhouette Attribute always defines the true volume of the Trooper for Line of Fire, contact, overlapping, and zone measurements. The base may be used for speed, but when precision matters (tight gaps, vertical cover, partial exposure), the Silhouette Template overrides the base.

Being “inside a Zone” does not require the entire Silhouette to be inside it. Any part of the Silhouette intersecting the Zone is sufficient, unless the rule explicitly requires total inclusion. This is especially relevant for ZoC AROs, scenario scoring Zones, and Coherency checks.

Contact between bases is not the same as contact between Silhouettes. Engaged State, base contact, and certain placement rules rely on base contact, while Line of Fire, visibility, and obstruction rely on Silhouette volume. Mixing these concepts leads to frequent errors, especially when large Silhouettes with small bases are involved.

Silhouettes are abstract and intentionally ignore miniature pose, limbs, weapons, banners, or scenic elements. A model that “looks exposed” may still be fully protected if the Silhouette Template is blocked. Conversely, a model crouching or leaning does not reduce its Silhouette for any purpose unless a rule explicitly changes it.

Markers use Silhouettes just like Models, even though they lack a physical shape. Players sometimes assume Markers are infinitely thin or point-based; this is incorrect. A Marker occupies a full Silhouette volume and interacts with Zones, LoF, and distance exactly as defined by its Silhouette value.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**  
When checking whether a Trooper is inside multiple Zones simultaneously (for example ZoC and a mission Zone), each Zone is checked independently using the same Silhouette reference. A Trooper can legally be inside overlapping Zones even if no single point of the Silhouette is shared by all Zones.

Replacing a Trooper with a different Silhouette (due to States, Skills, or Equipment) can retroactively change Zone interactions without “moving” the Trooper. This can result in gaining or losing ZoC contact, Cover, or Engaged State purely because the volume changed, which is rules-legal and often overlooked.

Coherency checks rely on ZoC, not on base-to-base distance. Two Troopers may appear very close on the table, but if their Silhouettes are positioned diagonally or vertically, they can still be out of Coherency. Always measure from the Silhouette, not from where the bases seem to touch.

Players often assume Zones are symmetric in timing. They are not. Entering a Zone, starting inside a Zone, and ending inside a Zone can all have different consequences depending on the rule. Always confirm when the Zone is checked, not just where the Trooper ends up.
