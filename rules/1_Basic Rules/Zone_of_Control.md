# Zone of Control

Troopers have an awareness of their surroundings. Zone of Control (ZoC) is defined as an imaginary cylinder with a radius of 8 inches from the outer edge of the Trooper’s Silhouette, extending 8 inches up from the top of the Silhouette and 8 inches down from the bottom of the Silhouette.

### Zone of Control and AROs

Enemies entering or acting inside the Zone of Control of a Trooper while remaining outside that Trooper’s LoF can be reacted to.

Players can check the Zone of Control (ZoC). Measurements must always be made from the Active Trooper, checking a maximum of 8 inches from any point along their path. If there are Reactive Troopers or Game Elements within the Zone of Control (ZoC) of the Active Trooper, they can declare an ARO (see Order Expenditure Sequence ).

Maybe you’re wondering when, or how, exactly you can check the Zone of Control. It’s easy: once the Active Model has been placed at the end point of its movement, the two players can measure the Zone of Control along the entire route that was traced on the game board, from its starting point to its final position, these both included. This is the most natural and convenient way to check the Zone of Control.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Zone of Control is often misunderstood as a “flat 8-inch bubble”. It is actually a **three-dimensional cylinder** measured from the outer edge of the Trooper’s Silhouette, extending 8 inches horizontally and also 8 inches up and down. Vertical separation almost never protects you from ZoC interactions unless the height difference exceeds that vertical limit. Players commonly forget that ZoC applies through floors, ceilings, and walls, because it does not require Line of Fire.

ZoC is checked from the **Active Trooper**, not from each Reactive Trooper individually. During movement, ZoC is evaluated along the entire declared path, from start point to end point. Even if the Active Trooper only briefly clips ZoC mid-movement, this can still generate AROs. This frequently catches players off guard when “sliding past” corners or narrow corridors.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
ZoC-based AROs do **not** require LoF. This makes them especially relevant against smoke, total cover, walls, and zero-visibility situations. Skills like Dodge, Reset, Discover, or certain Hacking Programs can be declared purely due to ZoC presence, even if the Reactive Trooper cannot see the enemy at all.

Entering ZoC alone does not always generate an ARO. Stealth, Marker States, and specific Skill requirements can suppress ZoC-based reactions. This is a common rules pitfall: players often assume ZoC automatically grants an ARO, when in reality several Skills explicitly prevent ZoC-triggered AROs unless additional conditions are met (such as an Attack declaration).

ZoC is a **key measurement reference** for many unrelated mechanics: Coherency, Minelayer placement, Peripherals, Sensor, Morpho-Scan, Deactivator, Baggage Reloads, and various States. Because these checks are independent, a Trooper can be “inside ZoC” for one rule while that same interaction does nothing for another. Always confirm which rule is actually calling for a ZoC check.

During Deployment, ZoC measurements may involve **Private Information**. When placing Deployables, Decoys, Holoechoes, or Minelayer equipment, opponents are required to look away while ZoC is checked. Forgetting this can accidentally leak Hidden Deployment or marker positioning information, especially in competitive play.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
ZoC is not blocked by terrain, models, or scenery. Total Cover, walls, and elevation do not interfere with ZoC unless a rule explicitly says otherwise.

ZoC interacts strongly with Cautious Movement. To avoid AROs entirely, a Cautious Movement must **begin and end** outside all enemy ZoC (and Hacking Area, if applicable). Passing through ZoC mid-movement is allowed only if both endpoints are clear; this distinction is easy to miss and often misplayed in tight terrain.

A Trooper is considered “inside” a Zone of Control if **any part of its Silhouette** is within or touching that zone. Only rules that specify “totally inside” require full Silhouette containment. This subtle wording difference matters for movement edges, objective interaction, and area-based effects tied to ZoC.

Strategically, ZoC represents **threat projection**, not vision. Even low-BS or defensively weak Troopers exert real board control simply by existing. Savvy players exploit ZoC to tax Orders, force Dodges, block safe movement lanes, and enable indirect interactions (Sensor sweeps, Reset traps, or ZoC-based hacking), even when no direct firefight is possible.
