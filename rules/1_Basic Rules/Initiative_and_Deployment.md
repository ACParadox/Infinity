## Initiative Roll

Before the game begins, players make a Face to Face Roll using their respective Lieutenant 's WIP Attributes. So, both players roll a D20 and compare their results and the one with the higher result (that does not exceed their Lieutenant's WIP Attribute) is the winner of the Initiative Roll. The winner of the Initiative Roll can choose between keeping Deployment and keeping Initiative. Whichever option the winner declined goes to the other player.

In the case of a tie, or both players failing, repeat the Initiative Roll. In the Initiative Roll, the WIP Attribute of the Lieutenant is Open Information.

### Keeping Initiative

The player who kept Initiative gets to choose which player has the first Player Turn and which player has the second Player Turn in each Game Round. This order is maintained throughout the game.

### Keeping Deployment

The player who kept Deployment gets to choose who deploys first, and in which Deployment Zone.

## Deployment Phase

This phase comes after the Initiative Roll, once the Initiative and Deployment have been chosen. During the Deployment Phase, before the game begins, players place their Troopers on the battlefield.

The Deployment Phase is divided into the following steps:

1. Player One Deployment: Following the order established in the Initiative Roll, as decided by the player who kept Deployment, the first player places all but one of his Troopers totally inside his Deployment Zone. If a player uses a rule that allows them to keep back more than one Trooper (such as Strategos, Command Tokens, or scenario conditions), they must declare how many Troopers they are keeping back.

2. Player Two Deployment: Once Player One is done, Player Two places all but one of her Troopers totally inside her Deployment Zone. If a player uses a rule that allows them to keep back more than one Trooper (such as Strategos, Command Tokens, or scenario conditions), they must declare how many Troopers they are keeping back.

3. Player One Last Trooper: Then, Player One places the Trooper he kept back during step one.

4. Player Two Last Trooper: Finally, Player Two places the Trooper she kept back during step two.

Some Special Skills, such as Forward Deployment, allow players to place certain Troopers outside their Deployment Zones.

Other Special Skills, like Combat Jump, allow players not to place certain Troopers on the battlefield at all during the Deployment Phase.

Finally, some Special Skills may alter the Deployment rules for both players.

## Deployment Rules

To deploy your Troopers on the table, follow these rules:

- The base of each Trooper must be totally inside the Deployment Zone.

- Troopers cannot be deployed in contact with enemy and neutral Models, Markers, or Tokens, or in contact with any mission objective, no matter the Special Skills they have.

- When a Trooper deploys, its owner can choose to deploy it Prone. To do so, simply place a Prone State Token beside the Trooper.

- Unless previously agreed upon, Troopers cannot deploy in a location without enough space to fit their entire base.

- Players can measure the size of the Deployment Zones, those areas where Troopers possessing the Forward Deployment Special Skill can deploy, the central line of the game table and any Exclusion Zone the game scenario specifies.

Climb, restrictions for Troopers and vertical surfaces:

- Troopers cannot be deployed on a vertical surface, neither during the Deployment Phase nor during the game.

## Initiative and Deployment FAQs

Q: During your main part of the Deployment Phase, can you reposition Troopers, Deployables, etc?

A: In their main Deployment Phase step, players can reposition Troopers, Deployables, etc. As soon as a Roll or measurement is made for those game elements, their position is fixed, and they cannot be repositioned during the Deployment Phase.

For example, you can reposition a Trooper as many times as you want, but after you create a Fireteam and adjust the placement of the Fireteam members during the Coherency Check, you can no longer reposition any member of that Fireteam.

Q: In the Deployment Phase do you have to check ZoC when a Trooper uses Minelayer, or Coherency when deploying Decoys or Holoechoes?

A: Yes, but your opponent must look away so that you can check the ZoC/Coherency without revealing Private Information.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The Initiative Roll is the only Face to Face Roll that happens *before* the game truly begins. Because it uses the Lieutenant’s WIP and that WIP is Open Information for this roll only, players often try to infer Lieutenant candidates during list-building. Remember that revealing the Lieutenant’s WIP here does NOT make the Lieutenant himself Open Information later.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Winning the Initiative Roll is extremely powerful. Deployment advantage can outweigh going first, depending on table layout and mission. Newer players tend to default to “I go first,” (for an alpha strike) but being able to choose the *better* Deployment Zone can swing the entire game by giving stronger firelanes, safer hiding spots, or better objective access.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Choosing “I want the second turn” is often good in objective-scoring missions. Infinity heavily rewards last-turn objective control, and most missions are scored at the end of Round 3. Only choose Initiative to go first if you intend to apply early pressure or cripple enemy order economy.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Deployment order matters: the player who deploys second often gains strong informational advantage. You get to see nearly the entire opposing layout before deciding where to place your reserve model(s). Use this to counter enemy attack pieces, protect vulnerable units, or set up strong ARO pieces.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Reserve models (the “last Trooper”) are extremely tactically important. Common uses:
- Positioning a sniper/Missile Launcher to counter an enemy attack piece.
- Hiding a key Specialist once the enemy has revealed troops or attack vectors.
- Placing a strong attack unit where it can exploit openings made by the opponent’s deployment.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
If using abilities that grant extra reserve models (Strategos, Command Tokens, scenario conditions), declare the number of held-back troops BEFORE deploying anything. Forgetting this timing is a common mistake.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Deployment Coherency checks for rules like Fireteams, Peripherals, Decoys, or Holoechoes allow the player to *measure* before finalizing positions, once a Coherency check or ZoC check is performed, placement is locked.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Your opponent must look away when you check ZoC for Minelayer, Coherency for Decoys, or Holoecho placement. Because these contain Private Information, any measurement errors must be corrected without exposing what the markers represent.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Troopers cannot deploy touching enemy models, markers, or objectives. This restriction often matters when placing mines, CrazyKoalas, or other Deployable Equipment with forward deployment abilities.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Troopers cannot deploy on vertical surfaces. This prevents corner-case abuse like deploying a Climbing Plus unit halfway up a wall to avoid LoF. Any elevated deployment requires a legal horizontal surface big enough for the base.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Forward Deployment and similar skills allow specific models to deploy farther up the table, but do not allow bending other deployment rules. They still cannot deploy in base contact with objectives, and still must be placed fully within their allowed distance.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Combat Jump, Hidden Deployment, and Parachutist troops keep their Orders “off-table” until they appear as Models/Markers. Their existence is Private Information. Because opponents cannot see these Orders, they must assume surprises are coming.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Deploying second can help you counter-drop against Combat Jump threats. Place strong AROs or widespread coverage units last, specifically to deter risky AD landings.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Deployment is the single most influential phase in Infinity. Good deployment can compensate for going second, losing Initiative, or even a list disadvantage. Bad deployment often determines the game before Turn 1 begins.
