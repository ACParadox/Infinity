# Line of Fire

Line of Fire (LoF) is the criterion by which players determine whether a Trooper can see its target. The Line of Fire (LoF) is an imaginary straight line that joins any point of the volume of a Model, Token, Marker or valid target to any point of the volume of another.

Remember that the volume of a Model, Marker, or Token is determined by the value of its Silhouette Attribute.

In Infinity, Troopers have a Line of Fire (LoF) angle of 180°, matching the front half of their base, as shown on the base of the miniatures.

For a Trooper to be able to draw LoF to its target, it must meet these conditions:

- The target must be totally or partially within the Trooper's front 180˚ arc, unless some Special Skill or piece of Equipment ignores this restriction.

- The Trooper must be able to see part of the volume of its target, with a minimum size of 3x3mm.

- LoF can be drawn from any point of the Trooper's Silhouette to any point of the target's Silhouette without being obstructed by any pieces of scenery or the Silhouette of any Model (friendly or enemy).

To facilitate movement and interactions during game, we must consider the following exceptions when drawing LoF.

- As long as a Trooper can draw LoF to their target, the target can draw LoF to that Trooper as well, as long as that Trooper is within the target's front 180˚ arc.

- Troopers that declared any Skill with the Movement Label can draw a 360˚ LoF all along their route.

- Troopers do not obstruct LoF all along their route.

- Markers do not obstruct LoF.

- Unless specified otherwise, Markers have a 360˚ LoF.

- Troopers who are in Engaged State cannot draw LoF to Troopers or game elements that are not in Engaged State with them. In addition, Troopers who are in Engaged State are not affected by Visibility Conditions or by other rules affecting LoF.

Sixth Sense

- Allows the user to respond to Attacks (and only Attacks) directed at them by enemies outside their LoF. For the purposes of drawing Line of Fire to the attackers, the user has a 360° LoF arc, and if they are in Engaged State, they can draw LoF to attackers outside their Close Combat.

360º Visor

- This type of Visor provides its user with a 360˚ LoF arc, rather than the usual 180˚ arc.

Zero Visibility Zone

- LoF cannot be drawn into, through, or out of a Zero Visibility Zone.

White Noise Zone

- A White Noise Zone acts as a Zero Visibility Zone, but only for Troopers with the Marksmanship Special Skill or equipped with a Multispectral Visor of any Level, or any other piece of Equipment that specifies the same.

Miniature poses and irregular scenery can make LoF hard to determine. In those cases, it is up to the players to reach an agreement. Trying to look at the target from the miniature’s point of view can be useful. To make checking LoF easier it is advised to make use of Silhouette Templates, rulers, laser pointers... to see whether anything obstructs the LoF.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Line of Fire – Interactions, Clarifications, and Common Pitfalls

• LoF checks always use the Silhouette Template, never the physical miniature. This avoids arguments caused by dynamic poses, leaning weapons, or scenic bases.

• You may draw LoF from ANY point of your Silhouette to ANY point of the target’s Silhouette. This means shifting around the edge of your cylinder is legal for LoF purposes even if the physical model cannot physically “lean.”

• Minimum visible area is 3x3mm on the target’s volume. Any amount ≥3mm in height AND width counts. People often forget this threshold—anything smaller does NOT grant LoF.

• The 180° front arc restriction applies **before** checking visibility. If the target is not inside the front arc, you cannot declare Skills requiring LoF unless a rule says otherwise (360º Visor, etc).

• If you have LoF to a target, that target automatically has LoF back as long as you are in its front arc. 

• Movement-granting Skills give **360° LoF during movement only**. The troop does NOT keep 360° after the Order is finished. This is easily forgotten and affects ARO generation and Attack validity.

• Troopers do not block LoF during movement. This includes friendly and enemy Troopers. However, after they stop moving, normal obstruction rules apply.

• Markers never block LoF, even if they represent a large unit (TO Camo, Holoecho, etc).

• Engaged State eliminates LoF to anything outside the Close Combat. Troopers in CC cannot draw LoF “past” their melee opponent even if physically visible.

• Zero Visibility Zones prevent LoF from being drawn through them entirely. This overrides the 3x3 mm visibility requirement—LoF simply cannot exist across the boundary.

• Silhouette replacement (such as with larger Holoecho or TAG states) can create new angles for LoF or expose new vulnerabilities. Players should check arcs strictly after any replacement.

• Checking LoF with a laser is recommended, but remember: the laser must represent ANY point of the silhouette cylinder, not just the centerline.

• LoF is not declared or “locked in” until Resolution. You may declare an Attack even if you are unsure—if LoF is not valid during Resolution, the Skill becomes Idle.

• When in doubt about LoF, always check from both Troopers’ perspectives. The game assumes symmetry except for arc restrictions.

• When multiple Levels, balconies, or scatter terrain are involved, remember that LoF is a straight 3D line. The Silhouette cylinder extends vertically. Being able to see the top slice of a model often counts.

• It is legal (and common) to position a Trooper so that it sees only one enemy model while denying LoF to others by using angles, elevation, or corners. This is intentional Infinity gameplay, not an exploit.

• The infamous “slice-the-pie” maneuver works because LoF is drawn point-to-point. You reveal one enemy at a time by adjusting your silhouette’s angle around terrain edges.

• LoF must be re-evaluated after each Short Skill. A Trooper might see nothing during the first movement path but reveal or lose LoF later based on its exact final point.
