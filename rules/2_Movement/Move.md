# Move

Movement Module

Common Skills
BASIC SHORT SKILL
Movement, No LoF, No Roll.

Basic Common Skill that allows Troopers to move about the battlefield.

Players must check the Requirements of this Skill when declaring it.

- At least half the Trooper's base must stay in contact with the surface on which they are moving, except when vaulting over obstacles (see General Movement Rules ).

- Any surface they move on must be at least half as wide as their base.

- The Trooper must be able to finish their movement on a surface that's equal to or larger than their base.

- Allows the user to traverse the battlefield up to a distance determined by his MOV Attribute.

- By declaring Move, the user may move up to the first value of his MOV Attribute in inches.

- If the user declares Move again as their second Basic Short Skill of the Order, they may then move up to the second value of their MOV Attribute in inches.

- Move must follow the General Movement Rules as well as the Moving and Measuring rules.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Enemies may declare AROs at any point along the movement path, not just the final position. This allows reactive players to “wait” and choose the most favorable moment (range band, cover state, or visibility condition) when resolving their ARO.
Move does not lock you into a straight line. The path may bend, curve, or zig-zag, as long as total distance does not exceed the trooper’s MOV value and the path is legal. This enables corner-peeking, hugging cover, and precise engagement control.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
The entire movement path must be declared conceptually before measuring. You cannot “test” a partial path, stop, and then change direction based on enemy reactions. The route is a commitment, even if measurement happens later.

Move interacts strongly with Line of Fire rules: enemy troopers can gain LoF during any segment of movement even if LoF does not exist at the start or end position. This is one of the most common sources of ARO disputes.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Troopers do not block Line of Fire while moving. You cannot use another moving model as temporary mobile cover during the same Order. This applies even if models physically overlap on the table.

Markers (Camouflage, Impersonation, etc.) moving with Move do not block Line of Fire either. This prevents using marker movement to shield other troopers or to deny AROs indirectly.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Move + Move is not “safer” by default than Move + Attack. If an enemy gains LoF at any point during the first Move, they can still declare a valid ARO even if the second Skill is also Move. 

Also moving a second time means that if the second move triggers an ARO, the Trooper won't be able to defend against it.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Careful movement can deny Cover bonuses to enemies. By stepping just far enough around a corner to see a target that is no longer in base contact with terrain, you can force them to lose Cover while still benefiting from it yourself.

Move is the backbone of objective play. Many objectives require base contact or proximity, and understanding exact movement distances (including vertical and diagonal paths) often decides games more than firepower.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Vertical movement is not free. Without declaring Jump or Climb, Move alone cannot traverse vertical surfaces. Players often incorrectly assume short vertical gaps or slopes are automatically included in Move.

When combined with skills like Cautious Movement, Stealth, or Super-Jump, Move becomes heavily modified. Always verify whether the interaction replaces Move, restricts ARO generation, or simply alters its conditions.

Move can be used aggressively to bait AROs. Forcing enemies to reveal ARO positions, expend limited-use defenses, or commit to unfavorable range bands is often more valuable than dealing damage.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategically, efficient Move usage reduces Order expenditure. Saving even 1–2 inches per Order across a turn can translate into an extra attack, objective action, or reposition later in the game.

In reactive play, understanding enemy Move options lets you pre-measure threat ranges mentally. Placing troopers so that a single Move cannot safely reach an objective or firing position is a core defensive skill.

Finally, Move is the Skill most affected by table quality. Dense, vertical, and well-balanced terrain dramatically increases the tactical depth of Move, while sparse tables turn it into a liability rather than a tool.
