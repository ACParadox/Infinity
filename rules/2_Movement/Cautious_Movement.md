# Cautious Movement

Movement Module

Common Skills
 LONG SKILL
Movement, No Roll.

Under certain circumstances, Troopers can declare this Common Skill to move about the battlefield without attracting enemy AROs.

- Allows the Trooper to move up to the first value of his MOV Attribute, generating no enemy AROs in the process.

- Restriction. For this Cautious Movement to generate no AROs, it must begin and end at two points on the game table that lie outside the LoF and ZoC of all enemy Models and Markers.

- If the Cautious Movement begins or ends within the LoF or ZoC of any enemy Trooper (in Model form) or Marker, the Trooper generates AROs as usual.

- Restriction. For the Cautious Movement of a Hackable Trooper in Model form to generate no AROs, it must also begin and end at two points on the game table that lie outside the Hacking Area of any enemy Hackers.

- If a Hackable Trooper is in Model form and its Cautious Movement begins or ends inside the Hacking Area of any enemy Hackers, the Trooper generates AROs as usual. If a non -Hackable Trooper is in Model form and its Cautious Movement begins or ends inside the Hacking Area of any enemy Hackers, this does not cause the Trooper to generate AROs.

- Cautious Movement must follow the General Movement Rules as well as the Moving and Measuring rules.

This Common Skill cannot be declared by TAGs, Remotes, Vehicles, Troopers with Motorcycles, Troopers with the Aerial Special Skill, or Troopers in Targeted State.

Troopers in Hidden Deployment are neither Models nor Markers, so they cannot react to a Cautious Movement by having LoF or being in ZoC.

Stealth

- If the user declares Cautious Movement, Idle, or a Basic Short Skill with the Movement Label, they only grant AROs to enemies that have LoF to them.

A PanOceanian Trooper in Model form intends to close in on a Nomad Trooper. Since the Trooper is in Total Cover behind a tall building, outside the LoF and ZoC of the Nomad Troopers and an enemy Camouflage Marker, the PanOceanian Trooper uses part of their first MOV value to move behind a container where they will also be outside the LoF and ZoC of the Nomad Troopers and the Camouflage Marker.

No Nomad Troopers, including the Camouflage Marker, can declare AROs, since the PanOceanian Trooper's Movement begins and ends outside their LoF and ZoC.

## FAQs

Q: Does a Trooper in Disconnected State or Unconscious State prevent an enemy from re-entering Marker State, or cancel their Cautious Movement?

A: No.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cautious Movement is often misunderstood as a “safe Move” rather than what it actually is: a very specific exception to the normal ARO generation rules. Its power is narrow, conditional, and positional. If any enemy Trooper can draw Line of Fire to the moving Trooper at any point along its path, the entire declaration becomes illegal and must be replaced with Idle.

A frequent mistake is checking only the starting and ending positions. Cautious Movement requires that the Trooper **never** enter any enemy LoF or ZoC at any point along the movement path. This includes partial silhouettes, elevated angles, and indirect LoF from higher terrain. If the Trooper clips LoF for even a millimeter mid-move, the Skill fails.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cautious Movement does not prevent AROs generated by Zone of Control, Hacking Area, or special Skills that do not require LoF. It only suppresses AROs that rely on Line of Fire. Enemies with ZoC-based AROs, perimeter weapons, or other non-LoF triggers can still react normally.

Because it is a Long Skill, Cautious Movement consumes the entire Order. You cannot combine it with another Short Skill like BS Attack, Discover, or Dodge. This makes it inefficient for aggressive advances, but excellent for precise repositioning.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Cautious Movement cannot be declared if the Trooper begins the Order already inside an enemy’s Line of Fire. This is checked at declaration, not during resolution. If the Trooper is currently visible, Cautious Movement is not an option at all.

Markers (Camouflage, Impersonation, etc.) benefit greatly from Cautious Movement, but it does not replace Stealth or marker-state protections. If an enemy could draw LoF to the Marker at any point, the same restrictions apply. Marker state does not “forgive” bad movement paths.

Verticality is a common pitfall. Players often forget that elevated enemies can see over low cover that blocks horizontal LoF. When using Cautious Movement near stairs, ramps, or buildings, always consider enemy silhouettes at different heights.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Strategically, Cautious Movement is best used to:
- Cross narrow firelanes fully blocked by terrain
- Adjust positioning behind total cover without provoking long-range ARO pieces
- Reposition support Troopers or specialists late in the turn
- Preserve fragile units that cannot afford even a single reactive shot

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Tactically, it shines in missions that reward board positioning over kills. Spending a full Order to move safely may feel inefficient, but avoiding a single unlucky ARO can preserve Orders, Specialists, and scoring potential in later turns.

House interpretation warning: if players disagree on whether LoF existed at any point, the safest resolution is to assume LoF existed unless both players clearly agree otherwise. Cautious Movement relies heavily on shared spatial understanding and good table etiquette.
