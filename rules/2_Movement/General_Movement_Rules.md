# General Movement Rules

Movement Module

- When moving Troopers around the battlefield, players must measure the complete route (including, for example, any detour to avoid obstacles) and must always use the same part of the base for their measurements.

- MOV values indicate the maximum distance the unit can move, but there is no minimum. Troopers do not have to exhaust their movement when they declare Move.

- A Trooper can vault over, onto, or off any scenery item whose height is equal to or lower than the height of the Trooper's Silhouette Template, with no need to declare Climb or Jump (see vaulting example ).

- This vertical movement is not taken into account when measuring how far the Trooper moves, but the Trooper counts as moving up and over the obstacle for the purposes of LoF.

- Likewise, while they Move, a Trooper may peek over the edge of a roof or other surface with up to half of its Silhouette, notably to have LoF to Targets located on the same level or on a different level (see the example and Sibylla explains).

- A Trooper can move through Allied or Neutral Models, Markers, and Tokens, but they cannot end their movement in the space occupied by another Model, Marker, or Token.

- Troopers have a LoF arc of 360˚ while they are moving.

- After Moving one of your models, you can leave it facing any direction.

- A Trooper's Movement ends automatically whenever he enters Silhouette contact with an enemy, even if the specified movement route is cut short as a result.

- In order to pass between an Enemy Trooper and an obstacle without entering Silhouette contact, the space in between must be wider than your Trooper's base.

- When performing a Short Skill or ARO with the Movement Label (except for Jump ) (See FAQs.), or when moving after failing a Guts Roll, a Trooper may enter or leave Prone State at either the start of their Movement or the end of their Movement, at no cost. However, if they move in Prone State, this will reduce the distance they can move.

- Independently of the type of movement performed (Move, Jump, Climb, etc.), the movement cannot, in any way, cause the Trooper to fall. If the Trooper cannot reach a new location with their Move, Jump, Climb... they do not perform the movement and they perform an Idle instead.

- Once declared, Troopers always reach the end of their Movement, even if they enter a Null or Immobilized State due to successful enemy Attacks along the way.

## Movement and Obstacles

The Trooper may move over the obstacle without declaring Jump or Climb, since the obstacle’s height is equal to or lower than the height of the Trooper’s Silhouette Template, on both sides. (See FAQs.Vaulting also applies while Jumping or Climbing.)

Conversely, in the second picture, the Trooper may not pass over the obstacle without declaring Jump or Climb, since the obstacle is higher than the height of the Trooper’s Silhouette Template on one side.

Distance is measured on the surface the Trooper is moving along. The player must keep in mind the Trooper's position all along the Trooper's route, as shown in the diagram.

During a single movement, a Trooper can vault up or down multiple levels that are each equal to or lower than the height of its Silhouette Template, even if their combined height is higher than its Silhouette Template. However, there must be a surface at least half the size of its base between each of those levels.

After making their movement, the Trooper can face in any direct the player chooses.

Therefore, at the end of the movement in the first image, the player turns the Model to face in any direction, without moving it from its final position.

When moving, only half of the Trooper's base is required to be in contact with the surface they are moving on. Therefore, when moving, a Trooper can move part of its base over the edge of the surface, for instance to gain LoF to Targets located on the same level or on a different level. To make movement and interactions during the game easier, scenery elements such as parapets and railings must be considered when drawing LoF (see example).

Once they declare movement, Troopers always reach the end of their Movement, even if they enter a Null or Immobilized State due to successful enemy Attacks along the way.

## Example

In his Active Turn, the Fennec is leaning against a wall in Total Cover. He does not have LoF to the Senku, on the other side of the building. The Fennec declares his first Short Skill: Move.

Therefore, the Player will measure the trajectory’s distance, determine his direction and path, then move the Fennec’s base so that his base peeks around the corner to gain LoF to his target while still in Partial Cover.

Once he has seen the Senku, the Fennec’s model returns to his original position in Total Cover. His Move declaration will take him to the corner and back.

Since the Senku also gains LoF to the Fennec, he declares his ARO: BS Attack. The Fennec then declares the second Short Skill of his Order, another BS Attack. In the subsequent Face to Face Roll, the Senku must apply the Partial Cover MODs.

However, he wins the Face to Face Roll, while the Fennec fails his Saving Roll. Then, the Fennec would end his Order in Unconscious State behind Total Cover, where he finished his movement.

## FAQs

Q: Which Skills and AROs allow a Trooper to enter or cancel Prone State?

A: A Trooper can enter or cancel Prone State when performing any Skill or ARO with the Movement Label (except Jump and Berserk).

Q: If a Trooper enters Prone State at the end of their move, how does this affect the distance they can move?

A: It doesn’t. They move normally and then finish the end of the move prone.

Q: Can you vault while Climbing or Jumping?

A: Yes. Climb and Jump follow the General Movement Rules, including vaulting.

Q: Can anything be placed on or end a move on Deployable Cover?

A: No.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Movement is declared before measuring

Movement in Infinity is entirely intent-driven. You must declare the Skill (usually Move) and the full route while measuring. This includes changes in direction, vertical movement, and whether the Trooper intends to end Prone. 

This is because moving can trigger AROs of enemy Troopers by entering their LoF or ZoC. This is especially important if an enemy trooper is in Hidden Deployment: once revealed because of your movement/path, they can't be "un-revealed".

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Movement generates AROs continuously along the route

A Trooper that declares a Skill with the Movement Label is considered to occupy every point along its declared path. Enemy Troopers can gain AROs at any point where Line of Fire or Zone of Control is met.

This often surprises players who think only the final position matters.

Key interaction:
- You cannot “outrun” Line of Fire by moving past it.
- AROs can be declared even if the Trooper only briefly crosses a LoF sliver.

Tactical use: Slice the pie carefully. Short, angled moves limit how many enemies can draw LoF at any one point of the route.

Declaring intent is useful here: saying things like:
- I move here hugging the obstacle while keeping cover from this Trooper.
- I move here until I slice the pie so I see Trooper A and not Trooper B.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## 360º Line of Fire while moving

While performing a Movement Skill, a Trooper is treated as having 360º Line of Fire along the entire route. This is frequently missed.

Important consequences:
- Troopers can draw LoF to enemies behind them while moving.
- This enables AROs and Attacks that would not be possible from a static facing.

Tactical use: This allows aggressive repositioning without worrying about model facing, especially for corner checks and backline repositioning.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Partial movement is allowed and often optimal

You are never required to use the full movement value. Declaring Move does not obligate you to move the full first or second Short Skill distance.

This is critical for:
- Remaining in Cover
- Avoiding triggering additional AROs
- Ending movement just outside ZoC

Tactical use: Micro-movements of 1–2 inches are a core skill in Infinity. Precision matters more than distance.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Vertical movement is still movement

Moving up or down is measured exactly like horizontal movement. Climb and Jump are separate Skills, but any vertical displacement consumes movement distance.

Common misconception:
- You cannot gain “free” vertical height during a normal Move.
- If you move onto a slope or staircase, the actual distance traveled counts.

Tactical note: Always measure the true path length, not just table projection, especially on dense terrain tables.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Prone declaration timing

Prone can only be entered or cancelled at the end of a movement, not mid-route.

This means:
- You cannot move, go Prone, then continue moving.
- Ending Prone can expose LoF only after movement is complete.

Tactical use: Ending Prone is often safer than starting upright, especially when moving into midfield under ARO pressure.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Silhouette matters more than the model

Movement, Line of Fire, and contact are always checked using the Silhouette volume, not the miniature pose.

Easily missed detail:
- Leaning weapons, wings, or scenic bases do not count.
- If the Silhouette fits, the Trooper fits.

Strategic implication: Learn silhouette sizes by memory. This improves movement efficiency and prevents accidental illegal placements.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Movement and Engaged State

A Trooper cannot Move out of base contact with an enemy without first cancelling the Engaged State. Any movement declaration that would break base contact without proper cancellation is invalid.

This includes:
- Sliding around the base
- “Backing away” while still touching

Tactical reminder: If you are Engaged, your movement options are heavily restricted. Plan CC positioning carefully.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Declared path is binding

Once the movement path is declared, you cannot change it after AROs are declared, even if new threats appear.

This is a major skill separator in Infinity:
- Poor path declaration leads to unnecessary AROs.
- Overly ambitious paths are punished.

Strategic advice: Always declare the safest legal path first, not the most optimistic one.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Movement as an information weapon

Movement is not just positioning; it is information warfare.

By moving:
- You reveal Marker intent
- You provoke ARO declarations
- You force your opponent to commit reactions early

Advanced tactic: Use short, non-committal moves to extract AROs before committing attack pieces.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Moving through allies
A Trooper can move through Allied or Neutral Models, Markers, and Tokens, but they cannot end their movement in the space occupied by another Model, Marker, or Token.
