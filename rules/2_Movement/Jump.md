# Jump

Movement Module

Common Skills
 LONG SKILL
Movement, No LoF, No Roll. 

This Common Skill allows the user to clear obstacles and leap over small distances.

Players must check the Requirements of this Skill when declaring it.

- The Trooper must be able to reach a horizontal landing spot, which must be equal to or larger than the Trooper's base.

- Allows the user to move horizontally, vertically, diagonally or tracing a parabola, without changing their heading. The user can Jump up to the first value of their MOV Attribute, plus 2 inches.

- Jump is a Common Skill that does not require a Roll.

- When Jumping, measure the horizontal, vertical, diagonal or parabolic distance between the starting location and the landing spot, measuring from the base's outer edge and underside.

- A jump ends when the Trooper touches a surface, which will be the landing spot.

- Troopers cannot benefit from Partial Cover MODs during an Order in which they declared Jump.

- Jumping movement must follow the General Movement Rules as well as the Moving and Measuring rules.

- Some Troopers have the Jump Skill listed in their Unit Profile with a distance shown in round brackets. When Jumping, the Trooper can add this distance to their Jump, instead of 2 inches.

- Declaring Jump automatically cancels Prone State.

Measuring parabolas: The easiest way to measure a parabolic route is using a flexible measuring tape, bending it for convenience.

Before the game begins, it is a good idea for both players to agree on which surfaces on the game table will require the use of the Jump Skill, taking into account potentially dubious cases.

Super-Jump

- With this Special Skill, the Jump Long Skill is turned into a Basic Short Skill, if declared as the first Basic Short Skill of the Order.

- The Super-Jump Special Skill allows its user to perform other Basic Short Skills or Short Skills while performing a Jump (for example: Jump + BS Attack ) (see Trooper Activation ).

## Examples

In their Active Turn, a Trooper has to clear a 1.75-inch-high obstacle. As this is higher than their Silhouette Template, the Trooper must declare Jump to be able to clear it.

In his Active Turn, the Trooper decides to get down from the roof of a building as fast as possible, by jumping down. He declares Jump and measures the height of the building, which is 10 inches. However, the Trooper can only Jump up to 6 inches. As the distance is more than 6 inches, he cannot make this Jump and, as there is no safe alternative landing spot, he performs an Idle instead.

In his Active Turn, the Trooper declares he is going to Jump from one roof to another. The player measures the distance and checks that it falls within the Jump distance of 6 inches, and that the landing spot is at least as large as his base, so he can choose the spot as the final location of his movement.

If the distance is too far, or the Model’s base cannot be placed on the landing spot, then the Trooper cannot Jump to that spot. He must choose a new landing spot he can reach safely and land on, or he performs an Idle instead and remains in place.

When jumping, the Trooper must follow a trajectory without changing heading. Therefore, they cannot change their trajectory in mid-air, and can only return to their starting point if they jumped directly up and down.

## FAQs

Q: Can you vault while Climbing or Jumping?

A: Yes. Climb and Jump follow the General Movement Rules, including vaulting.


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Jump is a Long Skill with the Movement Label that allows a Trooper to move vertically and horizontally in a single declaration, ignoring the need for climbable surfaces. This is often misunderstood as “free vertical movement”, but it is still normal movement measured in inches and fully constrained by the Trooper’s MOV value. You are not teleporting or vaulting arbitrarily; you are tracing a real movement path through space.

Because Jump is a Long Skill, it consumes the entire Order by itself. You cannot combine Jump with another Short Skill (such as BS Attack, Dodge, or Activate Objective). This is frequently overlooked when players attempt to “Jump + Shoot” as if it were Move + BS Attack. If you Jump, that is all you do for that Order.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Jump generates AROs exactly like any other movement. Since Troopers declaring Skills with the Movement Label gain 360° LoF along their entire route, enemies may declare AROs even if the jumper starts outside their front arc. This makes Jump inherently risky in open firelanes, especially against multiple Reactive Troopers.

All measurements for Jump are declared and checked at declaration, not retroactively optimized. You must declare the entire path of the Jump before measuring, including the takeoff point, arc, and landing point. If the declared path exceeds MOV, the Skill becomes Idle. This is a common source of “oops” moments during vertical play.

A Trooper using Jump does not gain Cover at any point during the movement unless the landing position clearly provides it. Being airborne or mid-trajectory never grants Cover bonuses. This matters both for enemy BS Attacks and for Guts Rolls afterward.

Jump does not bypass silhouette or volume restrictions. The Trooper’s Silhouette must physically fit at the landing point. If there is not enough space to place the Silhouette Template legally, the Jump is invalid. Players often focus on distance and forget volume.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
You cannot enter Engaged State during a Jump unless the landing point satisfies all Engaged requirements. Passing over an enemy base does not trigger Close Combat. Jump is movement, not a charge or attack.

Jump is particularly easy to misapply in Fireteams. If the Team Leader declares Jump, every member must be able to declare Jump and must remain in Coherency at the end of the Order. This often results in accidental Fireteam breaks when vertical spacing is misjudged.

Strategically, Jump is strongest as a positioning and threat-projection tool rather than an aggressive attack enabler. Use it to:
- Reach rooftops or elevated objectives that would otherwise require multiple Climb Orders.
- Bypass ladders, stairwells, or predictable access points covered by enemy ARO pieces.
- Reposition into angles that force enemies to split AROs on subsequent Orders.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
 Spending an Order to Jump into a dominant perch where the Trooper can start the next Order already in Cover and good Range Bands is often better than jumping directly into contested LoF.

One advantage of Jump over Climb is that the Trooper doesn't have to have contact with the vertical area, that often saves a full order just to get near the vertical surface.

A common high-level use is “safe Jump staging”: Jump behind total cover on a higher level, denying AROs during the movement, then exploit the elevation advantage with the next Order. This minimizes the inherent risk of exposing a Trooper mid-air.

In summary, Jump is powerful but unforgiving. It rewards precise spatial planning, strong table awareness, and disciplined Order sequencing. Misuse is usually punished immediately by AROs, while correct use quietly wins positioning battles that define the rest of the game.
