# Climb

> **N5.3 change (September 2026):** A Trooper **performing the Climb Skill, or holding onto a vertical surface, cannot benefit from Partial Cover MODs**. This replaces the older broader wording that simply referred to being “on a vertical surface.”

Movement Module

Common Skills
 CLIMB
LONG SKILL
Movement, No LoF, No Roll.

This Common Skill allows the user to climb and move on vertical surfaces.

Players must check the Requirements of this Skill when declaring it.

- The Trooper’s base must be in contact with a vertical surface.

- At least half the Trooper’s base must stay in contact with the surface on which they are moving.

- The Trooper must be able to finish their movement on a surface that is equal to or larger than their base.

- Allows the Trooper to move on a vertical surface. The user can Climb up to the first value of their MOV Attribute, plus 2 inches.

- Climbing is a Common Skill that does not require a Roll.

- When Climbing, players must measure the distance from the starting point to the destination, measuring from the base's outer edge and underside.

- For visualization purposes, Troopers using the Climb Skill move with at least half of their base's underside in contact with the vertical surface.

- Climb only allows movement on vertical surfaces. The Climb Skill does not allow the user to move on a horizontal surface, even if their modified MOV value exceeds the distance that needs to be Climbed. Therefore, a Trooper will finish their Climbing movement the moment the whole of their base in contact with a horizontal surface (see Example 1).

- Climbing movement must follow the General Movement Rules as well as the Moving and Measuring rules.

- Some Troopers have the Climb Skill listed in their Unit Profile with a distance shown in round brackets. When Climbing, the Trooper can add this distance to their Climb, instead of 2 inches.

- Troopers cannot be deployed on a vertical surface, neither during the Deployment Phase nor during the game.

- As long as a Trooper is holding on to a vertical surface, they cannot declare any Skills other than Climb.

- A Trooper who is Climbing or holding onto a vertical surface and becomes Unconscious will place the corresponding State Token beside them as usual. Even while climbing, Unconscious Troopers are still in Prone State, except for those Unit Types that cannot enter Prone State.

- A Trooper performing the Climb Skill, or holding onto a vertical surface, cannot benefit from Partial Cover MODs.

A Trooper in the Reactive Turn who is holding onto a vertical surface cannot declare an ARO. The reason for this is that, while on a vertical surface, the only available Skill is Climb, and Climb is a Long Skill that cannot be used in the Reactive Turn.

Any height on the game table that is higher than the height of a Trooper's Silhouette Template determined by the Trooper's Silhouette Attribute requires declaring the Jump or Climb Common Skills to pass it.

Before the game begins, it is a good idea for both players to agree on which surfaces on the game table will require the use of the Climb Skill, taking into account potentially dubious cases, such as buildings with slightly angled walls.

Climbing Plus

- With this Special Skill, the Climb Long Skill is turned into a Basic Short Skill, if declared as the first Basic Short Skill of the Order.

- Climbing Plus also allows the user to move on vertical surfaces as if performing normal Movement on a horizontal surface when declaring Move or Dodge, or during movement due to a failed Guts Roll. The General Movement Rules and Moving and Measuring chapter must be applied.

- The Climbing Plus Special Skill allows its user to perform other Basic Short Skills or Short Skills (e.g. Move + BS Attack ) while moving on a vertical surface, or if hanging from a height (see Trooper Activation ).

A Trooper declares the Climb Skill and moves until they are on the horizontal surface. At that moment, an enemy Trooper gains LoF to the Active Trooper and declares a BS Attack ARO. Although the enemy cannot fully see the Active Trooper, they do not apply MODs for Partial Cover since their target is performing the Climb Skill so cannot benefit from Partial Cover MODs.

## FAQs

Q: Can you vault while Climbing or Jumping?

A: Yes. Climb and Jump follow the General Movement Rules, including vaulting.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Climb — Interaction Notes and Common Pitfalls


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Climb Is a Long Skill (This Is the Big One)

Climb is a **Long Skill**, not a Basic Short Skill.

Practical consequences that are often missed:
- You cannot combine Climb with anything else in the same Order.
- You need to be in contact with the vertical surface already. So even if you're very close to the surface, you'll need another Order just to move near it, to climb as a Long Skill next Order.
- You cannot declare BS Attack, Dodge, Discover, Reset, or any other Skill while Climbing, so are very vulnerable.
- If you are still on a vertical surface at the end of the Order, you are effectively “locked” into Climb for future Orders.

This makes standard Climb an **order-inefficient and dangerous movement option**.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## No AROs While Hanging on a Wall

A Trooper holding onto a vertical surface:
- Cannot declare any Skill other than Climb.
- Therefore **cannot declare AROs** in the Reactive Turn.

This is frequently forgotten and leads to illegal Dodges or Reset declarations.
A Climbing Trooper caught mid-wall is completely defenseless.

Strategic implication:
- Ending an Order while still on the wall is extremely risky unless the Trooper is totally out of LoF.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Partial Cover While Climbing

Under N5.3, a Trooper **performing Climb or holding onto a vertical surface** cannot benefit from Partial Cover MODs.

This applies even if:
- The Trooper is barely visible.
- Only a small part of the Silhouette is exposed.
- The enemy only sees the Trooper at the very top or bottom of the climb.

This makes Climb especially dangerous against long-range ARO pieces.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Climb Ends Immediately on Horizontal Contact

A Climb movement **automatically ends** the moment:
- The entire base is in contact with a horizontal surface.

Important implications:
- You cannot “bank” extra Climb distance to reposition on the rooftop.
- Even if your Climb distance exceeds the height, you stop as soon as you reach flat ground.
- Any additional repositioning requires a new Order.

This is a common source of wasted movement and misplayed Orders.
---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Getting Shot at the Top of a Climb

A classic interaction:
- The Trooper finishes Climb and reaches a rooftop.
- At that exact point, an enemy gains LoF and declares a BS Attack ARO.

Key detail:
- The Trooper still **does not gain Partial Cover**, even though they are now on a horizontal surface, because the Order being resolved is a Climb Order.

This makes the final inches of a Climb particularly lethal.
---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Vaulting Still Applies During Climb

Climb follows the General Movement Rules.

This means:
- Vaulting over small obstacles (equal or lower than Silhouette height) is allowed during a Climb.
- Vaulting does not negate the need to declare Climb if the overall obstacle exceeds Silhouette height.

This matters most on cluttered rooftops or industrial terrain with railings and low barriers.

---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Prone, Unconscious, and Falling Assumptions

Common misconceptions:
- Troopers do not “fall” if they stop mid-climb.
- Movement can never cause a Trooper to fall.
- An Unconscious Trooper on a wall is treated as Prone (unless their Troop Type forbids Prone).

This can result in very awkward board states where a Trooper is stuck on a wall until removed or healed.
---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## When Climb Is Mandatory vs Optional
You must declare Climb (or Jump) when:
- The obstacle height exceeds the Trooper’s Silhouette height.

You do NOT need Climb when:
- The obstacle is equal to or lower than Silhouette height (vaulting applies).
- Using ladders or stairs, which are treated as horizontal movement.

Misjudging this is one of the most common movement errors in Infinity.
---

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Tactical Use of Standard Climb (When Is It Worth It?)

Despite its drawbacks, Climb has niche uses:
- **Reaching total safety**: Climb from total cover to total cover where no AROs are possible.
- **Vertical objective access**: Missions with objectives on rooftops may force Climb usage.
- **Sniper position**: High vantage points are really useful for ARO pieces, as long as they can get there safely.

General advice:
- If a Trooper does not have Climbing Plus, treat Climb as a **last resort**, not a standard movement tool (use ladders and vaulting instead if available).


**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
## Comparison Note: Climb vs Climbing Plus
Understanding standard Climb is essential because:
- Climbing Plus removes nearly all of Climb’s drawbacks.
- Many players accidentally play standard Climb as if it were Climbing Plus.

Rule of thumb:
If the Trooper does not explicitly have **Climbing Plus**, assume:
- No AROs
- No attacks
- No Dodges
- No Cover
- High risk
