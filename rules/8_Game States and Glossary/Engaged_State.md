# Engaged State

Game States and Glossary

- The Trooper is in Silhouette contact with an Enemy Trooper, and neither are in an Immobilized or Null State (except for Sepsitorized or Possessed ).

- Troopers in Engaged State are considered to be in Close Combat.

- Troopers in this state can only declare Berserk, CC Attack, Dodge, Idle, Reset, and Skills that specify that they can be used in Close Combat or in Engaged State.

- Troopers that are in Engaged State may not trace LoF outside their Close Combat.

- By declaring BS Attack on a Trooper in Engaged State, the BS Attack Into a Close Combat rule must be applied.

- Template Weapons placed on a group of Troopers in Engaged state will always affect all participating Troopers, even if the placement of the Template would only affect one of them.

- This state is canceled when the Trooper is no longer in Silhouette contact with any Enemy Troopers.

- A Trooper's Engaged State is automatically canceled if, in the 5.1 Effects step of any Order, all enemies in Silhouette contact are in an Immobilized or Null State (except for Sepsitorized or Possessed ).

- A Trooper may also cancel the Engaged State if they pass a Dodge Roll, either Normal or Face to Face. In this case, the Trooper must leave Silhouette contact, moving up to 2 inches to separate themselves from the enemy.

- If they cannot move to a valid position that is outside Silhouette contact, the Trooper will remain in Engaged State without moving.

Line of Fire

- Troopers who are in Engaged State cannot draw LoF to Troopers or game elements that are not in Engaged State with them. In addition, Troopers who are in Engaged State are not affected by Visibility Conditions or by other rules affecting LoF.

# Engaged State — Interaction Notes, Clarifications, and Tricky Cases (Infinity N5)

## What Engaged State Is (Summary of Official Rules)
A Trooper becomes **Engaged** when:
- They are in **Silhouette contact** with an enemy, and  
- Neither model is in **Immobilized** or **Null**, except for Sepsitorized/Possessed.

Effects:
- Troopers in Engaged are **in Close Combat**.
- They **may only declare**: Berserk, CC Attack, Dodge, Idle, Reset, or Skills marked usable **in Close Combat / Engaged State**.
- They **cannot trace LoF outside the Close Combat**.
- **BS Attacks into a Close Combat** must follow the special rule for shooting into CC where you are likely to hit your ally.
- **Templates placed on any Engaged group always affect all participants**, even if the template only touches one.
- Engaged ends when the Trooper is no longer in Silhouette contact, or when all opponents in contact enter Null/Immobilized (except Sepsitorized/Possessed).
- A Trooper may **cancel Engaged by passing a Dodge**, moving up to 2" away; if no legal landing spot exists, they remain Engaged.

## Key Interactions People Often Misplay or Forget

### 1. **No LoF Out of the Melee**
Troopers in Engaged cannot see or react to anything outside the fight.
This prevents:
- BS Attacks at distant enemies
- Hacking AROs (unless the program is specifically legal in CC)
- Discover attempts
- Any action requiring external LoF

### 3. **Engaged Prevents Guts Rolls**
Engaged Troopers **never make Guts Rolls**, which effectively protects them from involuntary movement that could break the melee.

This is a commonly forgotten rule and very relevant when templated or shot from outside.

---

### 4. **Dodge as an Escape Tool**
Passing a Dodge lets a Trooper:
- Move up to 2" to break base contact.
- If *any* legal position exists outside contact, Engaged is canceled.
- If none exists (e.g., boxed in), they stay Engaged.

This allows disengaging **without needing to win CC**, but LOS and placement constraints are crucial.

---

### 5. **Multiple Allies in CC (Dogpile Bonus)**
Each additional allied model in Silhouette contact grants:
- **+1 Burst** to the selected attacking Trooper
- Only one Trooper makes the attack roll
- Null or Immobilized allies do not contribute

This makes Coordinated CC swarms extremely deadly.

---

### 6. **Entering CC From Stealth**
If a Stealth user enters Silhouette contact without entering LoF:
- The defender may only declare: **CC Attack, Dodge, Reset**, or Skills usable in Engaged.

Stealth allows you to **force a melee on your terms**, denying ARO choices.

---

### Engaged Near Ledges or Vertical Terrain
If Dodge movement would cause falling or illegal placement:
- The Trooper remains Engaged.
- This can trap models on rooftops or balconies in perpetual melee.

---
### Engaged and Fireteams
Only the **Fireteam Leader** makes CC Attacks, but:
- Other engaged members still count for **+1B** if in Silhouette contact.

This makes Fireteam CC unusually efficient compared to standard CC duels.

## Tactical Principles

### 1. Use Engaged to Deny Shooting at High-Value Troops
Tie up a powerful enemy attack piece to:
- Prevent them from shooting or declaring other BS skills
- Force them into unfavorable CC trades

---

### 2. Use Dodge to Escape Bad Melees
A surprising number of units break CC by Dodging **after being attacked**, especially when their CC stats are poor.

---

### 3. Exploit Template Collateral Rules
If you have:
- High ARM / Wounds, or  
- A non-essential trooper engaging an enemy anchor piece,

Templating your own melee can be favorable.

---

### 4. Beware Enemy Reinforcements
Once multiple enemies join CC, your model becomes vulnerable to:
- Massive B increases
- Martial Arts stacking
- Burst-boosted crit fishing

---

### 5. CC as Movement Control
Engaging an enemy can stall:
- TAGs
- Fireteam leaders
- Hackers trying to reach objectives
- AD/Parachutist landing zones (post-landing)

## Summary
Engaged is a **highly restrictive** and **highly protective** state.  
It locks Troopers out of most options but shields them from outside threats.  
Mastering Engaged means understanding:
- When **to force** a melee
- When **to escape** one
- When **to exploit** the protection it provides
- When **templates** or **numbers** turn a melee into a slaughter
