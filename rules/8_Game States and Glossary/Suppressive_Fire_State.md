# Suppressive Fire

Common Skills
LONG SKILL
Attack 

In Suppressive Fire, the soldier is more intent on saturating the combat area with gunfire, rather than actually inflicting damage to the enemy forces.

The goal of Suppressive Fire is to restrict the capacity of the enemy forces to maneuver or successfully return fire, by unleashing a barrage of overwhelming gunfire upon them.

- The user must select a weapon with the Suppressive Fire Trait.

- The user enters the Suppressive Fire State, using the selected weapon.

- G: Jumper and Suppressive Fire: If a Trooper with G: Jumper has a Proxy in Suppressive Fire State, that Proxy stays in Suppressive Fire State even if another Proxy is activated.

- If a Trooper with G: Jumper has a Proxy in Suppressive Fire State, that Proxy stays in Suppressive Fire State even if another Proxy is activated.

Fireteam Integrity A Trooper automatically stops being part of a Fireteam if they activate Suppressive Fire State.

Command Tokens: Strategic Use Allows a Trooper to start the game in Suppressive Fire State.

# Suppressive Fire State
-------------------

Game States and Glossary

- The Trooper declared the Suppressive Fire Long Skill.

- Enemy Troopers within Suppressive Fire Range (0-24 inches) apply a -3 MOD to their Attribute in all Face to Face Rolls against a Trooper in Suppressive Fire State.

- While in Suppressive Fire State, the SF Mode profile replaces the selected weapon's usual BS Weapon profile. The SF Mode profile is the one that will be used while in Suppressive Fire State.

- Suppressive Fire allows the affected Trooper to react in ARO with the full B3 value of the SF Mode. The full Burst must always be directed against a single target and cannot be divided between several active enemies (when reacting against a Coordinated Order, for example).

- The SF Mode profile only alters the Range and B values of the weapon being used. The weapon's PS, Ammunition, etc. remain unchanged.

- This state does not interfere with Automatic Special Skills or Automatic Equipment, which will remain functioning normally.

Suppressive Fire State is automatically canceled in any of these cases:

- The Trooper declares an Order.

- The Trooper declares any ARO other than a BS Attack using the SF Mode profile.

- The Trooper uses a weapon that does not have the Suppressive Fire Trait.

- The Trooper fails a Guts Roll.

- The Trooper enters Engaged, Isolated or Retreat! State, any Null or Immobilized State, or any other State which specifies that it cancels Suppressive Fire.

- The Trooper's army enters a Loss of Lieutenant situation.

- The Trooper joins any Fireteam.

### Entering and Maintaining Suppressive Fire

Suppressive Fire is entered by declaring the Suppressive Fire Common Skill as the second Short Skill of an Order, and it lasts until the Trooper does something that cancels it.  
Things that *do not* cancel it (and players often think they do):  
- Being targeted by Attacks  
- Gaining AROs for BS Attacks
- Idle

Things that *do* cancel it:  
- Declaring an Order  
- BS Attack with any weapon other than the user’s designated Suppression weapon  
- Declaring ARO other than BS Attacl
- Engaged, Isolated or Retreat! State, any Null or Immobilized State, or any other State which specifies that it cancels Suppressive Fire
- Failing a Guts roll.

Remember: If the Trooper has multiple weapons, Suppressive Fire fixes which weapon is used (usually the Combi Rifle or equivalent). It cannot be swapped until the State is cancelled.

---

### Range Bands in Suppressive Fire

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Suppression Fire modifies Range Bands, regardless of normal weapon range, giving a flat and narrow effective zone (usually 0–24").  
This creates important tactical effects:
- Between 0–16", Suppression usually provides strong, consistent control.  
- At 16–24", Suppressive Fire becomes less usefull (flat -3 Range Band penalties).  
- Useless beyond 24"

### Burst and ARO Interactions

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
In the **Reactive Turn**, a Trooper in Suppressive Fire gains **Burst 3** with the designated weapon. This overrides the normal “ARO = B1” rule.

A common misunderstanding:
- **The Burst 3 is *not* a MOD**, it is the State’s fixed Burst. It cannot be stacked with other B bonuses, except Special Dice from Fireteam Level 2 (which do not increase Burst).


### Suppressive Fire and Movement / Positioning Traps

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
A very common mistake is leaving Suppressive Fire troops exposed on corners.  
Because the user must keep LoF to use Suppression:
- Enemies can force bad Face-to-Face rolls via pie-slicing.  
- Enemies can exploit angles where the Suppressive Fire user has poor Range Bands (<8", >24").

A subtle point:
- A Trooper can declare Suppressive Fire while already Prone, but cannot *become* Prone without cancelling the State.  
- Because Prone reduces LoF from elevated angles, this is a powerful tech piece when locking down corridors.

---

### Fireteams and Suppressive Fire

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
- A Fireteam member in Suppressive Fire can still ARO normally with B3  
- But if the Team Leader activates, that member loses Suppressive Fire if they perform any Fireteam action requiring movement or a non-suppression weapon.

This means Fireteams rarely maintain Suppressive Fire unless positioned separately for area denial.


### Comparison: Suppressive Fire vs. Guarding Corners with Normal ARO

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Both approaches defend space, but differently.

**Suppressive Fire**
Pros:
- High Burst (B3) in ARO  
- Consistent mid-range zone control  
- Works even against multiple short-range attackers  
- Excellent for pinning or dictating pathways  

Cons:
- Costs one order to get in Suppressive Fire State
- Breaks on movement  
- Strict 0–24" band  
- Vulnerable to Smoke blocking LoF  

**Normal ARO (no Suppression)**
Pros:
- Can use best weapon for the engagement (Snipers, HMGs, etc.)  
- Better reach (good for long-range platforms)  
- Not cancelled by movement  
- Synergizes with Fireteam bonuses and visors  

Cons:
- Burst 1 in ARO  
- Less area denial  
- Easier for attackers to trade efficiently  

Use Suppressive Fire when:
- You want to lock down midfield or hallways  
- You expect multiple low-quality attackers  
- You benefit from mid-range gunfighting

Use normal ARO posture when:
- You are using long-range weapons  
- You need to keep Fireteam synergy  
- You cannot risk losing the State by being forced to move

---
### Summary Practical Advice

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
Suppressive Fire is best used on:
- Mid-range shooters with solid BS (Combi Rifle platforms, SMG users in cover)  
- Durable troops that can win attrition  
- Troopers guarding objectives or short fire lanes  
- Inexpensive troops controlling midfield zones

Avoid using Suppressive Fire:
- On long-range weapons or long fire lanes
- If you expect to need to reposition next turn  
- When your Fireteam cohesion matters more than local area denial
