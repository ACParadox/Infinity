# Mines

## Mines

Mines are a type of Deployable Weapon used to control small areas of the battlefield and influence the movement of enemy troops. There are various types of Mines available, all of them work following the same mechanics, but they feature different types of Special Ammunition with their corresponding specific properties.

- When the player declares the Place Deployable Common Skill, instead of placing a Token to represent the Mine, he will place a Camouflaged Marker CAMO(-3).

- Replace the Camouflage Marker [CAMO (-3)] with a Mine Token [MINE (-3)] when it is discovered by an enemy.

- The Camouflage Marker and the Mine Token both benefit from the MODs provided by the Mimetism (-3) Special Skill.

- Mines have a 360˚ LoF arc.

- As a Direct Template Weapon, when any type of Mine is triggered, a Small Teardrop Template must be placed and the PS and Ammunition specified in the Weapons Chart must be applied.

- The Small Teardrop Template must be placed so that it affects the enemy Model or Marker that triggered the Mine.

- Restriction: A Mine never triggers if the Small Teardrop Template would affect an ally, even if that ally is Unconscious.

- Once on the game table, Mines must trigger when an enemy Model or Marker declares or executes a Skill or ARO inside their Trigger Area, checking it at that moment by placing the Small Teardrop Template. If it is determined that the Model or Marker is not within the Trigger Area, the Mine will neither detonate nor be revealed.

- Once a Mine triggers, it is removed from play.

- A Mine is a Template Weapon and a Deployable Weapon, so it imposes a -3 PH MOD to any attempt to Dodge its effects.

- The Trigger Area of a Mine (whether it is a Camouflage Marker or a Mine Token) is the area within the radius of the Small Teardrop Template, extended out from the edge of the base of the Mine.

- The Trigger Area excludes any areas in Total Cover from the Blast Focus of the Small Teardrop Template (see graphic).

- Restriction: A Mine cannot be placed if there is an enemy Camouflage Marker inside its Trigger Area. This restriction does not apply if there is a valid, non-camouflaged enemy inside the Trigger Area, or if an Intuitive Attack was declared.

## Mine - Cybermines

- CYBERMINES: As Cybermines are Deployable Template Weapons that make Comms Attacks, they can only be evaded with a Reset, rather than a Dodge, applying a -3 WIP MOD. Troopers who are hit by a Cybermine must make two Saving Rolls against BTS, with PS 5. If the target fails either Saving Roll, it enters Stunned State. However, any Troopers with the Hackable Characteristic ( HI, REM, TAG, VH...) and Hackers who fail either Saving Roll enter Immobilized-B State instead of Stunned State.

### Interaction Note
Related rules: ARO, Face-to-Face, Partial Cover.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

Mines are Deployable Direct Template Weapons that trigger automatically when an enemy enters their Trigger Area or performs certain actions. Because they use a Direct Template (Small Teardrop) and are not declared as Attacks by a Trooper, they create several important interaction quirks in Infinity N5.

Note: Troopers affected by a Template Weapon do not benefit from the +3 MOD to the Saving Roll (SR) for Partial Cover.

Note: Mines haves intuitive trait:
An Intuitive Attack must be declared in order to place a Deployable Weapon on the gaming table if there is an enemy Camouflaged Marker (CAMO) inside its Trigger Area. This restriction does not apply if there is a valid, non-camouflaged enemy Model inside the Trigger Area. If the WIP Roll is failed, the Deployable Weapon is not placed on the table and, if it also has the Disposable Trait, one use must be considered spent.

Note: Mine trigger through Eclipse or Smoke (Zero Visibility, Low Visibility, Poor Visibility) zones, but not total cover (scenery).

• **ARO Interactions**
  Mines themselves never declare AROs. They trigger automatically when their conditions are met, using the template specified in their profile. An enemy entering a Mine’s Trigger Area generates an ARO from *other* enemy troopers as normal, but the Mine’s detonation is not an ARO and cannot be delayed or canceled by ARO manipulation.
  Since Mines are Deployable Weapons, they can be removed by a successful Deactivator WIP Roll, but a successful Roll does not prevent them from triggering during the same Order.

• **Face-to-Face Interactions**
  Mine detonations are never Face-to-Face Rolls. They resolve as Normal Rolls because the Mine is not “attacking” as part of an Order declaration—its trigger is automatic. If a trooper triggers a Mine while also being the target of another enemy Attack, only the other Attack may be Face-to-Face; the Mine’s effects always resolve as Normal.
  This means that even if the active trooper wins a Face-to-Face against another enemy shooter, the Mine still applies its template if triggered.

• **Partial Cover Interactions**
  Partial Cover never applies against Mines because they use Direct Template Weapons. Direct Templates ignore Partial Cover bonuses completely. A trooper may still claim Cover against another simultaneous attack, but never against the Mine itself.
  Note that Mines do not “check LoF” to apply Cover; the template is simply placed to affect the triggering model.

• **Pros of Mines**
  - Excellent area-denial tool: they control space without Order expenditure once placed.
  - Automatic detonation cannot be prevented by winning Face-to-Face rolls.
  - Works against Markers.
  - Strong against low-ARM and low-BTS and units with mimetism units due to guaranteed template placement.
  - Can force awkward movement choices, especially around objectives.
  - Concealed versions (AP Mines, E/M Mines, Viral Mines) benefit from Mimetism (-3) until discovered.

• **Cons of Mines**
    - Vulnerable to Deactivators, Sensor, and Discover+Shoot interactions.
  - Require correct placement during the initial Order; movement during the same Order can invalidate a declared position.
  - Some Mines are non-lethal, which may reduce deterrence against resilient troops.

• **Mines - Comparison With Similar Deployable Threats**
  **Mines vs. Drop Bears**
  - Mines begin as CAMO(-3) markers; Drop Bears (Deployable Mode) start as visible Mine tokens.
  - Mines cannot be thrown; Drop Bears can be placed via BS Attack Mode, giving greater reach.
  - Mines are more subtle area control; Drop Bears offer rapid, flexible deployment.

  **Mines vs. CrazyKoalas / Perimeter Weapons**
  - Mines are static; Perimeter weapons move and chase targets.
  - Mines never perform AROs; Koalas trigger based on Boost and movement.
  - Perimeter weapons require careful positioning to avoid premature activation.

  **Mines vs. Direct Template Weapons (Chain Rifle, Nanopulser)**
  - Mines trigger automatically and require no Orders after deployment.
  - Direct Template Weapons require a declared attack and can be Face-to-Face.
  - For mines active troopers cannot “dodge LoF” to avoid triggering.

Overall, Mines provide strong positional control without further Order investment. Their biggest strength is that triggering them cannot be stopped by any Face-to-Face interaction, making them reliable tools to punish aggressive movement or objective runs.
