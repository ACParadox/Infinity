
# Perimeter Weapons

Within military confines, the term tactical perimeter devices is used for automated zone defense systems.

Some examples of Perimeter Weapons are CrazyKoalas, MadTraps, WildParrots, and all other Weapons or pieces of Equipment with the Perimeter Trait.

- These weapons are placed on the game table using the Place Deployable Common Skill, applying the Perimeter and Deployable rule.

- Therefore, when players declare the Place Deployable Common Skill, they place the Perimeter Weapon or piece of Equipment totally inside the Zone of Control of the Trooper, instead of placing it in Silhouette contact.

## Perimeter Weapons and the Boost Trait

These devices have been specifically designed to protect the immediate perimeter of their user, and also to detect and/or dissuade intrusion attempts during the user's security duties.

In the Glossary you can consult all the Traits that indicate how a weapon operates.

- Once the weapon with the Boost Trait is in play on the game table, it is triggered when an enemy Model declares or performs an Order or ARO in its Zone of Control. When triggered, the weapon must move until it enters Silhouette contact with the enemy Model, and then detonate. The only way to avoid this attack is a successful Normal Dodge Roll. Once the weapon has detonated, it is removed from the game.

- The weapon will not trigger if the path from the weapon to the enemy Model is blocked, for example by an impassable obstacle (for example a wall of infinite height, a closed door or sealed room...) or a gap too small for the Silhouette Template of the weapon to pass through.

- The weapon is not triggered by Camouflage Markers (CAMO), Impersonation Markers (IMP-1 and IMP-2), or by those Markers who specify so in their description.

- Due to the Deployable Trait, the weapon does not activate other Deployable Weapons or Equipment.

Ex.: During his Active Turn, a Moran Masai Hunter wants to intimidate an enemy Trooper that is clearly out of his range, dissuading him from carrying out an attack.

## Perimeter Weapons FAQs

Q: Do Troopers with Aerial Special Skill activate Weapons with the Boost Trait?

A: No, as it is not possible to enter Silhouette contact with Aerial Troopers.


### Interaction Note
Related rules: ARO, Face-to-Face, Partial Cover.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**

Perimeter Weapons (CrazyKoalas, MadTraps, WildParrots, FastPandas, and any Deployable with the Perimeter Trait) create a unique type of threat bubble that interacts with core game mechanics in ways different from Mines, Direct Template Weapons, or regular ARO pieces.

**Interaction with AROs**
Perimeter Weapons do not declare AROs. Their “reaction” comes from the Boost Trait or Mine-like detonation rules, which are not ARO declarations and therefore do not participate in Face-to-Face Rolls. This means:
- They cannot be stopped by suppressive fire, Sixth Sense, or Burst modifications.
- Only a successful Dodge (Normal Roll) prevents the detonation.
- The owner does not need LoF, WIP, or BS to trigger them.

**Interaction with Face-to-Face Rolls**
Because Perimeter Weapons do not declare Attacks, they do not enter Face-to-Face interactions. They generate automatic effects once triggered. A target’s Dodge is always a **Normal Roll**, even if the target is simultaneously engaging in a separate Face-to-Face elsewhere.  
This makes Perimeter Weapons extremely reliable at forcing movement taxes or punishing aggressive order expenditure.

**Interaction with Partial Cover**
Cover never applies:
- Boost attacks occur in Silhouette contact after movement.
- Mine-like attacks are Direct Templates, which never grant Partial Cover MODs.
Therefore, Perimeter Weapons are ideal for bypassing defensive positions, heavily armored troops in Cover, or units benefiting from high BS in a firefight.

**Interaction with Camouflage**
- Ignore Camouflage at placement, but doesn't trigger by Marker.

### Functional Comparison

**Perimeter Weapons vs. Mines**
- **Pros of Perimeter Weapons:**
  - Actively pursue the target (Boost) instead of waiting for movement/LoF triggers.
  - Cannot be avoided with clever movement paths—they follow until Silhouette contact unless blocked by terrain.
  - Can throw next to camo marker (but won't affect it)  
  - Can it if ally near

- **Cons of Perimeter Weapons:**
  - Must be placed in the user’s Zone of Control, limiting reach.
  - Terrain obstruction prevents triggering.
  - Boosted weapons detonate immediately and are removed—no persistent area denial.
  - Does not trigger on Marker
  - Is not itself camo or MIM-3
  - Not multi-target


**Perimeter Weapons vs. Direct Template Weapons**
- **Pros:**
  - No BS Attack declaration → cannot be opposed via Face-to-Face.
  - Trigger automatically based on enemy activity; no LoF required.
  - Excellent for area denial or punishing enemies for merely existing within ZoC.
- **Cons:**
  - Single-use (Disposable).
  - Require setup and Order investment to deploy.
  - Do not benefit from modifiers, coordinated orders, or Burst.

### Strategic Notes (Unofficial Commentary)

- **Area Denial:** Perimeter Weapons shape the battlefield more forcefully than Mines because opponents must always assume Boost will chase them immediately. This often forces enemies to spend Orders pre-emptively Dodging before entering ZoC.
- **Screening Attackers:** Placing them behind corners or on objectives punishes any attempt to engage your troops near that space, even with no LoF.
- **Baiting Dodges:** Moving a trooper near an enemy triggers Dodges that may break Suppressive Fire or reposition a target in ways you can exploit.
- **Anti-Skirmisher Tool:** Although they don’t trigger on Camo/Impersonation markers, once revealed, light skirmishers often lack the PH to reliably Dodge.
- **Boost vs. Mines:** Use Boost weapons aggressively to punish enemy advance; use Mines to create static traps and slow repositioning.
