# Smoke Ammunition

Smoke Ammunition is a non-lethal ammunition used to block enemy lines of fire, allowing allied forces to advance and maneuver.

- Smoke Ammunition generates a Zero Visibility Zone (see: Special Terrain, Visibility Conditions ) the size of a Circular Template and with infinite height.

- The Smoke Template remains on the table until the start of the States Phase.

- Smoke Ammunition is a non-offensive ammunition, so it does not require an enemy or, in fact, any trooper at all as a target, and can be thrown at any point on the table.

- Performing an Attack with a weapon with Smoke Ammunition allows the user to make a Face to Face Roll against all enemy Attacks that require a Roll and LoF, and whose LoF passes through the Zero Visibility Zone generated by the Smoke Template.

- Critical hits with Smoke Ammunition have no additional effect, other than winning the Face to Face Roll, if applicable.

- If the Trooper is making a Face to Face Roll against several Attacks, he will need to win every single Face to Face Roll in order to leave the Smoke Template on the table. If he loses any of them (or fails an unopposed Normal Roll ) the Smoke Template is removed in the Effects step of the Order.

- Any enemy in the Area of Effect of the Smoke Template that declares a Dodge ARO will make a Normal Roll, not a Face to Face Roll against the Smoke user.

The Area of Effect of a Template can affect Allied Troopers as long as the Template has no PS Attribute and does not inflict any State.

Multispectral Visors can draw Line of Fire through Zero Visibility Zones, so Troopers equipped with this piece of Equipment do not perform Face to Face Rolls against Smoke Ammunition.

Template Weapons and their Area of Effect are affected by Scenery Elements.

As the Area of Effect of the Smoke Templates has infinite height, they form a cylinder starting from the Template upwards that will be affected by scenery elements. The Area of Effect will be cut horizontally and vertically by such scenery elements (see the example).

The Active Trooper declares Move, Troopers 1, 2, and 3 declare BS Attack as their AROs

As the second half of their Order, the Active Trooper throws a Smoke Grenade, placing a Template on the gaming table to determine which Troopers will be affected.

Trooper 1 is not affected because his LoF does not cross the Template, so he makes a Normal Roll instead of a Face to Face Roll.

The LoF of Trooper 2 does cross the Circular Template, so he will perform a Face to Face Roll between his BS and the Active Trooper's PH. Trooper 3, despite the fact that his LoF crosses the Circular Template, will perform a Normal Roll thanks to his Multispectral Visor, which allows him to draw LoF through the Smoke.

The Circular Smoke Template will remain on the table until the States Phase if the Active Trooper wins the Face to Face Roll against Trooper 2.

**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Simplified Explanation

Smoke creates a **Circular Zero Visibility Zone** that **blocks LoF entirely** unless a special rule overrides this. Because Smoke is **Targetless**, the user does **not need LoF** to throw or launch it, and enemies only get AROs if they have LoF to the *thrower*, not to the landing point.

Inside Smoke:
- Troopers **cannot draw LoF** through it. (except with multispectral visor)
- Therefore, they usually **cannot declare BS Attack AROs**.
- Dodge is allowed because Dodge **does not require LoF** as a requirement to declare (though LoF may matter for FTF interactions).

A Trooper *being shot through Smoke* can **still respond**, but they treat the Zero Visibility Zone as a *Poor Visibility Zone* (–6) when trying to draw LoF back to the attacker.

---
**[UNOFFICIAL COMMENT]**
### Step-by-Step Resolution / Interaction

1. **Active Trooper declares Smoke (Targetless BS Attack).**  
   - Only enemies with LoF to the *active model* may ARO.  
   - No one may ARO “against the landing point” because Smoke is Targetless.

2. **If the Smoke lands and creates a Zero Visibility Zone:**  
   - No LoF can be drawn through it in either direction.  
   - Normal BS Attacks cannot be declared across it unless a model has a rule that bypasses it (e.g., MSV vs Smoke does *not* work; only Eclipse stops MSV entirely, but Smoke already blocks LoF regardless).

3. **Face-to-Face Interactions:**  
   - If both troopers can draw LoF (e.g., before Smoke template is placed), the rolls are Face-to-Face.  
   - Once Smoke is placed, no further LoF exists, so later interactions become **Normal Rolls or impossible**.

4. **ARO Timing:**  
   - If you throw Smoke on first Short Skill, enemies with LoF may ARO normally.  
   - If the Smoke template lands successfully, the Zero Visibility Zone applies **after** resolution of the Order.  
   - If the Active Trooper declares Move as the first Short Skill and enters LoF, then throws Smoke on the second Short Skill, enemies can ARO to the Move.  
   - If the Active Trooper begins the Order *already inside* Smoke, enemies with no LoF get no ARO.

5. **Troopers being attacked through Smoke:**  
   - They cannot normally be attacked except by rules that do not use LoF.  
   - If they are targeted by a BS Attack whose LoF crosses Smoke, they treat the Zone as Poor Visibility (–6) **when trying to look back** at the attacker.

6. Template weapon cannot attack through smoke unless the main target is outside smoke and hitting secondary targets that are in smoke.

### Unofficial Comment (Rules Interaction Analysis)

Smoke is one of the strongest positional tools in Infinity because it:
- Denies LoF completely (stronger than MODs, stronger than Mimetism).
- Allows safe movement or objective grabbing.
- Creates asymmetric situations where the attacker can act without retaliation.
- Enables powerful combos like Smoke + MSV2 shooting against targets outside the Smoke.

**Pros of Smoke:**
- Total LoF denial (except very rare situations like White Noise vs MSV).
- Reliable defense against ARO pieces.
- Allows repositioning without risk.
- Works against virtually all shooting, regardless of weapon or BS value.

**Cons of Smoke:**
- Does nothing against:
  - Intuitive Attack.
  - Sensor or other non-LoF mechanics.
- Requires a PH roll (Grenades) or BS roll (Launchers)—can fail at crucial moments.
- Can hinder your own shooting if not carefully placed.

**Comparison: Smoke vs Eclipse vs White Noise**
- **Smoke:** Blocks LoF for everyone. MSV cannot see through Smoke.  
  Pros: Cheap, common, strong general-purpose defense.  
  Cons: Can hamper friendly MSV shooters.

- **Eclipse:** Same as Smoke but *also* blocks MSV vision.  
  Pros: Bypasses enemy MSV entirely.  
  Cons: More rare, usually on specialized units, and you can't shoot through it with your MSV2 units.

- **White Noise:** Only blocks LoF for MSV users.  
  Pros: Offensive tool to neutralize MSV ARO pieces while allowing non-MSV allies to fire through.  
  Cons: Does not protect against non-MSV enemies.

**Common Tactical Uses:**
- Advancing warbands and CC specialists safely.  
- Enabling Smoke + MSV2 attacks (shooting *out* of Smoke at enemies *outside* Smoke).  
- Blocking enemy firelanes in the first Order of a Turn.  
- Creating safe zones for Specialists to complete objectives.  
- Blocking LoF during a coordinated push.

**Key interaction to remember:**  
Using Smoke to force an enemy into a Normal Roll (or prevent them from rolling at all) is the heart of its power. Proper timing of movement + Smoke placement can completely shut down enemy ARO plans.

Smoke is one of the few mechanics that consistently “rewrites” the battlefield by replacing direct confrontations with controlled engagements. Its interaction with LoF and ARO timing forms the tactical backbone of many factions.
